<Capitular letra="T"/>he point is the basic, indivisible entity constituting the fundamental unit of geometric space. As a <ConceptLink targetId="concepto-primitivo">primitive concept</ConceptLink>, it lacks a prior constructive definition; its mathematical nature is implicitly and relationally determined by the set of axioms satisfied by the geometric structure in which it resides.

<Definicion>
  Given a geometric structure $\mathcal{S} = (\mathcal{P}, \mathcal{L}, \mathcal{Pl}, \mathcal{I}, \mathcal{B}, \equiv)$ satisfying a formal system of axioms $\Phi$, the non-empty set $\mathcal{P}$ constitutes the point domain. A **point** is any element belonging to this carrier set:
  $$
  P \in \mathcal{P}
  $$
  whose properties are uniquely determined by the relations of incidence $\mathcal{I}$, betweenness $\mathcal{B}$, and congruence $\equiv$ prescribed by $\Phi$.
</Definicion>

### Axiomatic characterization by groups

The formal behavior of points is structured across five fundamental axiom groups:

#### Group I: Axioms of incidence
- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-i-1">Axiom I.1</ConceptLink>:** Two distinct points <VisualBind color="musgo" element="pA">$A$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind color="musgo" element="pB">$B$</VisualBind> uniquely determine a <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> $\overleftrightarrow{AB}$.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-i-2">Axiom I.2</ConceptLink>:** Every line contains at least two distinct points.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-i-3">Axiom I.3</ConceptLink>:** In a <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> there exist at least three non-collinear points.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-i-4">Axiom I.4</ConceptLink>:** Three non-collinear points uniquely determine a plane.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-i-5">Axiom I.5</ConceptLink>:** If two points of a line lie in a plane, then all points of that line lie in that plane.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-i-6">Axiom I.6</ConceptLink>:** If two planes share a common point, then they share at least one additional point.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-i-7">Axiom I.7</ConceptLink>:** Space contains at least four points that do not lie in the same plane.

#### Group II: Axioms of order and betweenness
- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-ii-1">Axiom II.1</ConceptLink>:** The <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">betweenness</ConceptLink> relation ($A * B * C$) involves triples of distinct collinear points and is symmetric ($C * B * A$).
- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-ii-2">Axiom II.2</ConceptLink>:** For any two distinct points $A$ and $B$, there exists at least one point $C$ such that $A * B * C$.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-ii-3">Axiom II.3</ConceptLink>:** Of three distinct collinear points, exactly one lies between the other two.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-ii-4">Axiom II.4 (Pasch)</ConceptLink>:** If a line coplanar with a triangle intersects a point on one of its sides, it necessarily intersects a point on one of the other two sides.

#### Group III: Axioms of congruence
- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-iii-1">Axiom III.1</ConceptLink>:** Two points bound a <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink> that can be congruently transported onto any ray from a given origin.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-iii-4">Axiom III.4</ConceptLink>:** Three non-collinear points determine an <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angle</ConceptLink> that can be congruently constructed with respect to a given ray.

#### Group IV: Axiom of parallels
- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-iv-1">Axiom IV.1 (Parallels)</ConceptLink>:** Given a line $\ell$ and a point $P \notin \ell$, there exists at most one coplanar line passing through $P$ that does not intersect $\ell$.

#### Group V: Axioms of continuity
- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-v-1">Axiom V.1 (Archimedes)</ConceptLink>:** Along a line, successive translations of a segment determined by two points can surpass any other specified point.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-v-2">Axiom V.2 (Completeness)</ConceptLink>:** The ordered set of points on a line admits no extension preserving both order and congruence.

### Model dependence and subsystems

The specific axioms a point satisfies depend on the chosen formal subsystem:
- **Incidence Geometry:** Considers solely Group I axioms.
- **Ordered Geometry:** Adds Group II axioms, endowing the point set with order and convexity.
- **Neutral or Absolute Geometry:** Incorporates Group III congruence axioms without assuming the parallel postulate.
- **Euclidean Geometry:** Integrates all five axiom groups.
