<Capitular letra="T" />he **absolute geometry system**, or *neutral geometry*, is the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-axiomatico">axiomatic system</ConceptLink> formed by the groups of <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-incidencia">incidence</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">order</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="continuidad">continuity</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink>, with no <ConceptLink targetId="axioma">axiom</ConceptLink> about <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallels</ConceptLink>. Every <ConceptLink targetId="teorema">theorem</ConceptLink> proved in it holds in <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-euclidiano">Euclidean geometry</ConceptLink> and in <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-hiperbolico">hyperbolic geometry</ConceptLink>.

Introduced by <ConceptLink targetId="bolyai">János Bolyai</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="lobachevski">Nikolai Lobachevsky</ConceptLink> by dispensing with <ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid</ConceptLink>'s fifth postulate, Hilbert gave it axiomatic form in the *Grundlagen der Geometrie*.

<Definicion title="Formal structure">
  An **absolute geometry structure** is a <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-geometrica">structure</ConceptLink> over Hilbert's <ConceptLink targetId="signatura">signature</ConceptLink>
  $$
  \mathcal{S}_{\text{abs}} = (\mathcal{P}, \mathcal{L}, \Pi; \ \mathbf{I}, \ *, \ \cong_{\mathrm{seg}}, \ \cong_{\angle})
  $$
  with <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-disjunto">disjoint</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="dominio">domains</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{P}$, <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{L}$ and <ConceptLink targetId="plano">planes</ConceptLink> $\Pi$, the <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence</ConceptLink> relation $\mathbf{I}$, the ternary <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">betweenness</ConceptLink> relation ($A * B * C$) and the <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink> relations for <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segments</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink>, which satisfies the theory
  $$
  \mathcal{T}_{\text{abs}} = \mathrm{I} \cup \mathrm{II} \cup \mathrm{III} \cup \mathrm{V}

  Neither <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">Axiom IV</ConceptLink> nor its negation belongs to $\mathcal{T}_{\text{abs}}$.

### Groups of axioms

1. **Incidence (I.1–I.8)**: from <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-1">Axiom I.1</ConceptLink> to <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-8">Axiom I.8</ConceptLink>, governing planes and the <ConceptLink targetId="dimension">three-dimensionality</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink>.
2. **Order (II.1–II.4)**: from <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-1">Axiom II.1</ConceptLink> to <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-4">Axiom II.4</ConceptLink> (<ConceptLink targetId="pasch">Pasch</ConceptLink>), governing $A * B * C$ for <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segments</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta">rays</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="semiplano">half-planes</ConceptLink>.
3. **Congruence (III.1–III.5)**: from <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-1">Axiom III.1</ConceptLink> to <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-5">Axiom III.5</ConceptLink>, governing segmental and angular transport and the SAS criterion.
4. **Continuity (V.1–V.2)**: the axioms of <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-arquimedes">Archimedes</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-completitud">line completeness</ConceptLink>, which assign <ConceptLink targetId="longitud">length</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="medida-angular">angle measure</ConceptLink>.

### Theorems of absolute geometry

The theorems of absolute geometry do not depend on the parallel postulate:

- **Congruence criteria**: the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-triangulo-isosceles">isosceles triangle theorem</ConceptLink> and the <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia-triangulos">triangle congruence</ConceptLink> criteria <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-lal">SAS</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-ala">ASA</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-lll">SSS</ConceptLink>.
- **Exterior angle and parallels**: the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-angulo-exterior">exterior angle theorem</ConceptLink> —where each <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-exterior">exterior angle</ConceptLink> of a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink> exceeds each non-<ConceptLink targetId="angulos-adyacentes">adjacent</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-interior">interior angle</ConceptLink>— and the existence of at least one parallel via a <ConceptLink targetId="perpendicular-comun">common perpendicular</ConceptLink> (<ConceptLink targetId="teorema-angulos-alternos-internos">alternate interior angles</ConceptLink>).
- **Angle sum**: the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-saccheri-legendre">Saccheri–Legendre theorem</ConceptLink> states that $|\alpha| + |\beta| + |\gamma| \le 180^\circ$ in every triangle using <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-arquimedes">Axiom V.1</ConceptLink>.

### Extensions, models and consistency

The system does not determine the uniqueness of parallels. <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">Axiom IV</ConceptLink> yields the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-euclidiano">Euclidean system</ConceptLink>; the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-hiperbolico">hyperbolic parallel axiom</ConceptLink> yields the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-hiperbolico">hyperbolic system</ConceptLink>.

Its canonical three-dimensional models are Cartesian space $\mathbb{R}^3$ (<ConceptLink targetId="modelo-cartesiano">Cartesian model</ConceptLink>) and the ball of Poincaré or <ConceptLink targetId="klein">Felix Klein</ConceptLink> (<ConceptLink targetId="modelo-poincare">Poincaré disk</ConceptLink> in the plane). Both prove the <ConceptLink targetId="consistencia-logica">consistency</ConceptLink> of the system and the <ConceptLink targetId="independencia-axiomatica">independence</ConceptLink> of the parallel axiom.
