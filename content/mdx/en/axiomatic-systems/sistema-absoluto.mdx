<Capitular letra="T" />he system of absolute geometry —also known as *neutral geometry*— encompasses the common deductive core shared by Euclidean and hyperbolic geometries. Founded upon the foundational investigations of <ConceptLink targetId="bolyai">János Bolyai</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="lobachevski">Nikolai Lobachevsky</ConceptLink>, and formalized by <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink>, it gathers all synthetic axioms of incidence, order, congruence, and continuity, while deliberately withholding any hypothesis regarding parallelism.

<Separador />

### Constituent axiomatic groups

Absolute geometry is structured upon four fundamental axiomatic pillars:

1. **<ConceptLink targetId="sistema-incidencia" isDependency={true}>Incidence axioms (I.1 to I.8)</ConceptLink>**: Govern containment and intersections among <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink>, and <ConceptLink targetId="plano">planes</ConceptLink> in space.
2. **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-1" isDependency={true}>Order axioms (II.1 to II.4)</ConceptLink>**: Formalize the ternary <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">betweenness</ConceptLink> relation ($A\text{-}B\text{-}C$), establishing the definition of line <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink> $\overline{AB}$, half-planes, and <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-4">Pasch's axiom</ConceptLink>.
3. **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-1" isDependency={true}>Congruence axioms (III.1 to III.5)</ConceptLink>**: Define metric rigidity for segments ($\overline{AB} \cong \overline{CD}$) and <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink>, culminating in the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-5">SAS triangle congruence criterion</ConceptLink>.
4. **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-arquimedes" isDependency={true}>Continuity axioms (V.1 and V.2)</ConceptLink>**: Incorporate the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-arquimedes">Archimedean axiom</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-completitud">line completeness</ConceptLink> (Dedekind cut property), guaranteeing bijective correspondence between geometric lines and the real field $\mathbb{R}$.

<Separador />

### Theorems valid in absolute geometry

Any proposition proved within this system remains universally valid regardless of spatial curvature:

- **<ConceptLink targetId="teorema-triangulo-isosceles">Isosceles triangle theorem</ConceptLink>**: In any triangle with two congruent sides, the angles opposite those sides are congruent.
- **Exterior angle theorem**: In any <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink>, the measure of an exterior angle is strictly greater than the measure of either non-adjacent interior angle.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-ala">ASA and SSS congruence criteria</ConceptLink>**: Unambiguous determination of triangles via three corresponding parts.
- **Existence of parallel lines**: Through any point not lying on a given line, there passes at least one line that does not intersect it (proved constructively via the common perpendicular).

### Models and metamathematical properties

- **Neutrality of angle sum**: The system proves that the angle sum of a triangle satisfies $\sum_{i=1}^3 \alpha_i \le 180^\circ$, but cannot decide whether equality holds strictly without a parallel postulate.
- **Geometric bifurcation**: Appending the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">Euclidean parallel axiom</ConceptLink> yields <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-euclidiano">Euclidean geometry</ConceptLink>; appending the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-hiperbolico">hyperbolic parallel axiom</ConceptLink> yields <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-hiperbolico">hyperbolic geometry</ConceptLink>.
- **Model verification**: Both the <ConceptLink targetId="modelo-cartesiano">Cartesian plane $\mathbb{R}^2$</ConceptLink> and the <ConceptLink targetId="modelo-poincare">Poincaré disk $\mathbb{D}^2$</ConceptLink> fully verify the absolute geometry system.
