<Capitular letra="T" />he system of hyperbolic geometry —discovered independently by <ConceptLink targetId="lobachevski">Nikolai Lobachevsky</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="bolyai">János Bolyai</ConceptLink>— constitutes the first non-Euclidean geometric theory developed formally. It arises from adjoining the hyperbolic parallel postulate to the foundational base of <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>, endowing space with a constant negative Gaussian curvature ($K < 0$).

<Separador />

### Axiomatic structure

The hyperbolic system unifies the entire neutral deductive apparatus with the plural parallel postulate:

1. **Base of <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>**: Incorporates all axioms of <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-incidencia">incidence</ConceptLink> (I.1-I.8), <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-1">order</ConceptLink> (II.1-II.4), <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-1">congruence</ConceptLink> (III.1-III.5), and continuity (V.1-V.2).
2. **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-hiperbolico" isDependency={true}>Hyperbolic parallel axiom (Lobachevsky-Bolyai axiom)</ConceptLink>**: Given a <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> $l$ and an external <ConceptLink targetId="punto">point</ConceptLink> $P \notin l$ in a <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{P}$, there exist at least two distinct lines $l_1, l_2$ passing through $P$ that do not intersect $l$:
   $$
   \forall l \in \mathcal{L}, \forall P \in \mathcal{P} \setminus l, \ \exists l_1, l_2 \in \mathcal{L} \ \bigl(l_1 \neq l_2 \land P \in l_1 \cap l_2 \land l_1 \cap l = \emptyset \land l_2 \cap l = \emptyset\bigr)
   $$

### Characteristic theorems of hyperbolic geometry

Constant negative curvature introduces profound structural differences compared to Euclidean space:

- **Angular defect and triangular bound**: The sum of interior <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> of any hyperbolic <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink> is strictly less than $180^\circ$:
  $$
  \alpha + \beta + \gamma < 180^\circ \quad (\alpha + \beta + \gamma < \pi)
  $$
- **Area proportionality**: The hyperbolic area of a triangle is directly proportional to its angular defect $\delta(\triangle) = \pi - (\alpha + \beta + \gamma)$, yielding an absolute upper bound for triangle areas ($\text{Area}(\triangle) < \pi R^2$).
- **Absence of rectangles and strict similarity**: No quadrilaterals with four right angles exist (Saccheri and Lambert quadrilaterals have acute summit angles). Two triangles with equal corresponding angles are necessarily congruent (AAA criterion).
- **Lobachevsky's angle of parallelism**: The limiting angle $\Pi(x)$ between the perpendicular and an asymptotic line depends on the distance $x$ via the formula $\tan(\Pi(x)/2) = e^{-x/k}$.

### Models and relative consistency

- **<ConceptLink targetId="modelo-poincare">Poincaré disk model $\mathbb{D}^2$</ConceptLink>**: The conformal analytical model on the unit disk $\{z \in \mathbb{C} : |z| < 1\}$ endowed with Riemannian metric $ds^2 = \frac{4|dz|^2}{(1-|z|^2)^2}$.
- **Equiconsistency**: Eugenio Beltrami, Henri Poincaré, and Felix Klein demonstrated that the consistency of hyperbolic geometry is equivalent to that of Euclidean geometry and the real number continuum $\mathbb{R}$.
