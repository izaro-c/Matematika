<Capitular letra="T" />he **hyperbolic geometry system** is the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-axiomatico">axiomatic system</ConceptLink> obtained by adding to <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink> the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-hiperbolico">hyperbolic parallel axiom</ConceptLink>: through a <ConceptLink targetId="punto">point</ConceptLink> not on a <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> there pass, in the <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> they determine, at least two distinct lines <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallel</ConceptLink> to it.

Developed independently by <ConceptLink targetId="lobachevski">Nikolai Lobachevsky</ConceptLink> (1829) and <ConceptLink targetId="bolyai">János Bolyai</ConceptLink> (1832), with earlier ideas by <ConceptLink targetId="gauss">Carl Friedrich Gauss</ConceptLink>, it was the first <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-no-euclidiana">non-Euclidean geometry</ConceptLink>. Its relative consistency was established later through geometric models.

<Definicion title="Formal structure">
  A **hyperbolic structure** is an absolute geometry structure $\mathcal{S}_{\text{abs}} = (\mathcal{P}, \mathcal{L}, \Pi; \ \mathbf{I}, \ *, \ \cong_{\mathrm{seg}}, \ \cong_{\angle})$ that also satisfies the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-hiperbolico">hyperbolic parallel axiom</ConceptLink>, so that $\mathcal{T}_{\text{hip}} = \mathrm{I} \cup \mathrm{II} \cup \mathrm{III} \cup \mathrm{IV}_{\text{hip}} \cup \mathrm{V}$. With planes $\sigma \in \Pi$ and the <ConceptLink targetId="traza">trace</ConceptLink> expressing <ConceptLink targetId="coplanaridad">coplanarity</ConceptLink>:
  $$
  \forall \sigma \in \Pi \ \forall \ell \in \mathcal{L} \ \forall P \in \mathcal{P} \ \Bigl( \bigl(\operatorname{tr}(\ell) \subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\sigma) \land P \in \operatorname{tr}(\sigma) \setminus \operatorname{tr}(\ell)\bigr) \implies \exists m, n \in \mathcal{L} \ \bigl( m \neq n \land \operatorname{tr}(m) \cup \operatorname{tr}(n) \subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\sigma) \land P \,\mathbf{I}\, m \land P \,\mathbf{I}\, n \land m \parallel \ell \land n \parallel \ell \bigr) \Bigr)
  $$
</Definicion>

### Characteristic theorems

- **Angle sum and defect**: every <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink> has angle sum less than two <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto">right angles</ConceptLink>. With <ConceptLink targetId="medida-angular">angle measure</ConceptLink>, its <ConceptLink targetId="defecto-angular">defect</ConceptLink> is
  $$
  \delta(\triangle ABC) = 180^\circ - \bigl(|\alpha| + |\beta| + |\gamma|\bigr) > 0
  $$
- **Length constant and area**: there is a characteristic <ConceptLink targetId="longitud">length</ConceptLink> $k > 0$ such that the <ConceptLink targetId="area">area</ConceptLink> of a triangle is proportional to its defect: $\operatorname{A}(\triangle ABC) = k^2 \frac{\pi}{180^\circ} \delta(\triangle ABC) < \pi k^2$.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="angulo-paralelismo">Angle of parallelism</ConceptLink>**: let $Q$ be the foot of the <ConceptLink targetId="perpendicular">perpendicular</ConceptLink> from $P$ to $\ell$ and $x = |PQ|$. The two <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas-limite">limiting parallels</ConceptLink> form with the <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta">ray</ConceptLink> $\overrightarrow{PQ}$ an <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-agudo">acute</ConceptLink> angle $\theta(x)$ (Lobachevsky's formula $\tan\frac{\theta(x)}{2} = e^{-x/k}$). The other parallels are <ConceptLink targetId="ultraparalelas">ultraparallel</ConceptLink>, with a unique <ConceptLink targetId="perpendicular-comun">common perpendicular</ConceptLink>.
- **No similarity**: two triangles with congruent corresponding angles are <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia-triangulos">congruent</ConceptLink> (AAA criterion); there is no <ConceptLink targetId="semejanza">similarity</ConceptLink> with scale change.
- **No rectangles**: no <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrilatero">quadrilateral</ConceptLink> has four right angles (<ConceptLink targetId="rectangulo">rectangles</ConceptLink>); in a <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrilatero-saccheri">Saccheri quadrilateral</ConceptLink> the summit angles are <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-agudo">acute</ConceptLink>.
- **Non-transitive parallelism**: parallelism is not an <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-equivalencia">equivalence relation</ConceptLink>; two lines parallel to a third may intersect or be ultraparallel.

### Models and relative consistency

<ConceptLink targetId="beltrami">Eugenio Beltrami</ConceptLink> (1868), <ConceptLink targetId="klein">Felix Klein</ConceptLink> (1871) and <ConceptLink targetId="poincare">Henri Poincaré</ConceptLink> (1882) built their models. The <ConceptLink targetId="modelo-poincare">Poincaré disk</ConceptLink> $\mathbb{D}^2$ models the planar axioms, and the ball in $\mathbb{R}^3$ the spatial system. These models prove that the hyperbolic system is <ConceptLink targetId="consistencia-logica">consistent</ConceptLink> if <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-euclidiano">Euclidean geometry</ConceptLink> is, and the <ConceptLink targetId="independencia-axiomatica">independence</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">Axiom IV</ConceptLink>.

In the analytic model, the plane has constant <ConceptLink targetId="curvatura">curvature</ConceptLink> $-1/k^2$. With the <ConceptLink targetId="continuidad">continuity axioms</ConceptLink>, the system is <ConceptLink targetId="categoricidad">categorical</ConceptLink>: all its models are <ConceptLink targetId="isomorfismo">isomorphic</ConceptLink>.
