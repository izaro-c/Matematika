<Capitular letra="T" />he Euclidean axiomatic system constitutes the classical formal framework of ordinary planar and spatial geometry. It is obtained by adjoining <ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid's</ConceptLink> parallel postulate (in Playfair's uniqueness formulation) to the foundational base of <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>, establishing zero spatial curvature and admitting similar figures at arbitrary scales.

<Separador />

### Axiomatic constitution

The Euclidean system incorporates all Hilbertian axiomatic groups alongside the uniqueness of parallels:

1. **Base of <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>**: Gathers all axioms of <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-incidencia">incidence</ConceptLink> (I.1-I.8), <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-1">order</ConceptLink> (II.1-II.4), <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-1">congruence</ConceptLink> (III.1-III.5), and continuity (V.1-V.2).
2. **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides" isDependency={true}>Euclidean parallel axiom (IV.1 / Playfair)</ConceptLink>**: Given a <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> $l$ and a <ConceptLink targetId="punto">point</ConceptLink> $P \notin l$ in a <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{P}$, there exists exactly one line $l'$ passing through $P$ that does not intersect $l$:
   $$
   \forall l \in \mathcal{L}, \forall P \in \mathcal{P} \setminus l, \ \exists! l' \in \mathcal{L} \ (P \in l' \land l \cap l' = \emptyset)
   $$

<Separador />

### Fundamental theorems of the system

The Euclidean parallel axiom unlocks the hallmark theorems of synthetic geometry:

- **<ConceptLink targetId="teorema-suma-angulos-triangulo">Constant angle sum</ConceptLink>**: The sum of interior <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> in any <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink> is exactly $180^\circ$ ($\pi\text{ rad}$).
- **<ConceptLink targetId="teorema-tales">Thales's theorem and similarity</ConceptLink>**: Parallel lines cut by transversals intercept proportional line <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segments</ConceptLink>, establishing the concept of similar figures of distinct sizes without angular distortion.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="teorema-pitagoras">Pythagorean theorem</ConceptLink>**: In every <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo-rectangulo">right-angled triangle</ConceptLink>, the area of the square on the <ConceptLink targetId="hipotenusa">hypotenuse</ConceptLink> equals the sum of the areas of the squares on the <ConceptLink targetId="cateto">legs</ConceptLink> ($a^2 + b^2 = c^2$).
- **Existence of parallelograms and rectangles**: Quadrilaterals with four right angles and congruent opposite sides exist.

<Separador />

### Canonical model and consistency

- **<ConceptLink targetId="modelo-cartesiano">Cartesian model $\mathbb{R}^n$</ConceptLink>**: Serves as the canonical analytical model of Euclidean geometry, equipped with the standard inner product $\langle u, v \rangle = \sum u_i v_i$.
- **Consistency and categoricity**: Hilbert proved that the consistency of the Euclidean axiomatic system is equiconsistent with the arithmetic of the real field $\mathbb{R}$. With the continuity axioms included, the system is categorical in every fixed dimension.
