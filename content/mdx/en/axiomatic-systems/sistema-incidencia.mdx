<Capitular letra="T" />he **incidence system** forms the first deductive stratum of synthetic geometry. It establishes the basic rules of membership among the <ConceptLink targetId="concepto-primitivo">primitive concepts</ConceptLink> of space (<ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink>, and <ConceptLink targetId="plano">planes</ConceptLink>), entirely disregarding distances, angular measures, or ordering.

<Definicion title="Formal Structure">
  An **incidence space** is a <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-geometrica">structure</ConceptLink> $(\mathcal{P}, \mathcal{L}, \Pi, \mathbf{I})$ where $\mathcal{P}$, $\mathcal{L}$, and $\Pi$ are non-empty, pairwise disjoint sets of points, lines, and planes, and $\mathbf{I}$ is the <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence relation</ConceptLink>:
  $$
  \mathbf{I} \subseteq (\mathcal{P} \times \mathcal{L}) \cup (\mathcal{P} \times \Pi) \cup (\mathcal{L} \times \Pi)
  $$
  satisfying the eight axioms of Hilbert's Group I.
</Definicion>

<Separador />

### Incidence Axioms (Hilbert's Group I)

The system consists of eight foundational axioms:

- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-1">Axiom I.1 (Determination of the line)</ConceptLink>:** Two distinct points determine a unique line passing through both.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-2">Axiom I.2 (Points on a line)</ConceptLink>:** Every line contains at least two distinct points.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-3">Axiom I.3 (Non-collinear points)</ConceptLink>:** There exist at least three points not belonging to the same line.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-4">Axiom I.4 (Determination of the plane)</ConceptLink>:** Three non-collinear points determine a unique plane containing them.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-5">Axiom I.5 (Non-collinear points on a plane)</ConceptLink>:** Every plane contains at least three non-collinear points.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-6">Axiom I.6 (Line inclusion in a plane)</ConceptLink>:** If two points of a line lie in a plane, the entire line is contained in that plane.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-7">Axiom I.7 (Intersection of planes)</ConceptLink>:** If two distinct planes share a point, their intersection is precisely a line.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-8">Axiom I.8 (Three-dimensional space)</ConceptLink>:** There exist at least four points not belonging to the same plane.

<Separador />

### Models of the incidence system

Requiring neither continuity nor metric, the incidence system admits diverse realizations:
- **Finite geometries:** Such as the <ConceptLink targetId="modelo-tres-puntos">three-point model</ConceptLink> or the <ConceptLink targetId="modelo-fano">Fano plane</ConceptLink>, with finitely many points and lines.
- **Continuous geometries:** Such as the real affine space $\mathbb{R}^3$ (<ConceptLink targetId="modelo-cartesiano">Cartesian model</ConceptLink>).
