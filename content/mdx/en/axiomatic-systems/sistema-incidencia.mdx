<Capitular letra="T" />he **incidence system** constitutes the foundational deductive layer of <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-sintetica">synthetic geometry</ConceptLink> formulated by <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink>. It establishes the formal rules of containment and mutual determination among the <ConceptLink targetId="concepto-primitivo">primitive concepts</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink> (<ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink>, and <ConceptLink targetId="plano">planes</ConceptLink>), setting aside metric notions of <ConceptLink targetId="distancia">distance</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angle</ConceptLink>, or <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">order</ConceptLink> relations.

Within this framework, lines and planes are not defined as sets of points, but as independent primitive entities connected by <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence relations</ConceptLink>. The points incident with a geometric object form its trace in <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal structure">
  In <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-modelos">model theory</ConceptLink>, an **incidence space** is a <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-geometrica">multi-sorted relational structure</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{S}_{\text{inc}} = (\mathcal{P}, \mathcal{L}, \Pi; \, \mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}}, \mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{P}\Pi})$ over a <ConceptLink targetId="universo-discurso">universe of discourse</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{U} = \mathcal{P} \sqcup \mathcal{L} \sqcup \Pi$ consisting of three <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-disjunto">disjoint</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="dominio">domains</ConceptLink>:
  - $\mathcal{P}$: primitive domain of <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink>.
  - $\mathcal{L}$: primitive domain of <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink>.
  - $\Pi$: primitive domain of <ConceptLink targetId="plano">planes</ConceptLink>.
  - $\mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}} \subseteq \mathcal{P} \times \mathcal{L}$ and $\mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{P}\Pi} \subseteq \mathcal{P} \times \Pi$: point-line and point-plane <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence relations</ConceptLink> (commonly denoted by $\mathbf{I}$).

  The structure satisfies Hilbert's Group I theory ($\mathcal{S}_{\text{inc}} \models \mathcal{T}_{\text{incidence}}$), where the point trace of a line $\ell \in \mathcal{L}$ or plane $\pi \in \Pi$ is:
  $$
  \operatorname{tr}(\ell) = \{ P \in \mathcal{P} \mid P \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell \}, \quad \operatorname{tr}(\pi) = \{ P \in \mathcal{P} \mid P \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi \}
  $$
</Definicion>

<Separador />

### Plane and spatial geometry

The incidence system is formulated across two dimensional levels:

- **Plane ($\dim = 2$):** Axioms <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-1">I.1</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-2">I.2</ConceptLink>, and <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-3">I.3</ConceptLink> suffice to establish two-dimensional geometry, ensuring the determination of lines by point pairs and the existence of non-<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> configurations.
- **Space ($\dim = 3$):** Axioms <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-4">I.4</ConceptLink> to <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-8">I.8</ConceptLink> introduce planes, their determination, and the non-<ConceptLink targetId="coplanaridad">coplanarity</ConceptLink> of four points to establish the three-dimensionality of space.

<Separador />

### Models and consistency

The formal consistency of the system is demonstrated through the existence of diverse models:
- **Finite models:** Discrete combinatorial geometries such as the <ConceptLink targetId="modelo-tres-puntos">three-point model</ConceptLink> or the <ConceptLink targetId="modelo-fano">Fano plane</ConceptLink>.
- **Continuous models:** The classical real affine space $\mathbb{R}^3$ (<ConceptLink targetId="modelo-cartesiano">Cartesian model</ConceptLink>).

Without order or continuity axioms, the pure incidence system is non-categorical and admits structures of both finite and infinite cardinalities.
