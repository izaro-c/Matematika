<Capitular letra="T" />he incidence system constitutes the first deductive stratum of formal synthetic geometry. It establishes primitive rules of membership and containment among undefined primitive entities of space (<ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink>, and <ConceptLink targetId="plano">planes</ConceptLink>), abstracting away any metric notion of distance, linear ordering, or angular congruence.

<Separador />

### Formal structure of the system

Formally, a three-dimensional incidence space is defined as a 4-tuple $(\mathcal{P}, \mathcal{L}, \mathcal{E}, \mathcal{I})$, where $\mathcal{P}$ denotes a non-empty set of points, $\mathcal{L}$ a set of lines, $\mathcal{E}$ a set of planes, and $\mathcal{I}$ the binary incidence relation:

$$
\mathcal{I} \subseteq (\mathcal{P} \times \mathcal{L}) \cup (\mathcal{P} \times \mathcal{E}) \cup (\mathcal{L} \times \mathcal{E})
$$

The system governs elementary spatial behavior through Hilbert's eight incidence axioms:

- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-1" isDependency={true}>Axiom I.1 (Line through two points)</ConceptLink>**: For any two distinct points $A, B \in \mathcal{P}$, there exists a unique line $l \in \mathcal{L}$ such that $A \mathcal{I} l$ and $B \mathcal{I} l$.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-2" isDependency={true}>Axiom I.2 (Points on a line)</ConceptLink>**: Every line $l \in \mathcal{L}$ is incident with at least two distinct points in $\mathcal{P}$.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-3" isDependency={true}>Axiom I.3 (Non-collinear points)</ConceptLink>**: There exist at least three points in $\mathcal{P}$ that do not lie on a single line.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-4" isDependency={true}>Axiom I.4 (Plane through three points)</ConceptLink>**: For any three non-collinear points $A, B, C \in \mathcal{P}$, there exists a unique plane $\pi \in \mathcal{E}$ incident with all three.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-5" isDependency={true}>Axiom I.5 (Non-collinear points in a plane)</ConceptLink>**: Every plane $\pi \in \mathcal{E}$ contains at least three non-collinear points.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-6" isDependency={true}>Axiom I.6 (Line inclusion in a plane)</ConceptLink>**: If two distinct points of a line $l$ belong to a plane $\pi$, then the entire line $l$ is contained in $\pi$.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-7" isDependency={true}>Axiom I.7 (Intersection of planes)</ConceptLink>**: If two distinct planes share a point, their intersection is an entire straight line.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-8" isDependency={true}>Axiom I.8 (Three-dimensional space)</ConceptLink>**: There exist at least four points in $\mathcal{P}$ that do not lie in the same plane.

### Models and representations

The consistency and flexibility of the incidence system are illustrated through various discrete and continuous structures:

- **<ConceptLink targetId="modelo-tres-puntos">Three-point model</ConceptLink>**: The minimal finite planar geometry consisting of 3 points and 3 lines.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="modelo-fano">Fano plane</ConceptLink>**: The finite projective geometry of order 2 with 7 points and 7 lines.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="modelo-cartesiano">Cartesian model $\mathbb{R}^3$</ConceptLink>**: Standard three-dimensional affine space governed by linear algebraic equations.

<Separador />

### Metamathematical properties

- **Logical independence**: Each incidence axiom is logically independent of the others within relational calculus.
- **Metric incompleteness**: The system does not fix any metric or topology, allowing direct extension to <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-euclidiano">Euclidean geometry</ConceptLink>, and <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-hiperbolico">hyperbolic geometry</ConceptLink>.
