<Capitular letra="T" />he **incidence system** is the first group of axioms of <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-hilbertiana">Hilbertian geometry</ConceptLink>, consisting of axioms I.1–I.8. It governs <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence</ConceptLink> among the <ConceptLink targetId="concepto-primitivo">primitive concepts</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="punto">point</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink>, and <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink>, without recourse to <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">betweenness</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink>, or metric notions such as <ConceptLink targetId="distancia">distance</ConceptLink>.

Lines and planes are not defined as sets of points, but as primitive objects of different sorts linked to points by the incidence relation. The points incident with a line or a plane form its <ConceptLink targetId="traza">point trace</ConceptLink>, which allows set-theoretic language to be used without confusing sorts.

<Definicion title="Formal structure">
  An **incidence space** is a <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-geometrica">many-sorted relational structure</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{S}_{\text{inc}} = (\mathcal{P}, \mathcal{L}, \Pi; \, \mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}}, \mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{P}\Pi})$, in the sense of <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-modelos">model theory</ConceptLink>, whose <ConceptLink targetId="universo-discurso">universe of discourse</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{U} = \mathcal{P} \sqcup \mathcal{L} \sqcup \Pi$ consists of three <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-disjunto">disjoint</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="dominio">domains</ConceptLink>:
  - $\mathcal{P}$: domain of <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink>.
  - $\mathcal{L}$: domain of <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink>.
  - $\Pi$: domain of <ConceptLink targetId="plano">planes</ConceptLink>.
  - $\mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}} \subseteq \mathcal{P} \times \mathcal{L}$ and $\mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{P}\Pi} \subseteq \mathcal{P} \times \Pi$: point-line and point-plane <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence</ConceptLink> relations, both denoted $\mathbf{I}$.

  The structure satisfies axioms I.1–I.8 ($\mathcal{S}_{\text{inc}} \models \mathcal{T}_{\text{inc}}$). The <ConceptLink targetId="traza">point trace</ConceptLink> of a line $\ell \in \mathcal{L}$ or of a plane $\pi \in \Pi$ is:
  $$
  \operatorname{tr}(\ell) = \{ P \in \mathcal{P} \mid P \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell \}, \quad \operatorname{tr}(\pi) = \{ P \in \mathcal{P} \mid P \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi \}
  $$
</Definicion>

<Separador />

### Planar part and spatial part

- **Planar part:** axioms <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-1">I.1</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-2">I.2</ConceptLink>, and <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-3">I.3</ConceptLink> speak only of points and lines: they determine lines by pairs of points and guarantee the existence of non-<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> points.
- **Spatial part:** axioms <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-4">I.4</ConceptLink> to <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-8">I.8</ConceptLink> introduce planes, their determination, the relation between lines and planes, and the existence of four non-<ConceptLink targetId="coplanaridad">coplanar</ConceptLink> points; I.7 and I.8 jointly fix the <ConceptLink targetId="dimension">dimension</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink> at three.

<Separador />

### Models and consistency

The <ConceptLink targetId="consistencia-logica">consistency</ConceptLink> of the system is proved by exhibiting models:
- **Finite model:** the <ConceptLink targetId="modelo-tetraedro">tetrahedron model</ConceptLink>, with four points, the six lines determined by their pairs, and the four planes determined by their triples, satisfies I.1–I.8. So does the finite <ConceptLink targetId="espacio-proyectivo">projective space</ConceptLink> $\mathrm{PG}(3, 2)$, with 15 points.
- **Continuous model:** Cartesian space $\mathbb{R}^3$ (<ConceptLink targetId="modelo-cartesiano-espacial">spatial Cartesian model</ConceptLink>).

The <ConceptLink targetId="modelo-tres-puntos">three-point model</ConceptLink>, the <ConceptLink targetId="modelo-fano">Fano plane</ConceptLink>, and the <ConceptLink targetId="modelo-cartesiano">Cartesian plane</ConceptLink> have no planes: they only model the planar part I.1–I.3. Lacking <ConceptLink targetId="orden">order</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="continuidad">continuity</ConceptLink> axioms, the incidence system is not <ConceptLink targetId="categoricidad">categorical</ConceptLink>: it admits non-<ConceptLink targetId="isomorfismo">isomorphic</ConceptLink> finite and infinite models.
