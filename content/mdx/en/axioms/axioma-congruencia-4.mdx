<Capitular letra="G" />iven any <ConceptLink targetId="angulo" isDependency={true}>angle</ConceptLink>, a <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta" isDependency={true}>ray</ConceptLink> lying on a <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> in a given <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink>, and a <ConceptLink targetId="semiplano" isDependency={true}>half-plane</ConceptLink> bounded by that line, there exists exactly one ray forming an angle <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink> to the given angle in that half-plane.

This postulate formalizes the unique transport of angular openings to any position and orientation in <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink>, without relying on numerical metric scales or trigonometric functions.

<Definicion title="Axiom III.4 (Angle Transport)">
Given an <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angle</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="angleAOB" color="terracota">$\angle(\overrightarrow{OA}, \overrightarrow{OB})$</VisualBind> with <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertex</ConceptLink> at <ConceptLink targetId="punto">point</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="pO" color="terracota">$O$</VisualBind> determined by <ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">non-collinear</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta">rays</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="rayOA" color="ocre">$\overrightarrow{OA}$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="rayOB" color="ocre">$\overrightarrow{OB}$</VisualBind> in the primitive <ConceptLink targetId="dominio">domain</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{P}$, a ray <VisualBind element="rayOOAA" color="ocre">$\overrightarrow{O'A'}$</VisualBind> with <ConceptLink targetId="punto">origin</ConceptLink> at <VisualBind element="pOO" color="terracota">$O'$</VisualBind> on a <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="r" color="pizarra">$r'$</VisualBind> in a <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> $\alpha'$, and a <ConceptLink targetId="semiplano">half-plane</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="Semiplano" color="mora">$\mathcal{H} \subset \alpha'$</VisualBind> bounded by $r'$, there exists a unique ray <VisualBind element="rayOOBB" color="pavo">$\overrightarrow{O'B'}$</VisualBind> with origin at $O'$ such that $\operatorname{tr}(\overrightarrow{O'B'}) \setminus \{O'\} \subset \mathcal{H}$ and the angle <VisualBind element="angleAOBPrime" color="terracota">$\angle(\overrightarrow{O'A'}, \overrightarrow{O'B'})$</VisualBind> is <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruent</ConceptLink> to the original angle (<VisualBind element="angles" color="terracota">$\angle AOB \cong \angle A'O'B'$</VisualBind>):

$$
\forall \angle AOB, \ \forall \overrightarrow{O'A'} \subset r' \subset \alpha', \ \forall \mathcal{H} \in \operatorname{Semiplanos}(r', \alpha') \ \Biggl( \exists! \overrightarrow{O'B'} \subset (\mathcal{H} \cup \{O'\}) \ \Bigl( \angle AOB \cong \angle A'O'B' \Bigr) \Biggr)
$$

Furthermore, every angle satisfies the <ConceptLink targetId="reflexividad">reflexivity</ConceptLink> of congruence: $\angle AOB \cong \angle AOB$ and $\angle(\overrightarrow{OA}, \overrightarrow{OB}) \cong \angle(\overrightarrow{OB}, \overrightarrow{OA})$.
</Definicion>
