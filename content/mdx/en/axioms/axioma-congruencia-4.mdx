<Capitular letra="T" />he fourth <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink> axiom of <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">Hilbert</ConceptLink> permits the transport of angular openings in the <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink>. It guarantees that any <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angle</ConceptLink> can be uniquely laid off into a specified half-plane starting from a reference <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta">ray</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="">
  Given an <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angle</ConceptLink> $\angle (h, k)$, a <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta">ray</ConceptLink> $h'$ on line $a'$, and a half-plane $H'$ bounded by $a'$, there exists a unique ray $k'$ in $H'$ sharing the same vertex as $h'$ such that angle $\angle (h, k)$ is <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruent</ConceptLink> to $\angle (h', k')$:
  $$
  \forall \angle (h, k), \forall h' \subset a', \forall H' \implies \exists! k' \subset H' \ (\angle (h, k) \cong \angle (h', k'))
  $$
  Furthermore, every angle is congruent to itself: $\angle (h, k) \cong \angle (h, k)$.
</Definicion>

### Axiomatic significance

This axiom acts as the angular counterpart to segment transport. It allows comparing angular openings synthetically without relying on degree measurements or trigonometric functions in <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-absoluta">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>.
