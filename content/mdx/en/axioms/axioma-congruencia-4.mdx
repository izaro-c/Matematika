<Capitular letra="G" />iven an <ConceptLink targetId="angulo" isDependency={true}>angle</ConceptLink>, a <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta" isDependency={true}>ray</ConceptLink>, and one of the two <ConceptLink targetId="semiplano" isDependency={true}>half-planes</ConceptLink> that the supporting <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink> of that ray determines in a <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink>, there exists exactly one ray with the same <ConceptLink targetId="origen-semirrecta">origin</ConceptLink>, lying in that half-plane, that forms with the first an angle <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink> to the given one. Moreover, every angle is congruent to itself.

This axiom makes it possible to transport angles to any position in <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink> without resorting to numerical measures or trigonometric functions.

<Definicion title="Axiom III.4 (Angle Transport)">
Let <VisualBind element="nonReflexAngleAOB" color="terracota">$\angle AOB = \{\overrightarrow{OA}, \overrightarrow{OB}\}$</VisualBind> be an <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angle</ConceptLink> with <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertex</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="pO" color="terracota">$O$</VisualBind> and <ConceptLink targetId="lado">sides</ConceptLink> the <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta">rays</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="rayOA" color="ocre">$\overrightarrow{OA}$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="rayOB" color="ocre">$\overrightarrow{OB}$</VisualBind>; let $\sigma$ be a <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink>, <VisualBind element="r" color="carbon">$r'$</VisualBind> a <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> with $\operatorname{tr}(r') \subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\sigma)$, <VisualBind element="rayOOAA" color="ocre">$\overrightarrow{O'A'}$</VisualBind> a ray with origin <VisualBind element="pOO" color="terracota">$O'$</VisualBind> and $O', A' \in \operatorname{tr}(r')$, and <VisualBind element="Semiplano" color="mora">$\mathcal{H}$</VisualBind> a <ConceptLink targetId="semiplano">half-plane</ConceptLink> of $\sigma$ bounded by $r'$. Then there exists a unique ray <VisualBind element="rayOOBB" color="pavo">$\overrightarrow{O'B'}$</VisualBind> with $\overrightarrow{O'B'} \setminus \{O'\} \subseteq \mathcal{H}$ such that the angle <VisualBind element="nonReflexAngleAAOOBB" color="terracota">$\angle A'O'B'$</VisualBind> is <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruent</ConceptLink> to $\angle AOB$ (<VisualBind element="equalAnglenonReflexAngleAAOOBB" color="terracota">$\angle AOB \cong \angle A'O'B'$</VisualBind>). Writing $\mathcal{H} = \mathcal{H}_{r'}(C)$ for a <ConceptLink targetId="punto">point</ConceptLink> $C$ of $\sigma$ not <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incident</ConceptLink> with $r'$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\forall O, A, B, O', A', C \in \mathcal{P} \ \forall \sigma \in \Pi \ \Bigl( \bigl( \neg\operatorname{Col}(A,O,B) \land \neg\operatorname{Col}(A',O',C) \land O' \,\mathbf{I}\, \sigma \land A' \,\mathbf{I}\, \sigma \land C \,\mathbf{I}\, \sigma \bigr) \implies \\
&\quad \exists B' \in \mathcal{H}_{r'}(C) \ \bigl( \angle AOB \cong \angle A'O'B' \land \forall B'' \in \mathcal{H}_{r'}(C) \ (\angle AOB \cong \angle A'O'B'' \implies B'' \in \overrightarrow{O'B'}) \bigr) \Bigr), \qquad r' = \overleftrightarrow{O'A'}
\end{aligned}
$$

Furthermore, every angle is congruent to itself (<ConceptLink targetId="relacion-reflexiva">reflexivity</ConceptLink>); since an angle is an unordered pair of rays, $\angle AOB = \angle BOA$:

$$
\forall O, A, B \in \mathcal{P} \ \bigl( \neg\operatorname{Col}(A,O,B) \implies \angle AOB \cong \angle AOB \bigr)
$$
</Definicion>

<Nota>
Axiom III.4 is the synthetic counterpart of the protractor: it guarantees the existence and uniqueness of angle transport in <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>. The <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-simetrica">symmetry</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-transitiva">transitivity</ConceptLink> of angle congruence are not postulated: they form a <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-angulos-equivalencia">theorem</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">Hilbert</ConceptLink> (§6) whose proof uses <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-5">Axiom III.5</ConceptLink>. Only from that theorem on is angle congruence an <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-equivalencia">equivalence relation</ConceptLink>, so that angles fall into <ConceptLink targetId="clase-equivalencia">equivalence classes</ConceptLink>.
</Nota>
