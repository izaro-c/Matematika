<Capitular letra="T" />he fourth congruence axiom of <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink> formalizes the rigid transportability of <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> in the plane. Given an angle $\angle(h, k)$, a ray $h'$ with origin $O'$, and a half-plane bounded by the line supporting $h'$, there exists a unique ray $k'$ in that half-plane such that $\angle(h, k) \cong \angle(h', k')$.

<Definicion title="Formal statement">
  Given a convex <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angle</ConceptLink> $\angle(h, k)$ and a ray $h'$ emanating from $O'$ bounding a half-plane $\mathcal{H}$, there exists a unique ray $k' \subset \mathcal{H}$ emanating from $O'$ such that:
  $$
  \forall \angle(h,k), \forall h' \subset \partial\mathcal{H}, \ \exists! k' \subset \mathcal{H} \ \bigl(\angle(h, k) \cong \angle(h', k')\bigr)
  $$
  Furthermore, every angle is <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink> to itself: $\angle(h, k) \cong \angle(h, k)$.
</Definicion>

### Geometric significance and scope

This postulate establishes the synthetic concept of angular rigidity without relying on analytical trigonometric functions or degree/radian numerical scales. It ensures that rotations and isometries preserve angular measures in <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>.

<Separador />

### Properties

- **Planar uniqueness:** In each designated half-plane, exactly one ray reproduces the desired angular aperture.
- **Vertical angles congruence:** Proves that <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-angulos-opuestos-verticales">vertically opposite angles</ConceptLink> are congruent.
- **Equivalence relation:** Angle congruence constitutes an equivalence relation (reflexive, symmetric, transitive) on the set of planar angles.
