<Capitular letra="T" />here exist at least four non-<ConceptLink targetId="coplanaridad">coplanar</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>points</ConceptLink>; that is, points such that no <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink> is simultaneously <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia" isDependency={true}>incident</ConceptLink> with all four.

In the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-incidencia">incidence system</ConceptLink>, this postulate sets the lower dimensional bound of <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink>, preventing all geometric entities from being confined to a single two-dimensional <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Axiom I.8 (Three-Dimensional Space)">
There exist at least four distinct <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="pA" color="terracota">$A$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pB" color="terracota">$B$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pC" color="terracota">$C$</VisualBind>, and <VisualBind element="pD" color="carbon">$D$</VisualBind> not belonging to the same <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="plano-pi" color="azul">$\pi$</VisualBind>:

$$
\exists A, B, C, D \in \mathcal{P} \ \forall \pi \in \Pi \ \neg(A \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi \land B \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi \land C \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi \land D \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi)
$$
</Definicion>

<Nota>
This axiom guarantees the existence of genuinely spatial configurations, establishing a minimum of three <ConceptLink targetId="dimension">dimensions</ConceptLink> for <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink>. This postulate concludes Hilbert's group of <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence</ConceptLink> axioms.
