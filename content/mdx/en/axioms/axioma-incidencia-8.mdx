<Capitular letra="T" />he eighth incidence axiom formulated by <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink> postulates the existence of at least four non-coplanar <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>points</ConceptLink> in <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink>. This axiom establishes the authentic three-dimensionality of spatial geometry, preventing the deductive domain from being restricted to a single <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal statement">
  There exist at least four <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>points</ConceptLink> $A, B, C, D \in \mathcal{S}$ such that no <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink> $\pi \in \mathcal{E}$ contains all four simultaneously:
  $$
  \exists A, B, C, D \in \mathcal{S} \ \forall \pi \in \mathcal{E} \ \neg\bigl(A \in \pi \land B \in \pi \land C \in \pi \land D \in \pi\bigr)
  $$
</Definicion>

### Geometric significance and scope

Formally sets the dimension of the general geometric space to at least $3$ ($\dim \mathcal{S} \ge 3$). It provides the foundational requirement for solid geometry, polyhedra, and volume theory in <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>.

<Separador />

### Properties

- **Guaranteed three-dimensionality:** Precludes the collapse of space into a single two-dimensional plane.
- **Tetrahedral base:** The four points $A, B, C, D$ construct the fundamental non-degenerate tetrahedron of space.
- **Affine span in $\mathbb{R}^3$:** Constitutes a non-coplanar affine reference frame with three spatial degrees of freedom.
