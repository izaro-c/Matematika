<Capitular letra="G" />iven any three non-<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>points</ConceptLink>, there exists at least one <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia" isDependency={true}>incident</ConceptLink> with all three. Every plane is incident with at least one point.

The first clause is the analogue, for planes, of the existence part of <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-1">Axiom I.1</ConceptLink>: just as two distinct points have a common <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink>, three non-collinear points have a common plane; the uniqueness of that plane is supplied by <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-5">Axiom I.5</ConceptLink>. The second clause rules out planes without points, so that no plane has an empty <ConceptLink targetId="traza">point trace</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal statement">
  For any three non-<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="pA" color="ocre">$A$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pB" color="ocre">$B$</VisualBind>, and <VisualBind element="pC" color="ocre">$C$</VisualBind>, there exists at least one <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="polyTriangulo" color="canela">$\pi$</VisualBind> such that all three points <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">are incident</ConceptLink> with $\pi$. Moreover, for every plane $\pi$ there exists a point $P$ incident with $\pi$:
  $$
  \begin{aligned}
  &\forall A, B, C \in \mathcal{P} \ \bigl(\neg \exists \ell \in \mathcal{L} \, (A \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell \land B \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell \land C \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell) \implies \exists \pi \in \Pi \ (A \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi \land B \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi \land C \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi)\bigr) \\
  &\land \ \forall \pi \in \Pi \ \exists P \in \mathcal{P} \ (P \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi)
  \end{aligned}
  $$
</Definicion>
