<Capitular letra="G" />iven any three non-<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink>, there exists at least one <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> incident with all three.

This postulate extends the principle of linear determination to two-dimensional space: just as two points fix a line, three non-aligned points guarantee the existence of a plane in <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal Statement">
  For any three non-<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="pA" color="ocre">$A$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pB" color="ocre">$B$</VisualBind>, and <VisualBind element="pC" color="ocre">$C$</VisualBind>, there exists at least one <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="polyTriangulo" color="canela">$\pi$</VisualBind> such that all three points <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">are incident</ConceptLink> with $\pi$:
  $$
  \forall A, B, C \in \mathcal{P} \ \bigl(\neg \exists \ell \in \mathcal{L} \, (A \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell \land B \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell \land C \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell) \implies \exists \pi \in \Pi \ (A \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi \land B \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi \land C \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi)\bigr)
  $$
