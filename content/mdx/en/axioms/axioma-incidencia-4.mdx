<Capitular letra="G" />iven any three non-collinear <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink>, there exists at least one <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> containing all three.

This postulate extends the principle of linear determination to two-dimensional space: just as two points fix a line, three non-aligned points fix the position of a plane in space.

<Definicion title="Formal Statement">
  For any three non-collinear <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="pA" color="ocre">$A$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pB" color="ocre">$B$</VisualBind>, and <VisualBind element="pC" color="ocre">$C$</VisualBind>, there exists at least one <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> $\pi$ such that all three points <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">are incident</ConceptLink> with $\pi$:
  $$
  \forall A, B, C \in \mathcal{P} \ \bigl(\text{non-collinear}(A, B, C) \implies \exists \pi \in \Pi \ (A \in \pi \land B \in \pi \land C \in \pi)\bigr)
  $$
</Definicion>

<Separador />

### Significance in the theory

Enables the construction of planes from minimal configurations of points and supports the study of three-dimensional geometry in the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-incidencia">incidence system</ConceptLink>.
