<Capitular letra="G" />iven any three non-collinear <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>points</ConceptLink> in <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink>, the fourth incidence axiom of <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink> guarantees the existence and uniqueness of a <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink> containing all three. This postulate defines the fundamental two-dimensional extension in <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal statement">
  Given three non-collinear <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>points</ConceptLink> $A, B, C \in \mathcal{S}$, there exists a unique <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink> $\pi \in \mathcal{E}$ incident with $A, B, C$:
  $$
  \forall A, B, C \in \mathcal{S} \ \Bigl(\neg\exists l \in \mathcal{L} \ (A,B,C \in l) \implies \exists! \pi \in \mathcal{E} \ (A \in \pi \land B \in \pi \land C \in \pi)\Bigr)
  $$
</Definicion>

### Geometric significance and scope

This postulate defines the plane as the elementary two-dimensional manifold spanned by three free points. It ensures that three non-aligned points admit no angular ambiguity or multiple planar orientations in three-dimensional space.

<Separador />

### Properties

- **Planar determination:** Three non-collinear points uniquely fix the position and orientation of a plane.
- **Triangle support:** Every <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink> $\triangle ABC$ lies on a single well-defined plane.
- **Dimensional transition:** Permits the rigorous transition from one-dimensional linear analysis to two-dimensional planar analysis.
