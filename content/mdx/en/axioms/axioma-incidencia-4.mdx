<Capitular letra="G" />iven any three non-collinear <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> in <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink>'s fourth <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence axiom</ConceptLink> asserts the existence and uniqueness of a <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> containing all three. Additionally, every plane is specified to contain at least one point.

<Definicion title="Formal statement">
  Given three non-collinear <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> $A, B, C \in \mathcal{S}$, there exists a unique <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{P} \in \mathcal{F}$ such that $A, B, C \in \mathcal{P}$:
  $$
  \forall A, B, C \in \mathcal{S} \ \Bigl(\neg\exists l \in \mathcal{L} \ (A,B,C \in l) \implies \exists! \mathcal{P} \in \mathcal{F} \ (A \in \mathcal{P} \land B \in \mathcal{P} \land C \in \mathcal{P})\Bigr)
  $$
</Definicion>

### Geometric significance and scope

This postulate defines a plane as the primitive two-dimensional extension spanned by a non-collinear triad. It guarantees that three non-aligned points fix a unique flat surface within <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-absoluta">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>.

<Separador />

### Properties

- **Planar determination:** Three non-aligned points uniquely establish bidimensional spatial orientation.
- **3D foundation:** Essential for constructing three-dimensional Euclidean and non-Euclidean space structures.
- **Triangle support:** Every <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink> lies on a unique supporting plane.
