<Capitular letra="T" />he hyperbolic parallel axiom (or the <ConceptLink targetId="lobachevsky">Lobachevsky</ConceptLink>-Bolyai postulate) replaces Euclid's fifth postulate to establish <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-hiperbolica">hyperbolic geometry</ConceptLink>. It asserts the existence of multiple disjoint <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink> passing through a single exterior <ConceptLink targetId="punto">point</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="">
  Given a <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> $l$ and a <ConceptLink targetId="punto">point</ConceptLink> $P$ not on $l$, there exist at least two distinct lines $m_1, m_2$ containing $P$ that are <ConceptLink targetId="paralelismo">parallel</ConceptLink> (disjoint) to $l$:
  $$
  \forall l \in \mathcal{L}, \forall P \notin l \implies \exists m_1, m_2 \in \mathcal{L} \ (m_1 \neq m_2 \land P \in m_1 \cap m_2 \land m_1 \parallel l \land m_2 \parallel l)
  $$
</Definicion>

### Axiomatic significance

Adopting this axiom in place of Euclid's parallel postulate demonstrates the logical independence of the parallel axiom and constructs a consistent geometric system of constant negative Gaussian curvature. In this space, the sum of interior angles of any <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink> is strictly less than $\pi$ radians.
