<Capitular letra="G" />iven a <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink> and a <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>point</ConceptLink> not <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia" isDependency={true}>incident</ConceptLink> with it in the same <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink>, there exist at least two distinct lines of that plane passing through the point and not meeting the given line; both are therefore <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas" isDependency={true}>parallel</ConceptLink> to it.

Formulated independently by <ConceptLink targetId="lobachevski">Nikolai Lobachevsky</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="bolyai">János Bolyai</ConceptLink>, this axiom replaces <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">Axiom IV</ConceptLink> on the uniqueness of the parallel. Added to the axioms of <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink> (groups I, II, III and V of <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">Hilbert</ConceptLink>), it founds the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-hiperbolico">hyperbolic system</ConceptLink>. Its relative <ConceptLink targetId="consistencia-logica">consistency</ConceptLink> was not proved by its discoverers: it is established by the models of <ConceptLink targetId="beltrami">Beltrami</ConceptLink> (1868), <ConceptLink targetId="klein">Klein</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="poincare">Poincaré</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal statement of the hyperbolic parallel axiom">
  For every <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink> $\pi \in \Pi$, every <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="lineL" color="pavo">$\ell \in \mathcal{L}$</VisualBind> whose <ConceptLink targetId="traza">point trace</ConceptLink> is contained in that of $\pi$ ($\operatorname{tr}(\ell) \subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\pi)$) and every <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>point</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="pP" color="terracota">$P \in \mathcal{P}$</VisualBind> <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia" isDependency={true}>incident</ConceptLink> with $\pi$ but not with $\ell$ ($P \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi \land \neg(P \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell)$), there exist two distinct lines <VisualBind element="lineM" color="terracota">$m \in \mathcal{L}$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="lineN" color="carbon">$n \in \mathcal{L}$</VisualBind> of $\pi$, incident with $P$, whose traces are <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-disjunto">disjoint</ConceptLink> from that of $\ell$:

  $$
  \forall \pi \in \Pi, \ \forall \ell \in \mathcal{L}, \ \forall P \in \mathcal{P} \ \Biggl( \Bigl( \operatorname{tr}(\ell) \subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\pi) \land P \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi \land \neg(P \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell) \Bigr) \implies \exists m, n \in \mathcal{L} \ \Bigl( m \neq n \land \operatorname{tr}(m) \subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\pi) \land \operatorname{tr}(n) \subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\pi) \land P \, \mathbf{I} \, m \land P \, \mathbf{I} \, n \land \operatorname{tr}(m) \cap \operatorname{tr}(\ell) = \emptyset \land \operatorname{tr}(n) \cap \operatorname{tr}(\ell) = \emptyset \Bigr) \Biggr)
  $$

  Since both lines are <ConceptLink targetId="coplanaridad">coplanar</ConceptLink> with $\ell$ and do not meet it, they are parallel to it:

  $$
  m \parallel \ell \quad \land \quad n \parallel \ell \quad \land \quad m \neq n
  $$
</Definicion>

### Representation in the Poincaré disk model

In the interactive simulation, the hyperbolic plane is represented by the <ConceptLink targetId="modelo-poincare">Poincaré disk</ConceptLink>, whose lines are the diameters and the arcs of <ConceptLink targetId="circunferencia">circles</ConceptLink> orthogonal to the boundary of the disk.

Through the point <VisualBind element="pP" color="terracota">$P$</VisualBind>, exterior to the line <VisualBind element="lineL" color="pavo">$\ell$</VisualBind>, pass the two <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas-limite">limiting parallels</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="lineM" color="terracota">$m$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="lineN" color="carbon">$n$</VisualBind>, which approach $\ell$ asymptotically and "touch" it at ideal points of the boundary. Together they determine at $P$ two pairs of <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-opuestos-vertice">vertical angles</ConceptLink>. Lines through $P$ lying in the pair that contains the <ConceptLink targetId="perpendicular">perpendicular</ConceptLink> dropped from $P$ to $\ell$ meet $\ell$; those lying in the other pair neither meet it nor share an ideal point with it, and they are the <ConceptLink targetId="ultraparalelas">ultraparallel lines</ConceptLink>. The angle that each limiting parallel makes with that perpendicular is the <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-paralelismo">angle of parallelism</ConceptLink> $\theta(d)$, always <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-agudo">acute</ConceptLink>, where $d$ is the <ConceptLink targetId="longitud">length</ConceptLink> of the perpendicular segment.

### Deductive consequences and geometric scope

The existence of several parallels through a point alters the deductive structure of geometry:

- **Angular defect of triangles:** the sum of the <ConceptLink targetId="medida-angular">measures</ConceptLink> of the interior <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> of every <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink> is less than two <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto">right angles</ConceptLink>: $|\alpha| + |\beta| + |\gamma| < 180^\circ$. The difference $180^\circ - (|\alpha| + |\beta| + |\gamma|)$ is the <ConceptLink targetId="defecto-angular">angular defect</ConceptLink>, proportional to the <ConceptLink targetId="area">area</ConceptLink> of the triangle.
- **No similar non-congruent triangles:** two triangles with respectively congruent angles are <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia-triangulos">congruent</ConceptLink> (AAA criterion); there is no <ConceptLink targetId="semejanza">similarity</ConceptLink> with change of scale.
- **Failure of <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-transitiva">transitivity</ConceptLink> of parallelism:** two lines parallel to a third are not necessarily parallel to each other.
- **No rectangles:** no <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrilatero">quadrilateral</ConceptLink> has four <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto">right angles</ConceptLink>; the summit angles of <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrilatero-saccheri">Saccheri quadrilaterals</ConceptLink> and the fourth angle of <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrilatero-lambert">Lambert</ConceptLink> quadrilaterals are acute.
