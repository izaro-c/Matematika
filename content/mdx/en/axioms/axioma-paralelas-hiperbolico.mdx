<Capitular letra="T" />he hyperbolic parallel axiom —formulated by <ConceptLink targetId="lobachevski">Nikolai Lobachevsky</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="bolyai">János Bolyai</ConceptLink>— is the fundamental postulate of hyperbolic geometry. It replaces Euclidean uniqueness with the existence of a plurality of distinct lines passing through an external <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>point</ConceptLink> that do not intersect a given <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink> in the same <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal statement">
  Given a <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink> $l \in \mathcal{L}$ and an external <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>point</ConceptLink> $P \notin l$ in a <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{P}$, there exist at least two distinct lines $l_1, l_2 \subset \mathcal{P}$ passing through $P$ that do not intersect $l$:
  $$
  \forall l \in \mathcal{L}, \forall P \in \mathcal{P} \setminus l, \ \exists l_1, l_2 \subset \mathcal{P} \ \bigl(l_1 \neq l_2 \land P \in l_1 \cap l_2 \land l_1 \cap l = \emptyset \land l_2 \cap l = \emptyset\bigr)
  $$
</Definicion>

### Geometric significance and scope

This axiom imparts a constant negative Gaussian curvature ($K < 0$) to the plane. It causes the sum of interior <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> of any <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink> to be strictly less than $180^\circ$ and bounds the area of any polygon by its angular defect.

<Separador />

### Properties

- **Angle of parallelism:** A characteristic acute angle $\Pi(x) < 90^\circ$ strictly separates secant lines from non-intersecting lines.
- **Angular defect:** In every hyperbolic triangle, $\alpha + \beta + \gamma < 180^\circ$.
- **Non-transitivity of parallelism:** Two lines parallel to a common third line are not necessarily parallel to each other.
- **Absence of rectangles:** No planar quadrilateral can possess four right angles.
