<Capitular letra="T" />he first <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink> axiom of <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">Hilbert</ConceptLink> formalizes the possibility of transferring rigid length magnitudes in <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-absoluta">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>. This postulate guarantees that any given <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink> can be uniquely laid off from the origin of a given <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta">ray</ConceptLink> in the <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="">
  Given a <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink> $\overline{AB}$ and a <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta">ray</ConceptLink> $r$ originating at <ConceptLink targetId="punto">point</ConceptLink> $C$, there exists a unique point $D$ belonging to $r$ such that segment $\overline{AB}$ is <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruent</ConceptLink> to segment $\overline{CD}$:
  $$
  \forall \overline{AB}, \forall r_C \implies \exists! D \in r_C \ (\overline{AB} \cong \overline{CD})
  $$
</Definicion>

### Axiomatic significance

This axiom establishes the primitive notion of geometric rigidity without relying on a prior real-valued <ConceptLink targetId="distancia">distance</ConceptLink> function. It enables the synthetic definition of segment comparison and metric equality within the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-axiomatico">axiomatic system</ConceptLink>.
