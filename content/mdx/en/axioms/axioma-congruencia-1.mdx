<Capitular letra="G" />iven a <ConceptLink targetId="segmento" isDependency={true}>segment</ConceptLink> and a <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta" isDependency={true}>ray</ConceptLink>, there exists on the ray a <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>point</ConceptLink>, distinct from its <ConceptLink targetId="origen-semirrecta">origin</ConceptLink>, that determines with it a segment <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink> to the given one.

This axiom makes it possible to transport a segment onto any ray in <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink> without resorting to numerical measures or coordinate systems.

<Definicion title="Axiom III.1 (Segment Transport)">
Given two distinct <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="pA" color="ocre">$A$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pB" color="ocre">$B$</VisualBind>, which determine the <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="segAB" color="ocre">$\overline{AB}$</VisualBind>, and a <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta">ray</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="rayCDir" color="pavo">$\overrightarrow{C\,\text{dir}}$</VisualBind> with origin at the point <VisualBind element="pC" color="terracota">$C$</VisualBind> and passing through the point <VisualBind element="pDir" color="pavo">$\text{dir} \neq C$</VisualBind>, there exists a point <VisualBind element="pD" color="terracota">$D \in \overrightarrow{C\,\text{dir}} \setminus \{C\}$</VisualBind> such that the segment <VisualBind element="segCD" color="terracota">$\overline{CD}$</VisualBind> is <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruent</ConceptLink> to $\overline{AB}$:

$$
\forall A, B, C, \text{dir} \in \mathcal{P} \ \Bigl( (A \neq B \land C \neq \text{dir}) \implies \exists D \in \overrightarrow{C\,\text{dir}} \setminus \{C\} \ (\overline{AB} \cong \overline{CD}) \Bigr)
$$

Furthermore, every segment is congruent to itself (<ConceptLink targetId="reflexividad">reflexivity</ConceptLink>):

$$
\forall A, B \in \mathcal{P} \ \bigl( A \neq B \implies (\overline{AB} \cong \overline{AB} \land \overline{AB} \cong \overline{BA}) \bigr)
$$
</Definicion>

<Nota>
Axiom III.1 is the synthetic counterpart of the compass in Euclidean constructions. It only postulates the existence of the point $D$: its uniqueness is a <ConceptLink targetId="teorema">theorem</ConceptLink>, the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-unicidad-transporte-segmentos">uniqueness of segment transport</ConceptLink>, which follows from axioms <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-4">III.4</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-5">III.5</ConceptLink> in <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>.
</Nota>
