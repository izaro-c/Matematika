<Capitular letra="T" />he first congruence axiom of <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink> formalizes the capacity to transport rigid linear quantities along any given direction in space. Given any line <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink> $\overline{AB}$ and a ray with origin $C$, there exists a unique <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>point</ConceptLink> $E$ on that ray defining a segment $\overline{CE}$ <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink> to $\overline{AB}$.

<Definicion title="Formal statement">
  Given a line <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink> $\overline{AB}$ and a ray $r = \overrightarrow{CD}$ with origin at <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>point</ConceptLink> $C$, there exists a unique point $E \in r$ such that $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{CE}$:
  $$
  \forall \overline{AB}, \forall r = \overrightarrow{CD}, \ \exists! E \in r \ (\overline{AB} \cong \overline{CE})
  $$
</Definicion>

### Geometric significance and scope

This postulate provides mathematical rigor to classical compass-and-straightedge constructions, allowing lengths to be transported synthetically without external metric coordinate systems in <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>.

<Separador />

### Properties

- **Uniqueness of transport:** On every ray there is exactly one endpoint satisfying the congruence relation.
- **Symmetric invariance:** Every segment is congruent to itself under endpoint reversal ($\overline{AB} \cong \overline{BA}$).
- **Synthetic metric foundation:** Enables defining relative length comparisons between segments ($\overline{AB} < \overline{CD}$).
