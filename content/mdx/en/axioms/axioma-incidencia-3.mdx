<Capitular letra="T" />here exist at least three <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> in <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink> that are not collinear, according to <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink>'s third <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence axiom</ConceptLink>. This postulate breaks the one-dimensional collapse of the geometric system, ensuring that geometry does not reduce to a single <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal statement">
  There exist at least three <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> $A, B, C \in \mathcal{S}$ such that no <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> $l \in \mathcal{L}$ contains all three simultaneously:
  $$
  \exists A, B, C \in \mathcal{S} \ \forall l \in \mathcal{L} \ \neg\bigl(A \in l \land B \in l \land C \in l\bigr)
  $$
</Definicion>

### Visual representation and interaction

In the interactive simulation, points <VisualBind element="pA" color="terracota">$A$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pB" color="terracota">$B$</VisualBind>, and <VisualBind element="pC" color="terracota">$C$</VisualBind> form a non-collinear configuration. Constructing a line through any pair leaves the third point demonstrably outside the line.

<Separador />

### Geometric significance and scope

By asserting the existence of at least three non-aligned points, geometry is forced to possess a minimum dimension of two (a plane). Without this axiom, a single line would satisfy all preceding axioms, reducing space to one dimension.

<Separador />

### Properties

- **Minimum multidimensionality:** Guarantees that space has bidimensional extension or higher.
- **Triangle formation:** Enables the primitive definition of a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink> via non-collinear vertices $A, B, C$.
- **Positional independence:** Establishes positional linear independence within <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-absoluta">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>.
