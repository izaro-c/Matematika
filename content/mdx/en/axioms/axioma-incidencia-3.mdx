<Capitular letra="T" />he third incidence axiom postulates the existence of at least three <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>points</ConceptLink> that are not aligned along a single <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink>. This principle guarantees that the geometric universe possesses at least two dimensions, preventing the space from collapsing into a one-dimensional line.

<Definicion title="Formal statement">
  There exist at least three <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>points</ConceptLink> $A, B, C \in \mathcal{S}$ such that no <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink> $l \in \mathcal{L}$ contains all three simultaneously:
  $$
  \exists A, B, C \in \mathcal{S} \ \forall l \in \mathcal{L} \ \neg\bigl(A \in l \land B \in l \land C \in l\bigr)
  $$
</Definicion>

### Geometric significance and scope

This axiom guarantees the multidimensional nature of geometry. By ensuring the existence of non-collinear triads, it provides the essential prerequisite for constructing positive-area figures such as <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangles</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="poligono">polygons</ConceptLink> in <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>.

<Separador />

### Properties

- **Minimal dimensionality:** Guarantees that the dimension of the geometric space is strictly at least two ($\dim \ge 2$).
- **Genesis of the plane:** Together with Axiom I.4, it permits fixing the existence of well-defined geometric <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>planes</ConceptLink>.
- **Affine independence:** The three points act as a free affine basis in two dimensions.
