<Capitular letra="T" />here exist at least three <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> not lying on the same <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink>.

This axiom ensures that geometry does not reduce to a single one-dimensional line, guaranteeing the existence of a two-dimensional plane with surface and polygonal figures such as the <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal Statement">
  There exist at least three <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="pA" color="ocre">$A$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pB" color="ocre">$B$</VisualBind>, and <VisualBind element="pC" color="ocre">$C$</VisualBind> such that no <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> $\ell$ contains all three simultaneously:
  $$
  \exists A, B, C \in \mathcal{P} \ \forall \ell \in \mathcal{L} \ \neg(A \in \ell \land B \in \ell \land C \in \ell)
  $$
</Definicion>

<Separador />

### Significance in the theory

- **<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">Non-collinearity</ConceptLink>:** Formally establishes the existence of triples of non-aligned points.
- **Basis for planes:** The three non-collinear points guaranteed by this axiom form the necessary basis to define the first <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> of space.
