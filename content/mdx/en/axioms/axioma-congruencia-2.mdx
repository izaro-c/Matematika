<Capitular letra="T" />he second <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink> axiom of <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">Hilbert</ConceptLink> specifies the equivalence relation properties for <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink> congruence. It ensures that segment congruence forms a symmetric and transitive relation in <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-absoluta">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="">
  If a <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink> $\overline{AB}$ is <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruent</ConceptLink> to segment $\overline{EF}$ and segment $\overline{CD}$ is also congruent to $\overline{EF}$, then $\overline{AB}$ is congruent to $\overline{CD}$. Furthermore, every segment is congruent to itself:
  $$
  (\overline{AB} \cong \overline{EF}) \land (\overline{CD} \cong \overline{EF}) \implies (\overline{AB} \cong \overline{CD})
  $$
  $$
  \forall \overline{AB} \implies \overline{AB} \cong \overline{AB}
  $$
</Definicion>

### Visual representation

Comparing the reference segment <VisualBind element="AB" color="ocre">$\overline{AB}$</VisualBind> with segments <VisualBind element="CD" color="pavo">$\overline{CD}$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="EF" color="terracota">$\overline{EF}$</VisualBind> visually demonstrates that shared congruence with $\overline{EF}$ invariably transmits equality of length between $\overline{AB}$ and $\overline{CD}$.

### Axiomatic significance

Thanks to this postulate, the <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink> relation partitions the set of all <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segments</ConceptLink> in the <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> into equivalence classes. Each class represents an abstract length magnitude without requiring a pre-established numerical measurement scale.
