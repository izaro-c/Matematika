<Capitular letra="G" />iven any two distinct <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> in <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink>, the first <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence axiom</ConceptLink> formulated by <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink> guarantees the existence and uniqueness of a <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> passing through both. This postulate forms the fundamental basis for defining linear connectivity in <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-absoluta">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal statement">
  Given two distinct <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> $A, B \in \mathcal{S}$ with $A \neq B$, there exists a unique <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> $l \in \mathcal{L}$ such that both $A$ and $B$ lie on $l$:
  $$
  \forall A, B \in \mathcal{S} \ \bigl(A \neq B \implies \exists! l \in \mathcal{L} \ (A \in l \land B \in l)\bigr)
  $$
</Definicion>

### Visual representation and interaction

In the interactive simulation, points <VisualBind element="pA" color="terracota">$A$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="pB" color="terracota">$B$</VisualBind> uniquely determine the supporting line <VisualBind element="lineAB" color="carbon">$l$</VisualBind>. Dragging either point in the plane dynamically recalculates the line's orientation while maintaining strict dual membership.

<Separador />

### Geometric significance and scope

This axiom establishes that two points exclusively determine a single direction and linear trajectory. It prevents multiple distinct lines from joining the same two primitive points without additional non-Euclidean postulates.

<Separador />

### Properties

- **Uniqueness of line:** Two distinct points cannot belong simultaneously to two different lines.
- **System independence:** Remains valid in both <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-euclidiana">Euclidean geometry</ConceptLink> and non-Euclidean geometries.
- **Interconnection foundation:** Enables the formal definition of the <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink> $\overline{AB}$ as a continuous subset of the supporting line.
