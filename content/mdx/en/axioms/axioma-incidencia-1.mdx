<Capitular letra="G" />iven any two distinct <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink>, there exists exactly one <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> passing through both.

This postulate establishes the most basic linear connection of geometry: two distinct points uniquely determine a direction and an entire line.

<Definicion title="Formal Statement">
  For any two distinct <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="pA" color="terracota">$A$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="pB" color="terracota">$B$</VisualBind> ($A \neq B$), there exists a unique <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="lineAB" color="carbon">$\ell$</VisualBind> such that $A$ and $B$ <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">are incident</ConceptLink> with $\ell$:
  $$
  \forall A, B \in \mathcal{P} \ \bigl(A \neq B \implies \exists! \ell \in \mathcal{L} \ (A \in \ell \land B \in \ell)\bigr)
  $$
</Definicion>

<Separador />

### Significance in the theory

- **Uniqueness:** Two distinct points cannot share more than one common line.
- **Maximum intersection:** As an immediate consequence, two distinct lines can intersect in at most one point.
