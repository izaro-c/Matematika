<Definicion title="Formal statement">
  If two distinct <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>planes</ConceptLink> $\pi, \sigma \in \mathcal{E}$ ($\pi \neq \sigma$) share a <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>point</ConceptLink> $A \in \pi \cap \sigma$, then their mutual intersection is exactly a <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink> $l \in \mathcal{L}$:
  $$
  \forall \pi, \sigma \in \mathcal{E} \ \bigl((\pi \neq \sigma \land A \in \pi \cap \sigma) \implies \exists! l \in \mathcal{L} \ (l = \pi \cap \sigma)\bigr)
  $$
</Definicion>

### Geometric significance and scope

Guarantees the dimensional integrity of three-dimensional space: two planes cannot meet tangentially at a single isolated point. Any non-empty intersection between distinct planes necessarily yields a continuous one-dimensional linear trace.

<Separador />

### Properties

- **Continuity of intersection:** Prohibits isolated point contacts between distinct planes in 3D space.
- **Foundation of dihedral angles:** Permits defining the dihedral angular opening formed between two half-planes meeting at edge line $l$.
- **Dimensional duality:** Represents the spatial dual of point determination via intersection of two coplanar secant lines.
