<Capitular letra="I" />f two distinct <ConceptLink targetId="plano">planes</ConceptLink> share a <ConceptLink targetId="punto">point</ConceptLink>, they share at least one other point, and their complete intersection is a <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink>.

This axiom describes how planes intersect in three-dimensional space: two planes cannot meet at a single isolated point.

<Definicion title="Formal Statement">
  If two distinct planes (<VisualBind element="plano-pi" color="canela">$\pi_1$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="plano-sigma" color="ocre">$\pi_2$</VisualBind>) share a point $A \in \mathcal{P}$, there exists a <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="recta-l" color="carbon">$\ell$</VisualBind> constituting their common intersection:
  $$
  (\pi_1 \neq \pi_2 \land A \in \pi_1 \cap \pi_2) \implies \exists \ell \in \mathcal{L} \ (\pi_1 \cap \pi_2 = \ell)
  $$
</Definicion>

<Separador />

### Significance in the theory

- **Spatial behavior:** Ensures that the intersection of two planes is never an isolated point or a deformed surface.
