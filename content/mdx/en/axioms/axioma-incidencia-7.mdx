<Capitular letra="I" />f two distinct <ConceptLink targetId="plano">planes</ConceptLink> share a <ConceptLink targetId="punto">point</ConceptLink>, then they share at least another common point.

This axiom describes the local behavior of planes in three-dimensional <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink>: two distinct planes cannot meet at a single isolated point.

<Definicion title="Formal Statement">
  If two distinct planes (<VisualBind element="plano-pi" color="canela">$\pi_1$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="plano-sigma" color="ocre">$\pi_2$</VisualBind>) <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">are incident</ConceptLink> with a common point $A \in \mathcal{P}$, there exists at least a second distinct point $B \in \mathcal{P}$ incident with both planes:
  $$
  \forall \pi_1, \pi_2 \in \Pi, \, \forall A \in \mathcal{P} \ \bigl((\pi_1 \neq \pi_2 \land A \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi_1 \land A \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi_2) \implies \exists B \in \mathcal{P} \ (B \neq A \land B \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi_1 \land B \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi_2)\bigr)
  $$
</Definicion>

<SeccionPropiedades>
  <PropiedadesGrupo title="Line Intersection Theorem and Consequences">
    <PropiedadItem id="ebakidura-lineala" title="Deduction of the planar intersection line">
      From the second common point $B$ guaranteed by this axiom, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-1">Axiom I.1</ConceptLink> determines a unique line $\overleftrightarrow{AB}$, and <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-6">Axiom I.6</ConceptLink> ensures the entire line lies in both planes ($\overleftrightarrow{AB} \subseteq \pi_1 \cap \pi_2$). Since any non-collinear point outside $\overleftrightarrow{AB}$ would force $\pi_1 = \pi_2$ by <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-5">Axiom I.5</ConceptLink>, it is formally proven that $\pi_1 \cap \pi_2 = \overleftrightarrow{AB}$.
    </PropiedadItem>
    <PropiedadItem id="posicion-relativa-planos" title="Relative positions of planes in space">
      In space, two planes are either disjoint (parallel), completely identical, or intersect along a single secant line.
    </PropiedadItem>
  </PropiedadesGrupo>
