<Capitular letra="I" />n every <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink>'s seventh <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence axiom</ConceptLink> guarantees the presence of at least three non-collinear <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink>. This postulate ensures that no plane degenerates or collapses into a single <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal statement">
  For every <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{P} \in \mathcal{F}$, there exist at least three <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> $A, B, C \in \mathcal{P}$ such that no <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> $l \in \mathcal{L}$ contains all three simultaneously:
  $$
  \forall \mathcal{P} \in \mathcal{F}, \ \exists A, B, C \in \mathcal{P} \ \forall l \in \mathcal{L} \ \neg\bigl(A \in l \land B \in l \land C \in l\bigr)
  $$
</Definicion>

### Geometric significance and scope

This axiom guarantees the intrinsic bidimensionality of every plane. It prevents the existence of degenerate planes composed solely of a single line or point pair, ensuring rich planar geometric structures.

<Separador />

### Properties

- **Planar non-degeneracy:** Prohibits any plane from being one-dimensional.
- **Triangles in every plane:** Permits the construction of <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangles</ConceptLink> within any plane of space.
- **Sub-plane structures:** Facilitates plane division via lines and induces planar <ConceptLink targetId="orden">order</ConceptLink> properties.
