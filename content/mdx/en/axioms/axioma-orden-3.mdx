<Capitular letra="O" />f any three distinct <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> lying on the same <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink>'s third <ConceptLink targetId="orden">order axiom</ConceptLink> establishes the law of tricotomy: exactly one and only one of those points lies between the other two.

<Definicion title="Formal statement">
  For any three distinct <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> $A, B, C \in l$ belonging to line $l \in \mathcal{L}$, exactly one of the following three betweenness relations holds exclusively:
  $$
  \forall A, B, C \in l \ (A \neq B \land B \neq C \land A \neq C) \implies (A\text{-}B\text{-}C \veebar B\text{-}C\text{-}A \veebar C\text{-}A\text{-}B)
  $$
</Definicion>

### Geometric significance and scope

This axiom guarantees that linear order is total and unambiguous. It excludes paradoxical configurations where two distinct points could simultaneously lie between the same pair in <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-absoluta">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>.

<Separador />

### Properties

- **Strict exclusivity:** Two betweenness relations cannot hold simultaneously among the same three points.
- **Linear completeness:** In every collinear triad of distinct points, an intermediate position necessarily exists.
- **Implicit coordinates:** Induces a linear ordering structure fully compatible with the real line $\mathbb{R}$.
