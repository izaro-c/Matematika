<Capitular letra="O" />f three distinct <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>points</ConceptLink> situated on the same <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink>, at most one lies <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre" isDependency={true}>between</ConceptLink> the other two.

This axiom establishes the mutual exclusion of the three possible betweenness relations among three points of a line, preventing the <ConceptLink targetId="orden">order</ConceptLink> along a line from becoming cyclic or closing upon itself like a <ConceptLink targetId="circunferencia">circle</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal statement">
For any <ConceptLink targetId="terna">triple</ConceptLink> of mutually distinct <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="pA" color="terracota">$A$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pB" color="terracota">$B$</VisualBind>, and <VisualBind element="pC" color="terracota">$C$</VisualBind> in $\mathcal{P}$ <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incident</ConceptLink> with the same <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="lineDE" color="carbon">$\ell$</VisualBind>, at most one of the betweenness relations $A * B * C$, $B * A * C$, or $A * C * B$ holds true:

$$
\forall A, B, C \in \mathcal{P} \ \Biggl( \Bigl(A \neq B \land B \neq C \land A \neq C \land \exists \ell \in \mathcal{L} \, (A \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell \land B \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell \land C \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell)\Bigr) \implies \neg\Bigl( (A * B * C \land B * A * C) \lor (A * B * C \land A * C * B) \lor (B * A * C \land A * C * B) \Bigr) \Biggr)
$$
</Definicion>

<Nota>
The axiom only asserts that at most one of the three points lies between the other two. That there is always one, that is, that of three distinct <ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> points exactly one lies between the other two, is the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-orden-tres-puntos">three-point ordering theorem</ConceptLink>; its proof uses the incidence axioms, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-1">Axiom II.1</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-2">Axiom II.2</ConceptLink>, this axiom and, essentially, Pasch's <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-4">Axiom II.4</ConceptLink>. From that theorem, each line admits exactly two <ConceptLink targetId="orden-total">total orders</ConceptLink> compatible with betweenness, one for each <ConceptLink targetId="sentido">sense</ConceptLink>; choosing one of them amounts to fixing an <ConceptLink targetId="orientacion">orientation</ConceptLink> of the line.
</Nota>
