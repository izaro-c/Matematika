<Capitular letra="A" />rchimedes' axiom represents the first axiom of <ConceptLink targetId="continuidad">continuity</ConceptLink> (Hilbert's Axiom IV.1). It excludes infinitely large or infinitesimal elements from metric geometry.

<Definicion title="">
  Let $\overline{AB}$ and $\overline{CD}$ be two arbitrary <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segments</ConceptLink>. There exists a positive integer $n \in \mathbb{N}^+$ such that, laying off a chain of $n$ segments congruent to $\overline{CD}$ along ray $AB$ $(A = A_0, A_1, A_2, \dots, A_n)$, point $B$ lies between $A$ and $A_n$:
  $$
  \forall \overline{AB}, \overline{CD} \implies \exists n \in \mathbb{N}^+ \ (n \cdot \overline{CD} > \overline{AB})
  $$
</Definicion>

### Visual representation

### Axiomatic significance

This postulate ensures that no non-zero infinitesimal magnitudes exist. It is essential for the theory of proportions and the calculation of areas and volumes, underpinning exhaustion methods in <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-euclidiana">Euclidean geometry</ConceptLink>.
