<Capitular letra="T" />he Archimedean axiom —incorporated by <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink> as the first principle of the continuity group— excludes the existence of infinitely small (infinitesimal) or infinitely large quantities from synthetic geometry. It establishes that any distance, however vast, can be exceeded by repeated finite concatenation of an elementary <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal statement">
  Given any two non-zero line <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segments</ConceptLink> $\overline{AB}$ and $\overline{CD}$, there exists a natural number $n \in \mathbb{N}$ such that laying $n$ consecutive copies of $\overline{AB}$ along ray $\overrightarrow{CD}$ starting at $C$ passes beyond <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>point</ConceptLink> $D$:
  $$
  \forall \overline{AB}, \overline{CD} \ \bigl(A \neq B \land C \neq D \implies \exists n \in \mathbb{N} \ (n \cdot \overline{AB} > \overline{CD})\bigr)
  $$
</Definicion>

### Geometric significance and scope

Ensures the Archimedean property of spatial geometry: there exist no infinitesimal segments or insurmountable distances. It connects synthetic geometric scale with the Archimedean ordered field of real numbers $\mathbb{R}$ in <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>.

<Separador />

### Properties

- **Exclusion of infinitesimals:** No non-zero segment is infinitely small relative to any other.
- **Boundedness principle:** Every bounded subset of a line can be covered by a finite number of steps of fixed length.
- **Metric compatibility:** Essential for defining angle measures, curve rectification, and polygon areas via convergent limits.
