<Capitular letra="G" />iven two arbitrary <ConceptLink targetId="segmento" isDependency={true}>segments</ConceptLink> $\overline{AB}$ and $\overline{CD}$ with $A \neq B$ and $C \neq D$, on the <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta" isDependency={true}>ray</ConceptLink> originating at $A$ containing $B$, the iterated transport of segments <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink> to $\overline{CD}$ generates a finite sequence of <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>points</ConceptLink> $A_1, A_2, \dots, A_n$ such that $B$ lies <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre" isDependency={true}>between</ConceptLink> $A$ and $A_n$ ($A * B * A_n$).

This postulate formalizes the absence of infinitesimal or infinitely large quantities in Archimedean geometry. It guarantees that no non-zero segment is negligibly small compared to any other: any prescribed <ConceptLink targetId="distancia">distance</ConceptLink>, no matter how vast, can be reached and surpassed by the repeated finite concatenation of an elementary base segment.

<Definicion title="Axiom V.1 (Archimedean Axiom)">
  Let $\overline{AB}$ and $\overline{CD}$ be two arbitrary <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segments</ConceptLink> with $A \neq B$ and $C \neq D$. On the <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta">ray</ConceptLink> originating at $A$ containing $B$, there exists a finite sequence of <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> $A_1, A_2, \dots, A_n$ such that setting $A_0 = A$, the initial segment satisfies $\overline{A_0 A_1} \cong \overline{CD}$, each consecutive segment is <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruent</ConceptLink> to the pattern ($\overline{A_{k-1} A_k} \cong \overline{CD}$ for all $k \in \{1, \dots, n\}$), the points satisfy the <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">order</ConceptLink> relation $A_{k-2} * A_{k-1} * A_k$ for all $k \in \{2, \dots, n\}$, and endpoint $B$ lies <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">between</ConceptLink> $A$ and $A_n$:
  $$
  \forall A, B, C, D \in \mathcal{P} \ \Biggl( A \neq B \land C \neq D \implies \exists n \in \mathbb{N}, \, \exists A_1, \dots, A_n \in \mathcal{P} \ \Bigl( A_0 = A \land \bigwedge_{k=1}^n \overline{A_{k-1} A_k} \cong \overline{CD} \land \bigwedge_{k=2}^n (A_{k-2} * A_{k-1} * A_k) \land A * B * A_n \Bigr) \Biggr)
  $$
</Definicion>

### Geometric significance and deductive scope

The axiom of <ConceptLink targetId="arquimedes">Archimedes</ConceptLink> serves as the foundational first principle of <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">Hilbert's</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="continuidad">continuity</ConceptLink> group. Its presence rules out non-Archimedean geometries in which elements of incomparable or infinitely distant scales would coexist.

In the architecture of <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>, the axiom enables the development of proportionality theory and underpins the computation of areas and lengths. Every bounded subset of a <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> can be covered by a finite number of <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruent</ConceptLink> steps of fixed size, providing the indispensable logical foundation for the classical method of exhaustion and the subsequent introduction of <ConceptLink targetId="distancia">real-valued metrics</ConceptLink> compatible with synthetic congruences.
