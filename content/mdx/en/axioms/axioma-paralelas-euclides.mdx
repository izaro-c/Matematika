<Capitular letra="E" />uclid's <ConceptLink targetId="paralelismo">parallel</ConceptLink> axiom, presented equivalently via Playfair's axiom, characterizes planar <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-euclidiana">Euclidean geometry</ConceptLink> in contrast to non-Euclidean geometries.

<Definicion title="">
  Given a <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> $l$ and a <ConceptLink targetId="punto">point</ConceptLink> $P$ not on $l$, there exists a unique line $m$ containing $P$ such that $m$ is <ConceptLink targetId="paralelismo">parallel</ConceptLink> to $l$:
  $$
  \forall l \in \mathcal{L}, \forall P \notin l \implies \exists! m \in \mathcal{L} \ (P \in m \land m \parallel l)
  $$
</Definicion>

### Visual representation

Given the base line <VisualBind element="lineBC" color="pavo">$l$</VisualBind> and exterior point <VisualBind element="pP" color="terracota">$P$</VisualBind>, the construction uniquely determines the parallel line <VisualBind element="parBCP" color="ocre">$m$</VisualBind> passing through $P$ without intersecting $l$.

### Axiomatic significance

This postulate asserts zero spatial curvature. It is logically independent of the axioms of <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="orden">order</ConceptLink>, and <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink>; its inclusion defines <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-euclidiana">Euclidean geometry</ConceptLink> and implies that the interior angle sum of any <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink> equals exactly two right angles ($\pi$ radians).
