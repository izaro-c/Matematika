<Capitular letra="E" />uclid's parallel axiom —established in the equivalent uniqueness formulation by John Playfair and formalized by <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink>— is the hallmark postulate of classical Euclidean geometry. It asserts that through a <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>point</ConceptLink> not on a given <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink>, there exists exactly one <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallel</ConceptLink> line in the same <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal statement">
  Given a <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink> $l \in \mathcal{L}$ and a <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>point</ConceptLink> $P \notin l$ in a <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{P}$, there exists exactly one line $l' \subset \mathcal{P}$ passing through $P$ that does not intersect $l$:
  $$
  \forall l \in \mathcal{L}, \forall P \in \mathcal{P} \setminus l, \ \exists! l' \subset \mathcal{P} \ (P \in l' \land l \cap l' = \emptyset)
  $$
</Definicion>

### Geometric significance and scope

This axiom fixes zero spatial curvature ($K = 0$). It is directly responsible for the constant angle sum of $180^\circ$ in every <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink>, the existence of rectangles, and the theory of similar figures at arbitrary scales.

<Separador />

### Properties

- **Playfair equivalence:** Logically equivalent to Euclid's original fifth postulate on interior angles.
- **Euclidean angle sum:** Directly proves the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-suma-angulos-triangulo">triangle angle sum theorem</ConceptLink> ($\alpha + \beta + \gamma = 180^\circ$).
- **Transitivity of parallelism:** In Euclidean geometry, parallelism is an equivalence relation ($l_1 \parallel l_2 \land l_2 \parallel l_3 \implies l_1 \parallel l_3$).
- **Metric foundation:** Enables proving <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-tales">Thales's theorem</ConceptLink> and the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-pitagoras">Pythagorean theorem</ConceptLink>.
