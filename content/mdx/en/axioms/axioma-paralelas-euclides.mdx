<Capitular letra="G" />iven a <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink> and a <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>point</ConceptLink> not <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia" isDependency={true}>incident</ConceptLink> with it in a <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink>, at most one line <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas" isDependency={true}>parallel</ConceptLink> to the given line passes through that point in the same plane.

The existence of at least one parallel need not be postulated: it is a <ConceptLink targetId="teorema">theorem</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>, a consequence of the converse direction of the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-angulos-alternos-internos">alternate interior angles theorem</ConceptLink>, which uses only the axioms of <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">order</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink>. The axiom imposes only the upper bound, which fixes uniqueness and separates the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-euclidiano">Euclidean system</ConceptLink> from the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-hiperbolico">hyperbolic system</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal statement (Hilbert's Axiom IV, Playfair form)">
  Let $\pi \in \Pi$ be a <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink>, let <VisualBind element="lineBC" color="carbon">$\ell \in \mathcal{L}$</VisualBind> be a <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink> whose <ConceptLink targetId="traza">trace</ConceptLink> satisfies $\operatorname{tr}(\ell) \subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\pi)$, and let <VisualBind element="pA" color="ocre">$P \in \mathcal{P}$</VisualBind> be a <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>point</ConceptLink> of $\pi$ not <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia" isDependency={true}>incident</ConceptLink> with $\ell$, that is, $P \in \operatorname{tr}(\pi) \setminus \operatorname{tr}(\ell)$. There is at most one line <VisualBind element="parBCP" color="ocre">$\ell' \in \mathcal{L}$</VisualBind> of $\pi$ passing through $P$ and <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas" isDependency={true}>parallel</ConceptLink> to $\ell$:
  $$
  \forall \ell'_1, \ell'_2 \in \mathcal{L} \ \Bigl(\bigl(\operatorname{tr}(\ell'_1) \cup \operatorname{tr}(\ell'_2) \subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\pi) \land P \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell'_1 \land P \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell'_2 \land \ell'_1 \parallel \ell \land \ell'_2 \parallel \ell\bigr) \implies \ell'_1 = \ell'_2\Bigr)
  $$
  Together with the existence <ConceptLink targetId="teorema">theorem</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>, the axiom yields existence and uniqueness of the parallel:
  $$
  \exists! \ell' \in \mathcal{L} \ \bigl(\operatorname{tr}(\ell') \subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\pi) \land P \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell' \land \ell' \parallel \ell\bigr)
  $$
</Definicion>

### Scope and role in the deductive system

Euclid formulated the <ConceptLink targetId="quinto-postulado-euclides">fifth postulate</ConceptLink> differently: if a <ConceptLink targetId="secante">transversal</ConceptLink> makes with two lines, on one side, two interior <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> whose sum is less than two <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto">right angles</ConceptLink>, those lines meet on that side. Over <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink> both formulations are <ConceptLink targetId="equivalencia-logica">equivalent</ConceptLink>: each follows from the other with the help of the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-angulos-alternos-internos">alternate interior angles theorem</ConceptLink>. The uniqueness form adopted by Hilbert goes back to <ConceptLink targetId="playfair">Playfair</ConceptLink> (1795) and is known as <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-playfair">Playfair's axiom</ConceptLink>. Among its consequences, developed in the corresponding <ConceptLink targetId="teorema">theorems</ConceptLink>, are:

- The <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-transitiva">transitivity</ConceptLink> of parallelism, which makes it an <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-equivalencia">equivalence relation</ConceptLink> on <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink>.
- The direct direction of the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-angulos-alternos-internos">alternate interior angles theorem</ConceptLink>: a <ConceptLink targetId="secante">transversal</ConceptLink> to two parallel lines forms <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruent</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-alternos-internos">alternate interior angles</ConceptLink>.
- The <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-suma-angulos-triangulo">triangle angle sum theorem</ConceptLink>: the interior angles of every <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink> add up to two <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto">right angles</ConceptLink>.
- The foundation of <ConceptLink targetId="semejanza">similarity</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangles</ConceptLink>, of <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-tales">Thales' theorem</ConceptLink> and of the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-pitagoras">Pythagorean theorem</ConceptLink>.
