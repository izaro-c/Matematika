<Capitular letra="T" />hree non-collinear <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> cannot belong to more than one <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink>, and every plane contains at least three points not lying on the same <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink>.

This postulate complements <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-4">Axiom I.4</ConceptLink>, completing the uniqueness property: three non-aligned points determine a single unique plane in <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal Statement">
  1. If three points <VisualBind element="pA" color="terracota">$A$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pB" color="terracota">$B$</VisualBind>, and <VisualBind element="pC" color="terracota">$C$</VisualBind> are non-collinear, there exists at most one <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="plano" color="canela">$\pi$</VisualBind> incident with all three:
  $$
  \forall A, B, C \in \mathcal{P}, \, \forall \pi_1, \pi_2 \in \Pi \ \bigl((\text{non-collinear}(A, B, C) \land A, B, C \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi_1 \land A, B, C \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi_2) \implies \pi_1 = \pi_2\bigr)
  $$
  2. Every plane $\pi \in \Pi$ contains at least three distinct non-collinear points incident with it.
</Definicion>
