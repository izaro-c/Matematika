This postulate asserts the absolute flatness and structural coherence of the geometric plane.

<Definicion title="Formal statement">
  For every <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> $l \in \mathcal{L}$ and every <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{P} \in \mathcal{F}$, if there exist two distinct <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> $A, B \in l$ such that $A \in \mathcal{P}$ and $B \in \mathcal{P}$, then $l \subset \mathcal{P}$:
  $$
  \forall l \in \mathcal{L}, \forall \mathcal{P} \in \mathcal{F} \ \bigl((\exists A, B \in l \ (A \neq B \land A, B \in \mathcal{P})) \implies l \subset \mathcal{P}\bigr)
  $$
</Definicion>

### Geometric significance and scope

This axiom ensures strict flatness: a plane cannot warp or have indents that allow a straight line to pierce two of its points without being completely submerged in it. It bridges one-dimensional linear geometry with two-dimensional plane geometry.

<Separador />

### Properties

- **Planar membership criterion:** Verifying the membership of two distinct points suffices to establish complete line inclusion.
- **Surface intersection restriction:** Prevents a line from intersecting a plane at exactly two distinct points without being entirely contained in it.
- **Convexity foundation:** Facilitates the construction of <ConceptLink targetId="convexo">convex</ConceptLink> sets and half-planes.
