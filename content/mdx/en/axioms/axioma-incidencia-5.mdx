<Capitular letra="T" />hrough three non-<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>points</ConceptLink> there passes at most one <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink>.

This axiom supplies the uniqueness that completes <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-4">Axiom I.4</ConceptLink>: three points not incident with the same <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink> determine one and only one plane. It is the analogue, for planes, of the uniqueness part of <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-1">Axiom I.1</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal statement">
Let <VisualBind element="pA" color="terracota">$A$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pB" color="terracota">$B$</VisualBind>, and <VisualBind element="pC" color="terracota">$C$</VisualBind> be three non-<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink>. There exists at most one <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="plano" color="pavo">$\pi$</VisualBind> <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia" isDependency={true}>incident</ConceptLink> with all three:

$$
\forall A, B, C \in \mathcal{P} \ \forall \pi_1, \pi_2 \in \Pi \ \Biggl(\Bigl(\neg \exists \ell \in \mathcal{L} \, (A \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell \land B \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell \land C \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell) \land \bigwedge_{X \in \{A, B, C\}} (X \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi_1 \land X \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi_2)\Bigr) \implies \pi_1 = \pi_2\Biggr)
$$
</Definicion>
