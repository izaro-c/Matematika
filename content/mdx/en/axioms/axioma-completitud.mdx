<Capitular letra="D" />edekind's completeness axiom represents the ultimate postulate of <ConceptLink targetId="continuidad">continuity</ConceptLink> in linear geometry. It guarantees the absence of "gaps" or discontinuities in the geometric <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="">
  If the points of a <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> $l$ are partitioned into two non-empty subsets $S_1$ and $S_2$ such that no point of $S_1$ lies between two points of $S_2$ and no point of $S_2$ lies between two of $S_1$, then there exists a unique <ConceptLink targetId="punto">point</ConceptLink> $O \in l$ that determines this partition:
  $$
  \forall (S_1, S_2) \text{ partition on } l \implies \exists! O \in l \ (\forall P \in S_1 \setminus \{O\}, \forall Q \in S_2 \setminus \{O\} \implies P \text{-} O \text{-} Q)
  $$
</Definicion>

### Axiomatic significance

This axiom establishes an isomorphism between the synthetic geometric line and the complete ordered field of real numbers $\mathbb{R}$. It ensures the existence of intersection points between circles and lines when relative distances mandate it, underpinning infinitesimal calculus and analytic geometry.
