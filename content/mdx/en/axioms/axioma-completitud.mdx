<Capitular letra="T" />he system formed by the <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>points</ConceptLink> of a <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink> and its linear relations of <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre" isDependency={true}>order</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruence</ConceptLink> is not susceptible to any extension or enlargement that preserves the validity of the axioms of incidence, order, congruence, and the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-arquimedes" isDependency={true}>Archimedean axiom</ConceptLink>.

In the equivalent synthetic formulation via the cut principle of <ConceptLink targetId="dedekind">Richard Dedekind</ConceptLink>, every connected <ConceptLink targetId="particion">partition</ConceptLink> of the points of a line into two non-empty classes possesses a unique separating element. This principle ensures the absolute <ConceptLink targetId="continuidad">continuity</ConceptLink> of the geometric line, ruling out any gap, break, or infinitesimal hole in geometry.

<Definicion title="Axiom V.2 (Line Completeness / Dedekind Continuity)">
  Let $\ell \in \mathcal{L}$ be a <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> with <ConceptLink targetId="traza">point trace</ConceptLink> $\operatorname{tr}(\ell)$. If all <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> of $\ell$ are partitioned into two non-empty, <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-disjunto">disjoint</ConceptLink> classes $\Sigma_1$ and $\Sigma_2$ ($\operatorname{tr}(\ell) = \Sigma_1 \cup \Sigma_2$ with $\Sigma_1 \cap \Sigma_2 = \emptyset$) such that no point of $\Sigma_1$ lies <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">between</ConceptLink> two points of $\Sigma_2$ and vice versa, then there exists a unique point $P \in \operatorname{tr}(\ell)$ such that for all $A \in \Sigma_1 \setminus \{P\}$ and $B \in \Sigma_2 \setminus \{P\}$, point $P$ lies between $A$ and $B$ ($A * P * B$):
  $$
  \forall \Sigma_1, \Sigma_2 \subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\ell) \ \Bigl(\operatorname{biparticion-conexa}(\Sigma_1, \Sigma_2, \ell) \implies \exists! P \in \operatorname{tr}(\ell) \ \forall A \in \Sigma_1 \setminus \{P\}, \, \forall B \in \Sigma_2 \setminus \{P\} \ (A * P * B)\Bigr)
  $$
  Under <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert's</ConceptLink> maximality principle, it is impossible to attach new points to a line in such a way that the extended structure still satisfies the axioms of <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">order</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink>, and the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-arquimedes">Archimedean axiom</ConceptLink>.
</Definicion>

### Geometric significance and deductive scope

The axiom of line completeness crowns Hilbert's foundation of continuous space, ensuring a complete structural <ConceptLink targetId="isomorfismo">isomorphism</ConceptLink> between the synthetic geometric <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> and the continuous ordered <ConceptLink targetId="cuerpo">field</ConceptLink> of real numbers $\mathbb{R}$.

This principle decisively resolves the deductive shortcomings of <ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid's</ConceptLink> *Elements*, where intersections between <ConceptLink targetId="secante">secant lines</ConceptLink> and closed curves such as the <ConceptLink targetId="circunferencia">circle</ConceptLink> —or between two intersecting circles— were taken for granted based on visual evidence without formal proof. In a model restricted to rational coordinates $\mathbb{Q}^2$, a secant line may pass through a circle without sharing any point of intersection; Axiom V.2 formally guarantees the existence of such intersection points and provides the indispensable cornerstone for mathematical analysis and differential geometry.
