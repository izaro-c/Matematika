<Capitular letra="G" />iven any two distinct <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>points</ConceptLink> $A$ and $B$, the second order axiom of <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink> guarantees that the line <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink> $\overline{AB}$ can always be extended beyond $B$, locating a new point $C$ such that $B$ occupies an intermediate position ($A\text{-}B\text{-}C$).

<Definicion title="Formal statement">
  For any two distinct <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>points</ConceptLink> $A, B \in \mathcal{S}$, there exists at least one point $C \in \mathcal{S}$ such that $B$ lies <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre" isDependency={true}>between</ConceptLink> $A$ and $C$:
  $$
  \forall A, B \in \mathcal{S} \ \bigl(A \neq B \implies \exists C \in \mathcal{S} \ (A\text{-}B\text{-}C)\bigr)
  $$
</Definicion>

<Separador />

### Geometric significance and scope

This axiom ensures that geometric lines are unbounded in both directions and possess no intrinsic endpoints or borders. Together with incidence axioms, it proves that every line contains infinitely many ordered points without spatial finiteness.

<Separador />

### Properties

- **Linear unboundedness:** No line terminates or possesses an extreme boundary point.
- **Segment extendibility:** Enables extending any rectilinear segment without limitation in <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>.
- **Infinitude of points:** Constitutes the primary principle excluding finite geometric systems in the presence of order relations.
