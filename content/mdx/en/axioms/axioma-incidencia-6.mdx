<Definicion title="Formal statement">
  If two distinct <ConceptLink targetId="plano">planes</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{P}_1, \mathcal{P}_2 \in \mathcal{F}$ ($\mathcal{P}_1 \neq \mathcal{P}_2$) share a <ConceptLink targetId="punto">point</ConceptLink> $A \in \mathcal{P}_1 \cap \mathcal{P}_2$, then there exists at least another point $B \in \mathcal{P}_1 \cap \mathcal{P}_2$ with $B \neq A$, and their exact intersection is a <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> $l \in \mathcal{L}$:
  $$
  \forall \mathcal{P}_1, \mathcal{P}_2 \in \mathcal{F} \ \bigl((\mathcal{P}_1 \neq \mathcal{P}_2 \land A \in \mathcal{P}_1 \cap \mathcal{P}_2) \implies \exists B \in \mathcal{P}_1 \cap \mathcal{P}_2 \ (B \neq A) \land \exists l \in \mathcal{L} \ (l = \mathcal{P}_1 \cap \mathcal{P}_2)\bigr)
  $$
</Definicion>

<Separador />

### Geometric significance and scope

This axiom guarantees dimensional consistency in three-dimensional space: two distinct planes cannot touch tangentially at an isolated point. Any non-empty intersection between distinct planes necessarily yields a one-dimensional linear trace.

<Separador />

### Properties

- **Intersection continuity:** Excludes isolated point contacts between distinct planes.
- **Dihedral angle foundation:** Serves as the basis for defining dihedral <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> and spatial separation.
- **Linear duality:** Demonstrates the dual relationship between joining non-collinear points and intersecting planes.
