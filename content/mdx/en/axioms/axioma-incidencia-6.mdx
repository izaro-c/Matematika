<Capitular letra="I" />f two distinct <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> of a <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> belong to a <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink>, then all points of that line belong to that plane.

This axiom formalizes the geometric rigidity and flatness of the plane: a line cannot enter and leave a plane, nor can it bend relative to it in <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal Statement">
  Let <VisualBind element="pA" color="terracota">$A$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="pB" color="terracota">$B$</VisualBind> be two distinct points of a <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="recta" color="carbon">$\ell$</VisualBind>. If $A$ and $B$ <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">are incident</ConceptLink> with a <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="plano" color="canela">$\pi$</VisualBind>, then the entire line $\ell$ is contained in $\pi$:
  $$
  \forall \ell \in \mathcal{L}, \, \forall \pi \in \Pi, \, \forall A, B \in \mathcal{P} \ \bigl((A \neq B \land A \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell \land B \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell \land A \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi \land B \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi) \implies \ell \subseteq \pi\bigr)
  $$
  where $\ell \subseteq \pi$ denotes that every point incident with $\ell$ is also incident with $\pi$.
</Definicion>
