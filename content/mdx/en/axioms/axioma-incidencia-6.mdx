<Capitular letra="I" />f two distinct <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>points</ConceptLink> of a <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink> are <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia" isDependency={true}>incident</ConceptLink> with a <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink>, then every point of that line is also incident with the plane.

In the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-incidencia">incidence system</ConceptLink>, this postulate complements planar flatness by ensuring that a <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> sharing two <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> with a <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> remains entirely embedded within its surface in <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Axiom I.6 (Inclusion of the Line in the Plane)">
Let <VisualBind element="pA" color="terracota">$A$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="pB" color="terracota">$B$</VisualBind> be two distinct <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> of a <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="recta" color="carbon">$\ell$</VisualBind>. If $A$ and $B$ are <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incident</ConceptLink> with a <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="plano" color="azul">$\pi$</VisualBind>, then any point incident with $\ell$ is also incident with $\pi$:

$$
\forall \ell \in \mathcal{L}, \, \forall \pi \in \Pi, \, \forall A, B \in \mathcal{P} \ \Biggl(\Bigl(A \neq B \land A \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell \land B \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell \land A \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi \land B \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi\Bigr) \implies \forall P \in \mathcal{P} \ (P \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell \implies P \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi)\Biggr)
$$
</Definicion>

<Nota>
In terms of <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto">sets</ConceptLink>, the conclusion of the axiom expresses the inclusion of <ConceptLink targetId="traza">point traces</ConceptLink> $\operatorname{tr}(\ell) \subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\pi)$. This property allows the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema">theorems</ConceptLink> of plane <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-sintetica">synthetic geometry</ConceptLink> to be systematically extended to the realm of three-dimensional <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink>.
</Nota>
