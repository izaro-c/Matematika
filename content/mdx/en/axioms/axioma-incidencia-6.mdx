<Capitular letra="I" />f two distinct <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> of a <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> belong to a <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink>, then all points of that line belong to that plane.

This axiom formalizes the rigidity and flatness of the plane: a line cannot enter and leave a plane, nor can it bend relative to it.

<Definicion title="Formal Statement">
  Let <VisualBind element="pA" color="terracota">$A$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="pB" color="terracota">$B$</VisualBind> be two distinct points of a <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="recta" color="carbon">$\ell$</VisualBind>. If $A$ and $B$ belong to a <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="plano" color="canela">$\pi$</VisualBind>, then the entire line $\ell$ is contained in $\pi$:
  $$
  (A \in \ell \land B \in \ell \land A \neq B \land A \in \pi \land B \in \pi) \implies \ell \subset \pi
  $$
</Definicion>

<Separador />

### Significance in the theory

Allows all properties of the geometry of lines to be transferred into the two-dimensional plane.
