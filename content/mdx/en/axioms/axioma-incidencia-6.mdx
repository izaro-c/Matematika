<Capitular letra="I" />f two distinct <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>points</ConceptLink> of a <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink> are <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia" isDependency={true}>incident</ConceptLink> with a <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink>, then every point of that line is also incident with the plane.

Since lines and planes are primitive objects of different sorts, the phrase "the line lies in the plane" cannot mean an inclusion between them. This axiom gives it a precise, checkable content: it suffices that two distinct points of the line be incident with the plane for all of them to be.

<Definicion title="Formal statement">
Let <VisualBind element="pA" color="terracota">$A$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="pB" color="terracota">$B$</VisualBind> be two distinct <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> of a <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="recta" color="carbon">$\ell$</VisualBind>. If $A$ and $B$ are <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incident</ConceptLink> with a <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="plano" color="pavo">$\pi$</VisualBind>, then every point incident with $\ell$ is also incident with $\pi$:

$$
\forall \ell \in \mathcal{L} \ \forall \pi \in \Pi \ \forall A, B \in \mathcal{P} \ \Biggl(\Bigl(A \neq B \land A \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell \land B \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell \land A \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi \land B \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi\Bigr) \implies \forall P \in \mathcal{P} \ (P \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell \implies P \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi)\Biggr)
$$
</Definicion>

<Nota>
In terms of <ConceptLink targetId="traza">point traces</ConceptLink>, the conclusion of the axiom is the inclusion of point <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto">sets</ConceptLink> $\operatorname{tr}(\ell) \subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\pi)$. This is how planar statements, such as Pasch's <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-4">Axiom II.4</ConceptLink>, express that a line lies in a given plane.
</Nota>
