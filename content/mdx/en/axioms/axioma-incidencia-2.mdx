<Capitular letra="E" />very <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> contains at least two distinct <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink>.

This axiom ensures that lines are not empty sets and do not consist of a single isolated point, guaranteeing that every line has real geometric content to operate upon.

<Definicion title="Formal Statement">
  For every <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="lineCD" color="carbon">$\ell$</VisualBind>, there exist at least two distinct <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="pA" color="terracota">$A$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="pB" color="terracota">$B$</VisualBind> <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incident</ConceptLink> with $\ell$:
  $$
  \forall \ell \in \mathcal{L} \ \exists A, B \in \mathcal{P} \ (A \neq B \land A \in \ell \land B \in \ell)
  $$
</Definicion>

<Separador />

### Significance in the theory

Together with <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-1">Axiom I.1</ConceptLink>, it establishes a reciprocal connection: two points determine a line, and every line contains at least two points to be identifiable.
