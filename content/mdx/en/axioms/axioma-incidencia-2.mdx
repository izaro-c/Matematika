<Capitular letra="E" />very <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> contains a minimum of two distinct <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> in <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink>, as stipulated by <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink>'s second <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence axiom</ConceptLink>. This condition prevents the occurrence of degenerate single-point lines within the geometric system.

<Definicion title="Formal statement">
  For every <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> $l \in \mathcal{L}$, there exist at least two distinct <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> $A, B \in \mathcal{S}$ belonging to $l$:
  $$
  \forall l \in \mathcal{L}, \ \exists A, B \in \mathcal{S} \ \bigl(A \in l \land B \in l \land A \neq B\bigr)
  $$
</Definicion>

### Geometric significance and scope

This axiom ensures that lines are not isolated point-like entities, but structures possessing multiple distinguishable positions. Combined with Axiom I.1, it guarantees a foundational linear framework.

<Separador />

### Properties

- **Non-degeneracy:** Excludes empty lines or lines comprising a single point.
- **Multitude foundation:** Provides the starting point to prove that every line contains infinitely many points once <ConceptLink targetId="orden">order axioms</ConceptLink> are introduced.
- **Segment viability:** Permits the formation of a non-trivial <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink> $\overline{AB}$.
