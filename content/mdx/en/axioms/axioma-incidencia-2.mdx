<Capitular letra="T" />he second incidence axiom stipulates that every <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink> possesses a minimal multiplicity of elements, containing no fewer than two distinct <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>points</ConceptLink>. This condition prevents the emergence of degenerate lines consisting of a single isolated point or lacking linear extension.

<Definicion title="Formal statement">
  For every <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink> $l \in \mathcal{L}$, there exist at least two distinct <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>points</ConceptLink> $A, B \in \mathcal{S}$ incident with $l$:
  $$
  \forall l \in \mathcal{L}, \ \exists A, B \in \mathcal{S} \ \bigl(A \in l \land B \in l \land A \neq B\bigr)
  $$
</Definicion>

### Visual representation and interaction

In the interactive simulation, line <VisualBind element="lineCD" color="carbon">$l$</VisualBind> contains points <VisualBind element="pA" color="terracota">$A$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="pB" color="terracota">$B$</VisualBind>. <Separador />

### Geometric significance and scope

This axiom ensures that lines are not isolated single points or empty sets, but continuous geometric structures composed of multiple distinguishable locations. Together with Axiom I.1, it secures a well-defined one-dimensional linear support.

<Separador />

### Properties

- **Non-degeneracy:** Prohibits lines consisting of a single point or empty sets.
- **Elemental multiplicity:** Serves as the starting point to prove that every line contains infinitely many points once the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-1">order axioms</ConceptLink> are introduced.
- **Segment viability:** Permits the formation of a non-trivial line <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink> $\overline{AB}$.
