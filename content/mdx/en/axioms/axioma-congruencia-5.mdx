<Capitular letra="T" />he fifth <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink> axiom of <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">Hilbert</ConceptLink> (classically known as the Side-Angle-Side or SAS criterion) establishes the primary link between <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink> congruence and <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angle</ConceptLink> congruence. It guarantees the rigid superposition of <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangles</ConceptLink> in <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-absoluta">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="">
  Let $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle A'B'C'$ be two <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangles</ConceptLink>. If segment $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{A'B'}$, segment $\overline{AC} \cong \overline{A'C'}$, and the included angle $\angle BAC \cong \angle B'A'C'$, then the remaining corresponding angles are also <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruent</ConceptLink>:
  $$
  (\overline{AB} \cong \overline{A'B'}) \land (\overline{AC} \cong \overline{A'C'}) \land (\angle BAC \cong \angle B'A'C') \implies (\angle ABC \cong \angle A'B'C') \land (\angle ACB \cong \angle A'C'B')
  $$
</Definicion>

### Visual representation

Fixing congruent sides <VisualBind element="AB" color="ocre">$\overline{AB}$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="AC" color="pavo">$\overline{AC}$</VisualBind> along with their included angle <VisualBind element="angA" color="terracota">$\angle BAC$</VisualBind> uniquely fixes the shape and orientation of the triangle, compelling total figure congruence.

### Axiomatic significance

Unlike Euclid, who attempted to "prove" superposition via unaxiommatized motion, Hilbert postulated SAS as a foundational axiom. From this single principle, all other congruence criteria (ASA, SSS) and theorems regarding the <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo-isosceles">isosceles triangle</ConceptLink> are rigorously derived.
