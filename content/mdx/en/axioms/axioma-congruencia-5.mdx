<Capitular letra="I" />f two <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangles</ConceptLink> have two <ConceptLink targetId="lado">sides</ConceptLink> and the included <ConceptLink targetId="angulo" isDependency={true}>angle</ConceptLink> respectively <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink>, then the remaining angles opposite those sides are also congruent to each other.

In the foundation of <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-sintetica">synthetic geometry</ConceptLink> by <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink>, this <ConceptLink targetId="axioma">postulate</ConceptLink> serves as the foundation of rigidity in the <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink>. Unlike the classical approach of <ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid</ConceptLink> —based on superposition via <ConceptLink targetId="movimiento-rigido">rigid motion</ConceptLink>—, Hilbert establishes it as an axiom of relational consistency between <ConceptLink targetId="segmento" isDependency={true}>line segments</ConceptLink> and angles.

<Definicion title="Axiom III.5 (Side-Angle-Side Criterion)">
Let <VisualBind element="triangleABC" color="ocre">$\triangle ABC$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="triangleAABBCC" color="mora">$\triangle A'B'C'$</VisualBind> be two <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangles</ConceptLink> determined by <ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">non-collinear</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> in the primitive <ConceptLink targetId="dominio">domain</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{P}$, with <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertices</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="pA" color="ocre">$A$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pB" color="ocre">$B$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pC" color="ocre">$C$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="pAA" color="mora">$A'$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pBB" color="mora">$B'$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pCC" color="mora">$C'$</VisualBind>. If the congruences of the <ConceptLink targetId="lado">sides</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="sidesAB" color="ocre">$\overline{AB} \cong \overline{A'B'}$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="sidesAC" color="pavo">$\overline{AC} \cong \overline{A'C'}$</VisualBind> hold, together with the congruence of the included <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angle</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="angles" color="terracota">$\angle BAC \cong \angle B'A'C'$</VisualBind>, then the angular congruences <VisualBind element="anglesB" color="ocre">$\angle ABC \cong \angle A'B'C'$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="anglesC" color="pavo">$\angle ACB \cong \angle A'C'B'$</VisualBind> necessarily hold:

$$
\forall A, B, C, A', B', C' \in \mathcal{P} \ \Biggl( (\neg\operatorname{Col}(A,B,C) \land \neg\operatorname{Col}(A',B',C')) \implies \Bigl( (\overline{AB} \cong \overline{A'B'} \land \overline{AC} \cong \overline{A'C'} \land \angle BAC \cong \angle B'A'C') \implies (\angle ABC \cong \angle A'B'C' \land \angle ACB \cong \angle A'C'B') \Bigr) \Biggr)
$$
</Definicion>
