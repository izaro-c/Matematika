<Capitular letra="T" />he fifth congruence axiom of <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink> rigorously formalizes the Side-Angle-Side (SAS) criterion for <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>triangle congruence</ConceptLink>. In contrast to Euclid's reliance upon the physical superposition of figures, Hilbert posits intrinsic triangular rigidity as a fundamental axiomatic principle.

<Definicion title="Formal statement">
  Let $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle A'B'C'$ be two <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangles</ConceptLink>. If $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{A'B'}$, $\overline{AC} \cong \overline{A'C'}$, and $\angle BAC \cong \angle B'A'C'$, then the remaining corresponding angle congruences hold:
  $$
  (\overline{AB} \cong \overline{A'B'} \land \overline{AC} \cong \overline{A'C'} \land \angle BAC \cong \angle B'A'C') \implies (\angle ABC \cong \angle A'B'C' \land \angle ACB \cong \angle A'C'B')
  $$
  Consequently, the third sides are congruent ($\overline{BC} \cong \overline{B'C'}$) and the two triangles are fully congruent ($\triangle ABC \cong \triangle A'B'C'$).
</Definicion>

### Geometric significance and scope

This axiom bypasses the logical ambiguities of "rigid motion" of figures across abstract space. It provides the essential deductive foundation for proving subsequent congruence criteria (<ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-ala">ASA</ConceptLink>, SSS) and the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-triangulo-isosceles">isosceles triangle theorem</ConceptLink> in <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>.

<Separador />

### Properties

- **Triangular rigidity:** Three independent elements uniquely determine the totality of a triangle's geometric dimensions.
- **Deductive bedrock:** Directly yields the ASA, AAS, and SSS congruence criteria without supplemental axioms.
- **Universal validity:** Holds equally across <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-euclidiano">Euclidean geometry</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-hiperbolico">hyperbolic geometry</ConceptLink>.
