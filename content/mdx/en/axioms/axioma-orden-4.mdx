<Capitular letra="F" />ormulated originally by <ConceptLink targetId="pasch">Moritz Pasch</ConceptLink> and incorporated by <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink> as the fourth axiom of order, this postulate resolves one of the most famous tacit deductive gaps in Euclid's *Elements*. It guarantees that a <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink> entering the interior of a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink> through one of its sides cannot become trapped or terminate inside, and must inevitably exit through a second side.

<Definicion title="Formal statement">
  Let $A, B, C \in \mathcal{S}$ be three non-collinear <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>points</ConceptLink>, and let $l \in \mathcal{L}$ be a <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink> in plane $\pi(ABC)$ passing through none of the vertices ($A, B, C \notin l$). If $l$ contains a point $P$ lying <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre" isDependency={true}>between</ConceptLink> $A$ and $B$ ($A\text{-}P\text{-}B$, so $l$ intersects <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink> $\overline{AB}$), then $l$ contains a point $Q$ with $A\text{-}Q\text{-}C$ or a point $R$ with $B\text{-}R\text{-}C$:
  $$
  (P \in l \land A\text{-}P\text{-}B) \implies \bigl(\exists Q \in l \ (A\text{-}Q\text{-}C) \lor \exists R \in l \ (B\text{-}R\text{-}C)\bigr)
  $$
</Definicion>

### Geometric significance and scope

Pasch's axiom extends one-dimensional linear ordering to planar topological separation. It provides the rigorous foundation for the Jordan curve theorem for simple polygons and establishes the concepts of convex interior and exterior regions in <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>.

<Separador />

### Properties

- **Plane separation:** Enables proving that every line $l$ separates the plane $\pi \setminus l$ into two disjoint, connected, convex half-planes.
- **Planar topology:** Precludes geometric discontinuities or "holes" that would allow lines to pass through without genuine intersections.
- **Polygon rigor:** Underpins the formal definitions of polygon interiors and crossbar theorems.
