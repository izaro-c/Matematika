<Capitular letra="F" />ormulated by <ConceptLink targetId="pasch">Moritz Pasch</ConceptLink> and integrated by <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink>, the fourth <ConceptLink targetId="orden">order axiom</ConceptLink> extends the notion of betweenness from a single line to the two-dimensional plane. It states that if a <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> enters the interior of a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink> by intersecting one of its sides without passing through any of its vertices, it must exit by intersecting one of the other two sides.

<Definicion title="Formal statement">
  Given three non-collinear <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> $A, B, C$ and a <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> $l$ in their supporting plane not passing through any vertex $A, B, C$: if $l$ intersects the interior of <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink> $\overline{AB}$, then $l$ intersects either segment $\overline{AC}$ or segment $\overline{BC}$:
  $$
  \forall A, B, C \text{ non-collinear}, \ \forall l \subset \mathcal{P}_{ABC} \ \bigl((A,B,C \notin l \land l \cap \overline{AB} \neq \emptyset) \implies (l \cap \overline{AC} \neq \emptyset \veebar l \cap \overline{BC} \neq \emptyset)\bigr)
  $$
</Definicion>

### Geometric significance and scope

Pasch's axiom is indispensable for proving that a line divides a plane into two connected regions or half-planes (the plane separation theorem). Without this postulate, Euclid made logical omissions by implicitly assuming that certain lines and intersections existed inside figures.

<Separador />

### Properties

- **Plane separation:** Enables proving that any line $l$ in a plane partitions it into two disjoint <ConceptLink targetId="convexo">convex</ConceptLink> half-planes.
- **Planar topology:** Establishes the intuitive notion of "interior" and "exterior" for closed figures like <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangles</ConceptLink> and polygons.
- **Parallel axiom independence:** Independent of the parallel postulate, remaining fully valid within <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-absoluta">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>.
