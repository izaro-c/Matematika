<Capitular letra="I" />f a coplanar <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink> in the same <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink> as a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink> determined by three <ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">non-collinear</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>points</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia" isDependency={true}>intersects</ConceptLink> one of its <ConceptLink targetId="lado">sides</ConceptLink> passing <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre" isDependency={true}>between</ConceptLink> two <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertices</ConceptLink> without passing through them, then it must intersect an interior point of one of the other two sides.

Formulated by <ConceptLink targetId="pasch">Moritz Pasch</ConceptLink> and adopted by <ConceptLink targetId="david-hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink> as the fourth axiom of <ConceptLink targetId="orden">order</ConceptLink>, this postulate extends linear <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">betweenness</ConceptLink> to two dimensions in <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-sintetica">synthetic geometry</ConceptLink>, establishing planar separation and the <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-convexo">convexity</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="semiplano">half-planes</ConceptLink> in <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-hilbertiana">Hilbertian geometry</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Axiom II.4 (Pasch's Axiom)">
Let <VisualBind element="pA" color="ocre">$A$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pB" color="ocre">$B$</VisualBind>, and <VisualBind element="pC" color="ocre">$C$</VisualBind> be three non-<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> in $\mathcal{P}$ <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incident</ConceptLink> with a <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> $\pi \in \Pi$, and let <VisualBind element="lineL" color="mora">$\ell$</VisualBind> be a coplanar <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> ($\operatorname{tr}(\ell) \subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\pi)$) avoiding the <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertices</ConceptLink> ($\neg(A \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell)$, $\neg(B \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell)$, $\neg(C \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell)$). If $\ell$ intersects a point <VisualBind element="pP" color="terracota">$P \in \mathcal{P}$</VisualBind> such that $A * P * B$ on the open segment <VisualBind element="segAB" color="ocre">$\operatorname{seg}(AB)$</VisualBind> of triangle <VisualBind element="triangle" color="ocre">$\triangle ABC$</VisualBind>, then $\ell$ also intersects a point <VisualBind element="pQ" color="terracota">$Q \in \mathcal{P}$</VisualBind> such that $A * Q * C$ or $B * Q * C$:

$$
\forall A, B, C \in \mathcal{P} \ \forall \ell \in \mathcal{L} \ \forall \pi \in \Pi \ \Biggl( \Bigl(\neg\exists m \in \mathcal{L} \, (A \, \mathbf{I} \, m \land B \, \mathbf{I} \, m \land C \, \mathbf{I} \, m) \land A \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi \land B \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi \land C \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi \land \operatorname{tr}(\ell) \subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\pi) \land \neg(A \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell) \land \neg(B \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell) \land \neg(C \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell)\Bigr) \implies \Bigl(\exists P \in \mathcal{P} \, (P \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell \land A * P * B) \implies \exists Q \in \mathcal{P} \, (Q \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell \land (A * Q * C \lor B * Q * C))\Bigr)\Biggr)
$$
</Definicion>

<Nota>
Pasch's axiom is the deductive key to proving the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-separacion-plana">plane separation theorem</ConceptLink>, according to which every line decomposes its plane into two <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-convexo">convex</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-disjunto">disjoint</ConceptLink> regions known as <ConceptLink targetId="semiplano">half-planes</ConceptLink>. In <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>, it guarantees that a line cannot enter the interior of a <ConceptLink targetId="poligono">polygon</ConceptLink> without crossing its <ConceptLink targetId="frontera">boundary</ConceptLink>.
</Nota>
