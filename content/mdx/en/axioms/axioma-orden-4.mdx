<Capitular letra="L" />et there be a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink> with <ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">non-collinear</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertices</ConceptLink>, and a <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink> in its <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink> that is not <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia" isDependency={true}>incident</ConceptLink> with any of the three <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>points</ConceptLink>. If the line passes through a point lying <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre" isDependency={true}>between</ConceptLink> two vertices, it also passes through a point lying between the third vertex and one of the other two; that is, if it meets one <ConceptLink targetId="lado">side</ConceptLink>, it meets another.

Formulated by <ConceptLink targetId="pasch">Moritz Pasch</ConceptLink> in 1882 and adopted by <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink> as the fourth axiom of <ConceptLink targetId="orden">order</ConceptLink>, it is the only order axiom relating points of different lines. It extends <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">betweenness</ConceptLink>, which axioms II.1–II.3 govern on each line separately, to the plane, and the separation of the plane by a line and the <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-convexo">convexity</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="semiplano">half-planes</ConceptLink> rest on it.

<Definicion title="Formal statement">
Let <VisualBind element="pA" color="ocre">$A$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pB" color="ocre">$B$</VisualBind>, and <VisualBind element="pC" color="ocre">$C$</VisualBind> be three non-<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incident</ConceptLink> with a <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> $\pi$, and let <VisualBind element="lineL" color="mora">$\ell$</VisualBind> be a <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> of that plane, $\operatorname{tr}(\ell) \subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\pi)$, not incident with any of the <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertices</ConceptLink> of the triangle <VisualBind element="triangle" color="ocre">$\triangle ABC$</VisualBind>. If $\ell$ is incident with a point <VisualBind element="pP" color="terracota">$P$</VisualBind> of the open <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="segAB" color="ocre">$\operatorname{seg}(AB)$</VisualBind>, that is, with $A * P * B$, then $\ell$ is also incident with a point <VisualBind element="pQ" color="terracota">$Q$</VisualBind> of $\operatorname{seg}(AC)$ or of $\operatorname{seg}(BC)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\forall A, B, C \in \mathcal{P} \ \forall \ell \in \mathcal{L} \ \forall \pi \in \Pi \ \Bigl( \bigl(\neg \operatorname{Col}(A, B, C) \land A \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi \land B \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi \land C \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi \land \operatorname{tr}(\ell) \subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\pi) \\
&\qquad \land \neg(A \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell) \land \neg(B \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell) \land \neg(C \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell) \land \operatorname{seg}(AB) \cap \operatorname{tr}(\ell) \neq \emptyset\bigr) \\
&\qquad \implies \bigl(\operatorname{seg}(AC) \cup \operatorname{seg}(BC)\bigr) \cap \operatorname{tr}(\ell) \neq \emptyset \Bigr)
\end{aligned}
$$
</Definicion>

<Nota>
The axiom guarantees that the line meets one of the other two sides, but does not exclude that it meets both. That a line passing through no vertex cannot meet all three sides of a triangle is a theorem deduced from axioms II.1–II.3; together with it, this axiom is the key to the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-separacion-plana">plane separation theorem</ConceptLink>, according to which every line divides its plane into two <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-convexo">convex</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-disjunto">disjoint</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="semiplano">half-planes</ConceptLink>.
</Nota>
