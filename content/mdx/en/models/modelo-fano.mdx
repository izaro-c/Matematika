<Capitular letra="T" />he **Fano plane** (denoted $\mathrm{PG}(2, \mathbb{F}_2)$) is the smallest finite <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-incidencia">incidence model</ConceptLink> and projective plane that exists. Introduced in 1892 by the Italian mathematician <ConceptLink targetId="fano">Gino Fano</ConceptLink>, it is composed of exactly 7 <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> and 7 <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink>, exhibiting complete combinatorial symmetry.

<Definicion title="Combinatorial Structure and Points">
  The Fano plane consists of:
  - **7 Points**: <VisualBind element="p1" color="terracota">1</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="p2" color="terracota">2</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="p3" color="terracota">3</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="p4" color="terracota">4</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="p5" color="terracota">5</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="p6" color="terracota">6</VisualBind>, and <VisualBind element="p7" color="musgo">7</VisualBind>.
  - **7 Lines** (each formed by an incident triple of points):
    - Three sides of the outer triangle: <VisualBind element="l1" color="carbon">$\{1, 2, 4\}$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="l2" color="carbon">$\{2, 3, 5\}$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="l3" color="carbon">$\{3, 1, 6\}$</VisualBind>.
    - Three concurrent median lines: <VisualBind element="l4" color="carbon">$\{1, 5, 7\}$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="l5" color="carbon">$\{2, 6, 7\}$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="l6" color="carbon">$\{3, 4, 7\}$</VisualBind>.
    - An inner circle (projective line): <VisualBind element="l7" color="musgo">$\{4, 5, 6\}$</VisualBind>.
</Definicion>

<SeccionPropiedades>
  <PropiedadesGrupo title="Structural and Axiomatic Properties">
    <PropiedadItem id="axioma-incidencia-1" title="Unambiguous determination of lines">
      Through any two distinct points passes exactly one line containing both (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-1">Axiom I.1</ConceptLink>).
    </PropiedadItem>
    <PropiedadItem id="interseccion-proyectiva" title="Universal projective intersection">
      Any two distinct lines intersect at exactly one point; consequently, there are no <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallel lines</ConceptLink>.
    </PropiedadItem>
    <PropiedadItem id="uniformidad-combinatoria" title="Combinatorial uniformity and duality">
      Symmetric configuration of type $(7_3, 7_3)$: every line contains exactly 3 points, and through every point pass exactly 3 lines.
    </PropiedadItem>
  </PropiedadesGrupo>

  <PropiedadesGrupo title="Algebraic Construction">
    <PropiedadItem id="espacio-vectorial-f2" title="Projective space over the finite field F2">
      Corresponds to the projective plane $\mathrm{PG}(2, \mathbb{F}_2) = (\mathbb{F}_2^3 \setminus \{(0,0,0)\}) / \sim$, where points are the 7 1-dimensional subspaces (lines) and lines are the 7 2-dimensional subspaces (planes) of $\mathbb{F}_2^3$.
    </PropiedadItem>
  </PropiedadesGrupo>
</SeccionPropiedades>
