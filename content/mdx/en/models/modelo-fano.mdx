<Capitular letra="T" />he **Fano plane** (denoted $\mathrm{PG}(2, \mathbb{F}_2)$) is a finite <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-geometrica">geometric structure</ConceptLink> constituting the minimal <ConceptLink targetId="espacio-proyectivo">projective plane</ConceptLink> and satisfying the planar incidence axioms of the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-incidencia">incidence system</ConceptLink>.

Introduced in 1892 by the mathematician <ConceptLink targetId="gino-fano">Gino Fano</ConceptLink>, it represents the projective space of minimal possible <ConceptLink targetId="cardinalidad">cardinality</ConceptLink>, combining <ConceptLink targetId="simetria">symmetry</ConceptLink> properties from <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-discreta">discrete geometry</ConceptLink> with <ConceptLink targetId="algebra-lineal">linear algebra</ConceptLink> over <ConceptLink targetId="cuerpo-finito">finite fields</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal Structure of the Model">
The Fano plane is formalized as a tuple $\mathcal{M}_{\text{Fano}} = (\mathcal{P}, \mathcal{L}, \mathbf{I})$ consisting of finite and <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-disjunto">disjoint sets</ConceptLink> ($\mathcal{P} \cap \mathcal{L} = \emptyset$):

- **<ConceptLink targetId="dominio">Domain</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink>**: $\mathcal{P} = \{$ <VisualBind element="p1" color="terracota">$1$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="p2" color="terracota">$2$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="p3" color="terracota">$3$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="p4" color="terracota">$4$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="p5" color="terracota">$5$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="p6" color="terracota">$6$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="p7" color="musgo">$7$</VisualBind> $\}$.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="dominio">Domain</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink>**: $\mathcal{L} = \{$ <VisualBind element="l1" color="carbon">$\ell_1$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="l2" color="carbon">$\ell_2$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="l3" color="carbon">$\ell_3$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="l4" color="carbon">$\ell_4$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="l5" color="carbon">$\ell_5$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="l6" color="carbon">$\ell_6$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="l7" color="musgo">$\ell_7$</VisualBind> $\}$.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">Incidence</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="relacion">relation</ConceptLink>** $\mathbf{I} \subseteq \mathcal{P} \times \mathcal{L}$, defined via the <ConceptLink targetId="traza">point traces</ConceptLink> of each line in the point <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto">set</ConceptLink>:
  - Outer <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink> sides: $\operatorname{tr}(\ell_1) = \{1, 2, 4\}$, $\operatorname{tr}(\ell_2) = \{2, 3, 5\}$, $\operatorname{tr}(\ell_3) = \{3, 1, 6\}$.
  - Concurrent medians: $\operatorname{tr}(\ell_4) = \{1, 5, 7\}$, $\operatorname{tr}(\ell_5) = \{2, 6, 7\}$, $\operatorname{tr}(\ell_6) = \{3, 4, 7\}$.
  - Inscribed circle (projective line): $\operatorname{tr}(\ell_7) = \{4, 5, 6\}$.
</Definicion>

  <PropiedadesGrupo title="Incidence Axiom Satisfaction">
    <PropiedadItem 
      id="axioma-incidencia-1" 
      title="Unique Line Determination"
      formula="\forall P, Q \in \mathcal{P} \ (P \neq Q \implies \exists! \ell \in \mathcal{L})"
    >
      For any two distinct points in $\mathcal{P}$, there exists a unique line in $\mathcal{L}$ incident with both ($\forall P, Q \in \mathcal{P} \ (P \neq Q \implies \exists! \ell \in \mathcal{L} \ (P \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell \land Q \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell))$), satisfying <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-1">Axiom I.1</ConceptLink>.
    </PropiedadItem>

    <PropiedadItem 
      id="axioma-incidencia-2" 
      title="Incident Points per Line"
      formula="|\operatorname{tr}(\ell)| = 3 \ge 2"
    >
      Every line $\ell \in \mathcal{L}$ is incident with at least two distinct points; in this model, each line contains exactly 3 points ($\lvert \operatorname{tr}(\ell) \rvert = 3 \ge 2$), satisfying <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-2">Axiom I.2</ConceptLink>.
    </PropiedadItem>

    <PropiedadItem 
      id="axioma-incidencia-3" 
      title="Existence of Non-Collinear Points"
      formula="|\operatorname{tr}(\ell)| = 3 < 7 \implies \exists \{P, Q, R\} \text{ non-collinear}"
    >
      No single line contains all points of the space within its trace ($\lvert \operatorname{tr}(\ell) \rvert = 3 < 7$); consequently, there exist triples of non-<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> points (e.g., $\{1, 2, 3\}$), satisfying <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-3">Axiom I.3</ConceptLink>.
    </PropiedadItem>
  </PropiedadesGrupo>

  <PropiedadesGrupo title="Projective, Algebraic, and Combinatorial Properties">
    <PropiedadItem 
      id="configuracion-simetrica" 
      title="Symmetric Configuration and Regularity"
      formula="(7_3, 7_3)"
    >
      The structure forms a symmetric configuration of type $(7_3, 7_3)$: it contains $\lvert \mathcal{P} \rvert = 7$ points and $\lvert \mathcal{L} \rvert = 7$ lines, where every line contains exactly 3 points and through every point pass exactly 3 lines.
    </PropiedadItem>

    <PropiedadItem 
      id="ausencia-paralelas" 
      title="Universal Projective Intersection"
      formula="\operatorname{tr}(\ell_i) \cap \operatorname{tr}(\ell_j) \neq \emptyset \quad (\forall i \neq j)"
    >
      Any two distinct lines in $\mathcal{L}$ have non-disjoint traces ($\operatorname{tr}(\ell_i) \cap \operatorname{tr}(\ell_j) \neq \emptyset$), intersecting at exactly one common point; therefore, the model contains no <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallel lines</ConceptLink>.
    </PropiedadItem>

    <PropiedadItem 
      id="autodualidad" 
      title="Self-Duality"
      formula="\mathcal{P} \cong \mathcal{L}, \quad \mathbf{I} \cong \mathbf{I}^T"
    >
      The structure is <ConceptLink targetId="dualidad">self-dual</ConceptLink>: formally interchanging the domains $\mathcal{P}$ and $\mathcal{L}$ along with the <ConceptLink targetId="transposicion">transposition</ConceptLink> of the relation $\mathbf{I}$ yields an incidence structure strictly <ConceptLink targetId="isomorfismo">isomorphic</ConceptLink> to the original one.
    </PropiedadItem>

    <PropiedadItem 
      id="construccion-algebraica" 
      title="Construction over the Finite Field F2"
      formula="\mathrm{PG}(2, \mathbb{F}_2) \cong (\mathbb{F}_2^3 \setminus \{\mathbf{0}\}) / \sim"
    >
      It is algebraically formalized as the projective plane $\mathrm{PG}(2, \mathbb{F}_2) = (\mathbb{F}_2^3 \setminus \{\mathbf{0}\}) / \sim$ over the <ConceptLink targetId="cuerpo-finito">finite field</ConceptLink> $\mathbb{F}_2$ under proportional equivalence. Points correspond to the 7 1-dimensional <ConceptLink targetId="subespacio">subspaces</ConceptLink> of the <ConceptLink targetId="espacio-vectorial">vector space</ConceptLink> $\mathbb{F}_2^3$, while lines correspond to the 7 2-dimensional subspaces.
    </PropiedadItem>
  </PropiedadesGrupo>
