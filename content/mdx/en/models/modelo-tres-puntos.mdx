<Capitular letra="T" />he **three-point model** is the simplest possible example of a finite geometry. With a universe consisting solely of three points and three lines, it practically demonstrates that the first axioms of the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-incidencia">incidence system</ConceptLink> are consistent and do not contradict one another.

<Definicion title="Model Structure">
  The model is defined via explicit finite sets:
  - **Set of <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink>**: $\mathcal{P} = \{ \text{<VisualBind element="A" color="terracota">A</VisualBind>}, \text{<VisualBind element="B" color="terracota">B</VisualBind>}, \text{<VisualBind element="C" color="terracota">C</VisualBind>} \}$.
  - **Set of <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink>**: $\mathcal{L} = \{ \text{<VisualBind element="rAB" color="carbon">r_{AB}</VisualBind>}, \text{<VisualBind element="rBC" color="carbon">r_{BC}</VisualBind>}, \text{<VisualBind element="rCA" color="carbon">r_{CA}</VisualBind>} \}$, where each line is the pair of points forming it:
    $$
    r_{AB} = \{A, B\}, \quad r_{BC} = \{B, C\}, \quad r_{CA} = \{C, A\}
    $$
  - **<ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">Incidence relation</ConceptLink>**: Standard set membership ($\in$). A point $P$ is incident with a line $\ell$ if $P \in \ell$.
</Definicion>

<Separador />

### Verification of incidence axioms

This model immediately satisfies Hilbert's first three axioms:

- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-1">Axiom I.1</ConceptLink>:** For any two distinct points (such as $A$ and $B$), there exists exactly one line ($r_{AB}$) containing both.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-2">Axiom I.2</ConceptLink>:** Each of the three lines contains at least two distinct points.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-3">Axiom I.3</ConceptLink>:** The three points $A, B, C$ do not belong to the same line (<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">non-collinear</ConceptLink>).

<Separador />

### Metamathematical significance

- **Consistency proof:** By providing a concrete model in which axioms I.1, I.2, and I.3 are simultaneously true, it is proven that these axioms are non-contradictory.
- **Absence of parallels:** In this model, any two lines always intersect at a common point ($r_{AB} \cap r_{BC} = \{B\}$); therefore, no <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallel lines</ConceptLink> exist, showing that parallelism requires additional independent axioms.
