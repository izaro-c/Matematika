<Capitular letra="T" />he three-point model is the most elementary <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-incidencia">incidence model</ConceptLink> in finite geometry. Featuring a discrete universe of three points, three lines, and a single plane, it provides an essential metamathematical tool to establish the logical consistency and mutual independence of Hilbert's initial incidence axioms.

<Separador />

### Model Definition

The system is defined via explicit finite sets:

- **Point Set**: Composed of exactly three abstract elements: <VisualBind element="A" color="terracota">$A$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="B" color="terracota">$B$</VisualBind>, and <VisualBind element="C" color="terracota">$C$</VisualBind>.
- **Line Set**: Three unordered pairs of distinct points:
  - <VisualBind element="rAB" color="carbon">$r_{AB} = \{A, B\}$</VisualBind>
  - <VisualBind element="rBC" color="carbon">$r_{BC} = \{B, C\}$</VisualBind>
  - <VisualBind element="rCA" color="carbon">$r_{CA} = \{C, A\}$</VisualBind>
- **<VisualBind element="plano" color="musgo">Plane</VisualBind>**: The total set $\mathcal{P} = \{A, B, C\}$.
- **Incidence Relation**: Standard set membership $\in$. A point $P$ is incident with a line $l$ if $P \in l$.

<Separador />

### Verification of Incidence Axioms

The model formally satisfies the first three axioms of the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-incidencia">incidence group</ConceptLink>:

- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-1">Axiom I-1</ConceptLink>**: For any two distinct points (such as $A$ and $B$), there exists a unique line ($r_{AB}$) containing both.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-2">Axiom I-2</ConceptLink>**: Every line ($r_{AB}, r_{BC}, r_{CA}$) contains at least two distinct points.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-3">Axiom I-3</ConceptLink>**: There exist at least three points ($A, B, C$) not lying on the same line (non-collinear).

<Separador />

### Metamathematical Significance

- **Consistency Proof**: By exhibiting a concrete finite interpretation where all incidence propositions evaluate to true, it establishes that the initial incidence axioms are mutually consistent.
- **Independence of Parallelism**: Any two lines in this model share a common point ($r_{AB} \cap r_{BC} = \{B\}$); hence, no <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallel lines</ConceptLink> exist, proving that parallelism requires additional independent postulates.
