<Capitular letra="T" />he **three-point model** is the simplest finite example of an incidence geometry. With a universe consisting solely of three points and three lines, it formally demonstrates that the first three axioms of <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">Hilbert's</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-incidencia">incidence system</ConceptLink> are consistent and free of contradictions.

<Definicion title="Formal Model Structure">
  The model is defined as a finite <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-geometrica">structure</ConceptLink> $(\mathcal{P}, \mathcal{L}, \mathbf{I})$:
  - **Set of <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink>**: $\mathcal{P} = \{ \text{<VisualBind element="A" color="terracota">A</VisualBind>}, \text{<VisualBind element="B" color="terracota">B</VisualBind>}, \text{<VisualBind element="C" color="terracota">C</VisualBind>} \}$ with $A \neq B \neq C$.
  - **Set of <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink>**: $\mathcal{L} = \{ \text{<VisualBind element="rAB" color="carbon">r_{AB}</VisualBind>}, \text{<VisualBind element="rBC" color="carbon">r_{BC}</VisualBind>}, \text{<VisualBind element="rCA" color="carbon">r_{CA}</VisualBind>} \}$, where each line is a two-point subset:
    $$
    r_{AB} = \{A, B\}, \quad r_{BC} = \{B, C\}, \quad r_{CA} = \{C, A\}
    $$
  - **<ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">Incidence relation</ConceptLink>**: Standard set membership ($\in$). A point $P$ is incident with a line $\ell$ if and only if $P \in \ell$.
</Definicion>

<SeccionPropiedades>
  <PropiedadesGrupo title="Axiomatic Incidence Verification">
    <PropiedadItem id="axioma-incidencia-1" title="Verification of Axiom I.1">
      For any two distinct points, there exists exactly one line containing both: $\{A, B\}$ determines $r_{AB}$, $\{B, C\}$ determines $r_{BC}$, and $\{C, A\}$ determines $r_{CA}$ (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-1">Axiom I.1</ConceptLink>).
    </PropiedadItem>
    <PropiedadItem id="axioma-incidencia-2" title="Verification of Axiom I.2">
      Each of the three lines contains exactly two distinct points ($\lvert r_{AB} \rvert = \lvert r_{BC} \rvert = \lvert r_{CA} \rvert = 2 \ge 2$), satisfying <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-2">Axiom I.2</ConceptLink>.
    </PropiedadItem>
    <PropiedadItem id="axioma-incidencia-3" title="Verification of Axiom I.3">
      No line contains all three points simultaneously ($C \notin r_{AB}$, $A \notin r_{BC}$, $B \notin r_{CA}$), ensuring the non-collinearity of the triple (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-3">Axiom I.3</ConceptLink>).
    </PropiedadItem>
  </PropiedadesGrupo>

  <PropiedadesGrupo title="Metamathematical Significance">
    <PropiedadItem id="consistencia-incidencia" title="Logical consistency proof">
      Constructing an explicit concrete model in which axioms I.1, I.2, and I.3 are simultaneously true proves that these postulates are logically consistent and non-contradictory.
    </PropiedadItem>
    <PropiedadItem id="ausencia-paralelas" title="Absence of parallel lines">
      Any two lines always intersect at exactly one common point ($r_{AB} \cap r_{BC} = \{B\}$); consequently, no <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallel lines</ConceptLink> exist, showing that Euclidean parallelism requires independent postulates.
    </PropiedadItem>
  </PropiedadesGrupo>
</SeccionPropiedades>
