<Capitular letra="T" />he **three-point model** is a finite <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-geometrica">geometric structure</ConceptLink> that satisfies the first three axioms of <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert's</ConceptLink> planar <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-incidencia">incidence system</ConceptLink>.

In <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-modelos">model theory</ConceptLink>, it constitutes a minimal <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence</ConceptLink> plane of finite <ConceptLink targetId="cardinalidad">cardinality</ConceptLink>, serving as a semantic witness to the <ConceptLink targetId="consistencia-logica">logical consistency</ConceptLink> of planar incidence postulates.

<Definicion title="Formal Structure of the Model">
The model is defined as a tuple $\mathcal{M} = (\mathcal{P}, \mathcal{L}, \mathbf{I})$ consisting of finite and <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-disjunto">disjoint sets</ConceptLink> ($\mathcal{P} \cap \mathcal{L} = \emptyset$):

- **<ConceptLink targetId="dominio">Domain</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink>**: $\mathcal{P} = \{$ <VisualBind element="A" color="terracota">$A$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="B" color="terracota">$B$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="C" color="terracota">$C$</VisualBind> $\}$ with $A \neq B \land B \neq C \land A \neq C$.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="dominio">Domain</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink>**: $\mathcal{L} = \{$ <VisualBind element="rAB" color="carbon">$\ell_{AB}$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="rBC" color="carbon">$\ell_{BC}$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="rCA" color="carbon">$\ell_{CA}$</VisualBind> $\}$.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">Incidence</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="relacion">relation</ConceptLink>** $\mathbf{I} \subseteq \mathcal{P} \times \mathcal{L}$:
  $$
  \mathbf{I} = \{ (A, \ell_{AB}), (B, \ell_{AB}), (B, \ell_{BC}), (C, \ell_{BC}), (C, \ell_{CA}), (A, \ell_{CA}) \}
  $$
  or equivalently, via the <ConceptLink targetId="traza">point traces</ConceptLink> of each line in the point <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto">set</ConceptLink>: $\operatorname{tr}(\ell_{AB}) = \{A, B\}$, $\operatorname{tr}(\ell_{BC}) = \{B, C\}$, and $\operatorname{tr}(\ell_{CA}) = \{C, A\}$.
</Definicion>

  <PropiedadesGrupo title="Incidence Axiom Satisfaction">
    <PropiedadItem

      title="Unique Line Determination"
      formula="\forall P, Q \in \mathcal{P} \ (P \neq Q \implies \exists! \ell \in \mathcal{L})"
    >
      For any two distinct points in $\mathcal{P}$, there exists a unique line in $\mathcal{L}$ incident with both ($\{A, B\}$ determines $\ell_{AB}$, $\{B, C\}$ determines $\ell_{BC}$, and $\{C, A\}$ determines $\ell_{CA}$), satisfying <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-1">Axiom I.1</ConceptLink>.
    </PropiedadItem>

    <PropiedadItem 
      id="axioma-incidencia-2" 
      title="Incident Points per Line"
      formula="|\operatorname{tr}(\ell)| = 2 \ge 2"
    >
      Every line $\ell \in \mathcal{L}$ is incident with exactly two distinct points ($\lvert \operatorname{tr}(\ell) \rvert = 2 \ge 2$), satisfying <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-2">Axiom I.2</ConceptLink>.
    </PropiedadItem>

    <PropiedadItem 
      id="axioma-incidencia-3" 
      title="Existence of Non-Collinear Points"
      formula="|\operatorname{tr}(\ell)| = 2 < 3 \implies \exists \{A, B, C\} \text{ non-collinear}"
    >
      No line contains all three points simultaneously in its trace ($C \notin \operatorname{tr}(\ell_{AB})$, $A \notin \operatorname{tr}(\ell_{BC})$, $B \notin \operatorname{tr}(\ell_{CA})$); consequently, there exist triples of non-<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> points, satisfying <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-3">Axiom I.3</ConceptLink>.
    </PropiedadItem>
  </PropiedadesGrupo>

  <PropiedadesGrupo title="Structural and Combinatorial Properties">
    <PropiedadItem

      The structure is homogeneous and regular, forming a configuration of type $(3_2, 3_2)$: it has cardinality $\lvert \mathcal{P} \rvert = 3$ and $\lvert \mathcal{L} \rvert = 3$, where every line contains exactly 2 points and through every point pass exactly 2 lines.
    </PropiedadItem>

    <PropiedadItem

      Any two distinct lines in $\mathcal{L}$ have non-disjoint traces ($\operatorname{tr}(\ell_i) \cap \operatorname{tr}(\ell_j) \neq \emptyset$), always intersecting at a unique common point; therefore, the model contains no <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallel lines</ConceptLink>.
    </PropiedadItem>

    </PropiedadItem>
