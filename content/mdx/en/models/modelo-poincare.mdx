<Capitular letra="T" />he Poincaré disk model $\mathbb{D}^2$ provides a conformal analytic representation of two-dimensional <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-hiperbolico">hyperbolic geometry</ConceptLink>. By embedding the hyperbolic plane within the open unit disk of the complex plane, it enables a rigorous visualization of the negation of Euclid's parallel postulate while faithfully preserving local Euclidean angles.

<Separador />

### Model Definition

The geometric domain is restricted to the open unit disk $\mathbb{D}^2 = \{(x, y) \in \mathbb{R}^2 \mid x^2 + y^2 < 1\}$:

- **<ConceptLink targetId="punto">Points</ConceptLink>**: Points belonging to the interior of the open disk. The <VisualBind element="frontera" color="carbon">boundary circle $\partial \mathbb{D}^2$</VisualBind> represents the absolute horizon (ideal points at infinity).
- **<ConceptLink targetId="recta">Hyperbolic Lines (Geodesics)</ConceptLink>**: Intersections with $\mathbb{D}^2$ formed by:
  - Euclidean diameters passing through the center.
  - Euclidean circular arcs that intersect the <VisualBind element="frontera" color="carbon">boundary circle</VisualBind> strictly <ConceptLink targetId="perpendicular">orthogonally</ConceptLink>, such as the <VisualBind element="geodesica-principal" color="terracota">principal line $l$</VisualBind>.
- **Conformal Hyperbolic Metric**: The Riemannian line element is defined by the Poincaré tensor:
  $$ ds = \frac{2\sqrt{dx^2 + dy^2}}{1 - (x^2 + y^2)} $$
  As a point approaches the boundary circle, its intrinsic metric distance diverges to $+\infty$.

<Separador />

### Verification of Hyperbolic Parallelism

The Poincaré disk satisfies the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-hiperbolico">hyperbolic parallel axiom</ConceptLink>:

- Given a <VisualBind element="geodesica-principal" color="terracota">hyperbolic line $l$</VisualBind> and an <VisualBind element="P" color="terracota">external point $P \notin l$</VisualBind>, there exist infinitely many coplanar hyperbolic lines passing through $P$ that do not intersect $l$.
- **Limiting (Asymptotic) Parallels**: There exist exactly two bounding geodesics, such as the <VisualBind element="limite1" color="ocre">limiting parallel</VisualBind>, which share an ideal boundary point at infinity with $l$.
- **Ultraparallels (Divergent Lines)**: Any geodesic strictly between the two limiting rays, such as the <VisualBind element="ultra1" color="canela">ultraparallel line</VisualBind>, never intersects $l$ in the disk or at infinity, and admits a unique common perpendicular.

<Separador />

### Geometric Properties

- **Conformality**: The hyperbolic angle between two intersecting geodesics matches the standard Euclidean angle between their tangent vectors.
- **Angular Defect and Area**: In any <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">hyperbolic triangle</ConceptLink> with interior angles $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$, the angle sum satisfies $\alpha + \beta + \gamma < \pi$, with its area directly proportional to the angular defect:
  $$ \text{Area}(\triangle) = \pi - (\alpha + \beta + \gamma) $$
