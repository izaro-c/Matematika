<Capitular letra="T" />he **Cartesian model** interprets the primitive terms of geometry by means of real numbers: the plane $\mathbb{R}^2$, with lines given by linear equations and congruence defined by the Euclidean <ConceptLink targetId="distancia">distance</ConceptLink>, satisfies the planar axioms of the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-euclidiano">Euclidean system</ConceptLink> (I.1–I.3, II, III, IV and V), and the space $\mathbb{R}^3$, with its planes, satisfies the full system.

The <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-analitica">analytic geometry</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="descartes">René Descartes</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="fermat">Pierre de Fermat</ConceptLink> translated geometric problems into equations; <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">Hilbert</ConceptLink> reversed the viewpoint and used this model, in Chapter II of the *Grundlagen*, as a witness of the <ConceptLink targetId="consistencia-logica">consistency</ConceptLink> of his axioms: if the arithmetic of $\mathbb{R}$ is consistent, so is Euclidean geometry.

<Definicion title="Structure of the planar model">
  The model is the <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-geometrica">structure</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{M} = (\mathcal{P}, \mathcal{L}; \ \mathbf{I}, \ *, \ \cong_{\mathrm{seg}}, \ \cong_{\angle})$ defined as follows:

  - **<ConceptLink targetId="punto">Points</ConceptLink>**: $\mathcal{P} = \mathbb{R}^2$, ordered pairs of real numbers, such as <VisualBind element="A" color="terracota">$A = (x_A, y_A)$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="B" color="terracota">$B = (x_B, y_B)$</VisualBind> or <VisualBind element="P" color="ocre">$P = (x_P, y_P)$</VisualBind>.
  - **<ConceptLink targetId="recta">Lines</ConceptLink>**: each triple $(a, b, c) \in \mathbb{R}^3$ with $(a, b) \neq (0, 0)$ determines a line $\ell_{a,b,c}$; two proportional triples determine the same line. In the figure, <VisualBind element="lineAB" color="musgo">$\ell$</VisualBind> is the line through $A$ and $B$, and <VisualBind element="parallelP" color="canela">$\ell'$</VisualBind> is the parallel to $\ell$ through $P$.
  - **<ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">Incidence</ConceptLink>**: $P \,\mathbf{I}\, \ell_{a,b,c} \iff a x_P + b y_P + c = 0$, so that $\operatorname{tr}(\ell_{a,b,c}) = \{(x, y) \in \mathbb{R}^2 \mid ax + by + c = 0\}$ is its <ConceptLink targetId="traza">trace</ConceptLink>.
  - **<ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">Betweenness</ConceptLink>**: $A * B * C$ if and only if $A$, $B$ and $C$ are three distinct points and $B = (1 - t)A + tC$ for some $t \in \mathbb{R}$ with $0 < t < 1$.
  - **Segment <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink>**: $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{CD}$ if and only if $d(A, B) = d(C, D)$, where
    $$
    d(A, B) = \sqrt{(x_B - x_A)^2 + (y_B - y_A)^2}
    $$
  - **Angle congruence**: $\angle AOB \cong \angle A'O'B'$ if and only if the cosines of both angles, computed with the <ConceptLink targetId="producto-escalar">dot product</ConceptLink> $\cos \angle AOB = \dfrac{\langle A - O, \, B - O \rangle}{d(O, A) \, d(O, B)}$, coincide.

  The spatial model is obtained in the same way with $\mathcal{P} = \mathbb{R}^3$: the <ConceptLink targetId="plano">planes</ConceptLink> are the solution sets of equations $ax + by + cz + e = 0$ with $(a, b, c) \neq (0, 0, 0)$, and the lines are the intersections of two non-parallel planes.
</Definicion>

<SeccionPropiedades>
  <PropiedadesGrupo title="Satisfaction of the axioms of the Euclidean system">
    <PropiedadItem
      id="cartesiano-incidencia"
      title="Planar incidence axioms"
      formula="(y_B - y_A)(x - x_A) - (x_B - x_A)(y - y_A) = 0"
    >
      Given two distinct points $A$ and $B$, the equation above defines the unique line through both (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-1">Axiom I.1</ConceptLink>); every line contains at least two points (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-2">Axiom I.2</ConceptLink>), and $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ are not <ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-3">Axiom I.3</ConceptLink>).
    </PropiedadItem>

    <PropiedadItem
      id="cartesiano-orden"
      title="Order axioms"
      formula="f(x, y) = ax + by + c"
    >
      The parametrisation $t \mapsto (1 - t)A + tC$ orders the points of each line like the real numbers, which yields axioms <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-1">II.1</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-2">II.2</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-3">II.3</ConceptLink>. Each line $\ell_{a,b,c}$ separates the plane into the two <ConceptLink targetId="semiplano">half-planes</ConceptLink> $f > 0$ and $f < 0$; since $f$ is affine, a <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink> meets $\ell$ exactly when $f$ changes sign between its endpoints, and <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-4">Pasch's axiom</ConceptLink> follows.
    </PropiedadItem>

    <PropiedadItem
      id="cartesiano-congruencia"
      title="Congruence axioms"
      formula="d(A, B)^2 = d(A, C)^2 + d(C, B)^2 - 2\, d(A, C)\, d(C, B) \cos \angle ACB"
    >
      Translations and rotations preserve $d$ and the cosine of angles, which allows segments and angles to be transported (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-1">Axiom III.1</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-4">Axiom III.4</ConceptLink>); equality of real numbers gives transitivity (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-2">Axiom III.2</ConceptLink>) and additivity of $d$ on collinear points gives <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-3">Axiom III.3</ConceptLink>. The <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-coseno">law of cosines</ConceptLink> above determines the third side and the remaining angles from two sides and the included angle (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-5">Axiom III.5</ConceptLink>).
    </PropiedadItem>

    <PropiedadItem
      id="cartesiano-paralelas"
      title="Parallel axiom"
      formula="\ell' : \ ax + by - (a x_P + b y_P) = 0"
    >
      Given $\ell_{a,b,c}$ and a point $P$ not incident with it, the lines through $P$ that do not meet $\ell$ are those with the same coefficients $(a, b)$; there is only one, the line $\ell'$ above, so <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">Axiom IV</ConceptLink> holds.
    </PropiedadItem>

    <PropiedadItem
      id="cartesiano-continuidad"
      title="Continuity axioms"
      formula="t \longmapsto A + t\,(B - A)"
    >
      The map above is an order-preserving <ConceptLink targetId="biyeccion">bijection</ConceptLink> between $\mathbb{R}$ and the trace of the line $AB$. Hence the Archimedean property of $\mathbb{R}$ gives <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-arquimedes">Axiom V.1</ConceptLink> and the <ConceptLink targetId="completitud-reales">completeness of $\mathbb{R}$</ConceptLink> gives <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-completitud">Axiom V.2</ConceptLink>.
    </PropiedadItem>
  </PropiedadesGrupo>

  <PropiedadesGrupo title="Structural and algebraic properties">
    <PropiedadItem
      id="cartesiano-modelo-espacial"
      title="Spatial model"
      formula="\mathbb{R}^3 \models \mathcal{T}_{\text{euc}}"
    >
      The plane $\mathbb{R}^2$ has no planes and therefore cannot satisfy the spatial axioms <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-4">I.4</ConceptLink>–<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-8">I.8</ConceptLink>. The space $\mathbb{R}^3$ does satisfy them and is a model of the whole <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-euclidiano">Euclidean system</ConceptLink> and, in particular, of the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute system</ConceptLink>.
    </PropiedadItem>

    <PropiedadItem
      id="cartesiano-isometrias"
      title="Isometry group"
      formula="\operatorname{E}(2) = \mathbb{R}^2 \rtimes \operatorname{O}(2)"
    >
      The <ConceptLink targetId="isometria">isometries</ConceptLink> of the plane (translations, rotations, reflections and their compositions) form the Euclidean <ConceptLink targetId="grupo">group</ConceptLink> $\operatorname{E}(2)$. Two figures of the model are congruent if and only if some isometry maps one onto the other.
    </PropiedadItem>

    <PropiedadItem
      id="cartesiano-categoricidad"
      title="Uniqueness up to isomorphism"
      formula="\mathcal{M} \models \mathcal{T} \implies \mathcal{M} \cong \mathbb{R}^3"
    >
      Every model of the full Euclidean system, with the continuity axioms, is <ConceptLink targetId="isomorfismo">isomorphic</ConceptLink> to $\mathbb{R}^3$ with the structure described (<ConceptLink targetId="categoricidad">categoricity</ConceptLink>); likewise, every model of the planar axioms is isomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^2$.
    </PropiedadItem>
  </PropiedadesGrupo>
