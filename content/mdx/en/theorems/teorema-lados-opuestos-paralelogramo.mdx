<Capitular letra="I" />n any <ConceptLink targetId="paralelogramo" isDependency={true}>parallelogram</ConceptLink>, opposite <ConceptLink targetId="lado">sides</ConceptLink> are mutually <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruent</ConceptLink> and opposite <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> are mutually congruent.

This result constitutes Proposition I.34 of <ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid's</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="elementos-euclides">Elements</ConceptLink> and is revisited by <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink> in §7 of the *Grundlagen der Geometrie*. Its deduction substantially requires the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides" isDependency={true}>parallel axiom</ConceptLink> via the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-angulos-alternos-internos" isDependency={true}>alternate interior angles theorem</ConceptLink>, and is completed by applying the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-ala" isDependency={true}>ASA congruence criterion</ConceptLink> to the two <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangles</ConceptLink> determined by a <ConceptLink targetId="diagonal">diagonal</ConceptLink>. In the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-hiperbolico">hyperbolic system</ConceptLink>, since the Euclidean postulate does not hold, the relationship between equidistance, parallelism, and quadrilateral congruence exhibits essentially different properties.

<Definicion title="Formal statement of the opposite sides of a parallelogram theorem">
Let $A, B, C, D \in \mathcal{P}$ be four coplanar <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> in a <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> $\sigma \in \Pi$ that determine a convex <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrilatero">quadrilateral</ConceptLink> $ABCD$.

If $ABCD$ is a <ConceptLink targetId="paralelogramo">parallelogram</ConceptLink>, that is, the supporting <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink> of its pairs of opposite sides are <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallel</ConceptLink>:

$$
\overleftrightarrow{AB} \parallel \overleftrightarrow{CD} \quad \land \quad \overleftrightarrow{BC} \parallel \overleftrightarrow{DA}
$$

then the corresponding opposite sides are mutually congruent:

$$
\overline{AB} \cong \overline{CD} \quad \land \quad \overline{BC} \cong \overline{DA}
$$

and the interior opposite angles are mutually congruent:

$$
\angle ABC \cong \angle CDA \quad \land \quad \angle BCD \cong \angle DAB
$$
</Definicion>
