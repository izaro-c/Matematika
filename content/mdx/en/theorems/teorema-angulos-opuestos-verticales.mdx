<Capitular letra="T" />he vertically opposite angles theorem constitutes one of the most fundamental angular symmetry properties in planar <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-euclidiana">Euclidean geometry</ConceptLink>. It guarantees that the intersection of two <ConceptLink targetId="recta">secant lines</ConceptLink> generates pairs of <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> with equal geometric measure.

<Separador />

### Formal statement

Let $r$ and $s$ be two <ConceptLink targetId="recta">secant lines</ConceptLink> in plane $\mathcal{P}$ intersecting at <VisualBind element="punto-interseccion" color="musgo">point $O$</VisualBind>. Let $\alpha$ and $\alpha'$ be a pair of <VisualBind element="angulos-opuestos" color="pavo">vertically opposite angles</VisualBind> determined by the intersection of the rays. Global <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink> holds necessarily:

<Formula>
  $$ \alpha \cong \alpha' $$
</Formula>

Metrically, the angular measures of both angles are strictly identical: $|\alpha| = |\alpha'|$.

<Separador />

### Proof via supplementary angles

The formal deduction relies on the axiom of <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-suplementarios">supplementary angles</ConceptLink> along a straight line:
1. Let $\beta$ be the common adjacent angle to $\alpha$ and $\alpha'$.
2. Lying on line $r$, $\alpha$ and $\beta$ form a <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-llano">straight angle</ConceptLink>, so $\alpha + \beta = 180^\circ$.
3. Likewise, along line $s$, $\beta$ and $\alpha'$ form a straight angle, so $\alpha' + \beta = 180^\circ$.
4. Subtracting $\beta$ from both equations yields $\alpha = 180^\circ - \beta = \alpha'$, proving congruence.

<Separador />

### Properties and significance

- **Independence from the parallel postulate**: The congruence of vertically opposite angles is a result of absolute geometry and does not depend on the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">parallel postulate</ConceptLink>.
- **Rotational invariance**: Corresponds to a $180^\circ$ point reflection symmetry about the intersection point $O$.
