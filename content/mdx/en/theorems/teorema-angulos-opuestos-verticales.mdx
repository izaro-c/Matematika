<Capitular letra="I" />f two <ConceptLink targetId="secante">intersecting</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>lines</ConceptLink> meet at a <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>point</ConceptLink>, the <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-opuestos-vertice" isDependency={true}>vertical angles</ConceptLink> formed by their <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta" isDependency={true}>rays</ConceptLink> are <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink>.

<ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid</ConceptLink> proves it in Proposition I.15 of the <ConceptLink targetId="elementos-euclides">Elements</ConceptLink>, and tradition attributes it to <ConceptLink targetId="tales">Thales of Miletus</ConceptLink>. It is a <ConceptLink targetId="teorema">theorem</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>, independent of the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">parallel axiom</ConceptLink>. It follows from the definition of <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-suplementarios" isDependency={true}>supplementary angles</ConceptLink>, the <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-reflexiva">reflexivity</ConceptLink> of angle congruence (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-4" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.4</ConceptLink>) and the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-suplementos" isDependency={true}>supplement congruence theorem</ConceptLink>, which is proved from axioms <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-1">III.1</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-3">III.3</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-5">III.5</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal statement of the vertical angles theorem">
Let <VisualBind element="lineL" color="carbon">$\ell$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="lineM" color="carbon">$m$</VisualBind> be two distinct <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>lines</ConceptLink> of a <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink> $\pi$ meeting at the <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>point</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="O" color="carbon">$O$</VisualBind>.

The <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="A" color="carbon">$A$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="Ap" color="carbon">$A'$</VisualBind> of $\ell$ satisfy the <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre" isDependency={true}>betweenness</ConceptLink> relation $A * O * A'$, so that $\overrightarrow{OA}$ and $\overrightarrow{OA'}$ are <ConceptLink targetId="semirrectas-opuestas">opposite rays</ConceptLink>. Likewise, the points <VisualBind element="B" color="carbon">$B$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="Bp" color="carbon">$B'$</VisualBind> of $m$ satisfy $B * O * B'$, so that $\overrightarrow{OB}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB'}$ are opposite rays.

If <VisualBind element="angle1" color="pavo">$\alpha = \angle AOB$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="angle3" color="pavo">$\alpha' = \angle A'OB'$</VisualBind> are the <ConceptLink targetId="angulo" isDependency={true}>angles</ConceptLink> opposite at the <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertex</ConceptLink> $O$, then they are <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink>:

$$
\alpha \cong \alpha'
$$

Likewise, for the second pair of opposite angles, <VisualBind element="angle2" color="musgo">$\beta = \angle BOA'$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="angle4" color="musgo">$\beta' = \angle B'OA$</VisualBind>:

$$
\beta \cong \beta'
$$
</Definicion>
