<Capitular letra="T" />he vertical angles theorem is one of the most fundamental and rigorous angular symmetry theorems in planar <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-euclidiana">Euclidean geometry</ConceptLink>. It guarantees that the intersection of two <ConceptLink targetId="recta">intersecting lines</ConceptLink> generates pairs of <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> of equal geometric measure.

<Separador />

### Formal statement

Let $r$ and $s$ be two <ConceptLink targetId="recta">intersecting lines</ConceptLink> in the plane $\mathcal{P}$ intersecting at the <VisualBind element="punto-interseccion" color="musgo">point $O$</VisualBind>. Let $\alpha$ and $\alpha'$ be a pair of <VisualBind element="angulos-opuestos" color="pavo">vertically opposite angles</VisualBind> determined by the intersecting rays. The <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink> relation holds necessarily:

<Formula>
  $$ \alpha \cong \alpha' $$
</Formula>

Metrically, their angular measures are identical: $|\alpha| = |\alpha'|$.

<Separador />

### Proof and axiomatic foundations

The formal deduction is based on adjacent <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-suplementarios">supplementary angles</ConceptLink> along a straight line and the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-4" isDependency={true}>angle congruence axioms</ConceptLink>:
1. Let $\beta$ be the common adjacent angle between $\alpha$ and $\alpha'$.
2. Lying on the same straight line $r$, $\alpha$ and $\beta$ form a <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-suplementarios">straight angle</ConceptLink>, so $\alpha + \beta = 180^\circ$.
3. Similarly, on line $s$, $\beta$ and $\alpha'$ form a straight angle, so $\alpha' + \beta = 180^\circ$.
4. Subtracting $\beta$ from both equations yields $\alpha = 180^\circ - \beta = \alpha'$, proving the required congruence $\alpha \cong \alpha'$.

<Separador />

### Properties and significance

- **Independence from the parallel postulate**: The congruence of vertical angles is a theorem of <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink> and does not require the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">parallel postulate</ConceptLink>.
- **Rotational invariance**: Corresponds to a central half-turn symmetry of $180^\circ$ ($\pi$ radians) about the intersection point $O$.
