This result, originally attributed to <ConceptLink targetId="tales">Thales of Miletus</ConceptLink> and formalized by <ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid</ConceptLink> in Proposition I.15 of the <ConceptLink targetId="elementos-euclides">Elements</ConceptLink>, constitutes a fundamental <ConceptLink targetId="teorema">theorem</ConceptLink> of angular <ConceptLink targetId="simetria">symmetry</ConceptLink> and rigidity in <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>. Its validity follows directly from the congruence axioms (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-4" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.4</ConceptLink>) and the definition of <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-suplementarios" isDependency={true}>supplementary angles</ConceptLink>, with complete independence from the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">parallel postulate</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal statement of the vertical angles theorem">
Let <VisualBind element="lineL" color="carbon">$l$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="lineM" color="carbon">$m$</VisualBind> be two coplanar <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>lines</ConceptLink> in a <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink> $\pi$ intersecting at a unique <VisualBind element="O" color="carbon"><ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>point</ConceptLink> $O$</VisualBind>.

The <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="A" color="carbon">$A$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="Ap" color="carbon">$A'$</VisualBind> on line $l$ satisfy the <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre" isDependency={true}>betweenness relation</ConceptLink> $A * O * A'$, determining the opposite <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta" isDependency={true}>rays</ConceptLink> $\overrightarrow{OA}$ and $\overrightarrow{OA'}$. Similarly, the points <VisualBind element="B" color="carbon">$B$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="Bp" color="carbon">$B'$</VisualBind> on line $m$ satisfy $B * O * B'$, determining the opposite rays $\overrightarrow{OB}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB'}$.

If <VisualBind element="angle1" color="pavo">$\alpha = \angle AOB = \angle(\overrightarrow{OA}, \overrightarrow{OB})$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="angle3" color="pavo">$\alpha' = \angle A'OB' = \angle(\overrightarrow{OA'}, \overrightarrow{OB'})$</VisualBind> are the vertical <ConceptLink targetId="angulo" isDependency={true}>angles</ConceptLink> formed across the <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertex</ConceptLink>, then they are mutually <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink>:

$$
\alpha \cong \alpha'
$$

Similarly, for the second pair of vertical angles formed by <VisualBind element="angle2" color="musgo">$\beta = \angle BOA' = \angle(\overrightarrow{OB}, \overrightarrow{OA'})$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="angle4" color="musgo">$\beta' = \angle B'OA = \angle(\overrightarrow{OB'}, \overrightarrow{OA})$</VisualBind>, we have:

$$
\beta \cong \beta'
$$
</Definicion>
