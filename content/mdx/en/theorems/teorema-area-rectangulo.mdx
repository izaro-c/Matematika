<Capitular letra="T" />he <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-medida-area" isDependency={true}>area</ConceptLink> of the region of a <ConceptLink targetId="rectangulo" isDependency={true}>rectangle</ConceptLink> is the product of the <ConceptLink targetId="longitud" isDependency={true}>lengths</ConceptLink> of two adjacent <ConceptLink targetId="lado">sides</ConceptLink>. It is a theorem of the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-euclidiano">Euclidean system</ConceptLink>: the existence of rectangles depends on <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides" isDependency={true}>Axiom IV</ConceptLink>, and in the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-hiperbolico">hyperbolic system</ConceptLink> no quadrilateral has four right angles.

The formula is not a definition: the <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-medida-area" isDependency={true}>area function</ConceptLink> only fixes the value $1$ for the unit <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrado">square</ConceptLink>, and the product $b \cdot h$ is derived from invariance, <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-aditividad" isDependency={true}>finite additivity</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="monotonia-area">monotonicity</ConceptLink>. The <ConceptLink targetId="demo-area-rectangulo">proof</ConceptLink> proceeds in three stages: integer sides (the rectangle is divided into $m \cdot n$ unit squares), rational sides (it is divided into squares of side $1/N$) and real sides, where the rational approximations from below and from above provided by the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-arquimedes">Archimedean axiom</ConceptLink> trap the area between ever closer bounds. This result is the starting point of the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-triangulo">area of the triangle</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal statement of the rectangle area theorem">
Let $ABCD$ be a <ConceptLink targetId="rectangulo" isDependency={true}>rectangle</ConceptLink> in a <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink> $\sigma$ of the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-euclidiano">Euclidean system</ConceptLink>, that is, a <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrilatero" isDependency={true}>quadrilateral</ConceptLink> whose four <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> are <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto" isDependency={true}>right angles</ConceptLink>, and let $b = |AB|$ and $h = |AD|$ be the <ConceptLink targetId="longitud" isDependency={true}>lengths</ConceptLink> of two adjacent sides, measured with the <ConceptLink targetId="segmento-unidad">unit segment</ConceptLink> that normalises the area function. Then the area of the <ConceptLink targetId="region-poligonal" isDependency={true}>polygonal region</ConceptLink> bounded by $ABCD$ is

$$
\operatorname{A}(ABCD) = b \cdot h
$$
</Definicion>
