<Capitular letra="T" />he area of a rectangle theorem provides the foundational multiplicative formula for planar <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-medida-area">area measurement</ConceptLink> in Euclidean geometry. It serves as the baseline benchmark from which area formulas for all other polygonal figures are derived.

<Separador />

### Formal statement

Let $R$ be a rectangle in the Euclidean plane $\mathcal{P}$ whose adjacent <ConceptLink targetId="perpendicular">perpendicular sides</ConceptLink> measure $b = |\overline{AB}|$ (base length) and $h = |\overline{AD}|$ (height length). The area measure of the rectangular region $R$ strictly satisfies:

<Formula>
  $$ \text{Area}(R) = b \cdot h $$
</Formula>

That is, the area of a rectangular region equals the arithmetic product of the linear measures of its two orthogonal dimensions.

<Separador />

### Proof and axiomatic foundations

The rigorous axiomatic foundation is developed in three stages using the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-aditividad" isDependency={true}>area additivity theorem</ConceptLink>:
1. **Unit standard**: The area of the <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrado">unit square</ConceptLink> ($b=1, h=1$) is axiomatically set to $1$.
2. **Rational dimensions**: If $b = \frac{p}{q}$ and $h = \frac{m}{n}$ with $p, q, m, n \in \mathbb{N}$, the rectangle is partitioned into $p \cdot m$ sub-squares of side length $\frac{1}{q \cdot n}$. The additive sum of their areas proves that $\text{Area}(R) = \frac{p \cdot m}{q \cdot n} = b \cdot h$.
3. **Real dimensions**: Via continuity and the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-arquimedes">Archimedean axiom</ConceptLink>, the result extends to irrational side lengths through standard limiting approximations.

<Separador />

### Geometric significance

- **Base of the parallelogram formula**: Any <ConceptLink targetId="paralelogramo">parallelogram</ConceptLink> is equidecomposable with a rectangle of equal base and height.
- **Base of the triangle formula**: A rectangle is split along its diagonal into two congruent <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangles</ConceptLink>, establishing that the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-triangulo">area of a triangle</ConceptLink> is $\frac{1}{2} b \cdot h$.
