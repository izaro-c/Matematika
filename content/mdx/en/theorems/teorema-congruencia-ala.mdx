<Capitular letra="T" />he Angle-Side-Angle (ASA) congruence theorem establishes one of the principal criteria for determining the metric identity and rigidity of two <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangles</ConceptLink> in Euclidean <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane geometry</ConceptLink>. It guarantees that fixing a line <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink> and the inclinations of two rays originating from its endpoints uniquely determines the geometry of the triangle.

<Separador />

### Formal statement

Let $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle A'B'C'$ be two <VisualBind element="triangulos" color="musgo"><ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangles</ConceptLink></VisualBind> in the Euclidean plane. Suppose the following <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink> relations hold:
1. The included side is congruent: $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{A'B'}$.
2. The adjacent interior angles are respectively congruent: $\angle CAB \cong \angle C'A'B'$ and $\angle ABC \cong \angle A'B'C'$.

Then, global metric <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink> between both triangles holds necessarily:

<Formula>
  $$ \triangle ABC \cong \triangle A'B'C' $$
</Formula>

Consequently, all corresponding sides and third angles are respectively congruent: $\overline{BC} \cong \overline{B'C'}$, $\overline{CA} \cong \overline{C'A'}$, and $\angle BCA \cong \angle B'C'A'$.

<Separador />

### Proof and axiomatic foundations

In <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert's</ConceptLink> foundations, the SAS criterion is taken as a basic <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-5" isDependency={true}>congruence axiom</ConceptLink>, while ASA is derived strictly by proof by contradiction:
- If $\overline{AC}$ were not congruent to $\overline{A'C'}$, there would exist a point $D$ on the ray such that $\overline{AD} \cong \overline{A'C'}$.
- By SAS, $\triangle ABD \cong \triangle A'B'C'$, which would imply $\angle ABD \cong \angle A'B'C'$.
- This contradicts the angle transport axiom by producing two distinct angles congruent to the same reference angle.

<Separador />

### Applications and extensions

- **Surveying and triangulation**: Serves as the theoretical backbone for determining unmeasurable distances by establishing a baseline segment and measuring two direction angles.
- **AAS Criterion**: Combined with the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-suma-angulos-triangulo">triangle angle sum theorem</ConceptLink>, the criterion extends to any pair of angles and a non-included side.
