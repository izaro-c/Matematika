<Capitular letra="I" />f two <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangles</ConceptLink> have one <ConceptLink targetId="lado" isDependency={true}>side</ConceptLink> and the two <ConceptLink targetId="angulo" isDependency={true}>angles</ConceptLink> adjacent to that side respectively <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink>, then the triangles are congruent.

<ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid</ConceptLink> states it as Proposition I.26 of the <ConceptLink targetId="elementos-euclides">Elements</ConceptLink>, and <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">Hilbert</ConceptLink> proves it in §6 of the *Grundlagen der Geometrie* as the second <ConceptLink targetId="criterio-congruencia">congruence criterion</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>. A <ConceptLink targetId="segmento" isDependency={true}>segment</ConceptLink> and the two angles resting on its endpoints determine the triangle up to congruence. The proof uses the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-incidencia">axioms of incidence</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre" isDependency={true}>order</ConceptLink>, segment transport (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-1" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.1</ConceptLink>), the uniqueness of angle transport (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-4" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.4</ConceptLink>) and the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-lal" isDependency={true}>SAS congruence theorem</ConceptLink>, a consequence of <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-5" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.5</ConceptLink>; it does not depend on the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">parallel axiom</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal statement of the ASA congruence theorem">
Let <VisualBind element="A1" color="canela">$A$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="B1" color="canela">$B$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="C1" color="canela">$C$</VisualBind> $\in \mathcal{P}$ and <VisualBind element="A2" color="pavo">$A'$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="B2" color="pavo">$B'$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="C2" color="pavo">$C'$</VisualBind> $\in \mathcal{P}$ be two triples of non-<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>points</ConceptLink>, determining the <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangles</ConceptLink> $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle A'B'C'$.

Suppose the following <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruence</ConceptLink> relations hold:
1. The included <ConceptLink targetId="lado" isDependency={true}>side</ConceptLink> is congruent:
   <VisualBind element="lado-ab" color="terracota">$\overline{AB} \cong \overline{A'B'}$</VisualBind>
2. The <ConceptLink targetId="angulo" isDependency={true}>angles</ConceptLink> adjacent to that side are respectively congruent:
   <VisualBind element="angulo-a" color="terracota">$\angle CAB \cong \angle C'A'B'$</VisualBind> $\quad\text{and}\quad$ <VisualBind element="angulo-b" color="musgo">$\angle ABC \cong \angle A'B'C'$</VisualBind>

Then the triangles are congruent (<ConceptLink targetId="congruencia-triangulos">triangle congruence</ConceptLink>) under the correspondence $A \mapsto A'$, $B \mapsto B'$, $C \mapsto C'$:

$$
\triangle ABC \cong \triangle A'B'C'
$$

In particular, the remaining corresponding sides and angles are congruent: <VisualBind element="globalmente-congruentes" color="terracota">$\overline{BC} \cong \overline{B'C'}$</VisualBind>, $\overline{CA} \cong \overline{C'A'}$ and $\angle BCA \cong \angle B'C'A'$.
</Definicion>
