<Capitular letra="E" />very <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink> contained in a <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink> decomposes the <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>points</ConceptLink> of the plane not incident with it into exactly two disjoint and convex regions.

This result, established by <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink> in §4 of the *Grundlagen der Geometrie*, deductively formalizes the geometric notion of a <ConceptLink targetId="semiplano">half-plane</ConceptLink>. Its proof does not appeal to metric concepts nor to continuity, but rather rests on the axioms of <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia" isDependency={true}>incidence</ConceptLink> and of <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre" isDependency={true}>order</ConceptLink>, relying on the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-4" isDependency={true}>Axiom of Pasch</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal statement of the plane separation theorem">
Let $\pi \in \Pi$ be a <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink> and let $\ell \in \mathcal{L}$ be a <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink> such that $\operatorname{tr}(\ell) \subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\pi)$. On the set $\operatorname{tr}(\pi) \setminus \operatorname{tr}(\ell)$ of <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>points</ConceptLink> of the plane not incident with $\ell$, the <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-binaria" isDependency={true}>binary relation</ConceptLink> $\sim_\ell$ is defined by:
$$
A \sim_\ell B \iff A = B \lor \operatorname{seg}(AB) \cap \operatorname{tr}(\ell) = \emptyset
$$
where $\operatorname{seg}(AB)$ denotes the open <ConceptLink targetId="segmento" isDependency={true}>segment</ConceptLink> with endpoints $A$ and $B$.

Under these conditions:
1. The relation $\sim_\ell$ is an <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-equivalencia" isDependency={true}>equivalence relation</ConceptLink> on $\operatorname{tr}(\pi) \setminus \operatorname{tr}(\ell)$.
2. The quotient set consists of exactly two <ConceptLink targetId="clase-equivalencia" isDependency={true}>equivalence classes</ConceptLink>:
$$
(\operatorname{tr}(\pi) \setminus \operatorname{tr}(\ell)) / {\sim_\ell} = \{ \mathcal{H}_1, \mathcal{H}_2 \}
$$
3. Two points $A$ and $B$ belong to distinct classes if and only if the open <ConceptLink targetId="segmento" isDependency={true}>segment</ConceptLink> joining them intersects the line $\ell$:
$$
A \in \mathcal{H}_1 \land B \in \mathcal{H}_2 \iff \operatorname{seg}(AB) \cap \operatorname{tr}(\ell) \neq \emptyset
$$
4. Each class $\mathcal{H}_i$ is a <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-convexo" isDependency={true}>convex set</ConceptLink>: if two points belong to $\mathcal{H}_i$, the entire open segment between them is contained in $\mathcal{H}_i$.
</Definicion>
