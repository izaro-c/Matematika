<Capitular letra="T" />he isosceles triangle theorem, historically designated in <ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid's</ConceptLink> Elements as the *Pons Asinorum* (bridge of asses), establishes the fundamental angular symmetry characterizing <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangles</ConceptLink> with two <ConceptLink targetId="lado">sides</ConceptLink> of equal length in the Euclidean <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink>.

<Separador />

### Formal statement

Let $\triangle ABC$ be a <VisualBind element="triangulo" color="musgo"><ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink></VisualBind> such that sides <VisualBind element="lado-ab" color="pavo">$\overline{AB}$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="lado-ac" color="terracota">$\overline{AC}$</VisualBind> are <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruent</ConceptLink> ($\overline{AB} \cong \overline{AC}$). The base angles $\angle ABC$ and $\angle ACB$ are necessarily congruent:

<Formula>
  $$ \overline{AB} \cong \overline{AC} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \angle ABC \cong \angle ACB $$
</Formula>

In any isosceles triangle, angles opposite to congruent sides are strictly congruent.

<Separador />

### Proof and axiomatic foundations

The theorem admits several classical proofs:
- **Pappus's proof**: Considers the self-correspondence between triangle $\triangle ABC$ and its reflected counterpart $\triangle ACB$. Since $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{AC}$, $\angle BAC \cong \angle CAB$, and $\overline{AC} \cong \overline{AB}$, the SAS criterion yields $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle ACB$, directly implying $\angle ABC \cong \angle ACB$.
- **Bisector proof**: Constructing the internal angle <ConceptLink targetId="bisectriz">bisector</ConceptLink> at vertex $A$ divides the original triangle into two congruent triangles.

<Separador />

### Converse and equilateral corollary

- **Converse theorem**: If in a triangle $\triangle ABC$ angles $\angle ABC$ and $\angle ACB$ are congruent, then the opposite sides are necessarily congruent ($\overline{AB} \cong \overline{AC}$).
- **Equilateral corollary**: A triangle is <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo-equilatero">equilateral</ConceptLink> if and only if it is equiangular (each interior angle measuring exactly $60^\circ$).
