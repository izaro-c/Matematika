<Capitular letra="T" />he <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruence</ConceptLink> relation between <ConceptLink targetId="angulo" isDependency={true}>angles</ConceptLink> constitutes a complete <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-equivalencia" isDependency={true}>equivalence relation</ConceptLink>: it is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive.

Unlike segment congruence, where transitivity is formulated expressly as a primitive postulate in <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-2">Axiom III.2</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-4" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.4</ConceptLink> only postulates the existence, uniqueness of angle transport, and <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-reflexiva">reflexivity</ConceptLink>. <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-simetrica">Symmetry</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-transitiva">transitivity</ConceptLink> are proved by <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink> in §6 of the *Grundlagen der Geometrie* using the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-lal" isDependency={true}>SAS congruence theorem</ConceptLink>, establishing the classification of angular openings in <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal statement of the equivalence theorem of angle congruence">
Let $\mathcal{A}$ be the <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto">set</ConceptLink> of all <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> determined by unordered pairs of non-collinear <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta">rays</ConceptLink> with a common origin. The binary <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink> relation $\cong$ on $\mathcal{A}$ satisfies the three defining properties of an <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-equivalencia">equivalence relation</ConceptLink>:

1. **<ConceptLink targetId="relacion-reflexiva">Reflexivity</ConceptLink>:** Every angle is congruent to itself:
$$
\forall \angle\alpha \in \mathcal{A}, \quad \angle\alpha \cong \angle\alpha
$$

2. **<ConceptLink targetId="relacion-simetrica">Symmetry</ConceptLink>:** If an angle is congruent to a second, the second is congruent to the first:
$$
\forall \angle\alpha, \angle\beta \in \mathcal{A}, \quad \angle\alpha \cong \angle\beta \implies \angle\beta \cong \angle\alpha
$$

3. **<ConceptLink targetId="relacion-transitiva">Transitivity</ConceptLink>:** If an angle is congruent to a second and that second to a third, the first is congruent to the third:
$$
\forall \angle\alpha, \angle\beta, \angle\gamma \in \mathcal{A}, \quad (\angle\alpha \cong \angle\beta \land \angle\beta \cong \angle\gamma) \implies \angle\alpha \cong \angle\gamma
$$
</Definicion>
