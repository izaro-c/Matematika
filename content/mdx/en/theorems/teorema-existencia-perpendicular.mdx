<Capitular letra="G" />iven a <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>point</ConceptLink> and a <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink> in a <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink>, there exists a line passing through that point and <ConceptLink targetId="perpendicular" isDependency={true}>perpendicular</ConceptLink> to the given line. If the point is not incident with the line, this perpendicular is unique in the plane.

This result, formulated by <ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid</ConceptLink> in Propositions I.11 and I.12 of the <ConceptLink targetId="elementos-euclides">Elements</ConceptLink> and axiomatized by <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink> in §6 of the *Grundlagen der Geometrie*, belongs to <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>. Its proof is independent of the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">parallel postulate</ConceptLink> and relies exclusively on angle transport (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-4" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.4</ConceptLink>), the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-lal" isDependency={true}>SAS congruence theorem</ConceptLink>, and the synthetic characterization of the <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto" isDependency={true}>right angle</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal statement of the perpendicular theorem">
Let $\pi \in \Pi$ be a <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink>, let $\ell \in \mathcal{L}$ be a <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink> with $\operatorname{tr}(\ell) \subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\pi)$, and let $P \in \operatorname{tr}(\pi)$ be a <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>point</ConceptLink> in the plane.

There exists a <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink> $m \in \mathcal{L}$ with $\operatorname{tr}(m) \subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\pi)$ such that $P \,\mathbf{I}\, m$ and $m$ is <ConceptLink targetId="perpendicular" isDependency={true}>perpendicular</ConceptLink> to $\ell$ ($m \perp \ell$):

$$
\exists m \in \mathcal{L} \;\bigl(\operatorname{tr}(m) \subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\pi) \land P \,\mathbf{I}\, m \land m \perp \ell\bigr)
$$

Furthermore, if the <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>point</ConceptLink> $P$ is not incident with the line $\ell$ ($\neg(P \,\mathbf{I}\, \ell)$), this perpendicular line $m$ is unique in the plane:

$$
\neg(P \,\mathbf{I}\, \ell) \implies \exists! m \in \mathcal{L} \;\bigl(\operatorname{tr}(m) \subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\pi) \land P \,\mathbf{I}\, m \land m \perp \ell\bigr)
$$
</Definicion>
