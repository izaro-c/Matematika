<Capitular letra="I" />f two <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangles</ConceptLink> have all three pairs of corresponding <ConceptLink targetId="lado" isDependency={true}>sides</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink>, then the two triangles are mutually congruent.

Formulated by <ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid</ConceptLink> as Proposition I.8 of the <ConceptLink targetId="elementos-euclides">Elements</ConceptLink> and deductively grounded by <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink> in §6 of the *Grundlagen der Geometrie*, this result is one of the congruence criteria of <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>. Its proof does not appeal to rigid motions or the parallel postulate, but is deduced from the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-lal" isDependency={true}>SAS congruence theorem</ConceptLink> and the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-triangulo-isosceles" isDependency={true}>isosceles triangle theorem</ConceptLink> via an auxiliary construction involving the transport of angles and segments.

<Definicion title="Formal statement of the SSS congruence theorem">
Let <VisualBind element="pA1" color="terracota">$A$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pB1" color="terracota">$B$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pC1" color="terracota">$C$</VisualBind> $\in \mathcal{P}$ and <VisualBind element="pA2" color="pavo">$A'$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pB2" color="pavo">$B'$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pC2" color="pavo">$C'$</VisualBind> $\in \mathcal{P}$ be two triples of non-<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> in a <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> $\pi \in \Pi$, determining the triangles <VisualBind element="polygonA1B1C1" color="terracota">$\triangle ABC$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="polygonA2B2C2" color="pavo">$\triangle A'B'C'$</VisualBind>.

If all three pairs of corresponding <ConceptLink targetId="lado">sides</ConceptLink> are <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruent</ConceptLink>:
1. First pair of sides: <VisualBind element="segA1B1" color="terracota">$\overline{AB}$</VisualBind> $\cong$ <VisualBind element="segA2B2" color="pavo">$\overline{A'B'}$</VisualBind>
2. Second pair of sides: <VisualBind element="segB1C1" color="terracota">$\overline{BC}$</VisualBind> $\cong$ <VisualBind element="segB2C2" color="pavo">$\overline{B'C'}$</VisualBind>
3. Third pair of sides: <VisualBind element="segA1C1" color="terracota">$\overline{CA}$</VisualBind> $\cong$ <VisualBind element="segA2C2" color="pavo">$\overline{C'A'}$</VisualBind>

then both triangles are congruent (<ConceptLink targetId="congruencia-triangulos" isDependency={true}>triangle congruence</ConceptLink>) with respect to the ordered correspondence $A \mapsto A'$, $B \mapsto B'$, $C \mapsto C'$:

$$
\overline{AB} \cong \overline{A'B'} \land \overline{BC} \cong \overline{B'C'} \land \overline{CA} \cong \overline{C'A'} \implies \triangle ABC \cong \triangle A'B'C'
$$

Consequently, all three pairs of corresponding <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> are mutually congruent: $\angle ABC \cong \angle A'B'C'$, $\angle BCA \cong \angle B'C'A'$, and $\angle CAB \cong \angle C'A'B'$.
</Definicion>
