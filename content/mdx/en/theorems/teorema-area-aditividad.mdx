<Capitular letra="T" />he area additivity theorem constitutes the fundamental pillar of <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-medida-area">surface measurement</ConceptLink> in planar geometry. It guarantees that the area measure function assigned to polygonal regions is a finitely additive measure invariant under finite partitions.

<Separador />

### Formal statement

Let $R \subset \mathcal{P}$ be a planar polygonal region. Suppose that $R$ is the finite union of polygonal regions $R_1, R_2, \dots, R_n$:

<Formula>
  $$ R = \bigcup_{i=1}^n R_i $$
</Formula>

such that for every pair $i \neq j$, the interior of $R_i$ and the interior of $R_j$ are disjoint ($\text{Int}(R_i) \cap \text{Int}(R_j) = \emptyset$). The additive property of the area measure function holds strictly:

<Formula>
  $$ \text{Area}(R) = \sum_{i=1}^n \text{Area}(R_i) $$
</Formula>

That is, the measure of the total region equals the sum of the area measures of each constituent subregion.

<Separador />

### Axiomatic foundations and equidecomposition

In <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert's</ConceptLink> formalization, the concept of area is constructed to bypass infinitesimal calculus via equidecomposability:
- Two polygons $P$ and $Q$ are called **equidecomposable** (or scissors-congruent) if they can be partitioned into the same finite number of pairwise <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruent</ConceptLink> triangles.
- By the additivity theorem, any two equidecomposable polygons necessarily have the same area ($\text{Area}(P) = \text{Area}(Q)$).
- The Bolyai-Gerwien theorem establishes that in the Euclidean plane, any two polygons with the same area are always equidecomposable.

<Separador />

### Invariance and normalization

The area measure function satisfies three fundamental properties:
1. **Positivity**: $\text{Area}(R) > 0$ for every non-empty polygonal region.
2. **Congruence invariance**: If $R_1 \cong R_2$, then $\text{Area}(R_1) = \text{Area}(R_2)$.
3. **Finite additivity**: $\text{Area}(R_1 \cup R_2) = \text{Area}(R_1) + \text{Area}(R_2)$ for interior-disjoint regions.
