<Capitular letra="T" />he area additivity theorem constitutes the foundational pillar of the theory of <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-medida-area">surface measurement</ConceptLink> in Euclidean <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane geometry</ConceptLink>. It guarantees that the area measure function assigned to <ConceptLink targetId="poligono">polygonal</ConceptLink> regions is a finitely additive, partition-invariant measure.

<Separador />

### Formal statement

Let $R \subset \mathcal{P}$ be a planar polygonal region. Suppose $R$ is the <ConceptLink targetId="union-conjuntos">union</ConceptLink> of a finite number of polygonal subregions $R_1, R_2, \dots, R_n$:

$$ R = \bigcup_{i=1}^n R_i $$

such that for all $i \neq j$, the interiors of $R_i$ and $R_j$ are disjoint ($\text{Int}(R_i) \cap \text{Int}(R_j) = \emptyset$). The additive property of the area measure function holds strictly:

<Formula>
  $$ \text{Area}(R) = \sum_{i=1}^n \text{Area}(R_i) $$
</Formula>

That is, the measure of the total surface equals the scalar sum of the measures of the areas of each constituent subregion.

<Separador />

### Axiomatic foundations and equidecomposability

In <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert's</ConceptLink> formalization, the concept of area is developed rigorously without calculus using the notion of equidecomposability:
- Two polygons $P$ and $Q$ are **equidecomposable** (or equidissectable) if they can be partitioned into the same finite number of respectively <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruent</ConceptLink> triangles.
- By area additivity, two equidecomposable polygons necessarily have equal area ($\text{Area}(P) = \text{Area}(Q)$).
- The Bolyai-Gerwien theorem establishes that in the Euclidean plane, any two simple polygons with equal area are always equidecomposable.

<Separador />

### Invariance and normalization axioms

The area measure function satisfies three fundamental axioms:
1. **Positivity**: $\text{Area}(R) > 0$ for every non-empty region.
2. **Congruence invariance**: If $R_1 \cong R_2$, then $\text{Area}(R_1) = \text{Area}(R_2)$.
3. **Finite additivity**: $\text{Area}(R_1 \cup R_2) = \text{Area}(R_1) + \text{Area}(R_2)$ for interior-disjoint regions.
