<Capitular letra="F" />or every convex <ConceptLink targetId="angulo" isDependency={true}>angle</ConceptLink> in a <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink>, there exists a unique <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta" isDependency={true}>ray</ConceptLink> originating at the <ConceptLink targetId="vertice" isDependency={true}>vertex</ConceptLink> and contained in the interior of the angle that divides it into two <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink> angles.

Formalized by <ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid</ConceptLink> in Proposition I.9 of the <ConceptLink targetId="elementos-euclides">Elements</ConceptLink>, this <ConceptLink targetId="teorema">theorem</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink> establishes symmetric angular partition. It is deduced from the axioms of incidence, <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">order</ConceptLink>, and congruence (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-1" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.1</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-4" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.4</ConceptLink>), independently of the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">parallel postulate</ConceptLink>. Such a ray is defined as the <ConceptLink targetId="bisectriz">bisector</ConceptLink> of the angle.

<Definicion title="Formal statement of the existence and uniqueness of the angle bisector">
Let <VisualBind element="pO" color="carbon">$O \in \mathcal{P}$</VisualBind> be a <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>point</ConceptLink> in a <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink> $\pi \in \Pi$, and let <VisualBind element="rayOA" color="pavo">$\overrightarrow{OA}$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="rayOB" color="pavo">$\overrightarrow{OB}$</VisualBind> be two non-opposite, non-coincident <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta" isDependency={true}>rays</ConceptLink> sharing origin $O$, determining the convex <ConceptLink targetId="angulo" isDependency={true}>angle</ConceptLink> $\angle AOB = \overrightarrow{OA} \cup \overrightarrow{OB}$.

There exists a unique <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta" isDependency={true}>ray</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="bisAOB" color="pavo">$\overrightarrow{OC}$</VisualBind> with origin at <ConceptLink targetId="vertice" isDependency={true}>vertex</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="pO" color="carbon">$O$</VisualBind>, contained in the <ConceptLink targetId="semiplano" isDependency={true}>half-plane</ConceptLink> bounded by line $\overleftrightarrow{OA}$ containing <VisualBind element="pB" color="terracota">$B$</VisualBind> and lying in the interior of angle $\angle AOB$, such that the two resulting angles are mutually <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink>:

$$
\angle AOC \cong \angle BOC
$$

The <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta" isDependency={true}>ray</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="bisAOB" color="pavo">$\overrightarrow{OC}$</VisualBind> is called the <ConceptLink targetId="bisectriz">bisector</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="angulo" isDependency={true}>angle</ConceptLink> $\angle AOB$.
</Definicion>
