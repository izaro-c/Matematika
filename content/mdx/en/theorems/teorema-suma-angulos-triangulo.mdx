<Capitular letra="T" />he angle sum theorem of a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink> is one of the most prominent and universal results in <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-euclidiana">Euclidean geometry</ConceptLink>. It characterizes the zero curvature of the Euclidean <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> by establishing that the combined measure of the three interior <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> of any triangle equals a straight angle.

<Separador />

### Formal statement

Let $\triangle ABC$ be an arbitrary <VisualBind element="triangulo" color="musgo"><ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink></VisualBind> in the Euclidean plane $\mathcal{P}$. Let $\alpha = \angle CAB$, $\beta = \angle ABC$, and $\gamma = \angle BCA$ denote the angular measures of its three <VisualBind element="angulos-interiores" color="terracota">interior angles</VisualBind>. The equality holds strictly:

<Formula>
  $$ \alpha + \beta + \gamma = 180^\circ \quad (\text{or } \pi \text{ rad}) $$
</Formula>

That is, the sum of the measures of the three interior angles of every triangle is strictly equal to two <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto">right angles</ConceptLink>.

<Separador />

### Proof via auxiliary parallel line

The classical Euclidean construction, formalised in <ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid's</ConceptLink> Elements, constructs through <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertex</ConceptLink> $C$ a line <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallel</ConceptLink> to the opposite side $\overline{AB}$:
1. The parallel line $r$ together with sides $\overline{AC}$ and $\overline{BC}$ form pairs of congruent <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-angulos-alternos-internos">alternate interior angles</ConceptLink>.
2. The three consecutive angles along line $r$ sum to a <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-llano">straight angle</ConceptLink> of $180^\circ$.
3. By direct substitution of angular equalities, one necessarily obtains $\alpha + \beta + \gamma = 180^\circ$.

<Separador />

### Equivalence with the Fifth Postulate

This theorem is logically equivalent to <ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid's</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides" isDependency={true}>parallel postulate</ConceptLink>:
- **Hyperbolic geometry**: The sum of the interior angles of a triangle is strictly less than $180^\circ$ ($\alpha + \beta + \gamma < 180^\circ$), with an angular defect proportional to its <ConceptLink targetId="area">area</ConceptLink>.
- **Spherical geometry**: The sum of the interior angles is strictly greater than $180^\circ$ ($\alpha + \beta + \gamma > 180^\circ$), producing a spherical excess.
