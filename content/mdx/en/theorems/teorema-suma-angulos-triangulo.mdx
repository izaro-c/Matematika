<Capitular letra="T" />he triangle angle sum theorem is one of the most famous and universal properties in <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-euclidiana">Euclidean geometry</ConceptLink>. It characterizes the zero curvature of the Euclidean plane by establishing that the sum of the three interior <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> of any <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink> equals a <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-suplementarios">straight angle</ConceptLink>.

<Separador />

### Formal statement

Let $\triangle ABC$ be an arbitrary <VisualBind element="triangulo" color="musgo"><ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink></VisualBind> in the Euclidean plane $\mathcal{P}$. Let $\alpha = \angle CAB$, $\beta = \angle ABC$, and $\gamma = \angle BCA$ be the measures of its three <VisualBind element="angulos-interiores" color="terracota">interior angles</VisualBind>. The equality holds strictly:

<Formula>
  $$ \alpha + \beta + \gamma = 180^\circ \quad (\text{or } \pi \text{ rad}) $$
</Formula>

That is, the sum of the interior angles of any triangle is equivalent to two right angles.

<Separador />

### Proof via the parallel line

The classical construction in <ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid's</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="elementos-euclides">Elements</ConceptLink> draws a <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallel line</ConceptLink> through vertex $C$ to the opposite side $\overline{AB}$:
1. The parallel line and sides $\overline{AC}$ and $\overline{BC}$ form pairs of congruent <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-angulos-alternos-internos" isDependency={true}>alternate interior angles</ConceptLink> ($\alpha \cong \alpha'$ and $\beta \cong \beta'$).
2. The three consecutive angles along the parallel line form a <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-suplementarios">straight angle</ConceptLink> of $180^\circ$: $\alpha' + \gamma + \beta' = 180^\circ$.
3. By angle substitution, one directly obtains $\alpha + \beta + \gamma = 180^\circ$.

<Separador />

### Equivalence to the Parallel Postulate

This theorem is logically equivalent to <ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid's</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides" isDependency={true}>parallel postulate</ConceptLink>:
- **Hyperbolic geometry**: The sum of the interior angles is strictly less than $180^\circ$ ($\alpha + \beta + \gamma < 180^\circ$), with the angular defect being proportional to the triangle's area.
- **Spherical geometry**: The sum is strictly greater than $180^\circ$ ($\alpha + \beta + \gamma > 180^\circ$), defining the spherical excess.
