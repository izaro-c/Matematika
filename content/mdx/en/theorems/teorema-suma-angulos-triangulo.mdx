<Capitular letra="I" />n any <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangle</ConceptLink> in the Euclidean plane, the sum of the measures of its three interior <ConceptLink targetId="angulo" isDependency={true}>angles</ConceptLink> is constant and equal to two <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto" isDependency={true}>right angles</ConceptLink> ($180^\circ$ or $\pi$ radians).

This property characterizes the zero curvature of flat space and depends on parallel line construction. Along a supporting <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink>, the combination of the three angles reproduces the measure of a <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-suplementarios" isDependency={true}>straight angle</ConceptLink>.

<Separador />

### Formal statement

Let <VisualBind element="triangulo" color="terracota">$\triangle ABC$</VisualBind> be a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangle</ConceptLink> in a Euclidean plane $\pi \in \Pi$, whose interior <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> are given by <VisualBind element="angulos" color="musgo">$\alpha = \angle CAB$</VisualBind>, $\beta = \angle ABC$, and $\gamma = \angle BCA$. The equality holds:

<Formula>
  $$ \alpha + \beta + \gamma = 180^\circ \quad (\text{or } \pi \text{ rad}) $$
</Formula>

In a synthetic formulation independent of metric magnitudes:

<Formula>
  $$ \alpha + \beta + \gamma \cong 2\text{ right angles} $$
</Formula>

<Separador />

### Deductive foundation and the parallel line

The classical proof of Proposition I.32 in <ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid's</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="elementos-euclides">Elements</ConceptLink> rests on the construction of a <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallel</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink>:
- Through vertex $C$ ($C\,\mathbf{I}\,\pi \land \neg(C\,\mathbf{I}\,\overleftrightarrow{AB})$), the unique <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> parallel to side $\overline{AB}$ is constructed, guaranteed by <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides" isDependency={true}>Euclid's parallel postulate</ConceptLink>.
- Sides $\overline{AC}$ and $\overline{BC}$ act as transversal <ConceptLink targetId="secante">secants</ConceptLink>, determining pairs of congruent alternate interior angles at $C$ ($\alpha \cong \alpha'$ and $\beta \cong \beta'$) via the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-angulos-alternos-internos" isDependency={true}>alternate interior angles theorem</ConceptLink> and angle <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruence</ConceptLink>.
- The three adjacent angles $\alpha'$, $\gamma$, and $\beta'$ lie along the parallel line forming a <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-suplementarios" isDependency={true}>straight angle</ConceptLink> ($\alpha' + \gamma + \beta' = 180^\circ$), immediately yielding $\alpha + \beta + \gamma = 180^\circ$.

### Equivalence to the parallel postulate and non-Euclidean curvature

This <ConceptLink targetId="teorema">theorem</ConceptLink> is logically equivalent to <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides" isDependency={true}>Euclid's parallel postulate</ConceptLink>, establishing a central criterion in geometry classification:
- **Hyperbolic geometry**: In the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-hiperbolico">hyperbolic system</ConceptLink>, the sum of the angles of any triangle is strictly less than two right angles ($\alpha + \beta + \gamma < 180^\circ$). The difference $\delta(\triangle) = 180^\circ - (\alpha + \beta + \gamma)$ defines the *angular defect*, a positive quantity directly proportional to the area of the triangle.
- **Elliptic and spherical geometry**: In <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-eliptica">elliptic geometry</ConceptLink>, the sum of the interior angles strictly exceeds two right angles ($\alpha + \beta + \gamma > 180^\circ$), generating the *spherical excess* $\varepsilon(\triangle) = (\alpha + \beta + \gamma) - 180^\circ$.
- **Absolute framework**: In <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink> (the Saccheri-Legendre theorem), it is proved that the angle sum can never exceed two right angles ($\alpha + \beta + \gamma \le 180^\circ$), but the strict equality to $180^\circ$ requires Euclid's postulate without exception.
