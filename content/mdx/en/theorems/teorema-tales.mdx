<Capitular letra="T" />hales's theorem forms the bedrock of geometric <ConceptLink targetId="semejanza">similarity</ConceptLink> and proportionality in the Euclidean plane. It establishes that the intersection of a system of <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallel lines</ConceptLink> with two <ConceptLink targetId="recta">transversal lines</ConceptLink> invariantly preserves the ratio between the lengths of the intercepted <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segments</ConceptLink>.

<Separador />

### Formal statement

Let $r$ and $r'$ be two <ConceptLink targetId="recta">transversal lines</ConceptLink> in the plane $\mathcal{P}$. Let $L_1, L_2, L_3$ be three <VisualBind element="rectas-paralelas" color="musgo"><ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">mutually parallel lines</ConceptLink></VisualBind> cutting $r$ at points <VisualBind element="punto-a" color="pavo">$A$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="punto-b" color="terracota">$B$</VisualBind>, $C$ and $r'$ at points <VisualBind element="punto-a-primo" color="pavo">$A'$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="punto-b-primo" color="terracota">$B'$</VisualBind>, $C'$ respectively. The proportionality equality holds:

<Formula>
  $$ \frac{|\overline{AB}|}{|\overline{BC}|} = \frac{|\overline{A'B'}|}{|\overline{B'C'}|} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \frac{|\overline{AB}|}{|\overline{A'B'}|} = \frac{|\overline{BC}|}{|\overline{B'C'}|} $$
</Formula>

Equivalently, the ratio of the lengths of any two segments along $r$ is identical to the ratio of their corresponding segments along $r'$.

<Separador />

### Triangle corollary

In any <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink> $\triangle ABC$, if a line $r$ is drawn <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallel</ConceptLink> to side $\overline{BC}$ cutting sides $\overline{AB}$ and $\overline{AC}$ at points $D$ and $E$ respectively:
- The segments determined on the sides are proportional: $\frac{|\overline{AD}|}{|\overline{DB}|} = \frac{|\overline{AE}|}{|\overline{EC}|}$.
- The intercepted triangle $\triangle ADE$ is <ConceptLink targetId="semejanza">similar</ConceptLink> to the original triangle $\triangle ABC$.

<Separador />

### Proof and axiomatic foundations

The classical Euclidean proof relies on computing <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-triangulo" isDependency={true}>areas of triangles</ConceptLink> with equal base or equal altitude between parallel lines. In modern axiomatic systems, the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-arquimedes">Archimedean property</ConceptLink> and real line completeness guarantee that the result holds for both rational and incommensurable ratios.
