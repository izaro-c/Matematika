<Capitular letra="T" />he <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-medida-area" isDependency={true}>area</ConceptLink> of a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangle</ConceptLink> is half the product of the <ConceptLink targetId="longitud" isDependency={true}>length</ConceptLink> of a <ConceptLink targetId="lado">side</ConceptLink>, taken as <ConceptLink targetId="base">base</ConceptLink>, and the length of the corresponding <ConceptLink targetId="altura-triangulo" isDependency={true}>altitude</ConceptLink>.

The proof rests on the foot of the altitude. If $H$ is the foot of the <ConceptLink targetId="perpendicular" isDependency={true}>perpendicular</ConceptLink> dropped from $C$ to the line $\overleftrightarrow{AB}$, the segment $\overline{CH}$ splits the triangle into two <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo-rectangulo">right triangles</ConceptLink>, each one half of a rectangle (<ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-rectangulo" isDependency={true}>rectangle area theorem</ConceptLink>), when $H$ lies between $A$ and $B$; when $H$ falls outside the side, which happens if the triangle has an obtuse angle at $A$ or at $B$, the triangle is the difference of two right triangles. In both cases the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-aditividad" isDependency={true}>additivity of area</ConceptLink> yields the formula (<ConceptLink targetId="demo-area-triangulo">proof</ConceptLink>). The result underlies the area proof of the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-tales">intercept theorem</ConceptLink> and the classical proofs of the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-pitagoras">Pythagorean theorem</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal statement of the triangle area theorem">
Let $\triangle ABC$ be a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangle</ConceptLink> in a <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink> $\sigma$ of the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-euclidiano">Euclidean system</ConceptLink> and let $H$ be the foot of the <ConceptLink targetId="perpendicular" isDependency={true}>perpendicular</ConceptLink> to the <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink> $\overleftrightarrow{AB}$ passing through $C$. If $b = |AB|$ and $h = |CH|$ are the <ConceptLink targetId="longitud" isDependency={true}>lengths</ConceptLink> of the base and of the <ConceptLink targetId="altura-triangulo" isDependency={true}>altitude</ConceptLink>, the area of the <ConceptLink targetId="region-triangular" isDependency={true}>triangular region</ConceptLink> determined by $\triangle ABC$ is

$$
\operatorname{A}(\triangle ABC) = \frac{1}{2}\, b \cdot h
$$
</Definicion>

  <PropiedadesGrupo title="Consequences and equivalent formulas">
    <PropiedadItem id="independencia-base-altura" title="Independence of the chosen base" formula="|BC| \cdot h_a = |CA| \cdot h_b = |AB| \cdot h_c">
      Since the area does not depend on the chosen side, the product of each side and its <ConceptLink targetId="altura-triangulo">altitude</ConceptLink> is the same for the three sides ($h_a$, $h_b$, $h_c$ denote the altitudes from $A$, $B$ and $C$).
    </PropiedadItem>
    <PropiedadItem id="area-triangulos-misma-altura" title="Triangles with the same altitude" formula="\frac{\operatorname{A}(\triangle ABC)}{\operatorname{A}(\triangle A'B'C)} = \frac{|AB|}{|A'B'|}">
      If the sides $\overline{AB}$ and $\overline{A'B'}$ lie on the same <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> and $C$ is not incident with it, the triangles $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle A'B'C$ have the same altitude and their areas are proportional to their bases. This is the lemma used by <ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid</ConceptLink>'s proof of the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-tales">intercept theorem</ConceptLink> (Elements VI.1–VI.2).
    </PropiedadItem>
    <PropiedadItem id="area-triangulo-trigonometrica" title="Trigonometric formula" formula="\operatorname{A}(\triangle ABC) = \tfrac{1}{2}\, |CA| \cdot |CB| \cdot \operatorname{sen} |\angle ACB|">
      The altitude from $A$ measures $|CA| \cdot \operatorname{sen} |\angle ACB|$, where $\operatorname{sen}$ is the <ConceptLink targetId="seno">sine</ConceptLink> function and $|\angle ACB|$ the <ConceptLink targetId="medida-angular">angle measure</ConceptLink> of the angle at $C$.
    </PropiedadItem>
    <PropiedadItem id="formula-heron" title="Heron's formula" formula="\operatorname{A}(\triangle ABC) = \sqrt{s(s - a)(s - b)(s - c)}">
      If $a = |BC|$, $b = |CA|$, $c = |AB|$ and $s = \tfrac{1}{2}(a + b + c)$ is the <ConceptLink targetId="semiperimetro">semiperimeter</ConceptLink>, the area depends only on the side lengths (<ConceptLink targetId="formula-heron">Heron's formula</ConceptLink>); it follows from this theorem and the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-pitagoras">Pythagorean theorem</ConceptLink>.
    </PropiedadItem>
  </PropiedadesGrupo>
</SeccionPropiedades>
