<Capitular letra="T" />he area of a triangle theorem determines the surface measurement of the foundational polygonal figure in planar geometry. It establishes that the area enclosed by three straight segments depends exclusively on the linear magnitude of one base and its perpendicular distance to the opposite vertex.

<Separador />

### Formal statement

Let $\triangle ABC$ be a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink> in the Euclidean plane $\mathcal{P}$. Let $b = |\overline{AB}|$ be the length of one of its sides taken as a base, and let $h$ be the length of the <ConceptLink targetId="perpendicular">perpendicular altitude</ConceptLink> from the opposite vertex $C$ to the line containing side $\overline{AB}$. The area measure of triangle $\triangle ABC$ satisfies:

<Formula>
  $$ \text{Area}(\triangle ABC) = \frac{1}{2} b \cdot h $$
</Formula>

That is, the area of any triangle is rigorously equal to one-half the product of its base and corresponding altitude.

<Separador />

### Proof and equidecomposition

The Euclidean proof is obtained by completing the triangle into a <ConceptLink targetId="paralelogramo">parallelogram</ConceptLink>:
1. A line <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallel</ConceptLink> to $\overline{AB}$ is drawn through $C$, and a line parallel to $\overline{AC}$ is drawn through $B$, intersecting at point $D$.
2. The quadrilateral $ABDC$ is a <ConceptLink targetId="paralelogramo">parallelogram</ConceptLink> with base $b$ and height $h$.
3. The diagonal $\overline{BC}$ divides the parallelogram into two congruent <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangles</ConceptLink>: $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DCB$.
4. By the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-aditividad" isDependency={true}>area additivity theorem</ConceptLink>, $\text{Area}(ABDC) = \text{Area}(\triangle ABC) + \text{Area}(\triangle DCB) = 2 \cdot \text{Area}(\triangle ABC)$.
5. Since $\text{Area}(ABDC) = b \cdot h$ from the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-rectangulo" isDependency={true}>area of a rectangle theorem</ConceptLink>, it follows that $\text{Area}(\triangle ABC) = \frac{1}{2} b \cdot h$.

<Separador />

### Equivalent area formulas

- **Heron's formula**: Given a triangle with side lengths $a, b, c$ and semiperimeter $s = \frac{a+b+c}{2}$, its area equals $\sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}$.
- **Trigonometric formula**: In terms of two sides and the included angle, $\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} a b \sin(\gamma)$.
