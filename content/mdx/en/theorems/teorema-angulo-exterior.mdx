<Capitular letra="I" />n any <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangle</ConceptLink>, every <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-exterior">exterior angle</ConceptLink> adjacent to a <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertex</ConceptLink> is strictly greater, according to the relation of <ConceptLink targetId="orden-angulos" isDependency={true}>order of angles</ConceptLink>, than either of the two non-adjacent interior <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink>.

This result, formulated by <ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid</ConceptLink> as Proposition I.16 of the <ConceptLink targetId="elementos-euclides">Elements</ConceptLink> and formalized by <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink> in §6 of the *Grundlagen der Geometrie*, belongs to <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>. Its proof is independent of the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">parallel axiom</ConceptLink> and continuity axioms: it is deduced in the plane from the axioms of order and congruence via the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-lal" isDependency={true}>SAS congruence theorem</ConceptLink> and the congruence of <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-opuestos-vertice" isDependency={true}>vertical angles</ConceptLink>. It constitutes an essential deductive tool to prove the triangle inequality and the existence of parallel lines.

<Definicion title="Formal statement of the exterior angle theorem">
Let $A, B, C \in \mathcal{P}$ be three non-<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> in a <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> $\pi \in \Pi$, determining the <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangle</ConceptLink> $\triangle ABC = \{\overline{AB}, \overline{BC}, \overline{CA}\}$.

Let $D \in \mathcal{P}$ be a point on the line containing $\overline{BC}$ such that $B * C * D$, so that the <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta">ray</ConceptLink> $\overrightarrow{CD}$ is opposite to $\overrightarrow{CB}$ and the <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angle</ConceptLink> $\angle ACD$ is an <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-exterior">exterior angle</ConceptLink> at <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertex</ConceptLink> $C$.

Then, with respect to the relation of <ConceptLink targetId="orden-angulos" isDependency={true}>order of angles</ConceptLink>, the strict inequalities hold:

$$
\angle ACD > \angle BAC \quad \land \quad \angle ACD > \angle ABC
$$

equivalently, $\angle BAC < \angle ACD$ and $\angle ABC < \angle ACD$. Similarly, the exterior angle determined by extending $\overline{AC}$ beyond $C$ satisfies the same inequalities, being congruent to $\angle ACD$ as <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-opuestos-vertice" isDependency={true}>vertical angles</ConceptLink>.
</Definicion>
