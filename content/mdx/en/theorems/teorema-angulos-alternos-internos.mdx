<Capitular letra="T" />wo <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>lines</ConceptLink> of a <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink>, cut by a <ConceptLink targetId="secante" isDependency={true}>transversal</ConceptLink> at two distinct points, are <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas" isDependency={true}>parallel</ConceptLink> if and only if the <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-alternos-internos" isDependency={true}>alternate interior angles</ConceptLink> they determine are <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink>. The direction "congruent ⇒ parallel" is a theorem of <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>; the direction "parallel ⇒ congruent" requires <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides" isDependency={true}>Axiom IV</ConceptLink> and is therefore a theorem of the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-euclidiano">Euclidean system</ConceptLink>.

The two directions differ in deductive nature. The first is Proposition I.27 of <ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid</ConceptLink>'s <ConceptLink targetId="elementos-euclides">Elements</ConceptLink>: it is proved with the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-angulo-exterior">exterior angle theorem</ConceptLink>, without the parallel axiom or the continuity axioms, and it yields the existence of at least one parallel through an exterior point. The second is Proposition I.29, the first in the Elements to use the <ConceptLink targetId="quinto-postulado-euclides">fifth postulate</ConceptLink>; it fails in the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-hiperbolico">hyperbolic system</ConceptLink>, where a transversal can cut two parallels forming non-congruent alternate interior angles.

<Definicion title="Formal statement">
  Let $\ell_1, \ell_2 \in \mathcal{L}$ be two <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>lines</ConceptLink> of a <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink> $\pi \in \Pi$ and let $t \in \mathcal{L}$ be a line of $\pi$, <ConceptLink targetId="secante" isDependency={true}>transversal</ConceptLink> to both, meeting $\ell_1$ at the <ConceptLink targetId="punto">point</ConceptLink> $A$ and $\ell_2$ at the point $B$, with $A \neq B$ (in particular $\ell_1 \neq t$, $\ell_2 \neq t$ and $\ell_1 \neq \ell_2$).

  Let $P_1 \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell_1$ and $P_2 \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell_2$ be two points lying in opposite <ConceptLink targetId="semiplano" isDependency={true}>half-planes</ConceptLink> with respect to $t$, that is, $P_2 \notin \mathcal{H}_t(P_1)$ and $\neg(P_2 \, \mathbf{I} \, t)$. The <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-alternos-internos" isDependency={true}>alternate interior angles</ConceptLink> are:

  $$
  \alpha = \angle P_1AB, \quad \beta = \angle P_2BA
  $$

  Then:

  $$
  \ell_1 \parallel \ell_2 \iff \alpha \cong \beta
  $$

  The equivalence splits into two propositions:

  1. **Direction (⇐), Euclid I.27, <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>** (without continuity axioms):
  $$
  \alpha \cong \beta \implies \ell_1 \parallel \ell_2
  $$

  2. **Direction (⇒), Euclid I.29, <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-euclidiano">Euclidean geometry</ConceptLink>** (uses <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides" isDependency={true}>Axiom IV</ConceptLink>):
  $$
  \ell_1 \parallel \ell_2 \implies \alpha \cong \beta
  $$
</Definicion>

    <PropiedadItem 
      id="congruencia-angulos-correspondientes" 
      title="Congruence of corresponding angles"
      formula="\ell_1 \parallel \ell_2 \iff \angle P_1AB \cong \angle P_2'BC \qquad (P_2 * B * P_2',\ A * B * C)"
    >
      Two lines of a plane cut by a <ConceptLink targetId="secante">transversal</ConceptLink> are parallel if and only if the <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-correspondientes">corresponding angles</ConceptLink> are congruent. Indeed, $\angle P_2'BC$ and $\beta = \angle P_2BA$ are <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-opuestos-vertice">vertical angles</ConceptLink> (by the <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">betweenness</ConceptLink> relations $P_2 * B * P_2'$ and $A * B * C$), hence congruent by the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-angulos-opuestos-verticales">vertical angles theorem</ConceptLink>.
    </PropiedadItem>

    <PropiedadItem 
      id="congruencia-angulos-alternos-externos" 
      title="Congruence of alternate exterior angles"
      formula="\ell_1 \parallel \ell_2 \iff \angle P_1AD \cong \angle P_2BC \qquad (B * A * D,\ A * B * C)"
    >
      Two lines of a plane cut by a <ConceptLink targetId="secante">transversal</ConceptLink> are parallel if and only if the <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-alternos-externos">alternate exterior angles</ConceptLink> are congruent. $\angle P_1AD$ and $\angle P_2BC$ are the <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-suplementarios">adjacent supplements</ConceptLink> of $\alpha$ and $\beta$, and two angles are congruent if and only if their supplements are (<ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-suplementos">congruence of supplements</ConceptLink>).
    </PropiedadItem>

    <PropiedadItem 
      id="suplementariedad-angulos-conjugados-internos" 
      title="Supplementarity of same-side interior angles"
      formula="\ell_1 \parallel \ell_2 \iff \operatorname{Supl}(\angle P_1AB, \angle P_2'BA) \qquad (P_2 * B * P_2')"
    >
      Two lines of a plane cut by a <ConceptLink targetId="secante">transversal</ConceptLink> are parallel if and only if the <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-conjugados-internos">same-side interior angles</ConceptLink> $\alpha = \angle P_1AB$ and $\delta = \angle P_2'BA$, lying in the same <ConceptLink targetId="semiplano">half-plane</ConceptLink> with respect to $t$, are <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-suplementarios">supplementary</ConceptLink>, that is, $\alpha$ is congruent to the adjacent supplement of $\delta$, which is $\beta$. This is how Euclid's <ConceptLink targetId="quinto-postulado-euclides">fifth postulate</ConceptLink> frames the question.
    </PropiedadItem>
  </PropiedadesGrupo>

  <PropiedadesGrupo title="Consequences in the Euclidean system">
    <PropiedadItem

      title="Equivalence of direction (⇒) with the parallel axiom"
      formula="\bigl(\ell_1 \parallel \ell_2 \implies \alpha \cong \beta\bigr) \iff \mathrm{IV}"

      Over <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>, direction (⇒) is <ConceptLink targetId="equivalencia-logica">equivalent</ConceptLink> to <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">Axiom IV</ConceptLink>. Axiom IV implies (⇒), as shown in the proof. Conversely, if (⇒) holds, given a line $\ell$ and an exterior point $P$, let $t$ be the <ConceptLink targetId="perpendicular">perpendicular</ConceptLink> to $\ell$ through $P$: every parallel to $\ell$ through $P$ makes with $t$ an angle congruent to a <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto">right angle</ConceptLink>, and by the uniqueness in <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-4">Axiom III.4</ConceptLink> there is only one such line.
    </PropiedadItem>
