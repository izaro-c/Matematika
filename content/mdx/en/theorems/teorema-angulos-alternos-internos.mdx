<Capitular letra="T" />he alternate interior angles theorem establishes the metric equivalence between <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> located on opposite sides of a transversal line that intersects two <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallel lines</ConceptLink> in the Euclidean plane.

<Separador />

### Formal statement

Let $r$ and $s$ be two <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallel lines</ConceptLink> in the plane $\mathcal{P}$ ($r \parallel s$), and let $t$ be a transversal line intersecting them at points $A$ and $B$ respectively. Let $\alpha$ and $\alpha'$ be two angles between the parallel lines $r$ and $s$, situated in opposite half-planes with respect to the transversal $t$. The <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink> relation holds:

<Formula>
  $$ \alpha \cong \alpha' $$
</Formula>

That is, the angular measures of both alternate interior angles are identical: $|\alpha| = |\alpha'|$.

<Separador />

### Axiomatic analysis and converse

Euclid structures the behavior of angles formed by a transversal into two foundational propositions:
- **Proposition I.27 (Absolute converse)**: If two coplanar lines cut by a transversal form congruent alternate interior angles, then the lines are <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallel</ConceptLink>. This result is independent of the fifth postulate and holds in <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>.
- **Proposition I.29 (Euclidean direct statement)**: If two lines are <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallel</ConceptLink>, the congruence of alternate interior angles strictly requires <ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid's</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides" isDependency={true}>parallel postulate</ConceptLink>.

<Separador />

### Derived angle relationships

Given two parallel lines cut by a transversal $t$:
- **Corresponding angles**: Are congruent ($\alpha \cong \beta$).
- **Alternate exterior angles**: Are congruent ($\gamma \cong \gamma'$).
- **Consecutive interior angles**: Are <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-suplementarios">supplementary</ConceptLink> ($\alpha + \beta' = 180^\circ$).
