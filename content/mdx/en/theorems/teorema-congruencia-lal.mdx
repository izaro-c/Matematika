<Capitular letra="I" />f two <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangles</ConceptLink> have two <ConceptLink targetId="lado">sides</ConceptLink> and the included <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angle</ConceptLink> respectively <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink>, then they are congruent in all their remaining elements: the third side is congruent and the remaining angles are respectively congruent, so that both triangles are mutually congruent.

In the classical tradition, this result corresponds to Proposition I.4 of <ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid's</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="elementos-euclides">Elements</ConceptLink>, proved by superposition based on <ConceptLink targetId="movimiento-rigido">rigid motion</ConceptLink>. In formalizing <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink> recognized that superposition conceals an unprovable principle without additional postulates and formulated <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-5" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.5</ConceptLink> as a primitive postulate, securing only the congruence of the remaining angles at the base. From it, together with segment transport (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-1" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.1</ConceptLink>) and the uniqueness of angle transport (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-4" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.4</ConceptLink>), this <ConceptLink targetId="teorema">theorem</ConceptLink> completes the <ConceptLink targetId="criterio-congruencia">congruence criterion</ConceptLink> by deducing the congruence of the third <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink> and the global <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia-triangulos" isDependency={true}>congruence of triangles</ConceptLink>, standing as the first fundamental criterion from which the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-ala">ASA</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-lll">SSS</ConceptLink> criteria are derived.

<Definicion title="Formal statement of the SAS congruence theorem">
Let $A, B, C \in \mathcal{P}$ and $A', B', C' \in \mathcal{P}$ be two triples of non-<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> in a <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> $\pi \in \Pi$, determining <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangles</ConceptLink> $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle A'B'C'$ with their respective <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertices</ConceptLink>.

If the congruence hypotheses hold:
1. First pair of corresponding <ConceptLink targetId="lado">sides</ConceptLink>: $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{A'B'}$
2. Second pair of corresponding sides: $\overline{AC} \cong \overline{A'C'}$
3. Included <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angle</ConceptLink> between them: $\angle BAC \cong \angle B'A'C'$

then the third side is congruent:
$$
\overline{BC} \cong \overline{B'C'}
$$
and the triangles are congruent (<ConceptLink targetId="congruencia-triangulos">triangle congruence</ConceptLink>) with respect to the ordered correspondence $A \mapsto A'$, $B \mapsto B'$, $C \mapsto C'$:
$$
\triangle ABC \cong \triangle A'B'C'
$$
satisfying as well $\angle ABC \cong \angle A'B'C'$ and $\angle ACB \cong \angle A'C'B'$.
</Definicion>
