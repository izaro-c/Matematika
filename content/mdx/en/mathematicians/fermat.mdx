<Capitular letra="P" />ierre de Fermat (1607–1665) was a French jurist and mathematician born in Beaumont-de-Lomagne who died in Castres. His date of birth is uncertain: for a long time 17 August 1601 was given, but recent research holds that this date belongs to an elder brother of the same name and places his birth at the end of 1607. He was a councillor at the parliament of Toulouse and pursued mathematics as an amateur, communicating his results by letter and hardly publishing them.

<Separador />

### Main Contributions

- **Analytic geometry**: In *Ad locos planos et solidos isagoge*, which circulated in manuscript from 1636, he described <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink> and conics by equations of first and second degree in two unknowns, independently of <ConceptLink targetId="descartes">René Descartes</ConceptLink>. He is, with him, co-creator of <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-analitica">analytic geometry</ConceptLink>.
- **Maxima, minima and tangents**: His method of *adequality* made it possible to find maxima, minima and tangents to algebraic curves, and anticipates the <ConceptLink targetId="derivada">derivative</ConceptLink>; he also computed areas under curves of the form $y = x^n$.
- **Number theory**: He stated <ConceptLink targetId="pequeno-teorema-fermat">Fermat's little theorem</ConceptLink> (1640), studied numbers of the form $2^{2^n} + 1$, characterized the primes that are sums of two squares, and developed the method of infinite descent, a form of <ConceptLink targetId="metodo-contradiccion">reductio ad absurdum</ConceptLink> over the natural numbers.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="ultimo-teorema-fermat">Fermat's Last Theorem</ConceptLink>**: In the margin of his copy of the *Arithmetica* of <ConceptLink targetId="diofanto">Diophantus</ConceptLink> he noted that the equation $x^n + y^n = z^n$ has no solutions in positive integers for $n > 2$, and that he had found a proof which the margin was too narrow to contain. The statement was proved by <ConceptLink targetId="wiles">Andrew Wiles</ConceptLink> in 1994–1995.
- **Probability and optics**: His correspondence with <ConceptLink targetId="pascal">Blaise Pascal</ConceptLink> in 1654 laid the foundations of probability theory. In optics he formulated the principle of least time, from which he deduced the law of refraction.

<Separador />

### Influence and Legacy

Fermat published very little during his lifetime; his son Samuel edited in 1670 Diophantus' *Arithmetica* with his annotations and in 1679 his *Varia opera mathematica*. His arithmetical problems, taken up by <ConceptLink targetId="euler">Leonhard Euler</ConceptLink>, guided number theory for more than three centuries.

<Separador />

### Notable Works

- *Ad locos planos et solidos isagoge* (c. 1636, published in 1679) — Introduction to analytic geometry.
- *Methodus ad disquirendam maximam et minimam* (c. 1636) — Method of maxima, minima and tangents.
- Observations on Diophantus' *Arithmetica* (published in 1670) — Including the statement of his Last Theorem.
