<Capitular letra="M" />oritz Pasch (1843–1930) was a German mathematician born in Breslau (now Wrocław). He obtained his doctorate in his native city in 1865 and spent his whole career at the University of Giessen, of which he was rector. His work inaugurated the modern axiomatic method in geometry: he demanded that proofs depend neither on intuition nor on figures, but only on explicitly accepted propositions.

<Separador />

### Main Contributions

- **Lectures on modern geometry (*Vorlesungen über neuere Geometrie*, 1882)**: It was the first exposition of geometry in which all <ConceptLink targetId="concepto-primitivo">primitive concepts</ConceptLink> (<ConceptLink targetId="punto">point</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink> and plane surface) and all basic propositions are stated explicitly, and in which the remaining results are deduced from them alone.
- **Axioms of order and <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-4">Pasch's axiom</ConceptLink>**: He observed that <ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid</ConceptLink> used, without stating them, properties of order and separation of the <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink>, and formulated the principle that bears his name: if a <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> in the plane of a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink> passes through none of its <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertices</ConceptLink> and meets one of its <ConceptLink targetId="lado">sides</ConceptLink>, then it meets another side.
- **The <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">betweenness</ConceptLink> relation**: His axioms on the relative position of the points of a segment are the origin of the group of axioms of order of <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink> (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-1">Axiom II.1</ConceptLink> to <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-4">Axiom II.4</ConceptLink>).

<Separador />

### Influence and Legacy

Pasch maintained that, for geometry to be truly deductive, the process of inference must be independent of the meaning of the geometric concepts, just as it must be independent of figures. This requirement directly influenced <ConceptLink targetId="peano">Giuseppe Peano</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="pieri">Mario Pieri</ConceptLink> and Hilbert's <ConceptLink targetId="fundamentos-geometria-hilbert">*Foundations of Geometry*</ConceptLink> (1899).
