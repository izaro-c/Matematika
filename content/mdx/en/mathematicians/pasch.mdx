<Capitular letra="M" />oritz Pasch (1843–1930) was a German mathematician and professor at the University of Giessen. His foundational work launched the modern axiomatic movement by establishing that deductive geometry must proceed without any reliance on visual diagrams or spatial intuition.

<Separador />

### Main Contributions

- **Lectures on Modern Geometry (*Vorlesungen über neuere Geometrie*, 1882)**: Introduced the first formal geometric system wherein primitive notions (point, segment, planar surface) are governed strictly by formal axioms rather than physical or empirical models.
- **Axioms of Order and <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-4">Pasch's Axiom</ConceptLink>**: Discovered that Euclid had implicitly relied on unstated topological properties of plane separation. He formulated the celebrated principle: *if a line intersects one side of a triangle without passing through any of its vertices, it must intersect one of the other two sides*.
- **Foundation of the <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">Betweenness</ConceptLink> Relation**: Formalized linear and planar geometric order, which directly became the second group of Hilbert's axioms (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-1">II-1</ConceptLink> through <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-4">II-4</ConceptLink>).

<Separador />

### Influence and Legacy

Pasch's methodological doctrine («*deduction must be so strict that reasoning remains valid even if the physical meaning of terms is completely ignored*») transformed mathematical logic. His work directly inspired Giuseppe Peano, Mario Pieri, and the definitive 1899 axiomatization of geometry by David Hilbert.
