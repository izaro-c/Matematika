<Capitular letra="R" />ené Descartes (1596–1650) was a French philosopher, mathematician and physicist born in La Haye (Touraine) who died in Stockholm. Educated at the Jesuit college of La Flèche, he spent most of his adult life in the Dutch Republic. Together with <ConceptLink targetId="fermat">Pierre de Fermat</ConceptLink>, who arrived at similar ideas independently, he is the creator of <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-analitica">analytic geometry</ConceptLink>.

<Separador />

### Main Contributions

- **Analytic geometry**: In *La Géométrie* (1637) he associated with each segment a magnitude on which one operates algebraically and described curves by equations between two variable magnitudes referred to a fixed line. Descartes did not systematically use two perpendicular axes; the <ConceptLink targetId="modelo-cartesiano">Cartesian coordinate system</ConceptLink> is named after him because of this work, although its present form was fixed later.
- **Pappus' problem**: He solved in general the locus problem of <ConceptLink targetId="pappus">Pappus</ConceptLink> on three or four lines, which Greek geometry had left open, and used it to show the power of the new method.
- **Algebra**: He fixed notational conventions still in use (the last letters of the alphabet for unknowns, exponents such as $x^3$) and stated the rule of signs, which bounds the number of positive roots of a polynomial.
- **Polyhedra**: In an unpublished manuscript, *De solidorum elementis*, he obtained relations between the vertices, faces and angles of <ConceptLink targetId="poliedro">polyhedra</ConceptLink> equivalent to the formula that <ConceptLink targetId="euler">Leonhard Euler</ConceptLink> published in 1758.

<Separador />

### Influence and Legacy

The Latin translation of *La Géométrie* by <ConceptLink targetId="van-schooten">Frans van Schooten</ConceptLink> (1649), with extensive commentary, spread analytic geometry throughout Europe and was one of the sources of <ConceptLink targetId="newton">Isaac Newton</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="leibniz">Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz</ConceptLink>. By translating geometric problems into algebra, Descartes opened the way to representing the <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> and the <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> by real numbers, which today provides models of the axioms of geometry.
