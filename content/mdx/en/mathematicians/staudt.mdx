<Capitular letra="K" />arl Georg Christian von Staudt (1798–1867) was a German mathematician born in Rothenburg ob der Tauber and died in Erlangen. He studied in Göttingen under <ConceptLink targetId="gauss">Carl Friedrich Gauss</ConceptLink> between 1819 and 1822 and earned his doctorate in Erlangen in 1822. After teaching in Würzburg and Nuremberg, from 1835 he held the chair of mathematics at the University of Erlangen until his death.

<Separador />

### Main Contributions

- **Projective geometry without metrics**: In *Geometrie der Lage* (1847) he constructed <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-proyectiva">projective geometry</ConceptLink> solely from relations of <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence</ConceptLink> and position, without using lengths or angles. The cross-ratio, which had previously been defined using distances, was replaced by purely projective constructions based on the complete quadrangle and the harmonic tetrad.
- **Algebra of throws (*Würfe*)**: In the *Beiträge zur Geometrie der Lage* (1856–1860) he defined addition and multiplication of points on a <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> through geometric constructions, so that the points form a <ConceptLink targetId="cuerpo">field</ConceptLink>. This was the first introduction of coordinates into a projective space from geometric axioms alone, an idea that <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink> took up in his calculus of segments.
- **Imaginary elements**: In the same *Beiträge* he provided a geometric definition of imaginary points as fixed-point-free involutions with an orientation of traversal, distinguishing each imaginary point from its conjugate.
- **Fundamental theorem of projective geometry**: He proved that a projectivity between lines is determined by the image of three points. <ConceptLink targetId="klein">Felix Klein</ConceptLink> pointed out in 1873 that the proof implicitly assumed a <ConceptLink targetId="continuidad">continuity</ConceptLink> property not included among its hypotheses; the gap was later closed by adding an axiom of continuity, with contributions by <ConceptLink targetId="cantor">Georg Cantor</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="luroth">Jacob Lüroth</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="zeuthen">Hieronymus Zeuthen</ConceptLink>, and <ConceptLink targetId="darboux">Gaston Darboux</ConceptLink>.
- **Number theory**: In 1840, independently and simultaneously with <ConceptLink targetId="clausen">Thomas Clausen</ConceptLink>, he proved the Von Staudt–Clausen theorem concerning the fractional parts of Bernoulli numbers.

<Separador />

### Influence and Legacy

Von Staudt's work demonstrated that projective geometry can be founded without metric notions, and his algebra of throws anticipated the algebraization of axiomatic theories: coordinates in a geometry arise from its own axioms. This idea is the foundation of the correspondence between projective planes satisfying <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-desargues">Desargues' theorem</ConceptLink> and division rings.
