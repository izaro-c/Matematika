<Capitular letra="W" />itold Hurewicz (1904–1956) was a Polish mathematician born in Łódź. He studied in Vienna, where he obtained his doctorate in 1926 under <ConceptLink targetId="hahn">Hans Hahn</ConceptLink>, in the same circle as <ConceptLink targetId="menger">Karl Menger</ConceptLink>. From 1928 to 1936 he was assistant to <ConceptLink targetId="brouwer">L. E. J. Brouwer</ConceptLink> in Amsterdam; he then moved to the United States, to the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton, the University of North Carolina and, from 1945, the Massachusetts Institute of Technology. He died in 1956 after falling from a pyramid at Uxmal (Mexico) during an excursion of a conference on algebraic topology.

<Separador />

### Main Contributions

- **Dimension theory**: In 1926–1927 he extended the dimension <ConceptLink targetId="teorema">theorems</ConceptLink> of Menger and <ConceptLink targetId="urysohn">Pavel Urysohn</ConceptLink>, first proved for <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto">subsets</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="espacio-euclidiano">Euclidean space</ConceptLink>, to separable <ConceptLink targetId="espacio-metrico">metric spaces</ConceptLink>, and in 1930 he proved that every separable metric space of finite <ConceptLink targetId="dimension">dimension</ConceptLink> $n$ embeds in a compact space of the same dimension. <ConceptLink targetId="borsuk">Karol Borsuk</ConceptLink> placed him, with Menger and Urysohn, among the creators of the theory.
- **Homotopy groups**: In 1935–1936 he introduced the higher homotopy <ConceptLink targetId="grupo">groups</ConceptLink> $\pi_n(X)$ and related the first non-zero one to homology (Hurewicz theorem).
- **Exact sequences and fibrations**: In 1941 he introduced exact sequences, which gave rise to homological algebra, and studied fibrations in relation to homotopy groups.

<Separador />

### Influence and Legacy

The book *Dimension Theory* (1941), written with <ConceptLink targetId="wallman">Henry Wallman</ConceptLink>, was for decades the standard account of the dimension theory of separable metric spaces, combining the inductive definition of Menger and Urysohn with the covering approach of <ConceptLink targetId="lebesgue">Henri Lebesgue</ConceptLink>. His homotopy groups are today a central object of algebraic <ConceptLink targetId="topologia">topology</ConceptLink>.
