<Capitular letra="R" />ichard Dedekind (1831–1916) was a German mathematician who was born and died in Brunswick. He obtained his doctorate at Göttingen in 1852 under <ConceptLink targetId="gauss">Carl Friedrich Gauss</ConceptLink> and was later trained alongside <ConceptLink targetId="dirichlet">Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="riemann">Bernhard Riemann</ConceptLink>. After teaching at the Zurich Polytechnic (1858–1862), he was professor at the Technical University of Brunswick for the rest of his life. His work contributed decisively to the arithmetization of analysis and to the structural approach of modern algebra.

### Main Contributions

- **Cuts and real numbers**: In *Stetigkeit und irrationale Zahlen* (1872) he constructed the <ConceptLink targetId="cuerpo">field</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="numero-real">real numbers</ConceptLink> from the <ConceptLink targetId="numero-racional">rationals</ConceptLink>. A <ConceptLink targetId="cortadura-dedekind">cut</ConceptLink> is a <ConceptLink targetId="particion">partition</ConceptLink> of the rationals into two non-empty classes $(A, B)$ such that every element of $A$ is less than every element of $B$; each real number corresponds to a cut. Dedekind took as the essence of the <ConceptLink targetId="continuidad">continuity</ConceptLink> of the <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> the property that every cut of its <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> is produced by exactly one point. In the axiomatics of <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink>, this Dedekind property is not the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-completitud">axiom of completeness</ConceptLink> (Axiom V.2) on its own: in the presence of the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma">axioms</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">order</ConceptLink>, it is equivalent to the conjunction of the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-arquimedes">Archimedean axiom</ConceptLink> (Axiom V.1) and the axiom of completeness.
- **Theory of ideals**: In the supplements to Dirichlet's *Vorlesungen über Zahlentheorie*, which he edited (1871, 1879 and 1894), he introduced <ConceptLink targetId="ideal">ideals</ConceptLink> to recover unique factorization in rings of algebraic <ConceptLink targetId="numero-entero">integers</ConceptLink>. In doing so he shaped notions such as <ConceptLink targetId="cuerpo">field</ConceptLink> (the term *Körper* is his), <ConceptLink targetId="anillo">ring</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="modulo">module</ConceptLink>, and made systematic use of the concept of <ConceptLink targetId="grupo">group</ConceptLink> in Galois theory.
- **Foundation of the natural numbers**: In *Was sind und was sollen die Zahlen?* (1888) he defined an <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-infinito">infinite set</ConceptLink> as one that admits a <ConceptLink targetId="biyeccion">bijection</ConceptLink> with a proper <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto">subset</ConceptLink>, characterized the <ConceptLink targetId="numero-natural">natural numbers</ConceptLink> through the notion of chain, and justified the principle of <ConceptLink targetId="metodo-induccion">mathematical induction</ConceptLink> and definitions by recursion. The axioms that <ConceptLink targetId="peano">Giuseppe Peano</ConceptLink> published in 1889 acknowledge this work as their source.
- **Lattices**: In 1897 and 1900 he studied the structures he called *Dualgruppen*, now <ConceptLink targetId="reticulo">lattices</ConceptLink>, including the modular law, decades before the theory was developed systematically.

### Influence and Legacy

Dedekind showed that continuity can be defined without appealing to geometric intuition. His ideals were the starting point of the algebra of <ConceptLink targetId="noether">Emmy Noether</ConceptLink>, who used to say "it is all already in Dedekind", and his correspondence with <ConceptLink targetId="cantor">Georg Cantor</ConceptLink> accompanied the birth of <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-conjuntos">set theory</ConceptLink>.
