<Capitular letra="R" />ichard Dedekind (1831–1916) was a German mathematician whose foundational work transformed the modern understanding of the real continuum, arithmetic, and abstract algebraic structures.

<Separador />

### Main Contributions

- **Dedekind Cuts**: In *Stetigkeit und irrationale Zahlen* (Continuity and Irrational Numbers, 1872), he constructed the real number continuum $\mathbb{R}$ purely from the rational numbers $\mathbb{Q}$ using ordered set partitions $(A, B)$, giving a precise definition to the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-completitud">axiom of completeness</ConceptLink>.
- **Theory of Ideals**: Introduced the foundational concept of an *ideal* in commutative rings to restore unique prime factorization in algebraic number fields, effectively founding commutative algebra and modern algebraic number theory.
- **Foundations of Natural Numbers**: In *Was sind und was sollen die Zahlen?* (What are numbers and what should they be?, 1888), he provided the first rigorous set-theoretic definition of infinite sets and the logic of mathematical induction, anticipating Peano's axioms.
- **Lattice and Module Theory**: Pioneered the structural study of modules, algebraic lattices (*Dualgruppen*), and abstract homomorphisms.

<Separador />

### Influence and Legacy

Dedekind freed mathematical analysis from its historical reliance on spatial intuition, completing the *arithmetization of analysis*. His conceptual and structural methodology directly influenced Emmy Noether, David Hilbert, and the twentieth-century Bourbaki group.
