<Capitular letra="D" />avid Hilbert (1862–1943) was an eminent German mathematician, professor at the University of Göttingen, and the preeminent mathematical leader at the dawn of the twentieth century. His structural perspective refounded Euclidean geometry upon pure axiomatic grounds and drove the formalist program.

<Separador />

### Main Contributions

- **Foundations of Geometry (*Grundlagen der Geometrie*, 1899)**: Established a complete and rigorous modern axiomatization of Euclidean geometry comprising 20 axioms organized into five independent groups:
  1. Incidence axioms (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-1">I-1</ConceptLink> through <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-8">I-8</ConceptLink>)
  2. Order axioms (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-1">II-1</ConceptLink> through <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-4">II-4</ConceptLink>)
  3. Congruence axioms (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-1">III-1</ConceptLink> through <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-5">III-5</ConceptLink>)
  4. Parallel axiom (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">IV</ConceptLink>)
  5. Continuity axioms (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-arquimedes">V-1</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-completitud">V-2</ConceptLink>)
- **Hilbert Spaces and Functional Analysis**: Developed the theory of infinite-dimensional inner product vector spaces, providing the indispensable mathematical language for quantum mechanics.
- **Invariant Theory and Hilbert's Basis Theorem**: Solved Gordan's problem using revolutionary non-constructive existence proofs.
- **Hilbert's 23 Problems (1900)**: Research agenda presented at the International Congress of Mathematicians in Paris that charted the trajectory of twentieth-century mathematical discovery.

<Separador />

### Influence and Legacy

Hilbert consecrated the principle that mathematical entities are defined entirely by the relational syntax of their governing axioms («*one must be able to say at all times—instead of points, straight lines, and planes—tables, chairs, and beer mugs*»). Under his stewardship, Göttingen became the global capital of mathematical research.
