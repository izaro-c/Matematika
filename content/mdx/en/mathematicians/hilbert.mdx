<Capitular letra="D" />avid Hilbert (1862–1943) was a German mathematician born in Königsberg, where he studied and taught until 1895, when he obtained a chair at Göttingen. He remained there until his retirement in 1930 and made the university the leading mathematical centre of his time. His work spans invariant theory, number theory, the foundations of geometry, analysis and logic.

<Separador />

### Main Contributions

- **Foundations of geometry**: In the <ConceptLink targetId="fundamentos-geometria-hilbert">*Grundlagen der Geometrie*</ConceptLink> (1899) he gave a complete <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-hilbertiana">axiomatization</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-euclidiano">Euclidean geometry</ConceptLink> with three kinds of objects (<ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="plano">planes</ConceptLink>) and undefined relations of <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">order</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink>. In the stable form of the 7th edition (1930), which this encyclopedia follows, it consists of 20 axioms in five groups:
  1. Incidence (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-1">Axiom I.1</ConceptLink> to <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-8">Axiom I.8</ConceptLink>).
  2. Order (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-1">Axiom II.1</ConceptLink> to <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-4">Axiom II.4</ConceptLink>).
  3. Congruence (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-1">Axiom III.1</ConceptLink> to <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-5">Axiom III.5</ConceptLink>).
  4. Parallels (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">Axiom IV</ConceptLink>).
  5. Continuity (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-arquimedes">Axiom V.1</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-completitud">Axiom V.2</ConceptLink>).

  The first edition ordered the groups differently (the parallels came before congruence), did not yet contain the axiom of completeness, added in the French translation of 1900 and in the second edition (1903), and included a redundant axiom of order, as <ConceptLink targetId="moore">E. H. Moore</ConceptLink> showed in 1902. Hilbert also studied the <ConceptLink targetId="consistencia-logica">consistency</ConceptLink> and independence of the axioms by means of <ConceptLink targetId="modelo-cartesiano">arithmetical models</ConceptLink>.
- **Invariant theory**: With the basis theorem (1888–1890) he solved the finiteness problem of <ConceptLink targetId="gordan">Paul Gordan</ConceptLink> by means of a non-constructive existence proof; in 1893 he proved the zero theorem (*Nullstellensatz*).
- **Number theory**: His *Zahlbericht* (1897) systematized the theory of algebraic number fields.
- **Integral equations and analysis**: Between 1904 and 1910 he studied integral equations and quadratic forms in infinitely many variables on the space of square-summable sequences $\ell^2$. The abstract concept of <ConceptLink targetId="espacio-hilbert">Hilbert space</ConceptLink> was formulated later by <ConceptLink targetId="von-neumann">John von Neumann</ConceptLink> (1927–1929), in connection with quantum mechanics.
- **The 23 problems (1900)**: At the International Congress of Mathematicians in Paris he presented a list of open problems that guided much of 20th-century research.
- **Formalist programme**: In the 1920s he proposed to found mathematics by proving with finitary means the consistency of formal systems; the incompleteness theorems of <ConceptLink targetId="godel">Kurt Gödel</ConceptLink> (1931) showed the limits of that programme.

### Influence and Legacy

Hilbert held that the primitive terms of an axiomatic theory are characterized only by the axioms. According to the testimony of <ConceptLink targetId="blumenthal">Otto Blumenthal</ConceptLink>, as early as 1891 he said that one must always be able to say "tables, chairs and beer mugs" instead of "points, lines and planes". This point of view, inherited from <ConceptLink targetId="pasch">Moritz Pasch</ConceptLink>, became the standard of the modern <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-axiomatico">axiomatic method</ConceptLink>.

### Notable Works

- <ConceptLink targetId="fundamentos-geometria-hilbert">*Grundlagen der Geometrie*</ConceptLink> (1899; 7th ed. 1930) — Axiomatization of Euclidean geometry.
- *Die Theorie der algebraischen Zahlkörper* (*Zahlbericht*, 1897) — Synthesis of algebraic number theory.
- *Grundzüge einer allgemeinen Theorie der linearen Integralgleichungen* (1912) — Collection of his papers on integral equations.
- *Grundlagen der Mathematik* (1934–1939), with <ConceptLink targetId="bernays">Paul Bernays</ConceptLink> — Exposition of proof theory.
