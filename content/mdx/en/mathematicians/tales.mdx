<Capitular letra="T" />hales of Miletus (c. 624–546 BC) was a pre-Socratic Greek philosopher, geometer, and astronomer, traditionally acclaimed as the first philosopher in the Western tradition and the originator of deductive mathematics.

<Separador />

### Main Contributions

- **<ConceptLink targetId="teorema-tales">Thales' Theorem on Intercepts and Similarity</ConceptLink>**: Proved that if two intersecting lines are cut by a family of <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallel lines</ConceptLink>, the corresponding <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segments</ConceptLink> marked off on one line are strictly proportional to those on the other, establishing the geometric theory of <ConceptLink targetId="semejanza">similarity</ConceptLink>.
- **Theorem on Inscribed Angles in a Semicircle**: Demonstrated that any angle subtended by a diameter inside a semicircle is a right angle ($90^\circ$).
- **Fundamental Properties of Triangles and Circles**:
  - A <ConceptLink targetId="circunferencia">circle</ConceptLink> is bisected into two equal halves by any of its diameters.
  - The base angles of any <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-triangulo-isosceles">isosceles triangle</ConceptLink> are equal.
  - <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-angulos-opuestos-verticales">Vertically opposite angles</ConceptLink> formed by intersecting lines are congruent.
- **Applied Geometry**: Used solar shadow proportions to calculate the height of the Egyptian pyramids and estimate the distances of ships from the shore.

<Separador />

### Influence and Legacy

Thales initiated the historical transition from myth to *logos*, replacing purely empirical rules with logical deductive arguments grounded in universal geometric principles. His legacy served as the direct foundation for Pythagoras and <ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid</ConceptLink>.
