<Capitular letra="K" />arl Menger (1902–1985) was an Austrian mathematician born in Vienna, son of the economist Carl Menger. He obtained his doctorate at the University of Vienna in 1924 under <ConceptLink targetId="hahn">Hans Hahn</ConceptLink>, was professor at that university and a member of the Vienna Circle. In 1937 he emigrated to the United States, where he taught at the University of Notre Dame and, from 1948, at the Illinois Institute of Technology in Chicago, the city where he died.

<Separador />

### Main Contributions

- **Dimension theory**: Starting from a course by Hahn on the concept of curve (1921), he formulated an inductive definition of <ConceptLink targetId="dimension">dimension</ConceptLink>: a space has dimension at most $n$ if every point has arbitrarily small neighbourhoods whose <ConceptLink targetId="frontera">boundary</ConceptLink> has dimension at most $n - 1$. <ConceptLink targetId="urysohn">Pavel Urysohn</ConceptLink> reached the same idea independently; Menger was unaware of his work. His book *Dimensionstheorie* (1928) was the first monograph on the subject.
- **Curve theory and Menger sponge**: In *Kurventheorie* (1932) he studied curves as one-dimensional continua. The Menger sponge (1926), obtained by successively removing subcubes from a <ConceptLink targetId="cubo">cube</ConceptLink>, is a universal curve: it contains a homeomorphic copy of every compact curve.
- **Menger's theorem (1927)**: In a <ConceptLink targetId="grafo">graph</ConceptLink>, the maximum number of <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-disjunto">disjoint</ConceptLink> paths between two sets of <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertices</ConceptLink> equals the minimum number of vertices separating them. It is a central result on connectivity.
- **Metric geometry**: He characterized the <ConceptLink targetId="espacio-metrico">metric spaces</ConceptLink> that can be isometrically embedded in a <ConceptLink targetId="espacio-euclidiano">Euclidean space</ConceptLink>, introduced a metric notion of convexity, and in 1942 initiated the study of probabilistic metric spaces.

<Separador />

### Influence and Legacy

In 1928 he founded the Mathematical Colloquium in Vienna, in which <ConceptLink targetId="godel">Kurt Gödel</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="wald">Abraham Wald</ConceptLink>, among others, took part, and whose proceedings were published until 1937. His approach to dimension, extended by <ConceptLink targetId="hurewicz">Witold Hurewicz</ConceptLink>, forms the basis of classical dimension theory. He also wrote on <ConceptLink targetId="logica">logic</ConceptLink>, ethics and mathematical economics.

### Notable Works

- *Dimensionstheorie* (1928) — First monograph on dimension theory.
- *Kurventheorie* (1932) — Curve theory, with Menger's theorem.
- *Moral, Wille und Weltgestaltung* (1934) — Essay on the logic of norms.
