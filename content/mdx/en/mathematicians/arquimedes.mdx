<Capitular letra="A" />rchimedes of Syracuse (c. 287 BC – 212 BC) was a Greek mathematician, physicist and engineer, regarded as the greatest geometer of the Hellenistic age. He combined the geometric deduction of the tradition of <ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid</ConceptLink> with mechanical methods of discovery and anticipated ideas of the <ConceptLink targetId="calculo-infinitesimal">infinitesimal calculus</ConceptLink> almost two millennia before their formalization. He died during the Roman sack of Syracuse in 212 BC.

<Separador />

### Main Contributions

- **The Archimedean axiom**: The principle that, given two non-zero homogeneous magnitudes (<ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segments</ConceptLink>, areas or volumes), some integer multiple of the smaller exceeds the larger already appears in the <ConceptLink targetId="elementos-euclides">*Elements*</ConceptLink> (Book V, Definition 4) and goes back to the theory of proportions of <ConceptLink targetId="eudoxo">Eudoxus</ConceptLink>. Archimedes states it as an explicit assumption in *On the Sphere and Cylinder* I and uses it systematically; hence the modern name <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-arquimedes">Archimedean axiom</ConceptLink>, the first axiom of the <ConceptLink targetId="continuidad">continuity</ConceptLink> group of <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink> (Axiom V.1).
- **Method of exhaustion and curvilinear areas**: He perfected the <ConceptLink targetId="metodo-exhaucion">method of exhaustion</ConceptLink> to determine <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-medida-area">areas</ConceptLink> and volumes of figures bounded by curves and surfaces. In *Quadrature of the Parabola* he proved, by summing a geometric series, that a parabolic segment has four thirds of the area of the inscribed <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink> with the same base and height. In *On the Sphere and Cylinder* he proved that the surface of the sphere equals four great circles and that its volume is two thirds of the circumscribed cylinder.
- **The circle and the number $\pi$**: In *Measurement of a Circle* he proved that the area of a circle equals that of a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo-rectangulo">right triangle</ConceptLink> whose <ConceptLink targetId="cateto">legs</ConceptLink> are the <ConceptLink targetId="radio">radius</ConceptLink> and the length of the <ConceptLink targetId="circunferencia">circumference</ConceptLink>. Using inscribed and circumscribed regular <ConceptLink targetId="poligono">polygons</ConceptLink> of 96 sides he obtained the bounds $3 + \frac{10}{71} < \pi < 3 + \frac{1}{7}$ for the ratio of the circumference to its <ConceptLink targetId="diametro">diameter</ConceptLink>.
- **Statics and hydrostatics**: In *On the Equilibrium of Planes* he deduced the law of the lever from postulates on centres of gravity; in *On Floating Bodies* he formulated the principle of buoyancy that bears his name.

### Influence and Legacy

His proofs by exhaustion, which conclude with a double <ConceptLink targetId="metodo-contradiccion">reductio ad absurdum</ConceptLink>, were the model of rigour for the founders of modern science, among them <ConceptLink targetId="galileo">Galileo Galilei</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="kepler">Johannes Kepler</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="newton">Isaac Newton</ConceptLink>. The identification in 1906, by J. L. Heiberg, of the *Archimedes Palimpsest*, which contains *The Method of Mechanical Theorems*, showed that he discovered his results by balancing infinitely thin slices on an ideal lever before giving the geometric <ConceptLink targetId="demostracion">proof</ConceptLink> by exhaustion. According to Cicero, a sphere inscribed in a cylinder was carved on his tomb.

### Notable Works

- *On the Sphere and Cylinder* — Explicit statement of the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-arquimedes">Archimedean axiom</ConceptLink>; surface and volume of the sphere.
- *Measurement of a Circle* — Area of the circle and bounds for $\pi$.
- *Quadrature of the Parabola* — Area of the parabolic segment by exhaustion.
- *On the Equilibrium of Planes* and *On Floating Bodies* — Foundations of statics and hydrostatics.
- *The Method of Mechanical Theorems* — Account of the heuristic method of discovery.
