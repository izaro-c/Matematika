<Capitular letra="A" />rchimedes of Syracuse (c. 287 BC – 212 BC) was a Greek mathematician, physicist and engineer, the pinnacle of Hellenistic geometry. He combined the geometric deduction of the tradition of <ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid</ConceptLink> with mechanical methods of discovery and anticipated ideas of the <ConceptLink targetId="calculo-infinitesimal">infinitesimal calculus</ConceptLink> almost two millennia before their modern formulation. He died during the Roman sack of Syracuse.

<Separador />

### Main Contributions

- **The Archimedean axiom**: States that, given two non-zero homogeneous magnitudes (<ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segments</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-medida-area">areas</ConceptLink> or volumes), some integer multiple of the smaller exceeds the larger. It appears in the <ConceptLink targetId="elementos-euclides">*Elements*</ConceptLink> stemming from <ConceptLink targetId="eudoxo">Eudoxus</ConceptLink>. Archimedes posited it in *On the Sphere and Cylinder*; today it forms the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-arquimedes">Archimedean axiom</ConceptLink>, the foundation of <ConceptLink targetId="continuidad">continuity</ConceptLink> in the system of <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink>.
- **Method of exhaustion and curvilinear areas**: He perfected the <ConceptLink targetId="metodo-exhaucion">method of exhaustion</ConceptLink> to determine areas and volumes of curved figures. In *Quadrature of the Parabola* he proved that a parabolic segment measures four thirds of the inscribed <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink> with equal base and height. In *On the Sphere and Cylinder* he showed that the sphere's surface equals four great circles and its volume two thirds of the circumscribed cylinder.
- **The circle and the number $\pi$**: In *Measurement of a Circle* he proved that circular area equals that of a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo-rectangulo">right triangle</ConceptLink> whose <ConceptLink targetId="cateto">legs</ConceptLink> are the <ConceptLink targetId="radio">radius</ConceptLink> and the <ConceptLink targetId="longitud">length</ConceptLink> of the <ConceptLink targetId="circunferencia">circumference</ConceptLink>. Using regular <ConceptLink targetId="poligono">polygons</ConceptLink> of 96 <ConceptLink targetId="lado">sides</ConceptLink> he established the bounds $3 + \frac{10}{71} < \pi < 3 + \frac{1}{7}$ with respect to the <ConceptLink targetId="diametro">diameter</ConceptLink>.
- **Statics and hydrostatics**: In *On the Equilibrium of <ConceptLink targetId="plano">Planes</ConceptLink>* he deduced the law of the lever from centres of gravity; in *On Floating Bodies* he formulated the principle of buoyancy.

### Influence and Legacy

His proofs by exhaustion, culminating in a double <ConceptLink targetId="metodo-contradiccion">reductio ad absurdum</ConceptLink>, served as a model for <ConceptLink targetId="galileo">Galileo Galilei</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="kepler">Johannes Kepler</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="newton">Isaac Newton</ConceptLink>. The discovery of the *Archimedes Palimpsest* (containing *The Method of Mechanical <ConceptLink targetId="teorema">Theorems</ConceptLink>*) showed that he found relations by balancing ideal sections before writing the geometric <ConceptLink targetId="demostracion">proof</ConceptLink>. Cicero reported identifying his tomb by a sphere inscribed in a cylinder.
