<Capitular letra="A" />rchimedes of Syracuse (c. 287–212 BC) represents the pinnacle of ancient classical physical and mathematical thought, universally regarded as the greatest geometer of antiquity. His deductive techniques anticipated modern infinitesimal calculus by more than two millennia.

<Separador />

### Main Contributions

- **Method of Exhaustion**: Perfected the technique of bounding areas through inscribed and circumscribed polygons, establishing rigorous bounds for $\pi$ and calculating the exact quadrature of the circle and the parabola.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-arquimedes">Archimedean Axiom</ConceptLink>**: Formalized the foundational geometric continuity principle: given two homogeneous magnitudes, there always exists an integer multiple of the smaller that strictly exceeds the larger.
- **Solid Geometry**: Demonstrated that the volume and surface area of a sphere inscribed inside a cylinder are exactly two-thirds of the cylinder's volume and surface area.
- **Mathematical Physics and Statics**: Established the law of the lever, hydrostatics and the principle of buoyancy (Archimedes' principle), and rigorous centers of gravity.

<Separador />

### Influence and Legacy

Archimedes' exceptional rigor inspired Renaissance and Scientific Revolution luminaries including Galileo Galilei, Johannes Kepler, René Descartes, and Isaac Newton. His integration of mechanical insight with uncompromising deductive geometry laid the foundations of mathematical physics.

<Separador />

### Selected Works

- *On the Sphere and Cylinder* — Two-volume treatise on the geometry and metric properties of spherical surfaces and volumes.
- *Measurement of a Circle* — Proofs establishing the metric properties of circles and bounds on $\pi$.
- *The Method of Mechanical Theorems* — Rediscovered in 1906, revealing his ingenious heuristic methods for finding geometric results.
