<Capitular letra="J" />ean-Victor Poncelet (1788–1867) was a French mathematician and engineer born in Metz and died in Paris. A student of <ConceptLink targetId="monge">Gaspard Monge</ConceptLink> at the École Polytechnique, he served as an engineering officer in the Russian campaign of 1812, was taken prisoner, and during his captivity in Saratov (1813–1814) reconstructed from memory the geometry he knew and developed the ideas of his projective work.

<Separador />

### Main Contributions

- **Projective geometry**: In the *Traité des propriétés projectives des figures* (1822) he studied the properties of figures that are preserved under central projection and section, turning <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-proyectiva">projective geometry</ConceptLink> into an autonomous discipline.
- **Elements at infinity and imaginary elements**: He added points and lines at infinity to <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="plano">planes</ConceptLink>, ensuring that two coplanar lines always intersect, and introduced imaginary elements such as the cyclic points common to all circles.
- **Principle of continuity**: He maintained that properties of a figure are preserved when it varies continuously, even if some elements become imaginary. The principle, fruitful but unproven, was criticized by <ConceptLink targetId="cauchy">Augustin-Louis Cauchy</ConceptLink> for its lack of rigor.
- **Polarity and duality**: He developed the theory of poles and polars with respect to a conic, which allows theorems about points to be transformed into theorems about lines; from this emerged the principle of <ConceptLink targetId="dualidad">duality</ConceptLink>, whose general formulation he disputed with <ConceptLink targetId="gergonne">Joseph Gergonne</ConceptLink>.
- **Poncelet closure theorem**: If there exists an $n$-sided polygon inscribed in one conic and circumscribed about another, there exist infinitely many, one starting from each point of the first.
- **Applied mechanics**: He designed a water wheel with curved vanes (1824) and was a professor of mechanics in Metz and Paris.

<Separador />

### Influence and Legacy

Poncelet conjectured that all ruler-and-compass constructions can be carried out with the straightedge alone if a fixed circle with its center is given; <ConceptLink targetId="steiner">Jakob Steiner</ConceptLink> proved this in 1833 (Poncelet–Steiner theorem). His work opened the development of synthetic projective geometry continued by Steiner and <ConceptLink targetId="staudt">Karl von Staudt</ConceptLink>.
