<Capitular letra="G" />ino Fano (1871–1952) was an Italian mathematician born in Mantua and a leading member of the Italian school of <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-algebraica">algebraic geometry</ConceptLink>. He trained in Turin under <ConceptLink targetId="segre">Corrado Segre</ConceptLink>, continued his studies in Göttingen with <ConceptLink targetId="klein">Felix Klein</ConceptLink> (1893) and, after a professorship in Messina, held from 1901 the chair of projective and descriptive geometry in Turin. The racial laws of 1938 expelled him from the university; he went into exile in Lausanne and was reinstated after the war.

### Main Contributions

- **Postulates of projective geometry and finite geometries (1892)**: In *Sui postulati fondamentali della geometria proiettiva* he studied the <ConceptLink targetId="independencia-logica">independence</ConceptLink> of the postulates of <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-proyectiva">projective geometry</ConceptLink>, treating "points" as arbitrary entities. To show that his postulate that the three <ConceptLink targetId="diagonal">diagonal</ConceptLink> points of a complete quadrangle are not <ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> (today "Fano's axiom") does not follow from the others, he constructed a finite <ConceptLink targetId="espacio-proyectivo">projective space</ConceptLink> of 15 points, $\mathrm{PG}(3, 2)$, in which those diagonal points are collinear. Each <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> of that <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink> is the <ConceptLink targetId="modelo-fano">Fano plane</ConceptLink> $\mathrm{PG}(2, 2)$, with 7 <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> and 7 <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink>, the projective plane over the <ConceptLink targetId="cuerpo-finito">finite field</ConceptLink> $\mathbb{F}_2$. This plane satisfies <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-desargues">Desargues' theorem</ConceptLink>, so it cannot be used to prove that theorem's independence.
- **Fano varieties**: He studied three-dimensional varieties whose sections by general linear spaces are canonical curves; the modern concept of <ConceptLink targetId="variedad-fano">Fano variety</ConceptLink> (smooth projective <ConceptLink targetId="variedad-algebraica">algebraic variety</ConceptLink> with ample anticanonical bundle) generalizes these objects to any <ConceptLink targetId="dimension">dimension</ConceptLink> and is central to birational classification.
- **Continuous groups and Cremona transformations**: He investigated continuous groups of Cremona transformations and the projective varieties invariant under them, as well as line geometry and non-Archimedean geometries.
- **Dissemination of the Erlangen Programme**: While still a student he translated into Italian (1890) the <ConceptLink targetId="programa-erlangen">Erlangen Programme</ConceptLink> of Klein, which characterizes each geometry by its <ConceptLink targetId="grupo">group</ConceptLink> of transformations.

<Separador />

### Influence and Legacy

Fano is regarded as the founder of <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-finita">finite geometries</ConceptLink>, now connected with <ConceptLink targetId="combinatoria">combinatorics</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-codigos">coding theory</ConceptLink>. His abstract treatment of postulates preceded that of <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink>, and his work on three-dimensional varieties remains a reference in algebraic geometry.
