<Capitular letra="G" />ino Fano (1871–1952) was a prominent Italian mathematician and a leading figure in the Italian school of algebraic geometry. He made seminal contributions to the foundations of projective geometry and algebraic differential geometry.

<Separador />

### Major Contributions

- **First finite projective plane (1892)**: Introduced the configuration now known as the <ConceptLink targetId="modelo-fano">Fano plane</ConceptLink> ($\mathrm{PG}(2, \mathbb{F}_2)$), a minimal non-Archimedean projective geometry consisting of exactly 7 points and 7 lines, establishing the independence of Desargues' theorem from continuous order axioms.
- **Fano varieties**: In higher-dimensional algebraic geometry, classified smooth projective varieties whose anticanonical bundle is ample, playing a central role in modern minimal model theory.
- **Translation of the Erlangen Program**: Translated Felix Klein's celebrated 1872 *Erlanger Programm* into Italian in 1890, significantly advancing the transformation group viewpoint in geometry.

<Separador />

### Influence and Legacy

Fano's pioneering work laid the groundwork for finite geometries, modern combinatorics, and algebraic coding theory, while his discoveries in algebraic geometry remain a cornerstone of contemporary birational geometry.
