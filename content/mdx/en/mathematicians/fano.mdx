<Capitular letra="G" />ino Fano (1871–1952) was an Italian mathematician and a member of the Italian school of <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-algebraica">algebraic geometry</ConceptLink>. He made seminal contributions to the foundations of <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-proyectiva">projective geometry</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-diferencial">differential geometry</ConceptLink>.

### Main Contributions

- **First finite projective plane (1892)**: In his memoir *Sui postulati fondamentali della geometria proiettiva*, he introduced the structure now known as the <ConceptLink targetId="modelo-fano">Fano plane</ConceptLink> ($\mathrm{PG}(2, \mathbb{F}_2)$), a minimal <ConceptLink targetId="espacio-proyectivo">projective space</ConceptLink> over the <ConceptLink targetId="cuerpo-finito">finite field</ConceptLink> $\mathbb{F}_2$ consisting of 7 <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> and 7 <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink>. This construction proved the independence of <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-desargues">Desargues' theorem</ConceptLink> from the axioms of <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">order</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="continuidad">continuity</ConceptLink>.
- **Fano varieties**: In algebraic geometry, he characterized <ConceptLink targetId="variedad-fano">Fano varieties</ConceptLink>—smooth projective <ConceptLink targetId="variedad-algebraica">algebraic varieties</ConceptLink> with an ample anticanonical bundle, which play a central role in birational classification and higher-<ConceptLink targetId="dimension">dimensional</ConceptLink> geometry.
- **Translation of the Erlangen Program (1890)**: He translated <ConceptLink targetId="klein">Felix Klein's</ConceptLink> landmark <ConceptLink targetId="programa-erlangen">Erlangen Program</ConceptLink> into Italian, promoting <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-grupos">group theory</ConceptLink> as a unifying framework for geometry.

### Influence and Legacy

Fano's work laid the groundwork for modern <ConceptLink targetId="combinatoria">combinatorics</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-finita">finite geometries</ConceptLink>, and <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-codigos">coding theory</ConceptLink>, while his insights in algebraic geometry remain a cornerstone of the minimal model program.
