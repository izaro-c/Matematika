<Capitular letra="E" />uclid of Alexandria (c. 325–265 BC) was an illustrious Greek mathematician active in Hellenistic Alexandria, universally revered as the «father of geometry». His magnum opus, the *Elements*, established the gold standard of deductive axiomatic reasoning for more than two millennia.

<Separador />

### Main Contributions

- **The *Elements* (13 books)**: Comprehensive synthesis of classical Greek geometric and arithmetic knowledge:
  - Books I–IV: Plane geometry, straightedge and compass constructions, properties of <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangles</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallel lines</ConceptLink>, and the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-pitagoras">Pythagorean theorem</ConceptLink> (I.47).
  - Books V–VI: Eudoxian proportion theory and <ConceptLink targetId="semejanza">similar figures</ConceptLink>, including <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-tales">Thales' theorem</ConceptLink>.
  - Books VII–IX: Elementary number theory, the Euclidean algorithm for $\gcd$, the infinitude of prime numbers, and perfect numbers.
  - Book X: Classification of incommensurable geometric magnitudes.
  - Books XI–XIII: Solid geometry, the method of exhaustion, and the rigorous construction of the five Platonic solids.
- **The Axiomatic Method**: Formulated mathematics deductively from 23 definitions, 5 common notions, and 5 geometric postulates, notably including <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">Euclid's parallel postulate</ConceptLink>.

<Separador />

### Influence and Legacy

The *Elements* is the most published, translated, and scrutinized mathematical work in human history second only to the Bible. The two-thousand-year quest to prove the independence of its fifth postulate gave rise to non-Euclidean geometries in the nineteenth century, culminating in David Hilbert's modern formalization in his 1899 *Foundations of Geometry*.
