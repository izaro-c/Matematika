<Capitular letra="J" />ános Bolyai (1802–1860) was a Hungarian mathematician born in Kolozsvár (now Cluj-Napoca) who died in Marosvásárhely (now Târgu Mureș), in Transylvania. Son of the mathematician <ConceptLink targetId="farkas-bolyai">Farkas Bolyai</ConceptLink>, he studied at the Military Engineering Academy in Vienna (1818–1822) and served as an officer in the Austrian army until 1833. He developed <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-hiperbolico">hyperbolic geometry</ConceptLink> independently of <ConceptLink targetId="lobachevski">Nikolai Lobachevsky</ConceptLink> and of <ConceptLink targetId="gauss">Carl Friedrich Gauss</ConceptLink>, who never published his ideas on the subject.

<Separador />

### Main Contributions

- **Hyperbolic geometry**: Around 1823 he gave up trying to deduce the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">parallel postulate</ConceptLink> and developed the consequences of its negation, in which through a <ConceptLink targetId="punto">point</ConceptLink> not on a <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> there pass at least two lines that do not meet it (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-hiperbolico">hyperbolic parallel axiom</ConceptLink>). He found no contradiction, but he did not prove the <ConceptLink targetId="consistencia-logica">consistency</ConceptLink> of the system: relative consistency was obtained later through the models of <ConceptLink targetId="beltrami">Eugenio Beltrami</ConceptLink> (1868), <ConceptLink targetId="klein">Felix Klein</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="poincare">Henri Poincaré</ConceptLink>.
- **Absolute geometry**: In the *Appendix* he separated the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema">theorems</ConceptLink> that do not depend on the parallel axiom, valid in both <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-euclidiano">Euclidean geometry</ConceptLink> and hyperbolic geometry; this common part is <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>.
- **Hyperbolic trigonometry and quadrature**: He obtained the formulas of hyperbolic trigonometry, showed that Euclidean geometry holds on the <ConceptLink targetId="horosfera">horosphere</ConceptLink>, and showed that in the hyperbolic plane it is possible, for certain <ConceptLink targetId="circunferencia">circles</ConceptLink>, to construct with straightedge and compass a <ConceptLink targetId="poligono">polygon</ConceptLink> of equal <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-medida-area">area</ConceptLink>.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="numero-complejo">Complex numbers</ConceptLink>**: In 1837 he submitted, for a prize competition of the Jablonowski Society of Leipzig, a theory of complex numbers as ordered pairs of <ConceptLink targetId="numero-real">real numbers</ConceptLink>, analogous to the one <ConceptLink targetId="hamilton">William Rowan Hamilton</ConceptLink> published that same year.

### Influence and Legacy

On 3 November 1823 he wrote to his father: "out of nothing I have created a strange new world". Gauss's reply to the *Appendix* (1832), in which he claimed to have reached similar results earlier, discouraged Bolyai, who never published again. His work was only recognized after his death, when the models of Beltrami and Klein showed that hyperbolic geometry is as consistent as Euclidean geometry and that the parallel postulate is independent of the other <ConceptLink targetId="axioma">axioms</ConceptLink>.
