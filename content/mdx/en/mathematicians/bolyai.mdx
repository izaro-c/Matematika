<Capitular letra="J" />ános Bolyai (1802–1860) was a Hungarian mathematician and military engineer who independently and simultaneously with Nikolai Lobachevsky conceived the logical foundations of hyperbolic non-Euclidean geometry.

<Separador />

### Main Contributions

- **Discovery of Hyperbolic Geometry**: Around 1823, Bolyai demonstrated that replacing <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">Euclid's parallel postulate</ConceptLink> with its negation yields an entirely consistent and non-contradictory geometric universe where infinitely many coplanar parallel lines pass through a point external to a line.
- **The Absolute Science of Space**: In 1832, he published his seminal 24-page *Appendix* to his father Farkas Bolyai's treatise (*Tentamen*), articulating what is known today as <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>—the collection of geometric theorems holding true independently of any parallel axiom.
- **Hyperbolic Trigonometry**: Formulated exact trigonometric formulas relating sides and interior angles of triangles in hyperbolic space.
- **Geometric Foundations of Complex Numbers**: Developed an early rigorous formalization of complex numbers as ordered pairs of real numbers.

<Separador />

### Influence and Legacy

Announcing his breakthrough to his father with the legendary words *«Out of nothing I have created a strange new universe»*, Bolyai dismantled the two-thousand-year-old dogma that Euclidean geometry was the only mathematically and physically coherent description of space. His vision directly paved the way for Riemannian differential geometry and Einstein's general theory of relativity.
