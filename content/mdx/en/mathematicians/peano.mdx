<Capitular letra="G" />iuseppe Peano (1858–1932) was an Italian mathematician and logician born in Spinetta, near Cuneo, and died in Turin, at whose university he studied and taught throughout his life. He worked in analysis, <ConceptLink targetId="logica">logic</ConceptLink>, foundations of arithmetic and geometry, and promoted a precise symbolic notation for all of mathematics.

<Separador />

### Main Contributions

- **Axioms of natural numbers**: In *Arithmetices principia, nova methodo exposita* (1889) he gave, in symbolic language, the <ConceptLink targetId="axiomas-peano">Peano axioms</ConceptLink>, which characterize the <ConceptLink targetId="numero-natural">natural numbers</ConceptLink> from zero, the successor operation, and the principle of <ConceptLink targetId="metodo-induccion">induction</ConceptLink>. Peano acknowledged the work of <ConceptLink targetId="dedekind">Richard Dedekind</ConceptLink> (1888) as a source.
- **Foundations of geometry**: In *I principii di geometria logicamente esposti* (1889) he axiomatized the geometry of position using two <ConceptLink targetId="concepto-primitivo">primitive</ConceptLink> notions, <ConceptLink targetId="punto">point</ConceptLink> and the relation <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">betweenness</ConceptLink>, developing the ideas of <ConceptLink targetId="pasch">Moritz Pasch</ConceptLink>; in 1894 he added motion as a primitive to treat <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink>. His school, particularly <ConceptLink targetId="pieri">Mario Pieri</ConceptLink>, continued this direction prior to <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink>'s <ConceptLink targetId="fundamentos-geometria-hilbert">*Foundations of Geometry*</ConceptLink>.
- **Vector spaces**: In *Calcolo geometrico* (1888) he gave the first axiomatic definition of a <ConceptLink targetId="numero-real">real</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="espacio-vectorial">vector space</ConceptLink>.
- **Analysis**: He proved an existence theorem for solutions of ordinary differential equations (1886, 1890) and constructed in 1890 a continuous curve that fills all points of a <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrado">square</ConceptLink> (the Peano curve), demonstrating that <ConceptLink targetId="dimension">dimension</ConceptLink> is not preserved under continuous surjective maps.
- **Logical symbolism**: He introduced the symbols $\in$ for membership and $\exists$ for the existential quantifier, as well as notation for union and intersection of <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto">sets</ConceptLink> that passed into modern logic.

<Separador />

### Influence and Legacy

Peano directed the *Formulario mathematico* (1895–1908), an attempt to express all known mathematics in symbolic notation. His logic directly influenced <ConceptLink targetId="russell">Bertrand Russell</ConceptLink>, who met him at the 1900 Paris congress. In his later years he devoted himself to the auxiliary international language *Latino sine flexione*.
