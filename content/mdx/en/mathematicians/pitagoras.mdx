<Capitular letra="P" />ythagoras of Samos (c. 570–495 BC) was an Ionian philosopher, geometer, and mystic who founded the influential Pythagorean brotherhood in Croton (Magna Graecia). His school pioneered the pursuit of mathematics as a pure, abstract deductive discipline, declaring that number is the fundamental reality of the cosmos.

<Separador />

### Main Contributions

- **<ConceptLink targetId="teorema-pitagoras">Pythagorean Theorem</ConceptLink>**: Elevated an empirical rule known to ancient Babylonians and Egyptians into a universal deductive theorem: in any <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo-rectangulo">right-angled triangle</ConceptLink>, the area of the square built upon the hypotenuse equals the sum of the areas of the squares on the two legs ($a^2 + b^2 = c^2$).
- **Discovery of Incommensurable Magnitudes**: Analyzing the diagonal of a unit <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrado">square</ConceptLink> revealed that $\sqrt{2}$ cannot be expressed as a ratio of integers, leading to the momentous discovery of irrational numbers.
- **Harmonics and Music Theory**: Discovered that musical consonances correspond to simple whole-number ratios of string lengths: the octave ($2:1$), the fifth ($3:2$), and the fourth ($4:3$).
- **Figurate Numbers**: Investigated triangular, square, and pentagonal numbers, interweaving arithmetic with plane geometry.

<Separador />

### Influence and Legacy

The Pythagorean maxim *«All is number»* laid the foundation for Western scientific and mathematical philosophy. The Pythagorean tradition deeply influenced Plato's dialogues, Kepler's celestial mechanics, Galileo's physics, and the axiomatic structure of <ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid's</ConceptLink> *Elements*.
