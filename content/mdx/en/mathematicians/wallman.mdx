<Capitular letra="H" />enry Wallman (1915–1992) was an American mathematician and electronic engineer. He obtained his doctorate at Princeton University in 1937 under <ConceptLink targetId="lefschetz">Solomon Lefschetz</ConceptLink> and then worked at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, where during the Second World War he took part in the Radiation Laboratory. In 1948 he moved to Chalmers University of Technology in Gothenburg (Sweden), where he was professor of electrical engineering until his retirement.

<Separador />

### Main Contributions

- **Dimension theory**: With <ConceptLink targetId="hurewicz">Witold Hurewicz</ConceptLink> he wrote *Dimension Theory* (1941), a systematic account of the <ConceptLink targetId="dimension">dimension</ConceptLink> of separable <ConceptLink targetId="espacio-metrico">metric spaces</ConceptLink> that brings together the inductive definition of <ConceptLink targetId="menger">Karl Menger</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="urysohn">Pavel Urysohn</ConceptLink> and the covering dimension of <ConceptLink targetId="lebesgue">Henri Lebesgue</ConceptLink>, and proves their equivalence.
- **Lattices and topology**: In "Lattices and topological spaces" (*Annals of Mathematics*, 1938) he constructed, from the <ConceptLink targetId="reticulo">lattice</ConceptLink> of closed sets of a space, the compactification that bears his name; after <ConceptLink targetId="stone">Marshall Stone</ConceptLink>, he was one of the first to apply lattice theory to <ConceptLink targetId="topologia">topology</ConceptLink>.
- **Electronics**: He worked on the design of amplifiers and electronic circuits, a field to which he devoted the second part of his career.

<Separador />

### Influence and Legacy

*Dimension Theory* was for decades the reference text of classical dimension theory, and the Wallman compactification remains a standard construction of general topology.

- "Lattices and topological spaces" (1938) — Wallman compactification.
- *Vacuum Tube Amplifiers* (1948), with George E. Valley.
