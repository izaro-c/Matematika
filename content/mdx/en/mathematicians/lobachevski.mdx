<Capitular letra="N" />ikolai Ivanovich Lobachevsky (1792–1856) was a Russian mathematician and long-time rector of Kazan University, hailed by history as the «Copernicus of Geometry» for being the first to publish a comprehensive and systematic formulation of non-Euclidean geometry.

<Separador />

### Main Contributions

- **Foundation of Hyperbolic Geometry**: In February 1826, Lobachevsky presented to the Kazan department and subsequently published in 1829 his treatise *On the Principles of Geometry*, replacing <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">Euclid's parallel postulate</ConceptLink> with the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-hiperbolico">hyperbolic parallel axiom</ConceptLink>: through a point outside a given line, at least two coplanar lines pass that never intersect it.
- **Imaginary Geometry (*Pangeometry*, 1855)**: Rigorously developed hyperbolic trigonometry, the geometry of horospheres (surfaces intrinsically isometric to the Euclidean plane), and hyperbolic area integration.
- **Deductive Consistency**: Established that his non-Euclidean system was internally consistent and free from contradiction, demonstrating that Euclidean geometry is not a necessary synthetic truth a priori of human intuition, but one coherent physical possibility among others.
- **Numerical Analysis**: Independently developed the root-squaring method (Dandelin-Gräffe method) for computing numerical roots of polynomials.

<Separador />

### Influence and Legacy

Lobachevsky's courage revolutionized mathematical epistemology and the philosophy of science. His work liberated geometry from the Kantian doctrine of immutable Euclidean space, laying the conceptual groundwork for differential geometry and Einstein's spacetime mechanics.
