### Phase 2: Constructing Figures and Congruence

Connecting three non-collinear points with straight segments creates the primary planar figure: the <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink>. Its first major deductive application is the isosceles triangle theorem.

### Phase 3: Parallelism and Angle Sum

The Pythagorean theorem is strictly a theorem of plane Euclidean geometry. This is because it depends directly on <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">Euclid's fifth postulate</ConceptLink>. Without this axiom, it is impossible to prove that the interior angles of a triangle sum to exactly $180^\circ$.
