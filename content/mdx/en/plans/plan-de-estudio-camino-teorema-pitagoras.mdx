### Phase 1: The Foundational Building Blocks

### Phase 2: Figures and Congruence

Three non-<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> points joined by <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segments</ConceptLink> form a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink>. To compare triangles without appealing to intuition, we use the <ConceptLink targetId="axiomas-congruencia">axioms of congruence</ConceptLink>, in particular <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-5">Axiom III.5</ConceptLink>, which gives the basic form of the side-angle-side (SAS) criterion. Its first application is the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-triangulo-isosceles">isosceles triangle theorem</ConceptLink>.

### Phase 3: Parallelism and the Angle Sum

The Pythagorean theorem is a Euclidean theorem: it depends on the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">parallel axiom</ConceptLink> (Axiom IV, heir to <ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid</ConceptLink>'s fifth postulate) and is false in the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-hiperbolico">hyperbolic system</ConceptLink>. In the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute system</ConceptLink>, without that axiom, one cannot prove that the sum of the <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-interior">interior angles</ConceptLink> of a triangle is congruent to two <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto">right angles</ConceptLink> ($180^\circ$).

### Phase 4: Proportionality and Similarity

In the Euclidean system there are triangles with the same angles and different sizes, and comparing them requires the <ConceptLink targetId="proporcionalidad">proportionality</ConceptLink> of segments. <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-tales">Thales' theorem</ConceptLink> is the foundation of the theory of <ConceptLink targetId="semejanza">similarity</ConceptLink>: a line <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallel</ConceptLink> to one side of a triangle cuts the other two sides into proportional segments. It is proved from the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-triangulo">area of the triangle</ConceptLink>.
