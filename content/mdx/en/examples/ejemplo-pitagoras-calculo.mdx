We calculate the exact length of the hypotenuse <VisualBind element="segAB" color="mora">$c = |\overline{AB}|$</VisualBind> and examine the arithmetic of the resulting Pythagorean triple.

### Problem Statement

Determine the exact length of hypotenuse $c$ using the fundamental metric theorem and verify the additive areas of the projected squares.

<Nota>
  A Pythagorean triple is a set of three positive integers $(a, b, c)$ satisfying the Diophantine equation $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$. When $\gcd(a, b, c) = 1$, the triple is primitive.
</Nota>

<Separador />

<Solucion label="Step-by-Step Resolution">
  <Paso numero={1} id="datos" titulo="Initial Data and Hypotheses">
    The known dimensions of the perpendicular legs are given as:
    
    - <VisualBind element="segCA" color="canela">Vertical leg $a$</VisualBind>: $a = 5$
    - <VisualBind element="segBC" color="terracota">Horizontal leg $b$</VisualBind>: $b = 12$
    - <VisualBind element="rightAng" color="carbon">Right angle</VisualBind> at vertex $C$: $\angle ACB = 90^\circ$
    
    The length of hypotenuse $c$ represents the unknown to be calculated, being the segment opposite to vertex $C$.
  </Paso>

  <Paso numero={2} id="calculo" titulo="Application of the Pythagorean Theorem">
    By the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-pitagoras" isDependency={true}>Pythagorean theorem</ConceptLink>, the fundamental metric relation in a right triangle is:
    
    <Formula>
      $$ a^2 + b^2 = c^2 $$
    </Formula>
    
    Substituting the known values:
    
    <Formula>
      $$ 5^2 + 12^2 = c^2 $$
    </Formula>
    
    Geometrically, this equality expresses the additivity of the areas of the squares on the sides: the <VisualBind element="sqCA" color="canela">square of area $a^2 = 25$</VisualBind> plus the <VisualBind element="sqBC" color="terracota">square of area $b^2 = 144$</VisualBind> equals the <VisualBind element="sqAB" color="mora">square of area $c^2$</VisualBind>:
    
    <Formula>
      $$ 25 + 144 = 169 $$
    </Formula>
  </Paso>

  <Paso numero={3} id="resolucion" titulo="Algebraic Resolution of the Hypotenuse">
    Equating the sum of areas with the square of the unknown:
    
    <Formula>
      $$ c^2 = 169 $$
    </Formula>
    
    Taking the positive principal square root on both sides (since a geometric distance satisfies $c > 0$):
    
    <Formula>
      $$ c = \sqrt{169} = 13 $$
    </Formula>
    
    Consequently, the length of the <VisualBind element="segAB" color="mora">hypotenuse $c$</VisualBind> is exactly $13$ units.
  </Paso>

  <Paso numero={4} id="terna" titulo="Verification of the Pythagorean Triple">
    The lengths $(5, 12, 13)$ are all positive integers satisfying the Pythagorean relation:
    
    <Formula>
      $$ 5^2 + 12^2 = 25 + 144 = 169 = 13^2 $$
    </Formula>
    
    Because $\gcd(5, 12, 13) = 1$, $(5, 12, 13)$ is a primitive Pythagorean triple.
  </Paso>
</Solucion>
