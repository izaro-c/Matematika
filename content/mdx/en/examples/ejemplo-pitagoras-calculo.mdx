<Capitular letra="L" />et <VisualBind element="polyABC" color="carbon">$\triangle ABC$</VisualBind> be a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo-rectangulo" isDependency={true}>right triangle</ConceptLink> with its <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto">right angle</ConceptLink> at the <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertex</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="pC" color="carbon">$C$</VisualBind>. The <ConceptLink targetId="longitud">lengths</ConceptLink> of the <ConceptLink targetId="cateto">legs</ConceptLink> are <VisualBind element="segBC" color="terracota">$a = |BC| = 12$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="segCA" color="canela">$b = |CA| = 5$</VisualBind> units. We compute the length of the <ConceptLink targetId="hipotenusa">hypotenuse</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="segAB" color="mora">$c = |AB|$</VisualBind> and check that the three lengths form a <ConceptLink targetId="terna-pitagorica">Pythagorean triple</ConceptLink>.

<Separador />

### Problem Statement

Compute the length $c$ of the hypotenuse using the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-pitagoras">Pythagorean theorem</ConceptLink> and interpret the computation as a relation between the <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-medida-area">areas</ConceptLink> of the <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrado">squares</ConceptLink> built on the sides.

<Nota>
  A <ConceptLink targetId="terna-pitagorica">Pythagorean triple</ConceptLink> is a triple $(a, b, c)$ of positive <ConceptLink targetId="numero-entero">integers</ConceptLink> satisfying the <ConceptLink targetId="ecuacion-diofantica">Diophantine equation</ConceptLink> $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$. If moreover $\gcd(a, b, c) = 1$ (<ConceptLink targetId="maximo-comun-divisor">greatest common divisor</ConceptLink>), the triple is primitive.
</Nota>

<Separador />

<Solucion label="Step-by-Step Resolution">
  <Paso numero={1} id="datos" titulo="Data of the Problem">
    The data of the <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo-rectangulo">right triangle</ConceptLink> are:
    
    - <VisualBind element="segBC" color="terracota">Leg $a$</VisualBind>: $a = |BC| = 12$
    - <VisualBind element="segCA" color="canela">Leg $b$</VisualBind>: $b = |CA| = 5$
    - <VisualBind element="rightAng" color="carbon">Right angle</VisualBind> at vertex $C$: $|\angle ACB| = 90^\circ$ (<ConceptLink targetId="medida-angular">angle measure</ConceptLink>)
    
    The unknown is the length $c = |AB|$ of the <ConceptLink targetId="hipotenusa">hypotenuse</ConceptLink>, the <ConceptLink targetId="lado">side</ConceptLink> opposite the right angle.
  </Paso>

  <Paso numero={2} id="calculo" titulo="Application of the Pythagorean Theorem">
    By the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-pitagoras" isDependency={true}>Pythagorean theorem</ConceptLink>, the right triangle satisfies:
    
    <Formula>
      $$ a^2 + b^2 = c^2 $$
    </Formula>
    
    Substituting the known lengths of the legs:
    
    <Formula>
      $$ 12^2 + 5^2 = c^2 $$
    </Formula>
    
    In terms of <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-medida-area">area</ConceptLink>, the <VisualBind element="sqBC" color="terracota">square of area $a^2 = 144$</VisualBind> and the <VisualBind element="sqCA" color="canela">square of area $b^2 = 25$</VisualBind> together add up to the area of the <VisualBind element="sqAB" color="mora">square of area $c^2$</VisualBind>:
    
    <Formula>
      $$ 144 + 25 = 169 $$
    </Formula>
  </Paso>

  <Paso numero={3} id="resolucion" titulo="Solving for the Hypotenuse">
    The sum of the areas gives the square of the unknown:
    
    <Formula>
      $$ c^2 = 169 $$
    </Formula>
    
    Since $c$ is a length, $c > 0$, and we take the positive <ConceptLink targetId="raiz-cuadrada">square root</ConceptLink>:
    
    <Formula>
      $$ c = \sqrt{169} = 13 $$
    </Formula>
    
    The <VisualBind element="segAB" color="mora">hypotenuse</VisualBind> measures $13$ units.
  </Paso>

  <Paso numero={4} id="terna" titulo="Verification of the Pythagorean Triple">
    The lengths $(a, b, c) = (12, 5, 13)$ are positive integers satisfying the Pythagorean relation:
    
    <Formula>
      $$ 12^2 + 5^2 = 144 + 25 = 169 = 13^2 $$
    </Formula>
    
    Since $\gcd(12, 5, 13) = 1$, the <ConceptLink targetId="terna-pitagorica">Pythagorean triple</ConceptLink> $(5, 12, 13)$ is primitive.
  </Paso>
</Solucion>
