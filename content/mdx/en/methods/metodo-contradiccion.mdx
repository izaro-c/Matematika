<Capitular letra="P" />roof by contradiction, historically known as *reductio ad absurdum*, is an indirect proof technique that establishes the truth of a statement <VisualBind element="pP" color="terracota">$P$</VisualBind> by provisionally assuming its negation $\neg P$ and deriving a formal contradiction of the form <VisualBind element="pAbsurdo" color="granada">$R \land \neg R \equiv \bot$</VisualBind>.

<Separador />

### Logical Foundations and Reductio ad Absurdum Stages

The method relies on the principles of classical <ConceptLink targetId="logica">logic</ConceptLink> (the law of non-contradiction $\neg (R \land \neg R)$ and the law of excluded middle $P \lor \neg P$). For a conditional implication $P \implies Q$, the demonstration unfolds across four canonical stages:

<ProofStep number={1} target={["pP", "pNotQ"]} diagramStep="paso-supuesto" title="Stating Provisional Hypothesis ¬Q">
  Alongside the initial hypothesis <VisualBind element="pP" color="terracota">$P$</VisualBind>, the falsity of the conclusion is provisionally assumed via assumption <VisualBind element="pNotQ" color="canela">$\neg Q$</VisualBind>:
  <Formula>
    $$ P \land \neg Q \text{ (Initial joint premise)} $$
  </Formula>
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={2} target={["pR", "pNotR", "segPR", "segNotQNotR"]} diagramStep="paso-deduccion-dual" title="Deriving Incompatible Consequences">
  From hypothesis <VisualBind element="pP" color="terracota">$P$</VisualBind>, consequence <VisualBind element="pR" color="terracota">$R$</VisualBind> is validly inferred; concurrently, assumption <VisualBind element="pNotQ" color="canela">$\neg Q$</VisualBind> deductively yields <VisualBind element="pNotR" color="canela">$\neg R$</VisualBind>:
  <Formula>
    $$ P \implies R \quad \text{and concurrently} \quad \neg Q \implies \neg R $$
  </Formula>
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={3} target={["pAbsurdo", "segRColision", "segNotRColision"]} diagramStep="paso-colision-absurdo" title="Formal Collision and Contradiction (⊥)">
  Both deduced consequences cannot simultaneously hold in the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-axiomatico">formal system</ConceptLink>. Their conjunction creates an unresolvable logical collision <VisualBind element="pAbsurdo" color="granada">$\bot$</VisualBind>:
  <Formula>
    $$ R \land \neg R \equiv \bot \text{ (Absurdity)} $$
  </Formula>
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={4} target={["pP", "pNotQ", "pAbsurdo"]} diagramStep="paso-conclusion-verdad" title="Rejecting Assumption ¬Q to Conclude Q">
  Because assuming $\neg Q$ inevitably forces formal absurdity $\bot$, the provisional assumption is rejected and conclusion $Q$ is definitively established:
  <Formula>
    $$ (\neg Q \implies \bot) \iff Q \implies (P \implies Q) $$
  </Formula>
</ProofStep>

### Classical Geometric and Analytic Examples

- **Irrationality of $\sqrt{2}$**: Assuming $\sqrt{2} = a/b$ with $\operatorname{mcd}(a, b) = 1$ leads deductively to the conclusion that both $a$ and $b$ are even, contradicting the irreducible fraction hypothesis.
- **Uniqueness of Perpendiculars**: To prove that at most one <ConceptLink targetId="perpendicular">perpendicular line</ConceptLink> passes through a <ConceptLink targetId="punto">point</ConceptLink> exterior to a given <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink>, one assumes two distinct perpendiculars exist. This would form a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink> with two <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto">right angles</ConceptLink>, contradicting the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-angulo-exterior">exterior angle theorem</ConceptLink> in <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink> or the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-suma-angulos-triangulo">triangle angle sum theorem</ConceptLink> in <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-euclidiano">Euclidean geometry</ConceptLink>.

### Methodological Features

- **Power in Non-Existence and Uniqueness Proofs**: The primary strategy for establishing that certain mathematical configurations or counterexamples cannot exist.
- **Non-Constructive Nature**: It proves that an object exists or a relation holds without necessarily constructing the witness object explicitly.
