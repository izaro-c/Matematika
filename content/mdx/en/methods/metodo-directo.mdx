<Capitular letra="T" />he **direct method** is the form of <ConceptLink targetId="demostracion">proof</ConceptLink> that establishes an <ConceptLink targetId="implicacion">implication</ConceptLink> $P \implies Q$ by deducing the <ConceptLink targetId="tesis">conclusion</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="pQ" color="canela">$Q$</VisualBind> from the <ConceptLink targetId="hipotesis">hypothesis</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="pP" color="terracota">$P$</VisualBind> through a finite sequence of justified steps.

It is the basic deductive pattern: indirect methods, such as <ConceptLink targetId="metodo-contradiccion">proof by contradiction</ConceptLink> or the <ConceptLink targetId="contraposicion">contrapositive</ConceptLink> $\neg Q \implies \neg P$, first transform the statement and then reason directly.

<Separador />

### Logical Structure and Chaining

Direct proof structures step-by-step inference by applying the rule of <ConceptLink targetId="modus-ponens">modus ponens</ConceptLink> ($A \land (A \implies B) \vdash B$):

<ProofStep number={1} target="pP" diagramStep="paso-hipotesis" title="Stating Hypothesis P">
  The hypothesis <VisualBind element="pP" color="terracota">$P$</VisualBind> is established as the starting premise:
  <Formula>
    $$ P \text{ (Initial premise)} $$
  </Formula>
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={2} target={["pP1", "segPP1"]} diagramStep="paso-primera-inferencia" title="First Deduction: P ⇒ P₁">
  Applying an <ConceptLink targetId="axioma">axiom</ConceptLink> or definition via modus ponens, from <VisualBind element="pP" color="terracota">$P$</VisualBind> one deduces proposition <VisualBind element="pP1" color="carbon">$P_1$</VisualBind>:
  <Formula>
    $$ P \implies P_1 $$
  </Formula>
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={3} target={["pP2", "segP1P2"]} diagramStep="paso-segunda-inferencia" title="Chaining Steps: P₁ ⇒ P₂">
  From <VisualBind element="pP1" color="carbon">$P_1$</VisualBind>, using a prior <ConceptLink targetId="teorema">theorem</ConceptLink> or <ConceptLink targetId="lema">lemma</ConceptLink>, one derives <VisualBind element="pP2" color="carbon">$P_2$</VisualBind>:
  <Formula>
    $$ P_1 \implies P_2 \implies \dots \implies P_n $$
  </Formula>
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={4} target={["pQ", "segP2Q"]} diagramStep="paso-conclusion" title="Final Conclusion: P₂ ⇒ Q">
  The deductive chain reaches the conclusion <VisualBind element="pQ" color="canela">$Q$</VisualBind>, completing the direct proof of $P \implies Q$:
  <Formula>
    $$ P \implies P_1 \implies P_2 \implies \dots \implies Q $$
  </Formula>
</ProofStep>

Each intermediate implication $P_k \implies P_{k+1}$ is justified by:
- the hypothesis $P$ or a statement already obtained in the chain;
- an <ConceptLink targetId="axioma">axiom</ConceptLink> of the system;
- a previously proved <ConceptLink targetId="teorema">theorem</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="lema">lemma</ConceptLink> or <ConceptLink targetId="corolario">corollary</ConceptLink>;
- a <ConceptLink targetId="definicion">definition</ConceptLink>;
- a rule of <ConceptLink targetId="inferencia">inference</ConceptLink> of first-order <ConceptLink targetId="logica">logic</ConceptLink>.

### Examples in geometry

- **<ConceptLink targetId="teorema-triangulo-isosceles">Isosceles triangle theorem</ConceptLink>**: from the hypothesis $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{AC}$, applying <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-5">Axiom III.5</ConceptLink> to the <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangles</ConceptLink> $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle ACB$ gives $\angle ABC \cong \angle ACB$.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="teorema-suma-angulos-triangulo">Angle sum of a triangle</ConceptLink>**: through a <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertex</ConceptLink> one draws the <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallel</ConceptLink> to the opposite <ConceptLink targetId="lado">side</ConceptLink>, unique by <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">Axiom IV</ConceptLink>, and congruence of <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-alternos-internos">alternate interior angles</ConceptLink> shows that the three angles sum to two <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto">right angles</ConceptLink>.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="teorema-pitagoras">Pythagorean theorem</ConceptLink>**: in <ConceptLink targetId="demo-pitagoras-euclides">Euclid's proof</ConceptLink>, congruence criteria and comparisons of <ConceptLink targetId="area">areas</ConceptLink> lead from the hypothesis "the angle at $C$ is right" to $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$.

<Separador />

### When to use it

The direct method is suitable when the hypothesis provides data that transform constructively into the conclusion. Each step can be checked in isolation. If the conclusion asserts impossibility or uniqueness, an indirect method is often preferred.
