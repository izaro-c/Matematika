<Capitular letra="T" />he direct method is the fundamental deductive technique for establishing mathematical conditional statements of the form $P \implies Q$. Its procedure formally assumes the truth of the hypothesis $P$ and constructs a finite, rigorous sequence of valid logical inferences that inevitably concludes with the truth of the thesis $Q$.

<Separador />

### Formal Logical Structure

The deductive argument chains propositions via the classical *modus ponens* rule of inference: whenever proposition $A$ holds and $A \implies B$ is valid, proposition $B$ is necessarily true:

<Formula>
  $$ P \implies P_1 \implies P_2 \implies \dots \implies P_n \implies Q $$
</Formula>

Each intermediate link $P_k \implies P_{k+1}$ in the deductive chain is justified solely by:

- Foundational axioms of the deductive system.
- Previously established theorems, lemmas, or corollaries.
- Rigorous formal definitions of the mathematical objects involved.
- Valid transformation rules of propositional and predicate logic.

<Separador />

### Methodological Properties

- **Constructivity**: Provides an explicit, reproducible pathway converting initial assumptions into the target theorem.
- **Local Verifiability**: Every intermediate deduction is logically self-contained and independently auditable.
- **Epistemological Preference**: It represents the primary choice whenever the hypothesis provides explicit structural relationships directly accessible without indirect reduction.
