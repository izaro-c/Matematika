<Capitular letra="T" />he **direct method** is the form of <ConceptLink targetId="demostracion">proof</ConceptLink> that establishes an <ConceptLink targetId="implicacion" isDependency={true}>implication</ConceptLink> $P \implies Q$ by assuming the <ConceptLink targetId="hipotesis">hypothesis</ConceptLink> $P$ and deducing from it the <ConceptLink targetId="tesis">conclusion</ConceptLink> $Q$ through a finite sequence of steps, each of them justified.

It is the basic pattern of every deduction: indirect methods, such as <ConceptLink targetId="metodo-contradiccion">proof by contradiction</ConceptLink> or proof of the <ConceptLink targetId="contraposicion">contrapositive</ConceptLink> $\neg Q \implies \neg P$, first transform the statement and then reason directly.

<Separador />

### Logical structure

Each step applies the rule of <ConceptLink targetId="modus-ponens" isDependency={true}>modus ponens</ConceptLink>: from $A$ and $A \implies B$ one infers $B$. Chaining steps gives

<Formula>
  $$ P \implies P_1 \implies P_2 \implies \dots \implies P_n \implies Q $$
</Formula>

and each implication $P_k \implies P_{k+1}$ is justified by one of these resources:

- the hypothesis $P$ or a statement already obtained in the chain;
- an <ConceptLink targetId="axioma">axiom</ConceptLink> of the system;
- a previously proved <ConceptLink targetId="teorema">theorem</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="lema">lemma</ConceptLink> or <ConceptLink targetId="corolario">corollary</ConceptLink>;
- a <ConceptLink targetId="definicion">definition</ConceptLink>;
- a rule of <ConceptLink targetId="inferencia">inference</ConceptLink> of first-order <ConceptLink targetId="logica">logic</ConceptLink>.

<Separador />

### Examples in geometry

- **<ConceptLink targetId="teorema-triangulo-isosceles">Isosceles triangle theorem</ConceptLink>**: from the hypothesis $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{AC}$ one obtains, by applying <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-5">Axiom III.5</ConceptLink> to the triangles $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle ACB$, that $\angle ABC \cong \angle ACB$.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="teorema-suma-angulos-triangulo">Angle sum of a triangle</ConceptLink>**: through a vertex one draws the <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallel</ConceptLink> to the opposite side, whose uniqueness is guaranteed by <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">Axiom IV</ConceptLink>, and the congruence of <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-alternos-internos">alternate interior angles</ConceptLink> shows that the three angles add up to two <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto">right angles</ConceptLink>.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="teorema-pitagoras">Pythagorean theorem</ConceptLink>**: in <ConceptLink targetId="demo-pitagoras-euclides">Euclid's proof</ConceptLink>, congruence criteria and comparisons of <ConceptLink targetId="area">areas</ConceptLink> lead directly from the hypothesis "the angle at $C$ is right" to the conclusion $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$.

<Separador />

### When to use it

The direct method is suitable when the hypothesis provides data that can be transformed step by step into the conclusion. Each step can be checked in isolation, and when the proof asserts the existence of an object it usually constructs it explicitly. If the conclusion is a statement of impossibility or uniqueness, an indirect method is often more natural.
