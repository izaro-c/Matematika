<Capitular letra="M" />athematical induction is the fundamental deductive proof technique for establishing propositions $P(n)$ whose validity extends over all <ConceptLink targetId="numero-natural">natural numbers</ConceptLink> $\mathbb{N}$. Grounded directly in the deductive framework of <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-peano">Peano's axioms</ConceptLink>, it guarantees universal truth via a base case and an inductive propagation step.

<Separador />

### Formal structure

To verify the universal validity $\forall n \in \mathbb{N}, P(n)$, the proof requires completing two consecutive steps:

1. **Base step**: The statement is shown to be true for the initial element $n_0$ (typically $n_0 = 0$ or $n_0 = 1$):
   $$ P(n_0) \text{ is true} $$
2. **Inductive step**: The truth of the proposition is assumed for an arbitrary integer $k \ge n_0$ (the **<ConceptLink targetId="hipotesis">inductive hypothesis</ConceptLink>** $P(k)$), and it is proven that the truth for the successor $k+1$ necessarily follows (the **<ConceptLink targetId="tesis">inductive thesis</ConceptLink>** $P(k+1)$):
   $$ \forall k \ge n_0, (P(k) \implies P(k+1)) $$

Upon completing both steps, the well-ordering principle of $\mathbb{N}$ implies universal validity:

<Formula>
  $$ (P(n_0) \land \forall k \ge n_0 (P(k) \implies P(k+1))) \implies \forall n \ge n_0 P(n) $$
</Formula>

<Separador />

### Classic examples

- **Sum of the first $n$ natural numbers**: Proving $\sum_{i=1}^n i = \frac{n(n+1)}{2}$. The base step verifies $1 = \frac{1(2)}{2}$, while the inductive step adds $(k+1)$ to both sides of the inductive hypothesis to derive the formula for $k+1$.
- **Number of diagonals of a polygon**: For a <ConceptLink targetId="poligono">polygon</ConceptLink> with $n$ vertices, the number of diagonals is $D(n) = \frac{n(n-3)}{2}$ for all $n \ge 3$.
- **Bernoulli's inequality**: For all $x > -1$ and $n \in \mathbb{N}$, the inequality $(1+x)^n \ge 1 + nx$ holds.

<Separador />

### Variants and properties

- **Strong (complete) induction**: The inductive hypothesis assumes the truth of $P(m)$ for all integers $n_0 \le m \le k$ to deduce $P(k+1)$.
- **Multiple base cases**: Necessary when system recurrences depend on two or more independent initial values (e.g. the Fibonacci sequence).
