<Capitular letra="A" />n **obtuse triangle** is a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangle</ConceptLink> possessing an <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-obtuso">obtuse</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-interior">interior angle</ConceptLink>, that is, strictly greater than a <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto" isDependency={true}>right angle</ConceptLink> under the <ConceptLink targetId="orden-angulos" isDependency={true}>order of angles</ConceptLink>.

The notion belongs to <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>. By the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-angulo-exterior">exterior angle theorem</ConceptLink>, a triangle cannot contain more than one obtuse angle nor combine it with a right angle, so that the other two angles are necessarily <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-agudo">acute</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal definition of obtuse triangle">
Let $\triangle ABC = \{\overline{AB}, \overline{BC}, \overline{CA}\}$ be a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangle</ConceptLink> in a <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> $\pi$, determined by three non-<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> $A$, $B$, and $C$.

It is said that $\triangle ABC$ is an **obtuse triangle** if one of its three <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-interior">interior angles</ConceptLink> is strictly greater than a <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto" isDependency={true}>right angle</ConceptLink> $R$ under the <ConceptLink targetId="orden-angulos" isDependency={true}>order of angles</ConceptLink>:

$$
\angle CAB > R \quad \lor \quad \angle ABC > R \quad \lor \quad \angle BCA > R
$$

The angle satisfying this condition is an <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-obtuso">obtuse angle</ConceptLink>, and the remaining two are acute angles.
</Definicion>

Two of the three <ConceptLink targetId="altura">altitudes</ConceptLink> have their feet on the extensions of the corresponding sides, so that their common point of concurrency lies outside the triangular region.

<Nota>
In <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>, the presence of an obtuse angle determines that the side opposite to that vertex is strictly greater than either of the other two, by virtue of the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-angulo-exterior">exterior angle theorem</ConceptLink> and the monotonic relation with the <ConceptLink targetId="orden-angulos">order of angles</ConceptLink>.
</Nota>
