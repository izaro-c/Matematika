<Capitular letra="A" />ssociativity (or the associative property) is an algebraic property that a <ConceptLink targetId="operacion-binaria" isDependency={true}>binary operation</ConceptLink> may possess, whereby the sequential evaluation of three or more operands remains invariant under the grouping of parentheses. It constitutes the indispensable condition for omitting parentheses when composing multiple operations in <ConceptLink targetId="algebra-abstracta">abstract algebra</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal definition of associativity">
  Let $S$ be a <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto">set</ConceptLink> and let $*\colon S \times S \longrightarrow S$ be a <ConceptLink targetId="operacion-binaria" isDependency={true}>binary operation</ConceptLink> on $S$. The operation $*$ is said to be **associative** if and only if it satisfies the identity:
  $$
  \forall a, b, c \in S, \quad (a * b) * c = a * (b * c)
  $$
  A pair $(S, *)$ consisting of a set and an associative binary operation constitutes a <ConceptLink targetId="semigrupo">semigroup</ConceptLink>.
</Definicion>

<Separador />

### Algebraic structures and counterexamples

Many familiar operations are associative and others are not; associativity is part of the definition of several <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-algebraica">algebraic structures</ConceptLink>:

- **Standard associative operations:** <ConceptLink targetId="adicion">Addition</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="multiplicacion">multiplication</ConceptLink> in the <ConceptLink targetId="numero-entero">integers</ConceptLink> $\mathbb{Z}$ and the <ConceptLink targetId="numero-real">reals</ConceptLink> $\mathbb{R}$; matrix multiplication; and <ConceptLink targetId="composicion-funciones">composition of functions</ConceptLink> from a set to itself.
- **Non-associative operations:** Subtraction in $\mathbb{R}$ is not associative, since $(8 - 4) - 2 = 2 \neq 6 = 8 - (4 - 2)$; analogously, <ConceptLink targetId="division">division</ConceptLink> in $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$ or the cross product in $\mathbb{R}^3$ lack associativity.
- **Algebraic hierarchy:** A semigroup with an <ConceptLink targetId="elemento-neutro">identity element</ConceptLink> constitutes a <ConceptLink targetId="monoide">monoid</ConceptLink>; if every element additionally admits an <ConceptLink targetId="elemento-inverso">inverse</ConceptLink>, it forms a <ConceptLink targetId="grupo">group</ConceptLink>.

    <PropiedadItem id="asociatividad-generalizada" title="Generalized associativity theorem">
      If $*$ is an associative <ConceptLink targetId="operacion-binaria">binary operation</ConceptLink> on $S$, any product of $n$ elements $a_1 * a_2 * \cdots * a_n$ (with $n \ge 3$) takes the same value under every placement of parentheses that keeps the order of the factors, so the parentheses can be omitted.
    </PropiedadItem>
    <PropiedadItem id="unicidad-inversos-asociatividad" title="Uniqueness of inverse elements">
      If $*$ is associative and has an <ConceptLink targetId="elemento-neutro">identity element</ConceptLink> $e$, and an element $a$ admits a left <ConceptLink targetId="elemento-inverso">inverse</ConceptLink> $b$ ($b * a = e$) and a right inverse $c$ ($a * c = e$), the two coincide: $b = b * e = b * (a * c) = (b * a) * c = e * c = c$. In particular, the inverse of $a$, if it exists, is unique.
    </PropiedadItem>
  </PropiedadesGrupo>
