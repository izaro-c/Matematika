<Capitular letra="A" />ssociativity (or the associative property) is the property of a <ConceptLink targetId="operacion-binaria" isDependency={true}>binary operation</ConceptLink> whereby, when three elements are combined in a given order, the result does not depend on how they are grouped. Thanks to it, products of several factors are written without parentheses in <ConceptLink targetId="algebra-abstracta">abstract algebra</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal definition of associativity">
  Let $S$ be a <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto">set</ConceptLink> and let $*\colon S \times S \longrightarrow S$ be a <ConceptLink targetId="operacion-binaria" isDependency={true}>binary operation</ConceptLink> on $S$. The operation $*$ is said to be **associative** if and only if it satisfies the identity:
  $$
  \forall a, b, c \in S, \quad (a * b) * c = a * (b * c)
  $$
</Definicion>

In the figure, the three operands always keep their order; only the <VisualBind element="parentesis" color="ocre">parentheses</VisualBind> move. When they group the first two, the tree computes <VisualBind element="agrupacionIzquierda" color="mora">$(a * b) * c$</VisualBind>; when they group the last two, it computes <VisualBind element="agrupacionDerecha" color="pavo">$a * (b * c)$</VisualBind>. With <ConceptLink targetId="adicion">addition</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="multiplicacion">multiplication</ConceptLink> the result does not change, whatever the operands are. With <ConceptLink targetId="sustraccion">subtraction</ConceptLink> it does: with $a = 8$, $b = 4$ and $c = 2$ it goes from $2$ to $6$.

### Algebraic structures and counterexamples

Many familiar operations are associative and others are not; associativity is part of the definition of several <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-algebraica">algebraic structures</ConceptLink>:

- **Standard associative operations:** <ConceptLink targetId="adicion">Addition</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="multiplicacion">multiplication</ConceptLink> in the <ConceptLink targetId="numero-entero">integers</ConceptLink> $\mathbb{Z}$ and the <ConceptLink targetId="numero-real">reals</ConceptLink> $\mathbb{R}$; <ConceptLink targetId="multiplicacion-matrices">matrix multiplication</ConceptLink>; and <ConceptLink targetId="composicion-funciones">composition of functions</ConceptLink> from a set to itself.
- **Non-associative operations:** <ConceptLink targetId="sustraccion">Subtraction</ConceptLink> in $\mathbb{R}$ is not associative, since $(8 - 4) - 2 = 2 \neq 6 = 8 - (4 - 2)$; neither are <ConceptLink targetId="division">division</ConceptLink> in $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$ nor the <ConceptLink targetId="producto-vectorial">cross product</ConceptLink> in $\mathbb{R}^3$.
- **Algebraic hierarchy:** A set with an associative binary operation is a <ConceptLink targetId="semigrupo">semigroup</ConceptLink>; a semigroup with an <ConceptLink targetId="elemento-neutro">identity element</ConceptLink> is a <ConceptLink targetId="monoide">monoid</ConceptLink>; and a monoid in which every element admits an <ConceptLink targetId="elemento-inverso">inverse</ConceptLink> is a <ConceptLink targetId="grupo">group</ConceptLink>.

  <PropiedadesGrupo title="Deductive consequences">
    <PropiedadItem id="asociatividad-generalizada" title="Generalized associativity theorem">
      If $*$ is an associative <ConceptLink targetId="operacion-binaria">binary operation</ConceptLink> on $S$, any product of $n$ elements $a_1 * a_2 * \cdots * a_n$ (with $n \ge 3$) takes the same value under every placement of parentheses that keeps the order of the factors, so the parentheses can be omitted.
    </PropiedadItem>
    <PropiedadItem id="unicidad-inversos-asociatividad" title="Uniqueness of inverse elements">
      If $*$ is associative and has an <ConceptLink targetId="elemento-neutro">identity element</ConceptLink> $e$, and an element $a$ admits a left <ConceptLink targetId="elemento-inverso">inverse</ConceptLink> $b$ ($b * a = e$) and a right inverse $c$ ($a * c = e$), the two coincide: $b = b * e = b * (a * c) = (b * a) * c = e * c = c$. In particular, the inverse of $a$, if it exists, is unique.
    </PropiedadItem>
  </PropiedadesGrupo>
</SeccionPropiedades>
