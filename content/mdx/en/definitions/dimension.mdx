<Capitular letra="D" />**imension** is the mathematical measure of the number of degrees of freedom or independent directions characterizing a <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink> or geometric figure.

In elementary geometry, objects are classified according to their degrees of freedom: a <ConceptLink targetId="punto">point</ConceptLink> has no extension (dimension 0), a <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> has a single direction of displacement (dimension 1), a <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> admits two independent directions (dimension 2), and <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">three-dimensional space</ConceptLink> admits three (dimension 3).

<Definicion title="Definition Across Mathematical Theories">
  The concept of dimension is rigorously formalized across branches of mathematics:
  - **In <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-sintetica">synthetic geometry</ConceptLink>:** It is bounded from below by <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert's</ConceptLink> existential axioms (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-3">Axiom I.3</ConceptLink> imposes $\dim \ge 2$ by requiring at least 3 non-<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> points, and <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-8">Axiom I.8</ConceptLink> imposes $\dim \ge 3$ by requiring 4 non-<ConceptLink targetId="coplanaridad">coplanar</ConceptLink> points), while the upper bound is established by intersection and spatial closure axioms.
  - **In <ConceptLink targetId="algebra-lineal">linear algebra</ConceptLink>:** It is the <ConceptLink targetId="cardinalidad">cardinality</ConceptLink> of any <ConceptLink targetId="base">basis</ConceptLink> $B$ of a <ConceptLink targetId="espacio-vectorial">vector space</ConceptLink> $V$ over a <ConceptLink targetId="cuerpo">field</ConceptLink> $\mathbb{K}$:
    $$
    \dim_{\mathbb{K}}(V) = |B|
    $$
  - **In <ConceptLink targetId="topologia">topology</ConceptLink>:** It is defined via <ConceptLink targetId="lebesgue">Henri Lebesgue's</ConceptLink> covering dimension or the inductive dimension of <ConceptLink targetId="menger">Karl Menger</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="urysohn">Pavel Urysohn</ConceptLink>, systematically developed by <ConceptLink targetId="hurewicz">Witold Hurewicz</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="wallman">Henry Wallman</ConceptLink>.
</Definicion>

<Separador />

### Dimensional hierarchy

- **Dimension 0:** The elementary <ConceptLink targetId="punto">point</ConceptLink> (lacking extension).
- **Dimension 1:** The <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink>, and <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta">ray</ConceptLink> (purely linear extension).
- **Dimension 2:** The <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="semiplano">half-plane</ConceptLink>, and <ConceptLink targetId="poligono">polygons</ConceptLink> (areal/planar extension, formalized synthetically through relations of equidecomposition and complementation).
- **Dimension 3:** <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">Three-dimensional space</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="poliedro">polyhedra</ConceptLink> (spatial volumetric extension).
