<Capitular letra="T" />wo <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>lines</ConceptLink> that are <ConceptLink targetId="coplanaridad" isDependency={true}>coplanar</ConceptLink> are **parallel** if and only if they are identical or their <ConceptLink targetId="traza" isDependency={true}>point traces</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia" isDependency={true}>incidence</ConceptLink> are <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-disjunto">disjoint</ConceptLink>.

The <ConceptLink targetId="coplanaridad">coplanarity</ConceptLink> requirement distinguishes parallelism from the position of <ConceptLink targetId="rectas-cruzadas">skew lines</ConceptLink> in <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink>, which share no <ConceptLink targetId="punto">point</ConceptLink> but are not contained in any common <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> either. Including identity makes parallelism a <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-binaria">binary relation</ConceptLink> with <ConceptLink targetId="reflexividad">reflexivity</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal definition of parallel lines">
  Given two <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>lines</ConceptLink>, distinct or coincident, <VisualBind element="recta-base" color="musgo">$\ell_1$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="recta-paralela" color="musgo">$\ell_2$</VisualBind> in <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink>, they are said to be <ConceptLink targetId="coplanaridad" isDependency={true}>coplanar</ConceptLink> if there exists at least one <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink> $\pi$ such that the <ConceptLink targetId="traza" isDependency={true}>point traces</ConceptLink> of both are contained in the trace of $\pi$:
  $$
  \operatorname{tr}(\ell_1) \cup \operatorname{tr}(\ell_2) \subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\pi)
  $$
  Under this coplanarity condition, the lines are **parallel** (<VisualBind element="paralelas" color="musgo">$\ell_1 \parallel \ell_2$</VisualBind>) if and only if they coincide or share no <ConceptLink targetId="punto">point</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia" isDependency={true}>incidence</ConceptLink>:
  $$
  \operatorname{tr}(\ell_1) \cup \operatorname{tr}(\ell_2) \subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\pi) \implies \bigl(\ell_1 \parallel \ell_2 \iff \ell_1 = \ell_2 \lor \operatorname{tr}(\ell_1) \cap \operatorname{tr}(\ell_2) = \emptyset\bigr)
  $$
  If there is no common <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> such that $\operatorname{tr}(\ell_1) \cup \operatorname{tr}(\ell_2) \subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\pi)$, the lines are called <ConceptLink targetId="rectas-cruzadas">skew lines</ConceptLink>; they satisfy $\operatorname{tr}(\ell_1) \cap \operatorname{tr}(\ell_2) = \emptyset$ without being parallel.
</Definicion>

  <PropiedadesGrupo title="Equivalence structure">
    <PropiedadItem 
      id="reflexividad-simetria-paralelismo" 
      title="Reflexivity and symmetry of the relation"
      formula="\ell \parallel \ell \quad \land \quad (\ell_1 \parallel \ell_2 \implies \ell_2 \parallel \ell_1)"
    >
      Every <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> is parallel to itself by identity ($\ell = \ell$), which gives <ConceptLink targetId="reflexividad">reflexivity</ConceptLink>. <ConceptLink targetId="simetria">Symmetry</ConceptLink> follows from the commutativity of disjunction, of union and of the <ConceptLink targetId="interseccion">intersection</ConceptLink> of traces: $\operatorname{tr}(\ell_1) \cap \operatorname{tr}(\ell_2) = \operatorname{tr}(\ell_2) \cap \operatorname{tr}(\ell_1)$. Both properties hold without the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">parallel axiom</ConceptLink>.
    </PropiedadItem>

    <PropiedadItem 
      id="transitividad-paralelismo-euclideo" 
      title="Transitivity in Euclidean geometry"
      formula="(\ell_1 \parallel \ell_2 \land \ell_2 \parallel \ell_3) \implies \ell_1 \parallel \ell_3"
    >
      In the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-euclidiano">Euclidean system</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">Axiom IV</ConceptLink> guarantees <ConceptLink targetId="transitividad">transitivity</ConceptLink>: in the <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink>, if $\ell_1$ and $\ell_3$ were distinct and met at a point $P$, two distinct parallels to $\ell_2$ would pass through $P$. Transitivity also holds for lines in <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink>: two lines parallel to a third are coplanar and parallel to each other. Consequently, parallelism is an <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-equivalencia">equivalence relation</ConceptLink> on $\mathcal{L}$, whose <ConceptLink targetId="clase-equivalencia">equivalence classes</ConceptLink> are the <ConceptLink targetId="direccion">directions</ConceptLink>. In the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-hiperbolico">hyperbolic system</ConceptLink> transitivity fails: two intersecting lines may both be parallel to a third.
    </PropiedadItem>
  </PropiedadesGrupo>

  <PropiedadesGrupo title="Spatial extensions of parallelism">
    <PropiedadItem 
      id="planos-paralelos-espacio" 
      title="Parallel planes in space"
      formula="\pi_1 \parallel \pi_2 \iff \pi_1 = \pi_2 \lor \operatorname{tr}(\pi_1) \cap \operatorname{tr}(\pi_2) = \emptyset"
    >
      Two <ConceptLink targetId="plano">planes</ConceptLink> in <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink> are <ConceptLink targetId="planos-paralelos">parallel planes</ConceptLink> if and only if they are identical or their <ConceptLink targetId="traza">traces</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence</ConceptLink> share no <ConceptLink targetId="punto">point</ConceptLink>. The relation is reflexive and symmetric by definition; its <ConceptLink targetId="transitividad">transitivity</ConceptLink> requires <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">Axiom IV</ConceptLink>, from which it follows that through a point outside a plane there passes a unique plane parallel to it.
    </PropiedadItem>

    <PropiedadItem 
      id="distincion-rectas-cruzadas" 
      title="Disjoint traces without parallelism (skew lines)"
      formula="\forall \pi \in \Pi \ \bigl(\operatorname{tr}(\ell_1) \cup \operatorname{tr}(\ell_2) \not\subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\pi)\bigr) \implies \operatorname{tr}(\ell_1) \cap \operatorname{tr}(\ell_2) = \emptyset \land \neg(\ell_1 \parallel \ell_2)"
    >
      In <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink>, disjoint traces of two distinct <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink> do not suffice for parallelism. If two lines are not <ConceptLink targetId="coplanaridad">coplanar</ConceptLink>, they cannot share a point, because two distinct lines with a common point lie in a plane (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-3">Axioms I.3</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-4">I.4</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-6">I.6</ConceptLink>); they are therefore <ConceptLink targetId="rectas-cruzadas">skew lines</ConceptLink> and not parallel.
    </PropiedadItem>
  </PropiedadesGrupo>
