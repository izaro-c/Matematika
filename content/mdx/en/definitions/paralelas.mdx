<Capitular letra="I" />n planar geometry, two coplanar <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink> are parallel if they do not intersect at any point or if they coincide identically. Parallelism constitutes a fundamental equivalence relation in the Euclidean <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink>, distinguishing Euclidean geometry from non-Euclidean geometries.

<Definicion title="">
  Given two <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>lines</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="recta-base" color="musgo">$L_1$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="recta-paralela" color="pavo">$L_2$</VisualBind> contained in the same <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{P}$, $L_1$ and $L_2$ are parallel (<VisualBind element="paralelas" color="musgo">$L_1 \parallel L_2$</VisualBind>) if and only if their intersection is empty or they coincide:
  $$
  L_1 \parallel L_2 \iff (L_1 \subset \mathcal{P} \land L_2 \subset \mathcal{P}) \land (L_1 \cap L_2 = \emptyset \lor L_1 = L_2)
  $$
  In the Euclidean affine plane, the perpendicular distance $d(P, L_2) = \inf_{Q \in L_2} \|P - Q\|$ is constant for all points $P \in L_1$.
</Definicion>

### Parallelism across axiomatic systems

The uniqueness of a parallel line through an external point characterizes the underlying geometric model:

| Geometric system | Parallel postulate | Number of parallels through an external point |
|---|---|---|
| <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-euclidiano">Euclidean geometry</ConceptLink> | Fifth Postulate / Playfair's Axiom | Exactly one parallel |
| <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-hiperbolico">Hyperbolic geometry</ConceptLink> | Bolyai-Lobachevsky Axiom | Infinitely many parallels |
| Elliptic geometry | Riemann Axiom | No parallels |
