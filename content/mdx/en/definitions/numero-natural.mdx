<Capitular letra="T" />he **natural numbers** are the numbers $0, 1, 2, 3, \dots$ used to count the elements of a <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-finito">finite set</ConceptLink> and to arrange them in sequence; <ConceptLink targetId="aritmetica">arithmetic</ConceptLink> is built on them.

<ConceptLink targetId="peano">Giuseppe Peano</ConceptLink> stated the axioms below in 1889, anticipated by <ConceptLink targetId="dedekind">Richard Dedekind</ConceptLink> in 1888. Peano started the sequence at $1$; here, as in <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-conjuntos">set theory</ConceptLink>, $0 \in \mathbb{N}$. In that theory the natural numbers are constructed as the <ConceptLink targetId="ordinales-von-neumann">von Neumann ordinals</ConceptLink>, where each number is the set of the natural numbers preceding it.

<Definicion title="Peano axioms for the natural numbers">
  A system of **natural numbers** is a triple $(\mathbb{N}, 0, S)$ consisting of a <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto" isDependency={true}>set</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="conjuntoN" color="carbon">$\mathbb{N}$</VisualBind>, a distinguished element <VisualBind element="e0" color="mora">$0 \in \mathbb{N}$</VisualBind>, and a <ConceptLink targetId="funcion" isDependency={true}>function</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="flechas" color="terracota">$S \colon \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$</VisualBind> (termed the <ConceptLink targetId="sucesor">successor function</ConceptLink>) such that:
  1. The element $0$ is not the successor of any natural number:
     $$
     \forall n \in \mathbb{N}, \quad S(n) \neq 0
     $$
  2. The successor function is <ConceptLink targetId="inyectividad">injective</ConceptLink>:
     $$
     \forall m, n \in \mathbb{N}, \quad S(m) = S(n) \implies m = n
     $$
  3. Principle of <ConceptLink targetId="metodo-induccion">mathematical induction</ConceptLink>: for every <ConceptLink targetId="subconjunto" isDependency={true}>subset</ConceptLink> $K \subseteq \mathbb{N}$, if $0 \in K$ and for every $n \in K$ one has $S(n) \in K$, then $K = \mathbb{N}$:
     $$
     \forall K \subseteq \mathbb{N} \ \big( (0 \in K \land \forall n \in K \ (S(n) \in K)) \implies K = \mathbb{N} \big)
     $$
</Definicion>

In the figure, the arrow leaving $3$ can be moved to $0$ or to $1$. If $S(3) = 0$, axiom 1 fails; if $S(3) = 1$, axiom 2 fails, because $S(0) = S(3)$. The button adds two elements $a$ and $b$ with $S(a) = b$ and $S(b) = a$: the set <VisualBind element="regionK" color="pavo">$K$</VisualBind> of the elements reached from $0$ by applying $S$ repeatedly contains $0$ and is closed under $S$, but it is not all of $\mathbb{N}$, so axiom 3 fails.

<SeccionPropiedades>
  <PropiedadItem id="estructura-algebraica-semianillo" title="Commutative semiring structure">
    Defining <ConceptLink targetId="adicion">addition</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="multiplicacion">multiplication</ConceptLink> recursively from the successor function, the <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-algebraica">structure</ConceptLink> $(\mathbb{N}, +, \cdot)$ is a unital commutative <ConceptLink targetId="semianillo">semiring</ConceptLink> endowed with the additive cancellation property.
  </PropiedadItem>
  <PropiedadItem id="principio-buen-orden" title="Well-ordering principle">
    Every non-empty <ConceptLink targetId="subconjunto">subset</ConceptLink> of natural numbers $A \subseteq \mathbb{N}$ possesses a least element with respect to the <ConceptLink targetId="orden-total">order</ConceptLink> defined by $m \le n \iff \exists k \in \mathbb{N} \ (m + k = n)$; hence $(\mathbb{N}, \le)$ is a <ConceptLink targetId="buen-orden">well-ordered set</ConceptLink>.
  </PropiedadItem>
  <PropiedadItem id="cardinalidad-numerable" title="Countable cardinality">
    The set $\mathbb{N}$ is an <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-infinito">infinite set</ConceptLink>. Its <ConceptLink targetId="cardinalidad">cardinality</ConceptLink>, denoted by $\aleph_0 = |\mathbb{N}|$, is called countable cardinality and, under the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-eleccion">axiom of choice</ConceptLink>, it is the least infinite cardinality.
  </PropiedadItem>
</SeccionPropiedades>
