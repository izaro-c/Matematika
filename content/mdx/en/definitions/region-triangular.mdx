<Capitular letra="A" /> **triangular region** is the <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto">set</ConceptLink> of points of a <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> formed by the union of the <ConceptLink targetId="traza">trace</ConceptLink> of a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangle</ConceptLink> and its <ConceptLink targetId="interior-triangulo" isDependency={true}>interior</ConceptLink>.

Unlike the triangle, which consists solely of the three boundary segments, the triangular region includes all points enclosed by it. It constitutes the elementary closed and convex figure from which <ConceptLink targetId="region-poligonal">polygonal regions</ConceptLink> are built and upon which the <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-medida-area">theory of area measure</ConceptLink> operates.

<Definicion title="Formal Definition of Triangular Region">
Given a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangle</ConceptLink> $	riangle ABC = {overline{AB}, overline{BC}, overline{CA}}$ lying in a <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> $pi$, with <ConceptLink targetId="traza">trace</ConceptLink> $operatorname{tr}(	riangle ABC) = overline{AB} cup overline{BC} cup overline{CA}$ and <ConceptLink targetId="interior-triangulo" isDependency={true}>interior</ConceptLink> $operatorname{Int}(	riangle ABC)$, the **triangular region** determined by $	riangle ABC$, denoted $operatorname{Reg}(	riangle ABC)$, is the <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto">set</ConceptLink> of points:

$$
operatorname{Reg}(	riangle ABC) = operatorname{tr}(	riangle ABC) cup operatorname{Int}(	riangle ABC)
$$

The trace $operatorname{tr}(	riangle ABC)$ constitutes the boundary of the region, while $operatorname{Int}(	riangle ABC)$ is its open, convex interior.
</Definicion>

If two points $P$ and $Q$ belong to $operatorname{Reg}(	riangle ABC)$, the closed <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink> $overline{PQ}$ is entirely contained in $operatorname{Reg}(	riangle ABC)$, a direct consequence of the convexity of the interior and of the half-planes defining it.

      The trace and interior of the triangle are disjoint sets. No point of the sides or vertices belongs to $operatorname{Int}(	riangle ABC)$, so that the triangular region is disjointly decomposed into its boundary and its interior.
