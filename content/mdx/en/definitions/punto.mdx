<Capitular letra="A" /> point is the fundamental and indivisible primitive entity of geometry and topology. As an undefined primitive notion, it possesses no intrinsic spatial dimensions (length, area, or volume) and serves as the structural foundation for defining <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="plano">planes</ConceptLink>, and abstract spaces.

### Synthetic geometry

In Hilbert's axiomatic foundation, a point is an undefined concept whose mathematical properties are entirely characterized by the axioms:
- **Incidence**: Any two distinct points $A \neq B$ determine a unique <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> $\ell$.
- **Betweenness**: Given three distinct collinear points, exactly one lies between the other two ($A * B * C$).
- **Existence**: Every line contains at least two distinct points, and there exist at least four non-coplanar points in space.

### Analytic geometry

In the real affine space $\mathbb{R}^n$, a point $P$ is represented by an ordered $n$-tuple of real numbers:
$$
P = (x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n) \in \mathbb{R}^n
$$
The metric distance between two points $P = (x_1, \dots, x_n)$ and $Q = (y_1, \dots, y_n)$ is given by the standard Euclidean metric:
$$
d(P, Q) = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^n (x_i - y_i)^2}
$$

### Topology and set theory

In a topological space $(X, \tau)$, a point is an element of the underlying set $x \in X$. The singleton $\{x\}$ is closed whenever the space satisfies the $T_1$ separation axiom, and its inductive topological dimension is identically zero ($\text{dim}(\{x\}) = 0$).
