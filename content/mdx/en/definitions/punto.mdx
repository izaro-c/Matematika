<Capitular letra="A" /> <VisualBind element="pPoint" color="musgo">**point**</VisualBind> is a <ConceptLink targetId="concepto-primitivo">primitive concept</ConceptLink> of geometry: the simplest object in space, having no parts and possessing neither <ConceptLink targetId="longitud">length</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="area">area</ConceptLink>, nor <ConceptLink targetId="volumen">volume</ConceptLink>.

Being a basic notion, it is not defined in terms of other figures. Instead, its behavior and properties are established by the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma">axioms</ConceptLink> of the adopted <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-axiomatico">formal system</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal Definition">
  In a <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-geometrica">geometric structure</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{S}$, a **point** <VisualBind element="pPoint" color="musgo">$P$</VisualBind> is any element of the non-empty <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto">base set</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{P}$:
  $$
  P \in \mathcal{P}
  $$
  In isolation, a point possesses no internal properties; its mathematical meaning arises exclusively from the relations that the axioms establish between the elements of $\mathcal{P}$ and other geometric objects, such as <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="plano">planes</ConceptLink>.
</Definicion>

### Fundamental axiomatic relations

Since a point lacks internal structure, what can be asserted about it depends on which groups of axioms are incorporated into the adopted <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-axiomatico">geometric system</ConceptLink> (a specific system may be restricted to only the first few):

- **In <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence</ConceptLink> systems:** They establish solely the membership of points in <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="plano">planes</ConceptLink> (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-1">I.1</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-2">I.2</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-3">I.3</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-4">I.4</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-5">I.5</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-6">I.6</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-7">I.7</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-8">I.8</ConceptLink>).
- **Upon adding <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">order or betweenness</ConceptLink>:** Describes when a point lies between two others on a line, enabling the definition of figures such as the <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink> and the <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta">ray</ConceptLink> (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-1">II.1</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-2">II.2</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-3">II.3</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-4">II.4</ConceptLink>).
- **Upon adding <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink>:** Allows pairs of points to be compared to determine whether they form equal (congruent) segments, without yet presupposing a numerical notion of <ConceptLink targetId="distancia">distance</ConceptLink> (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-1">III.1</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-2">III.2</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-3">III.3</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-4">III.4</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-5">III.5</ConceptLink>).
- **Upon fixing <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallelism</ConceptLink>:** Determines how many parallel lines can pass through a point not on a given line, distinguishing Euclidean geometry (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">Euclid's postulate</ConceptLink>, a unique parallel) from hyperbolic geometry (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-hiperbolico">hyperbolic postulate</ConceptLink>, multiple parallels).
- **Upon adding <ConceptLink targetId="continuidad">continuity</ConceptLink>:** Guarantees that the line of points behaves as a measurable continuum: free of infinitesimal magnitudes (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-arquimedes">Archimedes, V.1</ConceptLink>) and gaps (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-completitud">completeness, V.2</ConceptLink>).
