<Capitular letra="A" /> square is a <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrilatero" isDependency={true}>quadrilateral</ConceptLink> whose four <VisualBind element="lados" color="terracota"><ConceptLink targetId="lado" isDependency={true}>sides</ConceptLink></VisualBind> are <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink> and whose four <VisualBind element="angulos-rectos" color="ocre"><ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink></VisualBind> are <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto" isDependency={true}>right</ConceptLink>.

It is both a <ConceptLink targetId="rombo">rhombus</ConceptLink> and a <ConceptLink targetId="rectangulo">rectangle</ConceptLink>, and hence a <ConceptLink targetId="paralelogramo">parallelogram</ConceptLink>. The square of unit side fixes the unit of <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-medida-area">area</ConceptLink>; its existence depends on the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">parallel axiom</ConceptLink>, and Euclid establishes it by constructing a square on a given segment (Elements I.46).

<Definicion title="Definition of square">
A <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrilatero">quadrilateral</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="cuadrilatero" color="canela">$ABCD$</VisualBind> is a **square** if its four sides (<VisualBind element="segAB" color="terracota">$\overline{AB}$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="segBC" color="terracota">$\overline{BC}$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="segCD" color="terracota">$\overline{CD}$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="segDA" color="terracota">$\overline{DA}$</VisualBind>) are congruent and its four angles (<VisualBind element="angDAB" color="ocre">$\angle DAB$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="angABC" color="ocre">$\angle ABC$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="angBCD" color="ocre">$\angle BCD$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="angCDA" color="ocre">$\angle CDA$</VisualBind>) are right:

$$
\overline{AB} \cong \overline{BC} \cong \overline{CD} \cong \overline{DA}
\quad \text{and} \quad
\angle DAB,\ \angle ABC,\ \angle BCD,\ \angle CDA \ \text{are right}.
$$

Equivalently, each of the four <VisualBind element="angulos-rectos" color="ocre"><ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink></VisualBind> is congruent to its adjacent <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-suplementarios">supplement</ConceptLink>; in angle measure, $|\angle DAB| = |\angle ABC| = |\angle BCD| = |\angle CDA| = 90^\circ$.
</Definicion>

<Nota>
In <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>, the existence of a single <ConceptLink targetId="rectangulo">rectangle</ConceptLink> is equivalent to the parallel axiom (Saccheri, Legendre). Hence in <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-hiperbolico">hyperbolic geometry</ConceptLink> there are no squares: a quadrilateral with four congruent sides and four congruent angles has acute angles.
</Nota>
