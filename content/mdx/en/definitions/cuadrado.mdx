<Capitular letra="A" /> square is the regular four-sided polygon in Euclidean planar geometry. By combining the metric properties of a rhombus (four congruent sides) and a rectangle (four right angles), it constitutes the figure of maximal symmetry within the family of <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrilatero">quadrilaterals</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="">
  A convex <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrilatero" isDependency={true}>quadrilateral</ConceptLink> $ABCD$ is a square if and only if its four sides are mutually <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink> and its four interior angles are <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto" isDependency={true}>right angles</ConceptLink>:
  $$
  (\overline{AB} \cong \overline{BC} \cong \overline{CD} \cong \overline{DA}) \land (\angle A \cong \angle B \cong \angle C \cong \angle D \cong 90^\circ)
  $$
</Definicion>

<Separador />

### Geometric properties and symmetry

- **Diagonals**: The two diagonals have equal length ($d = l\sqrt{2}$), bisect each other, and are mutually <ConceptLink targetId="perpendicular">perpendicular</ConceptLink>.
- **Symmetry group**: The Euclidean automorphism group of the square is the dihedral group $D_4$, consisting of 4 rotations and 4 reflections across axes of symmetry.
- **Incircle and circumcircle**: It admits concentric inscribed ($r = \frac{l}{2}$) and circumscribed ($R = \frac{l\sqrt{2}}{2}$) circles.
