<Capitular letra="A" />n **inverse element** (or symmetric element) of a given element in an <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-algebraica">algebraic structure</ConceptLink> is one that, when operated with it through a <ConceptLink targetId="operacion-binaria" isDependency={true}>binary operation</ConceptLink> equipped with an <ConceptLink targetId="elemento-neutro" isDependency={true}>identity element</ConceptLink>, produces that identity as a result. The existence of an inverse for every element is the distinguishing axiom separating <ConceptLink targetId="grupo">groups</ConceptLink> from <ConceptLink targetId="monoide">monoids</ConceptLink> and establishes the reversibility of transformations in <ConceptLink targetId="algebra-abstracta">abstract algebra</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal definition of inverse element">
  Let $(S, *)$ be a <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto">set</ConceptLink> equipped with a <ConceptLink targetId="operacion-binaria" isDependency={true}>binary operation</ConceptLink> and provided with a two-sided <ConceptLink targetId="elemento-neutro" isDependency={true}>identity element</ConceptLink> $e \in S$. Given an element $a \in S$, an element $a' \in S$ is said to be a **two-sided inverse** of $a$ if and only if it satisfies:
  $$
  a * a' = e \quad \land \quad a' * a = e
  $$
  If $a * a_R = e$, then $a_R \in S$ is said to be a right inverse of $a$; if $a_L * a = e$, then $a_L \in S$ is said to be a left inverse of $a$. When an element admits a two-sided inverse, it is said to be an invertible element.
</Definicion>

    <PropiedadItem id="unicidad-elemento-inverso" title="Uniqueness of the inverse element under associativity" formula="a * b = e \land c * a = e \implies b = c">
      If the <ConceptLink targetId="operacion-binaria">binary operation</ConceptLink> $*$ satisfies the property of <ConceptLink targetId="asociatividad">associativity</ConceptLink>, the inverse element of any invertible element $a \in S$ is unique. Indeed, if $b \in S$ is a right inverse of $a$ ($a * b = e$) and $c \in S$ is a left inverse of $a$ ($c * a = e$), then:
      $$
      c = c * e = c * (a * b) = (c * a) * b = e * b = b
      $$
      By virtue of this uniqueness, the two-sided inverse of $a$ is canonically denoted by $a^{-1}$ in multiplicative notation, or by $-a$ (opposite element) in additive notation.
    </PropiedadItem>
  </PropiedadesGrupo>
</SeccionPropiedades>
