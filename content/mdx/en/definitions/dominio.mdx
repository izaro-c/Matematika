<Capitular letra="I" />n <ConceptLink targetId="logica">mathematical logic</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-modelos">model theory</ConceptLink>, a **domain** is the non-empty <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto" isDependency={true}>set</ConceptLink> gathering the objects of a given sort in a <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-geometrica">mathematical structure</ConceptLink>. In a single-sorted theory, the domain coincides with the <ConceptLink targetId="universo-discurso">universe of discourse</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{U}$; in a many-sorted theory several mutually <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-disjunto">disjoint</ConceptLink> domains coexist, whose <ConceptLink targetId="union">union</ConceptLink> is $\mathcal{U}$. Requiring the domain to be non-empty is a convention of classical logic; some texts allow structures with an empty domain. It is not to be confused with the <ConceptLink targetId="dominio-funcion">domain of a function</ConceptLink>.

<ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert's</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-sintetica">synthetic geometry</ConceptLink> has three primitive domains: that of <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> ($\mathcal{P}$), that of <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink> ($\mathcal{L}$), and that of <ConceptLink targetId="plano">planes</ConceptLink> ($\Pi$), with $\mathcal{U} = \mathcal{P} \sqcup \mathcal{L} \sqcup \Pi$. This universe must not be confused with <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink> as a figure, which is the set $\mathcal{P}$ of all points.
