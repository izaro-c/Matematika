<Capitular letra="I" />n <ConceptLink targetId="logica">mathematical logic</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-modelos">model theory</ConceptLink>, a **domain** is the non-empty <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto">set</ConceptLink> gathering the objects of a given sort in a <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-geometrica">mathematical structure</ConceptLink>. While in single-sorted theories the domain coincides with the universe of discourse $\mathcal{U}$, in many-sorted theories multiple mutually <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-disjunto">disjoint</ConceptLink> domains coexist whose union forms the universe.

In many-sorted theories (such as <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert's</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-sintetica">synthetic geometry</ConceptLink>), three mutually <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-disjunto">disjoint</ConceptLink> primitive domains coexist: the domain of <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> ($\mathcal{P}$), the domain of <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink> ($\mathcal{L}$), and the domain of <ConceptLink targetId="plano">planes</ConceptLink> ($\Pi$), with the global universe of the model (<ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink>) being their disjoint union $\mathcal{U} = \mathcal{P} \sqcup \mathcal{L} \sqcup \Pi$.
