<Capitular letra="A" />n **axiomatic system** is a formal deductive framework in which all propositions of a mathematical theory are derived from an explicit, delimited set of postulates or <ConceptLink targetId="axioma">axioms</ConceptLink>.

The classical tradition of <ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid</ConceptLink>'s <ConceptLink targetId="elementos-euclides">Elements</ConceptLink> relied on definitions rooted in spatial intuition. In contrast, modern axiomatics developed by Moritz <ConceptLink targetId="pasch">Pasch</ConceptLink>, Giuseppe <ConceptLink targetId="peano">Peano</ConceptLink>, and <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink> dispenses with external interpretations: the formal meaning of <ConceptLink targetId="concepto-primitivo">primitive concepts</ConceptLink> is determined exclusively by the mutual relations that axioms establish among them.

<Definicion title="Formal structure of an axiomatic system">
  A formalized axiomatic system is a triple:
  $$
  \mathcal{S} = (\sigma, \Phi, \vdash)
  $$
  where:
  - $\sigma$ is the <ConceptLink targetId="signatura">signature</ConceptLink> specifying the primitive sorts or <ConceptLink targetId="dominio">domains</ConceptLink>, relations, and operations.
  - $\Phi \subseteq \mathcal{L}_\sigma$ is the <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto">set</ConceptLink> of proper <ConceptLink targetId="axioma">axioms</ConceptLink> of the theory formulated in the first-order formal language $\mathcal{L}_\sigma$.
  - $\vdash$ is the deductive calculus provided by the rules of <ConceptLink targetId="inferencia">inference</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="logica">logic</ConceptLink>.
</Definicion>

### Global metatheoretical properties

An axiomatic system is appraised from a metamathematical perspective through criteria of sufficiency and coherence:

1. **<ConceptLink targetId="consistencia-logica">Consistency</ConceptLink>:** The system must not generate contradictions ($\Phi \nvdash \bot$). By the completeness theorem, consistency is guaranteed if and only if the theory admits at least one <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-modelos">model</ConceptLink>.
2. **Axiomatic <ConceptLink targetId="independencia-logica">independence</ConceptLink>:** No axiom can be deduced from the remainder ($\Phi \setminus \{\alpha_i\} \nvdash \alpha_i$), eliminating superfluous postulates.
3. **<ConceptLink targetId="categoricidad">Categoricity</ConceptLink>:** All models of a given <ConceptLink targetId="cardinalidad">cardinality</ConceptLink> are mutually <ConceptLink targetId="isomorfismo">isomorphic</ConceptLink>, ensuring that the system uniquely pins down the described structure.

In the architecture of mathematical knowledge, axiomatic systems are expanded modularly through extension relations: the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-incidencia">incidence system</ConceptLink> extends to the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute system</ConceptLink>, from which the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-euclidiano">Euclidean system</ConceptLink> and the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-hiperbolico">hyperbolic system</ConceptLink> branch off.
