<Capitular letra="A" />n **axiom** (or postulate) is a mathematical statement accepted as true without prior proof within the framework of a formal deductive theory. It constitutes the elementary, irreducible premise from which all other propositions of the discipline are logically derived.

In formal axiomatic thought, axioms do not claim to reflect self-evident empirical realities or absolute physical truths; their metatheoretical function is to specify explicitly the conditions, constraints, and mutual relations that must be satisfied by the <ConceptLink targetId="concepto-primitivo">primitive concepts</ConceptLink> of the theory. Consequently, any subsequent result deduced as a <ConceptLink targetId="teorema">theorem</ConceptLink> via rules of <ConceptLink targetId="inferencia">inference</ConceptLink> remains indissolubly conditioned on the validity of the initial postulates.

<Definicion title="Axiom in a formal system">
  Let $\mathcal{S} = (\sigma, \Phi)$ be an <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-axiomatico">axiomatic system</ConceptLink> formalized over a <ConceptLink targetId="signatura">signature</ConceptLink> $\sigma$ with formal language $\mathcal{L}_\sigma$. A **proper** or non-logical axiom of the theory is a closed formula $\alpha \in \Phi$ belonging to the distinguished set of postulates:
  $$
  \Phi \vdash \alpha
  $$
  A mathematical <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-geometrica">structure</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{M}$ constitutes a <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-modelos">model</ConceptLink> of the system if it universally satisfies every axiom in $\Phi$:
  $$
  \forall \alpha \in \Phi, \quad \mathcal{M} \models \alpha
  $$
</Definicion>

### Metatheoretical criteria for an axiomatic repertoire

When formulating or evaluating an axiomatic collection $\Phi$, mathematical methodology examines three metatheoretical properties:

1. **<ConceptLink targetId="consistencia-logica">Logical consistency</ConceptLink>:** The set of axioms must not harbor internal contradictions, meaning it must be impossible to derive from $\Phi$ both a proposition and its negation simultaneously ($\Phi \nvdash \varphi \land \neg \varphi$).
2. **Axiomatic <ConceptLink targetId="independencia-logica">independence</ConceptLink>:** No axiom should be redundant or deducible from the remaining ones ($\forall \alpha_i \in \Phi, \ \Phi \setminus \{\alpha_i\} \nvdash \alpha_i$). To prove independence, one constructs dissenting models satisfying all axioms except the one tested.
3. **Structuring into axiom families:** In the *Grundlagen der Geometrie*, <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink> organized geometric axioms into five families with specific roles: <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence</ConceptLink> (I), <ConceptLink targetId="orden-total">order</ConceptLink> (II), <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink> (III), <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallels</ConceptLink> (IV), and <ConceptLink targetId="continuidad">continuity</ConceptLink> (V).
