<Capitular letra="A" />n **axiom** (or postulate) is a mathematical statement accepted as true without prior <ConceptLink targetId="demostracion">proof</ConceptLink> within the framework of a <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-formal">formal deductive theory</ConceptLink>. The axioms of a theory are the premises from which all its <ConceptLink targetId="teorema">theorems</ConceptLink> are deduced.

In formal axiomatic thought, axioms do not claim to reflect empirical evidence or absolute physical truths; their role is to specify explicitly the conditions, constraints, and mutual relations that must be satisfied by the <ConceptLink targetId="concepto-primitivo">primitive concepts</ConceptLink> of the theory. Consequently, any result deduced as a theorem via rules of <ConceptLink targetId="inferencia">inference</ConceptLink> is conditioned on the axioms: it holds in every <ConceptLink targetId="modelo">model</ConceptLink> of the theory.

<Definicion title="Axiom in a formal system">
  Let $\mathcal{S} = (\sigma, \Phi, \vdash)$ be an <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-axiomatico">axiomatic system</ConceptLink> formalized over a <ConceptLink targetId="signatura">signature</ConceptLink> $\sigma$ with <ConceptLink targetId="lenguaje-formal">formal language</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{L}_\sigma$. A **proper** or non-logical axiom of the theory is a <ConceptLink targetId="formula">closed formula</ConceptLink> $\alpha$ of $\mathcal{L}_\sigma$ that belongs to the distinguished set $\Phi$ of postulates:
  $$
  \alpha \in \Phi
  $$
</Definicion>

In the *Grundlagen der Geometrie*, <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink> organized the axioms of geometry into five groups: <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence</ConceptLink> (I), <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">order</ConceptLink> (II), <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink> (III), <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallels</ConceptLink> (IV), and <ConceptLink targetId="continuidad">continuity</ConceptLink> (V).

Consistency and independence are properties of the set $\Phi$, not of a single axiom. <ConceptLink targetId="consistencia-logica">Consistency</ConceptLink> is always required: no formula and its <ConceptLink targetId="negacion">negation</ConceptLink> can both be deduced from $\Phi$. <ConceptLink targetId="independencia-logica">Independence</ConceptLink>, that is, no axiom being deducible from the remaining ones, is desirable.
