<Capitular letra="A" /> circle (or circumference) is the one-dimensional geometric locus of all <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> in a plane equidistant from a fixed center. It is the fundamental closed curve of planar geometry, bounding the two-dimensional convex region known as a disk or <VisualBind element="circulo" color="pavo">circle</VisualBind>.

<Definicion title="">
  Given a <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>point</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="centro" color="musgo">$O$</VisualBind> in a <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{P}$ and a positive real number $r > 0$, the circle <VisualBind element="circunferencia" color="musgo">$\mathcal{C}(O, r)$</VisualBind> of center $O$ and <VisualBind element="radio" color="pavo">radius</VisualBind> $r$ is the set of points $P \in \mathcal{P}$ whose distance to $O$ is identically $r$:
  $$
  \mathcal{C}(O, r) = \{ P \in \mathcal{P} \mid d(O, P) = r \}
  $$
  In Cartesian coordinates on $\mathbb{R}^2$ with center at $(x_0, y_0)$:
  $$
  (x - x_0)^2 + (y - y_0)^2 = r^2
  $$
</Definicion>

### Linear elements and associated lines

The geometry of the circle includes various fundamental linear elements:

| Element | Geometric definition | Expression or property |
|---|---|---|
| <VisualBind element="cuerda" color="ocre">Chord</VisualBind> | Line segment joining two points on the curve | $\overline{AB} \text{ with } A, B \in \mathcal{C}$ |
| <VisualBind element="diametro" color="terracota">Diameter</VisualBind> | Longest chord passing through the center | $d = 2r$ |
| <VisualBind element="arco" color="canela">Arc</VisualBind> | Continuous portion of the circle between two points | $\widehat{AB} \subset \mathcal{C}$ |
| <VisualBind element="tangente" color="mora">Tangent line</VisualBind> | Line intersecting the circle at exactly one point | Orthogonal to the radius at the tangency point |
