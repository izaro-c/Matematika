<Capitular letra="T" />he circle with centre $O$ through a point $A$ is the set of the <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>points</ConceptLink> $P$ of the <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink> for which the <ConceptLink targetId="segmento" isDependency={true}>segment</ConceptLink> $\overline{OP}$ is <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink> to $\overline{OA}$.

The definition uses only congruence of segments, without numbers or distances, and therefore makes sense in <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>. When <ConceptLink targetId="longitud">length</ConceptLink> is available, it admits the usual metric description: the points at distance $r$ from the centre.

<Definicion title="Definition of circle">
Let <VisualBind element="centro" color="terracota">$O$</VisualBind> and $A$ be two distinct <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> of a <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> $\sigma$. The **circle** with centre $O$ through $A$ is

$$
\mathcal{C}(O, A) = \{\, P \in \operatorname{tr}(\sigma) \mid \overline{OP} \cong \overline{OA} \,\}.
$$

Each segment $\overline{OP}$ with $P \in \mathcal{C}(O, A)$ is a <VisualBind element="radio" color="carbon"><ConceptLink targetId="radio">radius</ConceptLink></VisualBind>. The **disc** <VisualBind element="circulo" color="pavo">$\mathcal{D}(O, A)$</VisualBind> is the region formed by the circle and its <ConceptLink targetId="interior">interior</ConceptLink>, the points $P$ with $\overline{OP} < \overline{OA}$ (<ConceptLink targetId="orden-segmentos">order of segments</ConceptLink>).

**Metric version.** If $r = |OA|$, the <VisualBind element="circunferencia" color="carbon">circle</VisualBind> is $\mathcal{C}(O, r) = \{\, P \in \operatorname{tr}(\sigma) \mid |OP| = r \,\}$.

**Analytic version.** In the <ConceptLink targetId="modelo-cartesiano">Cartesian model</ConceptLink> $\mathbb{R}^2$, with centre $(x_0, y_0)$, it is the solution set of

$$
(x - x_0)^2 + (y - y_0)^2 = r^2.
$$
</Definicion>

    <PropiedadItem
      id="interseccion-recta-circunferencia"
      title="Intersection with a line"
      formula="\lvert \operatorname{tr}(\ell) \cap \mathcal{C}(O, A) \rvert \leq 2"
    >
      A <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> meets a circle in at most two points: if there were three, $O$ would form with two pairs of them <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo-isosceles">isosceles triangles</ConceptLink> with congruent base angles, contradicting the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-angulo-exterior">exterior angle theorem</ConceptLink>. If the line passes through an interior point, it meets the circle in exactly two points; this existence is proved with the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-completitud">completeness axiom</ConceptLink>.
    </PropiedadItem>

    <PropiedadItem
      id="tangente-perpendicular-radio"
      title="Tangent and radius"
      formula="\ell \text{ tangent at } T \iff \ell \perp \overleftrightarrow{OT}"
    >
      A line through a point $T$ of the circle is tangent at $T$ if and only if it is <ConceptLink targetId="perpendicular">perpendicular</ConceptLink> to the radius $\overline{OT}$.
    </PropiedadItem>
  </PropiedadesGrupo>

  <PropiedadesGrupo title="Analytic properties">
    <PropiedadItem
      id="longitud-circunferencia"
      title="Length"
      formula="L = 2\pi r"
    >
      The <ConceptLink targetId="longitud-curva">length</ConceptLink> of the circle, defined as the supremum of the perimeters of inscribed polygons, is $2\pi r$; the ratio of length to diameter is the <ConceptLink targetId="numero-pi">number $\pi$</ConceptLink>.
    </PropiedadItem>

    <PropiedadItem
      id="area-circulo"
      title="Area of the disc"
      formula="\operatorname{A}(\mathcal{D}(O, r)) = \pi r^2"
    >
      The disc is not a <ConceptLink targetId="region-poligonal">polygonal region</ConceptLink>: its <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-medida-area">area</ConceptLink> is obtained as a limit of areas of inscribed and circumscribed polygons, by the <ConceptLink targetId="metodo-exhaucion">method of exhaustion</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="arquimedes">Archimedes</ConceptLink>, and equals $\pi r^2$.
    </PropiedadItem>
  </PropiedadesGrupo>
