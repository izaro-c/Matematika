<Capitular letra="T" />he **betweenness** relation (or intermediation) is a <ConceptLink targetId="concepto-primitivo">primitive</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-ternaria">ternary relation</ConceptLink> that formalizes the strict <ConceptLink targetId="orden">linear order</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> along a <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> in <ConceptLink targetId="david-hilbert">David Hilbert's</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-sintetica">synthetic geometry</ConceptLink>.

In the abstract <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-geometrica">geometric structure</ConceptLink>, this primitive notion does not presuppose numerical <ConceptLink targetId="coordenadas">coordinates</ConceptLink> or external <ConceptLink targetId="metrica">metric functions</ConceptLink>; its formal behavior is governed by the group of order <ConceptLink targetId="axioma">axioms</ConceptLink> (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-1">II.1</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-2">II.2</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-3">II.3</ConceptLink>, and <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-4">II.4</ConceptLink>), formally introduced to resolve the logical gaps in classical postulates identified by <ConceptLink targetId="pasch">Moritz Pasch</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Primitive betweenness relation">
In Hilbert's <ConceptLink targetId="signatura">signature</ConceptLink> $\sigma_{\text{Hil}}$, betweenness is a ternary relation symbol $\mathbf{B}$ with <ConceptLink targetId="aridad">arity</ConceptLink> $\text{ar}(\mathbf{B}) = \mathcal{P} \times \mathcal{P} \times \mathcal{P}$ over the <ConceptLink targetId="dominio">primitive domain</ConceptLink> of points $\mathcal{P}$.

For three points <VisualBind element="pA" color="terracota">$A$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pB" color="azul">$B$</VisualBind>, and <VisualBind element="pC" color="terracota">$C$</VisualBind> lying on the same line <VisualBind element="lineAC" color="carbon">$\ell$</VisualBind>, the relation is formally denoted as:

$$
A * B * C \quad \text{or} \quad \mathbf{B}(A, B, C)
$$

and expresses the predicate "point $B$ lies between $A$ and $C$". Satisfying the relation formally requires all three points to be distinct and mutually <ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> via <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence</ConceptLink>:

$$
\forall A, B, C \in \mathcal{P} \ \Biggl(A * B * C \implies \Bigl(A \neq B \land B \neq C \land A \neq C \land \exists \ell \in \mathcal{L} \, (A \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell \land B \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell \land C \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell)\Bigr)\Biggr)
$$
</Definicion>

<SeccionPropiedades>
  <PropiedadesGrupo title="Relational properties of the predicate">
    <PropiedadItem id="simetria-extremos" title="Endpoint symmetry">
      The relation is invariant under reversal of the endpoint order: $A * B * C \iff C * B * A$.
    </PropiedadItem>
    <PropiedadItem id="asimetria-posicional" title="Exclusivity and internal asymmetry">
      The intermediate position is unique for a given point triplet; if a point lies between two others, no other point in the triplet can lie between the remaining ones: $A * B * C \implies \neg(B * A * C) \land \neg(A * C * B)$.
    </PropiedadItem>
    <PropiedadItem id="irreflexividad-estricta" title="Strict irreflexivity">
      No point can lie between itself and another point: $\neg(A * A * B) \land \neg(A * B * B) \land \neg(A * B * A)$.
    </PropiedadItem>
  </PropiedadesGrupo>
</SeccionPropiedades>
