<Capitular letra="A" /> quadrilateral is the figure formed by four <ConceptLink targetId="segmento" isDependency={true}>segments</ConceptLink> joining cyclically four <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>points</ConceptLink> of a <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink>, in such a way that no three of them are <ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad" isDependency={true}>collinear</ConceptLink> and non-consecutive sides do not meet.

It is the four-sided <ConceptLink targetId="poligono">polygon</ConceptLink> and the first one in which the distinction between convex and non-convex figures appears. Its special cases (<ConceptLink targetId="trapecio">trapezoid</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="paralelogramo">parallelogram</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="rectangulo">rectangle</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="rombo">rhombus</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrado">square</ConceptLink>) are defined by relations of <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallelism</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink> among its elements.

<Definicion title="Definition of quadrilateral">
Let <VisualBind element="vertices" color="terracota">$A, B, C, D$</VisualBind> be four <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> of a <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> $\sigma$ such that no three of them are <ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> and the non-consecutive <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segments</ConceptLink> are <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-disjunto">disjoint</ConceptLink>:

$$
\overline{AB} \cap \overline{CD} = \emptyset, \qquad \overline{BC} \cap \overline{DA} = \emptyset.
$$

The **quadrilateral** <VisualBind element="poligono" color="carbon">$ABCD$</VisualBind> is the set of its four <ConceptLink targetId="lado">sides</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="lados" color="carbon">$\overline{AB}, \overline{BC}, \overline{CD}, \overline{DA}$</VisualBind>, and its <ConceptLink targetId="traza">trace</ConceptLink> is their union:

$$
ABCD = \{\overline{AB}, \overline{BC}, \overline{CD}, \overline{DA}\}, \qquad \operatorname{tr}(ABCD) = \overline{AB} \cup \overline{BC} \cup \overline{CD} \cup \overline{DA}.
$$

The points $A, B, C, D$ are the <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertices</ConceptLink>; the <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="angulos" color="ocre">$\angle DAB$, $\angle ABC$, $\angle BCD$, $\angle CDA$</VisualBind> are its angles, and the segments <VisualBind element="diagonales" color="pavo">$\overline{AC}$ and $\overline{BD}$</VisualBind> joining non-consecutive vertices are its <ConceptLink targetId="diagonal">diagonals</ConceptLink>.

The quadrilateral is **convex** if, for each side, the other two vertices lie in the same <ConceptLink targetId="semiplano">half-plane</ConceptLink> with respect to the supporting <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> of that side; for instance, for $\overline{AB}$: $C, D \in \mathcal{H}_{\overleftrightarrow{AB}}(C)$.
</Definicion>

### Classification

| Type | Condition | Validity |
|---|---|---|
| <ConceptLink targetId="trapecio">Trapezoid</ConceptLink> | At least one pair of opposite sides with parallel supporting lines | absolute |
| <ConceptLink targetId="paralelogramo">Parallelogram</ConceptLink> | Both pairs of opposite sides with parallel supporting lines | absolute |
| <ConceptLink targetId="rombo">Rhombus</ConceptLink> | Four congruent sides | absolute |
| <ConceptLink targetId="rectangulo">Rectangle</ConceptLink> | Four right angles | Euclidean (does not exist in hyperbolic geometry) |
| <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrado">Square</ConceptLink> | Four congruent sides and four right angles | Euclidean (does not exist in hyperbolic geometry) |
