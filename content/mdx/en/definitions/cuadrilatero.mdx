<Capitular letra="A" /> quadrilateral is a four-sided <ConceptLink targetId="poligono">polygon</ConceptLink> in Euclidean planar geometry bounded by the union of four consecutive line <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segments</ConceptLink>. It constitutes one of the foundational polygonal figures for studying geometric <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-medida-area">area</ConceptLink> decompositions in the <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="">
  Given four coplanar <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>points</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="vertices" color="musgo">$A, B, C, D$</VisualBind> in a <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{P}$ such that no three are collinear and the connecting segments intersect only at their endpoints, the quadrilateral <VisualBind element="poligono" color="musgo">$ABCD$</VisualBind> is defined by the union of its four <ConceptLink targetId="segmento" isDependency={true}>line segments</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="lados" color="musgo">$\overline{AB}, \overline{BC}, \overline{CD}, \overline{DA}$</VisualBind>:
  $$
  ABCD = \overline{AB} \cup \overline{BC} \cup \overline{CD} \cup \overline{DA}
  $$
  The points $A, B, C, D$ are the <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertices</ConceptLink> and the segments <VisualBind element="diagonales" color="musgo">$\overline{AC}, \overline{BD}$</VisualBind> are the diagonals.
</Definicion>

### Structural classification

Planar quadrilaterals are classified according to convexity and the <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallelism</ConceptLink> relations among their sides:

| Type | Characteristic geometric condition | Main subclasses |
|---|---|---|
| Convex | The two diagonals intersect at an interior point | <ConceptLink targetId="paralelogramo">Paralellogram</ConceptLink>, Trapezoid, Trapezium |
| Concave | One diagonal lies entirely in the exterior | Dart (arrowhead) |
| <ConceptLink targetId="paralelogramo">Parallelogram</ConceptLink> | Opposite sides parallel ($\overline{AB} \parallel \overline{CD} \land \overline{BC} \parallel \overline{DA}$) | Rectangle, Rhombus, <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrado">Square</ConceptLink> |
