<Capitular letra="A" /> quadrilateral is a four-sided <ConceptLink targetId="poligono">polygon</ConceptLink> in planar <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-euclidiana">Euclidean geometry</ConceptLink> bounded by the <ConceptLink targetId="union-conjuntos">union</ConceptLink> of four consecutive line <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segments</ConceptLink>. It serves as a foundational polygonal figure in the study of <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-medida-area">area measure decompositions</ConceptLink> in the <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="">
  Given four coplanar points <VisualBind element="vertice-a" color="musgo">$A$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="vertice-b" color="musgo">$B$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="vertice-c" color="musgo">$C$</VisualBind>, and <VisualBind element="vertice-d" color="musgo">$D$</VisualBind> in a plane $\mathcal{P}$, such that no three are <ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> and the line segments intersect only at their endpoints, the quadrilateral <VisualBind element="cuadrilatero" color="musgo">$ABCD$</VisualBind> is defined by:
  $$
  ABCD = \overline{AB} \cup \overline{BC} \cup \overline{CD} \cup \overline{DA}
  $$
  Points $A, B, C, D$ are called <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertices</ConceptLink>, segments $\overline{AB}, \overline{BC}, \overline{CD}, \overline{DA}$ are the <ConceptLink targetId="lado">sides</ConceptLink>, and segments $\overline{AC}, \overline{BD}$ are the <ConceptLink targetId="diagonal">diagonals</ConceptLink>.
</Definicion>

### Structural classification

Planar quadrilaterals are classified based on convexity properties and <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallelism</ConceptLink> relationships between opposite sides:

| Type | Characteristic geometric condition | Main subclasses |
|---|---|---|
| <ConceptLink targetId="region-convexa">Convex</ConceptLink> | Both diagonals intersect at an interior point | <ConceptLink targetId="paralelogramo">Parallelogram</ConceptLink>, Trapezoid, Trapezium |
| Concave | One diagonal lies entirely in the exterior region | Dart / Arrowhead quadrilateral |
| <ConceptLink targetId="paralelogramo">Parallelogram</ConceptLink> | Both pairs of opposite sides are parallel ($\overline{AB} \parallel \overline{CD} \land \overline{BC} \parallel \overline{DA}$) | Rectangle, Rhombus, <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrado">Square</ConceptLink> |

### Properties

- **Interior angle sum**: The sum of the four interior angles of any convex quadrilateral is always equal to $360^\circ$ ($2\pi\text{ rad}$).
- **Triangular decomposition**: Any convex quadrilateral can be partitioned by a diagonal into two <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangles</ConceptLink> disjoint except along their common boundary.
- **Area additivity**: The surface measure of a convex quadrilateral satisfies strict <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-aditividad">additivity</ConceptLink>: $\mu(ABCD) = \mu(\triangle ABC) + \mu(\triangle ACD)$.
