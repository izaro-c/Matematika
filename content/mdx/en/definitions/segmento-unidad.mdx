<Capitular letra="A" /> **unit segment** is a <ConceptLink targetId="segmento" isDependency={true}>segment</ConceptLink> conventionally fixed as a standard of metric reference in a geometric space, to which a <ConceptLink targetId="longitud">length</ConceptLink> equal to $1$ is assigned.

Fixing a unit segment allows graduation of the line through transport by <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink> and addition of segments, establishing a scale to measure any other segment. In the geometry of the Euclidean plane, the unit segment likewise serves as the basis for constructing the unit <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrado">square</ConceptLink>, whose region provides the normalization standard in the <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-medida-area">theory of area measure</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal Definition of Unit Segment">
Let $mathcal{S}$ be the <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto">set</ConceptLink> of all non-degenerate <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segments</ConceptLink> of the geometric space. A **unit segment** is a fixed element $u in mathcal{S}$ such that, in the corresponding <ConceptLink targetId="longitud">length function</ConceptLink> $|cdot| colon mathcal{S} 	o mathbb{R}_{>0}$, it satisfies the normalization condition:

$$
|u| = 1
$$

Every segment $overline{AB} in mathcal{S}$ <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruent</ConceptLink> to $u$ likewise has unit length, $|overline{AB}| = 1$.
</Definicion>

    <PropiedadItem

      formula="u, u' in mathcal{S} implies exists k > 0 : |cdot|_{u'} = k , |cdot|_u"

      The choice of unit segment does not alter the ratios between geometric magnitudes. If $u$ is replaced by another unit segment $u'$, the lengths of all segments are multiplied by a constant scale factor determined by the ratio $|u'|_u$.
    </PropiedadItem>

    <PropiedadItem

      title="Construction of the Unit Square"
      formula="operatorname{A}(Q_u) = 1"
    >
      On the unit segment $u$, a <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrado">square</ConceptLink> whose four sides are congruent to $u$ is constructed. In the <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-medida-area">theory of area measure</ConceptLink>, the region determined by this unit square $Q_u$ is normalized by assigning it an area exactly equal to $1$.
    </PropiedadItem>
