<Capitular letra="A" /> **right angle** is an <ConceptLink targetId="angulo" isDependency={true}>angle</ConceptLink> that is <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink> to its <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-suplementarios" isDependency={true}>adjacent supplementary</ConceptLink> angle.

The notion is formulated without invoking any quantitative <ConceptLink targetId="medida-angular">angular measure</ConceptLink> or numerical assignment of $90^\circ$: it belongs entirely to <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>. It rests on the <ConceptLink targetId="particion">partition</ConceptLink> of a <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink> into two opposite <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta" isDependency={true}>rays</ConceptLink> via the <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre" isDependency={true}>betweenness</ConceptLink> relation, combined with the axioms of linear and angular congruence.

<Definicion title="Formal definition of a right angle">
Let <VisualBind element="recta-l" color="carbon">$\ell \in \mathcal{L}$</VisualBind> be a <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink> in a <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink> $\pi \in \Pi$, with $\operatorname{tr}(\ell) \subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\pi)$, and let <VisualBind element="pO" color="carbon">$O \in \operatorname{tr}(\ell)$</VisualBind> be a <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>point</ConceptLink>. Let <VisualBind element="pA" color="canela">$A$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pB" color="pavo">$B$</VisualBind> $\in \operatorname{tr}(\ell) \setminus \{O\}$ be two points such that $O$ <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre" isDependency={true}>is between</ConceptLink> $A$ and $B$ ($A * O * B$), so that <VisualBind element="semirrecta-h" color="canela">$h = \overrightarrow{OA}$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="semirrecta-hp" color="pavo">$h' = \overrightarrow{OB}$</VisualBind> are <ConceptLink targetId="semirrectas-opuestas">opposite rays</ConceptLink> on $\ell$. Let <VisualBind element="pC" color="terracota">$C$</VisualBind> $\in \operatorname{tr}(\pi) \setminus \operatorname{tr}(\ell)$, defining the ray <VisualBind element="semirrecta-k" color="terracota">$k = \overrightarrow{OC}$</VisualBind> not contained in $\ell$.

The rays $h$ and $k$, and $h'$ and $k$, determine at the <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertex</ConceptLink> $O$ the <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-suplementarios" isDependency={true}>adjacent supplementary angles</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="angAOC" color="ocre">$\angle(h, k)$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="angBOC" color="musgo">$\angle(h', k)$</VisualBind>. The angle $\angle(h, k)$ is said to be a **right angle** if and only if it is congruent to its adjacent supplementary angle $\angle(h', k)$:

$$
\angle(h, k) \text{ is right} \iff \angle(h, k) \cong \angle(h', k)
$$

If $\angle(h, k)$ is a right angle, its adjacent supplementary angle $\angle(h', k)$ is also a right angle, and the <ConceptLink targetId="recta-soporte">supporting lines</ConceptLink> $\overleftrightarrow{h} = \ell$ and $\overleftrightarrow{k} = \overleftrightarrow{OC}$ are <ConceptLink targetId="perpendicular">perpendicular</ConceptLink>.
</Definicion>

<Nota>
In <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">Hilbert's</ConceptLink> axiomatization (§6), the definition of a right angle precedes that of perpendicular lines: two <ConceptLink targetId="secante">intersecting</ConceptLink> lines are declared perpendicular because they form right angles at their point of intersection, reversing the circular dependency commonly found in elementary geometry.
</Nota>
