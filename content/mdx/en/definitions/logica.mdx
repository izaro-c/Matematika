<Capitular letra="M" />athematical **logic** is the formal discipline that studies the validity of deductive reasoning, the structure of <ConceptLink targetId="lenguaje-formal">formal languages</ConceptLink>, and the foundations of mathematical theories. It provides the framework through which mathematical statements are formulated and <ConceptLink targetId="demostracion">proofs</ConceptLink> are established from explicit premises.

Metamathematical analysis distinguishes two aspects. The syntactic aspect examines the formal manipulation of strings of symbols according to grammatical rules and rules of <ConceptLink targetId="inferencia">inference</ConceptLink> ($\Gamma \vdash \varphi$). The semantic aspect, addressed by <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-modelos">model theory</ConceptLink>, investigates the interpretation of symbols within a mathematical <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-geometrica">structure</ConceptLink> and the notion of truth in a structure ($\mathcal{M} \models \varphi$).

<Definicion title="Formal first-order deductive system">
  A **first-order logical system** over a <ConceptLink targetId="signatura">signature</ConceptLink> $\sigma$ comprises the following constituent elements:
  1. **<ConceptLink targetId="lenguaje-formal">Formal language</ConceptLink>** $\mathcal{L}_\sigma$: the <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto">set</ConceptLink> of well-formed <ConceptLink targetId="formula">formulas</ConceptLink> generated from variables, logical connectives ($\neg, \land, \lor, \implies, \iff$), quantifiers ($\forall, \exists$), and equality ($=$), combined with the non-logical symbols of $\sigma$.
  2. **Logical <ConceptLink targetId="axioma">axioms</ConceptLink>**: universally valid formulas governing general deduction independently of any specific mathematical theory.
  3. **Rules of <ConceptLink targetId="inferencia">inference</ConceptLink>**: algorithmic mechanisms allowing the derivation of new formulas from established premises, such as the classical <ConceptLink targetId="modus-ponens">*modus ponens*</ConceptLink> rule:
     $$
     \frac{\varphi, \quad \varphi \implies \psi}{\psi}
     $$
  4. **<ConceptLink targetId="demostracion">Proof</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="teorema">theorem</ConceptLink>**: a finite sequence of formulas $\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_n$ where each term is an axiom or is obtained from preceding terms via a rule of inference; the final term $\varphi_n$ is called a formal theorem of the system.
</Definicion>

### Many-sorted logic in the foundations of geometry

In the contemporary formalization of the <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-sintetica">synthetic geometry</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink>, the system is modeled using **many-sorted logic**. Within this framework, three pairwise <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-disjunto">disjoint</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="concepto-primitivo">primitive</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="dominio">domains</ConceptLink> coexist: the domain of <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{P}$, that of <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{L}$, and that of <ConceptLink targetId="plano">planes</ConceptLink> $\Pi$, satisfying $\mathcal{P} \cap \mathcal{L} = \emptyset$, $\mathcal{P} \cap \Pi = \emptyset$, and $\mathcal{L} \cap \Pi = \emptyset$.

Many-sorted typing ensures that primitive binary <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-binaria">relations</ConceptLink>, such as point-line <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence</ConceptLink> ($\mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}} \subseteq \mathcal{P} \times \mathcal{L}$) or point-plane incidence ($\mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{P}\Pi} \subseteq \mathcal{P} \times \Pi$), receive arguments strictly of the appropriate sort. Hence $\ell \,\mathbf{I}\, \pi$ is not a formula of the language: that a line lies in a plane is expressed by quantifying over points, $\forall P \in \mathcal{P}\ (P \,\mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}}\, \ell \implies P \,\mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{P}\Pi}\, \pi)$.
