<Capitular letra="M" />athematical **logic** is the formal discipline that studies the validity of deductive reasoning, the structure of formal languages, and the foundations of mathematical theories. It provides the framework through which mathematical statements are stated and proofs are established from explicit premises.

Metamathematical analysis distinguishes two aspects: the syntactic domain and the semantic domain. On the one hand, the syntactic aspect examines the formal manipulation of strings of symbols according to grammatical rules and rules of <ConceptLink targetId="inferencia">inference</ConceptLink> ($\Gamma \vdash \varphi$). On the other hand, the semantic aspect, addressed by <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-modelos">model theory</ConceptLink>, investigates the interpretation of symbols within a mathematical <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-geometrica">structure</ConceptLink> and the notion of truth under an assignment ($\mathcal{M} \models \varphi$).

<Definicion title="Formal first-order deductive system">
  A **first-order logical system** over a <ConceptLink targetId="signatura">signature</ConceptLink> $\sigma$ comprises the following constituent elements:
  1. **Formal language** $\mathcal{L}_\sigma$: the <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto">set</ConceptLink> of well-formed formulas generated from variables, logical connectives ($\neg, \land, \lor, \implies, \iff$), quantifiers ($\forall, \exists$), and equality ($=$), combined with the non-logical symbols of $\sigma$.
  2. **Logical <ConceptLink targetId="axioma">axioms</ConceptLink>**: universally valid formulas governing general deduction independently of any specific mathematical theory.
  3. **Rules of <ConceptLink targetId="inferencia">inference</ConceptLink>**: algorithmic mechanisms allowing the derivation of new formulas from established premises, such as the classical *modus ponens* rule:
     $$
     \frac{\varphi, \quad \varphi \implies \psi}{\psi}
     $$
  4. **Proof and <ConceptLink targetId="teorema">theorem</ConceptLink>**: a finite sequence of formulas $\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_n$ where each term is an axiom or is obtained from preceding terms via a rule of inference; the final term $\varphi_n$ is called a formal theorem of the system.
</Definicion>

### Many-sorted logic in the foundations of geometry

In the contemporary formalization of the <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-sintetica">synthetic geometry</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink>, the system is accurately modeled using **many-sorted logic**. Within this framework, three pairwise <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-disjunto">disjoint</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="concepto-primitivo">primitive</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="dominio">domains</ConceptLink> coexist: the domain of <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{P}$, that of <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{L}$, and that of <ConceptLink targetId="plano">planes</ConceptLink> $\Pi$, satisfying $\mathcal{P} \cap \mathcal{L} = \emptyset$, $\mathcal{P} \cap \Pi = \emptyset$, and $\mathcal{L} \cap \Pi = \emptyset$.

Many-sorted typing ensures that primitive binary <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-binaria">relations</ConceptLink>, such as point-line <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence</ConceptLink> ($\mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}} \subseteq \mathcal{P} \times \mathcal{L}$) or point-plane incidence ($\mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{P}\Pi} \subseteq \mathcal{P} \times \Pi$), receive arguments strictly of the appropriate sort. This sort discipline prevents ill-formed expressions and enables the formal verification of the <ConceptLink targetId="consistencia-logica">logical consistency</ConceptLink> of the system without ambiguity in the types of objects.
