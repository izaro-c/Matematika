<Capitular letra="M" />etric **distance** is a <ConceptLink targetId="funcion" isDependency={true}>function</ConceptLink> that quantifies the spatial separation between pairs of elements of a <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto" isDependency={true}>set</ConceptLink>, assigning them a non-negative <ConceptLink targetId="numero-real" isDependency={true}>real</ConceptLink> value in accordance with a metric <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-axiomatico">axiomatic system</ConceptLink>.

Scalar distance is not a <ConceptLink targetId="concepto-primitivo">primitive concept</ConceptLink> in synthetic axiomatics nor part of its axiomatic <ConceptLink targetId="signatura">signature</ConceptLink>: <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink> is organized qualitatively through the primitive relation of <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink> ($\cong$). The distance function $d(A, B) \in \mathbb{R}$ intervenes only when space is equipped with analytic coordinates or when a concrete <ConceptLink targetId="espacio-metrico">metric space</ConceptLink> is constructed.

<Definicion title="Formal definition of metric">
  Let $X$ be a non-empty <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto" isDependency={true}>set</ConceptLink>. A function $d\colon X \times X \longrightarrow \mathbb{R}$ is a **distance** (or metric) on $X$ if and only if it satisfies the following conditions for all <VisualBind element="pA" color="terracota">$A$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pB" color="terracota">$B$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pC" color="canela">$C \in X$</VisualBind>:
  1. **Non-negativity and separation:** $d(A, B) \ge 0$, and $d(A, B) = 0 \iff A = B$.
  2. **<ConceptLink targetId="relacion-simetrica">Symmetry</ConceptLink>:** $d(A, B) = d(B, A)$.
  3. **<ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">Triangle</ConceptLink> inequality:**
     $$
     d(A, C) \le d(A, B) + d(B, C)
     $$
  
  The pair $(X, d)$ constitutes a <ConceptLink targetId="espacio-metrico">metric space</ConceptLink>, illustrated by <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segments</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="segAB" color="terracota">$\overline{AB}$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="segBC" color="canela">$\overline{BC}$</VisualBind>, and <VisualBind element="segAC" color="musgo">$\overline{AC}$</VisualBind>.
</Definicion>

### Euclidean distance and geometric models

In the Cartesian <ConceptLink targetId="modelo-cartesiano">model</ConceptLink> $\mathbb{R}^2$, the standard Euclidean distance between two <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> $A = (x_A, y_A)$ and $B = (x_B, y_B)$ is defined analytically by:
$$
d(A, B) = \sqrt{(x_B - x_A)^2 + (y_B - y_A)^2}
$$

This distance is invariant under <ConceptLink targetId="traslacion">translations</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="rotacion">rotations</ConceptLink>, and reflections, interpreting the geometric congruence postulates of the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-euclidiano">Euclidean system</ConceptLink>.
