<Capitular letra="M" />etric **distance** is a function that numerically quantifies the spatial separation between pairs of elements of a set, assigning them a non-negative real value in accordance with a formal system of metric axioms.

Scalar distance is not a primitive concept in synthetic axiomatics nor part of its axiomatic signature: space is organized qualitatively through the primitive relation of segment <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink> ($\cong$). The distance function $d(A, B) \in \mathbb{R}$ intervenes only when space is equipped with analytic coordinates or when a concrete metric model is constructed.

<Definicion title="Formal definition of metric">
  Let $X$ be a non-empty <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto" isDependency={true}>set</ConceptLink>. A function $d\colon X \times X \longrightarrow \mathbb{R}$ is a **distance** (or metric) on $X$ if and only if it satisfies the following conditions for all <VisualBind element="pA" color="terracota">$A$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pB" color="terracota">$B$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pC" color="canela">$C \in X$</VisualBind>:
  1. **Non-negativity and separation:** $d(A, B) \ge 0$, and $d(A, B) = 0 \iff A = B$.
  2. **Symmetry:** $d(A, B) = d(B, A)$.
  3. **Triangle inequality:**
     $$
     d(A, C) \le d(A, B) + d(B, C)
     $$
  
  The pair $(X, d)$ constitutes a metric space, illustrated by segments <VisualBind element="segAB" color="terracota">$\overline{AB}$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="segBC" color="canela">$\overline{BC}$</VisualBind>, and <VisualBind element="segAC" color="musgo">$\overline{AC}$</VisualBind>.
</Definicion>

### Euclidean distance and geometric models

In the Cartesian <ConceptLink targetId="modelo-cartesiano">model</ConceptLink> $\mathbb{R}^2$, the standard Euclidean distance between two points $A = (x_A, y_A)$ and $B = (x_B, y_B)$ is defined analytically by:
$$
d(A, B) = \sqrt{(x_B - x_A)^2 + (y_B - y_A)^2}
$$

This distance is invariant under translations, rotations, and reflections, interpreting with analytical rigor the geometric congruence postulates of the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-euclidiano">Euclidean system</ConceptLink>.
