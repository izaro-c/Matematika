<Capitular letra="A" /> **function** (or map) between two <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto" isDependency={true}>sets</ConceptLink> $A$ and $B$ is a <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-binaria" isDependency={true}>binary relation</ConceptLink> that associates with each element of the starting set a unique element in the target set. Since the function is itself the set of pairs $(x, f(x))$, the concept is formulated entirely within <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-conjuntos">set theory</ConceptLink>, from which <ConceptLink targetId="analisis-matematico">analysis</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="algebra">algebra</ConceptLink> take it.

<Definicion title="Formal definition of function">
  Let $A$ and $B$ be two <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto" isDependency={true}>sets</ConceptLink>. A **function** $f$ from $A$ to $B$ (denoted $f\colon A \longrightarrow B$) is a <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-binaria" isDependency={true}>binary relation</ConceptLink> $f \subseteq A \times B$ that satisfies two conditions of existence and uniqueness:
  1. **Totality (existence of image):** Every element of the <ConceptLink targetId="dominio-funcion">domain</ConceptLink> has at least one associated element:
     $$
     \forall x \in A, \quad \exists y \in B \quad ((x, y) \in f)
     $$
  2. **Uniqueness (functionality):** No element of the domain is associated with more than one element of the <ConceptLink targetId="codominio">codomain</ConceptLink>:
     $$
     \forall x \in A \ \forall y_1, y_2 \in B, \quad ((x, y_1) \in f \land (x, y_2) \in f) \implies y_1 = y_2
     $$
  
  When $(x, y) \in f$, one writes $y = f(x)$, where $y$ is the <ConceptLink targetId="imagen">image</ConceptLink> of $x$ under $f$, and $x$ is a preimage of $y$. The set $A$ is called the domain of $f$, $B$ is its codomain, and the set $\operatorname{Im}(f) = \{f(x) \mid x \in A\} \subseteq B$ is the image or range of the function.
</Definicion>

<Separador />

### Classes of functions

A function $f\colon A \longrightarrow B$ is classified according to the behavior of its assignments:

- **<ConceptLink targetId="inyectividad">Injective</ConceptLink>:** Distinct elements have distinct images: $\forall x_1, x_2 \in A, \ f(x_1) = f(x_2) \implies x_1 = x_2$.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="sobreyectividad">Surjective</ConceptLink>:** The image exhausts the entire codomain: $\forall y \in B, \ \exists x \in A \ (f(x) = y)$, that is, $\operatorname{Im}(f) = B$.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="biyeccion">Bijective</ConceptLink>:** It is simultaneously injective and surjective, which guarantees the existence of an inverse function $f^{-1}\colon B \longrightarrow A$.

    <PropiedadItem id="igualdad-funciones" title="Equality criterion for functions">
      Two functions $f\colon A \longrightarrow B$ and $g\colon C \longrightarrow D$ are equal ($f = g$) if and only if they have the same <ConceptLink targetId="dominio-funcion">domain</ConceptLink> ($A = C$) and agree on every element: $\forall x \in A, \ f(x) = g(x)$. The <ConceptLink targetId="codominio">codomain</ConceptLink> plays no part, because $f$ and $g$ are sets of pairs: $\{(0, 0)\}$ is at once a function from $\{0\}$ to $\{0\}$ and from $\{0\}$ to $\{0, 1\}$.
    </PropiedadItem>
    <PropiedadItem id="asociatividad-composicion-funciones" title="Associativity of composition">
      Given three functions $f\colon A \longrightarrow B$, $g\colon B \longrightarrow C$, and $h\colon C \longrightarrow D$, <ConceptLink targetId="composicion-funciones">function composition</ConceptLink> satisfies the <ConceptLink targetId="asociatividad">associative property</ConceptLink>: $h \circ (g \circ f) = (h \circ g) \circ f$.
    </PropiedadItem>
  </PropiedadesGrupo>
