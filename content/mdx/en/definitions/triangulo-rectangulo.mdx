<Capitular letra="A" /> right triangle is a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangle</ConceptLink> having one of its interior angles congruent to a <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto" isDependency={true}>right angle</ConceptLink> ($90^\circ$ or $\frac{\pi}{2}\text{ rad}$). It constitutes the foundational geometric figure for Euclidean trigonometry and the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-pitagoras">Pythagorean theorem</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="">
  A triangle $\triangle ABC$ in a plane $\mathcal{P}$ is a right triangle at vertex $C$ if and only if its sides $\overline{AC}$ and $\overline{BC}$ are <ConceptLink targetId="perpendicular" isDependency={true}>perpendicular</ConceptLink>, making the interior angle $\angle C$ a right angle:
  $$
  \overline{AC} \perp \overline{BC} \iff \angle C \cong 90^\circ
  $$
  The side opposite the right angle $\overline{AB}$ is the <ConceptLink targetId="hipotenusa">hypotenuse</ConceptLink> ($c = \|\overline{AB}\|$), and the orthogonal sides $\overline{BC}$ and $\overline{CA}$ are the <ConceptLink targetId="cateto">legs</ConceptLink> ($a = \|\overline{BC}\|, b = \|\overline{CA}\|$).
</Definicion>

<Separador />

### Constituent elements and metric relations

- **Hypotenuse**: The side opposite the right angle. It is strictly the longest side of the triangle.
- **Legs**: The pair of mutually perpendicular sides enclosing the right angle.
- **Complementary acute angles**: The remaining two acute angles satisfy $\alpha + \beta = 90^\circ$ ($\frac{\pi}{2}\text{ rad}$).
