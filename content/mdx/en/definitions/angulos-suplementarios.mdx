<Capitular letra="T" />wo <ConceptLink targetId="angulo" isDependency={true}>angles</ConceptLink> are <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-adyacentes">adjacent</ConceptLink> supplementary angles if they share their <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertex</ConceptLink> and a common <ConceptLink targetId="lado">side</ConceptLink>, while their remaining two sides form <ConceptLink targetId="semirrectas-opuestas">opposite rays</ConceptLink> along the same supporting <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink>.

The notion of supplement does not presuppose the assignment of <ConceptLink targetId="distancia">numerical measures</ConceptLink> or a metric sum of $180^\circ$, but is rigorously grounded in the primitive relation of linear <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre" isDependency={true}>betweenness</ConceptLink> on the line and the <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-disjunto">partition</ConceptLink> into opposite rays induced by an origin <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>point</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal Definition of Adjacent Supplementary Angles">
Let $\ell \in \mathcal{L}$ be a <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink> in a <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink> $\pi \in \Pi$ and let $O \in \operatorname{tr}(\ell)$ be an origin <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>point</ConceptLink>. Let $A, B \in \operatorname{tr}(\ell) \setminus \{O\}$ be two points such that $O$ <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre" isDependency={true}>is between</ConceptLink> $A$ and $B$ ($A * O * B$), and let $C \in \operatorname{tr}(\pi) \setminus \operatorname{tr}(\ell)$ be a point exterior to the <ConceptLink targetId="traza">point trace</ConceptLink> $\operatorname{tr}(\ell)$, guaranteeing the non-<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinearity</ConceptLink> of the triples $\{O, A, C\}$ and $\{O, B, C\}$.

Define the <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta" isDependency={true}>rays</ConceptLink> $h = \overrightarrow{OA}$, $h' = \overrightarrow{OB}$, and $k = \overrightarrow{OC}$. The angles:

$$
\angle(h, k) = \{ \overrightarrow{OA}, \overrightarrow{OC} \} = \angle AOC \quad \text{and} \quad \angle(h', k) = \{ \overrightarrow{OB}, \overrightarrow{OC} \} = \angle BOC
$$

formed by their respective <ConceptLink targetId="lado">sides</ConceptLink> are called **adjacent supplementary angles**. Two arbitrary angles $\angle \alpha$ and $\angle \beta$ in the plane are said to be **supplementary** if $\angle \beta$ is <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink> to the adjacent supplement of $\angle \alpha$.
</Definicion>
