<Capitular letra="T" />wo <ConceptLink targetId="angulo" isDependency={true}>angles</ConceptLink> are adjacent supplementary angles if they share their <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertex</ConceptLink> and a common <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta" isDependency={true}>ray</ConceptLink>, while their remaining two rays constitute <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta">opposite rays</ConceptLink> along the same supporting <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink>.

In <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert's</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-sintetica">synthetic geometry</ConceptLink>, the notion of supplement does not presuppose the assignment of <ConceptLink targetId="distancia">numerical measures</ConceptLink> or a <ConceptLink targetId="espacio-metrico">metric sum</ConceptLink> of $180^\circ$, but is rigorously grounded in the primitive relation of linear <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre" isDependency={true}>betweenness</ConceptLink> on the line and the <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-disjunto">partition</ConceptLink> into opposite rays induced by an origin <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>point</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal Definition of Adjacent Supplementary Angles">
Let $\ell \in \mathcal{L}$ be a <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink> in a <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink> $\pi \in \Pi$ and let <VisualBind element="pO" color="carbon">$O$</VisualBind> $\in \operatorname{tr}(\ell)$ be an origin <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>point</ConceptLink>. Let <VisualBind element="pA" color="terracota">$A$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pB" color="terracota">$B$</VisualBind> $\in \operatorname{tr}(\ell) \setminus \{O\}$ be two points such that $O$ is between $A$ and $B$ (<ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre" isDependency={true}>$A * O * B$</ConceptLink>), and let <VisualBind element="pC" color="terracota">$C$</VisualBind> $\in \operatorname{tr}(\pi) \setminus \operatorname{tr}(\ell)$ be a point exterior to the <ConceptLink targetId="traza">point trace</ConceptLink> $\operatorname{tr}(\ell)$, guaranteeing the non-<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinearity</ConceptLink> of the triples $\{O, A, C\}$ and $\{O, B, C\}$.

Define the <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta" isDependency={true}>rays</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="rayH" color="pavo">$h = \overrightarrow{OA}$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="rayHPrime" color="pavo">$h' = \overrightarrow{OB}$</VisualBind>, and <VisualBind element="rayK" color="pavo">$k = \overrightarrow{OC}$</VisualBind>. The angles:

$$
\angle(h, k) = \{ \overrightarrow{OA}, \overrightarrow{OC} \} = \angle AOC \quad \text{and} \quad \angle(h', k) = \{ \overrightarrow{OB}, \overrightarrow{OC} \} = \angle BOC
$$

formed by their respective <ConceptLink targetId="lado">sides</ConceptLink> are called **adjacent supplementary angles** (*Nebenwinkel*). Two arbitrary angles $\angle \alpha$ and $\angle \beta$ in the plane are said to be **supplementary** if $\angle \beta$ is <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink> to the adjacent supplement of $\angle \alpha$.
</Definicion>
