<Capitular letra="T" />wo coplanar angles are called supplementary when the sum of their angular measures equals a <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-llano">straight angle</ConceptLink> ($180^\circ$ or $\pi\text{ rad}$). This relation is fundamental for the synthetic characterization of <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto">right angles</ConceptLink> and the study of intersecting lines.

<Definicion title="">
  Two coplanar <ConceptLink targetId="angulo" isDependency={true}>angles</ConceptLink> $\alpha$ and $\beta$ in a plane $\mathcal{P}$ are supplementary if and only if the sum of their angular measures satisfies:
  $$
  \mu(\alpha) + \mu(\beta) = \pi \quad (180^\circ)
  $$
</Definicion>

<Separador />

### Adjacent supplementary angles

In synthetic geometry, if two adjacent angles share a common side and their non-common sides form a pair of opposite <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta">rays</ConceptLink> along a single <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink>, the sum of their apertures is necessarily equal to two right angles.

### Characterization of the right angle

In Hilbert's axiomatization, a <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto">right angle</ConceptLink> is defined axiomatically as an angle congruent to its own adjacent supplementary angle:
$$
\alpha \cong \beta \land \mu(\alpha) + \mu(\beta) = 180^\circ \implies \mu(\alpha) = 90^\circ
$$
