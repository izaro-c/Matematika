<Capitular letra="T" />wo <ConceptLink targetId="angulo" isDependency={true}>angles</ConceptLink> are <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-adyacentes">adjacent</ConceptLink> supplementary angles if they share their <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertex</ConceptLink> and one <ConceptLink targetId="lado">side</ConceptLink>, and their other two sides are <ConceptLink targetId="semirrectas-opuestas">opposite rays</ConceptLink> of the same <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink>. Two arbitrary angles are supplementary if one is congruent to an adjacent supplement of the other.

The notion presupposes no <ConceptLink targetId="medida-angular">angle measure</ConceptLink> and no sum of $180^\circ$: it rests on the primitive <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre" isDependency={true}>betweenness</ConceptLink> relation on the line and on the <ConceptLink targetId="particion">partition</ConceptLink> of a line into two opposite rays by one of its <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>points</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal Definition of Supplementary Angles">
Let <VisualBind element="recta-l" color="carbon">$\ell \in \mathcal{L}$</VisualBind> be a <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink> of a <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink> $\pi \in \Pi$, that is, $\operatorname{tr}(\ell) \subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\pi)$, and let <VisualBind element="pO" color="carbon">$O \in \operatorname{tr}(\ell)$</VisualBind> be a <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>point</ConceptLink>. Let <VisualBind element="pA" color="canela">$A$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pB" color="pavo">$B$</VisualBind> $\in \operatorname{tr}(\ell) \setminus \{O\}$ be two points such that $O$ <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre" isDependency={true}>is between</ConceptLink> $A$ and $B$ ($A * O * B$), and let <VisualBind element="pC" color="terracota">$C$</VisualBind> $\in \operatorname{tr}(\pi) \setminus \operatorname{tr}(\ell)$, so that the triples $O, A, C$ and $O, B, C$ are not <ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink>.

With the <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta" isDependency={true}>rays</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="semirrecta-h" color="canela">$h = \overrightarrow{OA}$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="semirrecta-hp" color="pavo">$h' = \overrightarrow{OB}$</VisualBind>, and <VisualBind element="semirrecta-k" color="terracota">$k = \overrightarrow{OC}$</VisualBind>, the angles <VisualBind element="angAOC" color="canela">$\angle(h, k) = \angle AOC$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="angBOC" color="pavo">$\angle(h', k) = \angle BOC$</VisualBind>

$$
\angle(h, k) = \{ \overrightarrow{OA}, \overrightarrow{OC} \} = \angle AOC \quad \text{and} \quad \angle(h', k) = \{ \overrightarrow{OB}, \overrightarrow{OC} \} = \angle BOC
$$

are called <VisualBind element="angulos-suplementarios" color="canela">**adjacent supplementary angles**</VisualBind>: they share the <ConceptLink targetId="lado">side</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="semirrecta-k" color="terracota">$k$</VisualBind>, and <VisualBind element="semirrecta-h" color="canela">$h$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="semirrecta-hp" color="pavo">$h'$</VisualBind> are opposite. Two arbitrary angles $\angle \alpha$ and $\angle \beta$ are called **supplementary** if $\angle \beta$ is <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink> to an adjacent supplement of $\angle \alpha$.

An angle congruent to its adjacent supplement, $\angle(h, k) \cong \angle(h', k)$, is called a <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto">right angle</ConceptLink>, and the lines forming it are <ConceptLink targetId="perpendicular">perpendicular</ConceptLink>.
</Definicion>

<SeccionPropiedades>
  <PropiedadesGrupo title="Congruence Properties">
    <PropiedadItem 
      id="teorema-congruencia-suplementos" 
      title="Congruence of Supplements"
      formula="\angle(h_1, k_1) \cong \angle(h_2, k_2) \implies \angle(h'_1, k_1) \cong \angle(h'_2, k_2)"
    >
      If two <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> $\angle(h_1, k_1)$ and $\angle(h_2, k_2)$ are <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruent</ConceptLink>, and $h'_1$, $h'_2$ are the rays opposite to $h_1$, $h_2$, then their adjacent supplements are also congruent. This <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-suplementos">congruence-of-supplements theorem</ConceptLink> (Hilbert, §6) follows from <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink> transport (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-1">Axiom III.1</ConceptLink>), segment addition (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-3">Axiom III.3</ConceptLink>), and the Side-Angle-Side criterion (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-5">Axiom III.5</ConceptLink>). In particular, two supplements of the same angle are congruent to each other.
    </PropiedadItem>
  </PropiedadesGrupo>

  <PropiedadesGrupo title="Order and Betweenness Properties">
    <PropiedadItem 
      id="existencia-unicidad-adyacente-suplementario" 
      title="Existence and Uniqueness of the Adjacent Supplement"
      formula="\operatorname{tr}(\ell) \setminus \{O\} = \operatorname{sem}(OA) \sqcup \operatorname{sem}(OB) \quad (A * O * B)"
    >
      By <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-2">Axiom II.2</ConceptLink>, for every point $A \neq O$ of $\ell$ there is a $B$ with $A * O * B$, and the point $O$ <ConceptLink targetId="particion">splits</ConceptLink> the rest of the <ConceptLink targetId="traza">trace</ConceptLink> of $\ell$ into exactly two open, <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-disjunto">disjoint</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta">rays</ConceptLink>. Hence each side $h$ of an angle $\angle(h, k)$ has a unique opposite ray $h'$, and each of its two sides thus determines a unique adjacent supplement.
    </PropiedadItem>
  </PropiedadesGrupo>
</SeccionPropiedades>

<Nota>
The congruence of supplements uses only the axioms of incidence, order, and congruence, without axioms of continuity or parallels, and therefore holds in <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>. It underlies the proof of the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-angulos-opuestos-verticales">vertical angles theorem</ConceptLink> without any angle measure.
</Nota>
