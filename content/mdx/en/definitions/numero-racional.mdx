<Capitular letra="T" />he **rational numbers** constitute the <ConceptLink targetId="cuerpo-ordenado">ordered field</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="fraccion">fractions</ConceptLink> formed by all expressible quotients between two integers, formalizing geometric <ConceptLink targetId="conmensurabilidad">commensurability</ConceptLink> and exact <ConceptLink targetId="division">division</ConceptLink> between non-zero quantities.

Universally designated by the letter $\mathbb{Q}$ (from the Italian *quoziente*), the rational numbers arise algebraically as the <ConceptLink targetId="cuerpo-fracciones">field of fractions</ConceptLink> of the <ConceptLink targetId="dominio-integridad">integral domain</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="numero-entero" isDependency={true}>integers</ConceptLink>, endowing <ConceptLink targetId="aritmetica">arithmetic</ConceptLink> with <ConceptLink targetId="elemento-inverso">multiplicative inverses</ConceptLink> for every non-zero element. Nonetheless, as historically demonstrated by the Pythagorean discovery of <ConceptLink targetId="inconmensurabilidad">incommensurable</ConceptLink> magnitudes such as $\sqrt{2}$, the rationals exhibit gaps or discontinuities that prevent identifying the continuous geometric <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> solely with $\mathbb{Q}$.

<Definicion title="Formal Construction of the Rational Numbers">
  The set of **rational numbers** $\mathbb{Q}$ is defined as the quotient set of the <ConceptLink targetId="producto-cartesiano">Cartesian product</ConceptLink> $\mathbb{Z} \times (\mathbb{Z} \setminus \{0\})$ under the <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-equivalencia" isDependency={true}>equivalence relation</ConceptLink> identifying two fractions whenever their cross products coincide:
  $$
  (a, b) \sim (c, d) \iff a \cdot d = b \cdot c
  $$
  The <ConceptLink targetId="clase-equivalencia">equivalence class</ConceptLink> $[(a, b)]$ is conventionally denoted as the fraction $\frac{a}{b}$. On this quotient set, <ConceptLink targetId="adicion">addition</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="multiplicacion">multiplication</ConceptLink> are defined by:
  $$
  \frac{a}{b} + \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a \cdot d + b \cdot c}{b \cdot d}
  $$
  $$
  \frac{a}{b} \cdot \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a \cdot c}{b \cdot d}
  $$
  Endowed with these operations, the structure $(\mathbb{Q}, +, \cdot)$ is a commutative <ConceptLink targetId="cuerpo" isDependency={true}>field</ConceptLink> with additive <ConceptLink targetId="elemento-neutro">identity</ConceptLink> $\frac{0}{1} = 0$, multiplicative identity $\frac{1}{1} = 1$, and multiplicative inverse $\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{-1} = \frac{b}{a}$ for every $a \neq 0$.
</Definicion>

  <PropiedadItem id="cuerpo-conmutativo-fracciones" titulo="Field of fractions and characteristic zero">
    The structure $(\mathbb{Q}, +, \cdot)$ is the canonical field of fractions of $\mathbb{Z}$, constituting the minimal field of <ConceptLink targetId="caracteristica-cuerpo">characteristic zero</ConceptLink> in the sense that it is contained as a prime <ConceptLink targetId="subcuerpo">subfield</ConceptLink> in every other field of characteristic zero.
  </PropiedadItem>
  <PropiedadItem id="cuerpo-ordenado-arquimediano" titulo="Archimedean property">
    The field $\mathbb{Q}$ is Archimedean with respect to its standard <ConceptLink targetId="orden-total">order</ConceptLink>: given any two positive rational numbers $x, y \in \mathbb{Q}$ with $x > 0$, there always exists a <ConceptLink targetId="numero-natural">natural number</ConceptLink> $n \in \mathbb{N}$ such that $n \cdot x > y$, satisfying the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-arquimedes">Archimedean property</ConceptLink>.
  </PropiedadItem>
  <PropiedadItem id="densidad-lineal-incompletitud" titulo="Linear density and analytic incompleteness">
    The order of $\mathbb{Q}$ is <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-denso">dense</ConceptLink> in itself, yet it fails to satisfy the property of the supremum: there exist non-empty <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto">subsets</ConceptLink> bounded above (such as $\{q \in \mathbb{Q} : q^2 < 2\}$) that lack a least upper bound in $\mathbb{Q}$, motivating the completion toward the <ConceptLink targetId="numero-real">real numbers</ConceptLink>.
  </PropiedadItem>
