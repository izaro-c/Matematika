<Capitular letra="T" />he **rational numbers** constitute the <ConceptLink targetId="cuerpo-ordenado">ordered field</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="fraccion">fractions</ConceptLink> formed by all quotients of two integers with non-zero divisor, formalizing geometric <ConceptLink targetId="conmensurabilidad">commensurability</ConceptLink> and exact <ConceptLink targetId="division">division</ConceptLink> between non-zero quantities.

Universally designated by the letter $\mathbb{Q}$ (from the Italian *quoziente*), the rational numbers arise algebraically as the <ConceptLink targetId="cuerpo-fracciones">field of fractions</ConceptLink> of the <ConceptLink targetId="dominio-integridad">integral domain</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="numero-entero" isDependency={true}>integers</ConceptLink>, endowing <ConceptLink targetId="aritmetica">arithmetic</ConceptLink> with <ConceptLink targetId="elemento-inverso">multiplicative inverses</ConceptLink> for every non-zero element. Nonetheless, as historically demonstrated by the Pythagorean discovery of <ConceptLink targetId="inconmensurabilidad">incommensurable</ConceptLink> magnitudes such as $\sqrt{2}$, the rationals exhibit gaps or discontinuities that prevent identifying the continuous geometric <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> solely with $\mathbb{Q}$.

<Definicion title="Formal Construction of the Rational Numbers">
  The set of **rational numbers** $\mathbb{Q}$ is defined as the <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-cociente" isDependency={true}>quotient set</ConceptLink> of the <ConceptLink targetId="producto-cartesiano" isDependency={true}>Cartesian product</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="pares" color="carbon">$\mathbb{Z} \times (\mathbb{Z} \setminus \{0\})$</VisualBind> under the <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-equivalencia" isDependency={true}>equivalence relation</ConceptLink> identifying two fractions whenever their cross products coincide:
  $$
  (a, b) \sim (c, d) \iff a \cdot d = b \cdot c
  $$
  The <ConceptLink targetId="clase-equivalencia">equivalence class</ConceptLink> $[(a, b)]$ is conventionally denoted as the fraction $\frac{a}{b}$. On this quotient set, <ConceptLink targetId="adicion">addition</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="multiplicacion">multiplication</ConceptLink> are defined by:
  $$
  \frac{a}{b} + \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a \cdot d + b \cdot c}{b \cdot d}
  $$
  $$
  \frac{a}{b} \cdot \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a \cdot c}{b \cdot d}
  $$
  Both formulas are independent of the chosen representatives, and $b \cdot d \neq 0$ because $b \neq 0$ and $d \neq 0$ in the <ConceptLink targetId="dominio-integridad">integral domain</ConceptLink> $\mathbb{Z}$. Endowed with these operations, the structure $(\mathbb{Q}, +, \cdot)$ is a <ConceptLink targetId="conmutatividad">commutative</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="cuerpo" isDependency={true}>field</ConceptLink> with additive <ConceptLink targetId="elemento-neutro">identity</ConceptLink> $\frac{0}{1} = 0$, multiplicative identity $\frac{1}{1} = 1$, and multiplicative inverse $\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{-1} = \frac{b}{a}$ for every $a \neq 0$. Since $(a, b) \sim (-a, -b)$, every class has a representative with $b > 0$; for $b, d > 0$ one defines $\frac{a}{b} < \frac{c}{d} \iff a \cdot d < b \cdot c$, and this definition is likewise independent of the chosen representatives. With this order, $\mathbb{Q}$ is an <ConceptLink targetId="cuerpo-ordenado">ordered field</ConceptLink>.
</Definicion>

In the figure, the pair $(a, b)$ sits in column $b$ and row $a$. The equality $a \cdot d = b \cdot c$ says that $(b, a)$ and $(d, c)$ are aligned with the origin, so each class gathers the pairs on one line through the origin: <VisualBind element="claseUnMedio" color="terracota">$\frac{1}{2} = \{(1, 2), (2, 4), (-1, -2), \dots\}$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="claseMenosUno" color="pavo">$-1 = [(-1, 1)]$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="claseCero" color="mora">$0 = [(0, 1)]$</VisualBind>. The column <VisualBind element="excluidos" color="granada">$b = 0$</VisualBind> is empty: no pair has a zero denominator.

    The structure $(\mathbb{Q}, +, \cdot)$ is the canonical field of fractions of $\mathbb{Z}$, constituting the smallest field of <ConceptLink targetId="caracteristica-cuerpo">characteristic zero</ConceptLink>: every field of characteristic zero has a prime <ConceptLink targetId="subcuerpo">subfield</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="isomorfismo">isomorphic</ConceptLink> to $\mathbb{Q}$.
  </PropiedadItem>
  <PropiedadItem id="cuerpo-ordenado-arquimediano" title="Archimedean property">
    The field $\mathbb{Q}$ is Archimedean with respect to its standard <ConceptLink targetId="orden-total">order</ConceptLink>: for all $x, y \in \mathbb{Q}$ with $x > 0$ there exists a <ConceptLink targetId="numero-natural">natural number</ConceptLink> $n \in \mathbb{N}$ such that $n \cdot x > y$, satisfying the <ConceptLink targetId="propiedad-arquimediana">Archimedean property</ConceptLink>.
  </PropiedadItem>
  <PropiedadItem id="densidad-lineal-incompletitud" title="Linear density and analytic incompleteness">
    The order of $\mathbb{Q}$ is <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-denso">dense</ConceptLink>, yet it fails to satisfy the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-supremo">least-upper-bound property</ConceptLink>: there exist non-empty <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto">subsets</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="cota-superior">bounded above</ConceptLink> (such as $\{q \in \mathbb{Q} : q^2 < 2\}$) that lack a least upper bound in $\mathbb{Q}$, motivating the completion toward the <ConceptLink targetId="numero-real">real numbers</ConceptLink>.
  </PropiedadItem>
