<Capitular letra="S" />**ynthetic geometry** (or pure geometry) is the axiomatic and deductive approach that investigates the intrinsic properties of geometric figures directly from <ConceptLink targetId="concepto-primitivo">primitive concepts</ConceptLink> (such as <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink>, and <ConceptLink targetId="plano">planes</ConceptLink>) and <ConceptLink targetId="axioma">axioms</ConceptLink>, without relying on numerical coordinate systems or algebraic formulas.

This methodology conceptually contrasts with <ConceptLink targetId="descartes">René Descartes</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="fermat">Pierre de Fermat's</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-analitica">analytic geometry</ConceptLink>, where points are represented as coordinate tuples $(\mathbb{R}^n)$ and geometric proofs are reduced to <ConceptLink targetId="algebra">algebraic computations</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Conceptual Definition">
  **Synthetic geometry** is characterized by:
  1. **Structural autonomy:** Geometric properties are deduced purely through logical reasoning and constructions from a formal <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-axiomatico">axiomatic system</ConceptLink>.
  2. **Coordinate independence:** It presupposes no pre-existing numeric metric scale or <ConceptLink targetId="modelo-cartesiano">Cartesian</ConceptLink> coordinate frame.
</Definicion>

### Historical evolution of the synthetic method

- **<ConceptLink targetId="sistema-euclidiano">Classical Euclidean geometry</ConceptLink>:** Founded by <ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid</ConceptLink> in the *Elements*, grounded in plain definitions, five postulates, and common notions.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="espacio-proyectivo">Synthetic projective geometry</ConceptLink> (19th century):** Developed by <ConceptLink targetId="poncelet">Jean-Victor Poncelet</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="steiner">Jakob Steiner</ConceptLink>, and <ConceptLink targetId="staudt">Karl von Staudt</ConceptLink>, establishing cross-ratio and projective theorems without metric concepts.
- **Formal axiomatic refundation:** Initiated by <ConceptLink targetId="pasch">Moritz Pasch</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="peano">Giuseppe Peano</ConceptLink> and brought to fruition by <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink> in <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-hilbertiana">Hilbertian geometry</ConceptLink>, eliminating implicit assumptions and establishing modern formal axiomatics.
