<Capitular letra="A" /> line segment is the bounded, connected, and convex subset of a <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> delimited by two <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> called endpoints. Unlike the infinite line, it possesses a well-defined metric length and forms the basic constituent entity for constructing <ConceptLink targetId="poligono">polygons</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangles</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="">
  Given two distinct points <VisualBind element="pA" color="musgo">$A$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="pB" color="musgo">$B$</VisualBind> on a <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="lineL" color="carbon">$\ell$</VisualBind>, the closed line segment <VisualBind element="segmentAB" color="musgo">$\overline{AB}$</VisualBind> is the set consisting of the <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>points</ConceptLink> $A$, $B$ and all points $P \in \ell$ lying <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre" isDependency={true}>between</ConceptLink> $A$ and $B$:
  $$
  \overline{AB} = \{ P \in \ell \mid P = A \lor P = B \lor A * P * B \}
  $$
  In the vector space $\mathbb{R}^n$, it is expressed as the convex combination:
  $$
  \overline{AB} = \{ P \in \mathbb{R}^n \mid P = (1 - t)A + tB, \, t \in [0, 1] \}
  $$
</Definicion>

### Synthetic and axiomatic geometry

In Hilbert's framework:
- **Open segment**: Defined as $(AB) = \{ P \in \ell \mid A * P * B \}$, omitting endpoints $A$ and $B$.
- **Segment congruence**: The primitive relation $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{CD}$ ensures rigid translation of distances without distortion.
- **Midpoint**: For every segment $\overline{AB}$, there exists a unique interior point $M \in (AB)$ such that $\overline{AM} \cong \overline{MB}$.

### Metric and analytic geometry

The scalar length of the segment is the Euclidean distance between its endpoints:
$$
\|\overline{AB}\| = d(A, B) = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^n (b_i - a_i)^2}
$$
