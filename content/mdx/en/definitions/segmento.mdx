<Capitular letra="A" /> line segment is the <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto">set</ConceptLink> consisting of two distinct <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>points</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incident</ConceptLink> with a <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink>, called endpoints, together with all points lying <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre" isDependency={true}>between</ConceptLink> them.

The line segment is derived from the <ConceptLink targetId="concepto-primitivo">primitive</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">betweenness</ConceptLink> relation and <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-1">Axiom II.1</ConceptLink>, and is the elementary bounded figure upon which <ConceptLink targetId="poligono">polygons</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangles</ConceptLink>, and <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">segment congruence</ConceptLink> are built.

<Definicion title="Formal Definition of Line Segment">
Given two distinct points <VisualBind element="pA" color="musgo">$A$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="pB" color="musgo">$B$</VisualBind> in the primitive <ConceptLink targetId="dominio">domain</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{P}$ <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incident</ConceptLink> with a <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="lineL" color="carbon">$\ell$</VisualBind>, the **open segment** $\operatorname{seg}(AB)$ is the set of all points $P \in \mathcal{P}$ lying between $A$ and $B$:

$$
\operatorname{seg}(AB) = \{ P \in \mathcal{P} \mid A * P * B \}
$$

The **closed segment** <VisualBind element="segmentAB" color="musgo">$\overline{AB}$</VisualBind> is the union of this set with the endpoints $A$ and $B$:

$$
\overline{AB} = \operatorname{seg}(AB) \cup \{A, B\} = \{ P \in \mathcal{P} \mid P = A \lor P = B \lor A * P * B \}
$$
</Definicion>

  <PropiedadesGrupo title="Structural and Order Properties">
    <PropiedadItem 
      id="simetria-extremos" 
      title="Symmetry of Endpoints"
      formula="\overline{AB} = \overline{BA} \land \operatorname{seg}(AB) = \operatorname{seg}(BA)"
    >
      By the <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-simetrica">symmetry</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-1">Axiom II.1</ConceptLink> ($A * P * B \iff B * P * A$), a segment does not depend on the order in which its endpoints are named: $\overline{AB} = \overline{BA}$ and $\operatorname{seg}(AB) = \operatorname{seg}(BA)$ as an <ConceptLink targetId="igualdad-matematica">equality of sets</ConceptLink>.
    </PropiedadItem>

    <PropiedadItem 
      id="convexidad-segmento" 
      title="Linear Convexity"
      formula="P, Q \in \overline{AB} \land P * X * Q \implies X \in \overline{AB}"
    >
      Every closed segment $\overline{AB}$ is a <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-convexo">convex set</ConceptLink>: if $P, Q \in \overline{AB}$, every point $X \in \mathcal{P}$ with $P * X * Q$ satisfies $X \in \overline{AB}$. This is not part of the definition but a <ConceptLink targetId="teorema">theorem</ConceptLink>: it follows from the <ConceptLink targetId="orden">linear order</ConceptLink> of the points of a line, which <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">Hilbert</ConceptLink> derives from the order axioms <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-1">II.1</ConceptLink>–<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-4">II.4</ConceptLink> (§4).
    </PropiedadItem>

    <PropiedadItem 
      id="unicidad-soporte" 
      title="Uniqueness of the Supporting Line"
      formula="\exists! \ell \in \mathcal{L} : \overline{AB} \subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\ell) \quad (\ell = \overleftrightarrow{AB})"
    >
      By <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-1">Axiom I.1</ConceptLink>, the distinct endpoints $A \neq B$ determine a unique <ConceptLink targetId="recta-soporte">supporting line</ConceptLink> $\overleftrightarrow{AB}$, and the segment is contained in its <ConceptLink targetId="traza">trace</ConceptLink>: $\overline{AB} \subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\overleftrightarrow{AB})$. The inclusion holds because, by <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-1">Axiom II.1</ConceptLink>, every point between $A$ and $B$ is <ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> with them.
    </PropiedadItem>
  </PropiedadesGrupo>
</SeccionPropiedades>
