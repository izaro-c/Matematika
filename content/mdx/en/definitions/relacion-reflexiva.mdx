<Capitular letra="A" /> homogeneous binary relation is **reflexive** if every element of the set upon which it operates is related to itself.

It is one of the three conditions that define an <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-equivalencia">equivalence relation</ConceptLink>, together with <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-simetrica">symmetry</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-transitiva">transitivity</ConceptLink>, and one of those that define a <ConceptLink targetId="orden-parcial">partial order</ConceptLink>, together with <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-antisimetrica">antisymmetry</ConceptLink> and transitivity.

<Definicion title="Formal definition of reflexive relation">
  Let $A$ be a <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto" isDependency={true}>set</ConceptLink> and let $R \subseteq A \times A$ be a <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-binaria" isDependency={true}>binary relation</ConceptLink> on $A$. The relation $R$ is **reflexive** if and only if:
  $$
  \forall x \in A, \quad x \, R \, x \quad (\text{that is, } (x, x) \in R)
  $$
  In set-theoretic terms, denoting by $\Delta_A = \{(x, x) \in A \times A \mid x \in A\}$ the diagonal or identity relation on $A$, reflexivity is equivalent to the inclusion:
  $$
  \Delta_A \subseteq R
  $$
</Definicion>

### Manifestations in formal geometry

In the deductive foundations of <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-hilbertiana">Hilbertian geometry</ConceptLink>, reflexivity is postulated or derived as a property of comparison relations:

- **Congruence of segments and angles:** <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-1">Axiom III.1</ConceptLink> stipulates that every <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink> is congruent to itself ($\overline{AB} \cong \overline{AB}$), endowing the <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink> of segments with a reflexive character. Similarly, in <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-4">Axiom III.4</ConceptLink>, every <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angle</ConceptLink> is congruent to itself ($\angle AOB \cong \angle AOB$).
- **Parallelism of lines:** Every <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> is <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallel</ConceptLink> to itself by definition ($\ell \parallel \ell$), without appeal to the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">parallel axiom</ConceptLink>. Parallelism thus satisfies the reflexivity required of every <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-equivalencia">equivalence relation</ConceptLink>; in <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-euclidiano">Euclidean geometry</ConceptLink>, where that axiom also provides <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-transitiva">transitivity</ConceptLink>, it is one.
