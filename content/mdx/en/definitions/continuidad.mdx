<Capitular letra="C" />ontinuity **is** a fundamental mathematical principle expressing the fullness, cohesion, and absence of gaps or jumps in geometric lines as well as in functions and topological spaces.

In the conceptual architecture of mathematics, continuity manifests under two complementary perspectives: synthetic geometric foundations and the algebraic-topological analysis of the real continuum.

From a synthetic standpoint, continuity does not presuppose an antecedent numerical system, but is articulated through axiom Group V: the Archimedean principle (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-arquimedes">Axiom V.1</ConceptLink>) and the axiom of linear completeness (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-completitud">Axiom V.2</ConceptLink>). Together they ensure that the line admits no extensions preserving previous axioms, resolving classical gaps in Euclid such as the necessary intersection between lines and curves like the <ConceptLink targetId="circunferencia">circle</ConceptLink>.

Meanwhile, in analysis over the <ConceptLink targetId="cuerpo">field</ConceptLink> of real numbers $\mathbb{R}$, continuity is formalized through the <ConceptLink targetId="completitud-reales">completeness of the real numbers</ConceptLink>, whether via Dedekind cuts, the least upper bound principle, or the convergence of Cauchy sequences.

<Definicion title="Dedekind cut principle">
  Let $L$ be a linear continuum endowed with a dense order. If $L$ is divided into two non-empty, disjoint classes $\Sigma_1$ and $\Sigma_2$ such that every element of the first precedes every element of the second:
  $$
  L = \Sigma_1 \cup \Sigma_2 \quad \land \quad \Sigma_1 \cap \Sigma_2 = \emptyset \quad \land \quad \forall x \in \Sigma_1, \, \forall y \in \Sigma_2 \ (x < y)
  $$
  then the continuity principle stipulates that there exists a unique element $p \in L$ producing that partition, acting either as the maximum of $\Sigma_1$ or as the minimum of $\Sigma_2$.
</Definicion>

### Deductive necessity in the absolute system

In the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute system</ConceptLink>, continuity axioms are indispensable for defining the length of curves, continuous angular measurement, and the rigorous computation of polygonal areas.

In geometries lacking continuity axioms (such as the rational plane $\mathbb{Q}^2$), a secant line may cross the interior of a circle without sharing any <ConceptLink targetId="punto">point</ConceptLink> with its boundary; continuity postulates the formal existence of those contact points, definitively bridging the gap between spatial intuition and logical deduction.
