<Capitular letra="G" />eometric similarity is an equivalence relation between figures in the <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> or space that preserve identical shape while possibly differing in overall scale. Two figures are similar if there exists a similarity transformation (the composition of an <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">isometry</ConceptLink> and a homothety) that preserves the proportionality of all distances between points and preserves all <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="">
  Let $F_1, F_2 \subset \mathcal{E}$ be subsets of points in a Euclidean metric space $\mathcal{E}$. $F_1$ and $F_2$ are similar ($F_1 \sim F_2$) if and only if there exists a scale factor $k > 0$ and a bijection $g: \mathcal{E} \to \mathcal{E}$ such that $g(F_1) = F_2$ and for all pairs $A, B \in \mathcal{E}$:
  $$
  d(g(A), g(B)) = k \cdot d(A, B)
  $$
  When $k = 1$, similarity reduces to <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>geometric congruence</ConceptLink>.
</Definicion>

### Triangle similarity criteria

In planar Euclidean geometry, similarity between two <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangles</ConceptLink> is established through the following minimal criteria:

| Criterion | Formulation | Geometric condition |
|---|---|---|
| AA | Angle-Angle | Two pairs of corresponding angles are congruent ($\alpha \cong \alpha' \land \beta \cong \beta'$) |
| SAS | Side-Angle-Side | One congruent angle and adjacent sides in the same ratio ($\frac{a'}{a} = \frac{b'}{b} = k$) |
| SSS | Side-Side-Side | All three pairs of corresponding sides share the same ratio $k$ |
