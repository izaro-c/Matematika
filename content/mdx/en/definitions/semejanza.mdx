<Capitular letra="T" />wo <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangles</ConceptLink> are similar if, under a correspondence between their <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertices</ConceptLink>, corresponding <ConceptLink targetId="angulo" isDependency={true}>angles</ConceptLink> are <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink> and corresponding <ConceptLink targetId="lado">sides</ConceptLink> are <ConceptLink targetId="proporcionalidad">proportional</ConceptLink>.

Similarity separates shape from size: two similar triangles have the same angles and differ at most by a scale factor common to all their <ConceptLink targetId="longitud" isDependency={true}>lengths</ConceptLink>. It is a notion proper to the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-euclidiano">Euclidean system</ConceptLink>: its theory rests on <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-tales">Thales's theorem</ConceptLink> and hence on the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">parallel axiom</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Similarity of triangles">
Let $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle A'B'C'$ be two <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangles</ConceptLink> of the Euclidean plane, taken with the ordered correspondence $A \mapsto A'$, $B \mapsto B'$, $C \mapsto C'$. We say that $\triangle ABC$ is **similar** to $\triangle A'B'C'$, written $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle A'B'C'$, if the three pairs of corresponding <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> are <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruent</ConceptLink> and there is a real number $k > 0$ such that the <ConceptLink targetId="longitud">lengths</ConceptLink> of corresponding <ConceptLink targetId="lado">sides</ConceptLink> are in the ratio $k$:

$$
\triangle ABC \sim \triangle A'B'C' \iff
\begin{cases}
\angle CAB \cong \angle C'A'B', \quad \angle ABC \cong \angle A'B'C', \quad \angle BCA \cong \angle B'C'A', \\[4pt]
\dfrac{|A'B'|}{|AB|} = \dfrac{|B'C'|}{|BC|} = \dfrac{|C'A'|}{|CA|} = k.
\end{cases}
$$

The number $k$ is the **ratio of similarity**. If $k = 1$, corresponding sides are congruent and the triangles are <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia-triangulos">congruent</ConceptLink>.
</Definicion>

### Similarity of figures

For arbitrary figures the notion is formulated through transformations of the Euclidean plane $\sigma$. A **similarity of ratio $k > 0$** is a <ConceptLink targetId="biyeccion">bijection</ConceptLink> $f\colon \operatorname{tr}(\sigma) \longrightarrow \operatorname{tr}(\sigma)$ that multiplies all lengths by $k$:

$$
|f(P)f(Q)| = k\,|PQ| \qquad \text{for all } P, Q \in \operatorname{tr}(\sigma).
$$

Every similarity is the <ConceptLink targetId="composicion-funciones">composition</ConceptLink> of a <ConceptLink targetId="homotecia">homothety</ConceptLink> of ratio $k$ and an <ConceptLink targetId="isometria">isometry</ConceptLink>, and it preserves congruence of angles. Two figures $F, F' \subseteq \mathcal{P}$ are similar if there is a similarity $f$ with $f(F) = F'$. For triangles both definitions agree: a similarity maps $\triangle ABC$ onto a triangle similar in the previous sense and, conversely, two similar triangles are images of each other under a similarity. An isometry is a similarity of ratio $1$; it should not be confused with the <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink> relation, which is primitive for segments and angles.

    <PropiedadItem
      id="criterio-semejanza-aa"
      title="Angle-angle criterion (AA)"
      formula="\angle CAB \cong \angle C'A'B' \land \angle ABC \cong \angle A'B'C' \implies \triangle ABC \sim \triangle A'B'C'"
    >
      If two <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> of a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink> are <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruent</ConceptLink> to two angles of another, the triangles are similar. The third pair of angles is congruent by the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-suma-angulos-triangulo">angle sum theorem for triangles</ConceptLink>, and proportionality of the sides follows from <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-tales">Thales's theorem</ConceptLink> after laying one triangle off on the other.
    </PropiedadItem>

    <PropiedadItem
      id="criterio-semejanza-lal"
      title="Side-angle-side criterion (SAS)"
      formula="\angle CAB \cong \angle C'A'B' \land \frac{|A'B'|}{|AB|} = \frac{|A'C'|}{|AC|} \implies \triangle ABC \sim \triangle A'B'C'"
    >
      If an <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angle</ConceptLink> of one triangle is congruent to an angle of another and the <ConceptLink targetId="lado">sides</ConceptLink> forming it are <ConceptLink targetId="proporcionalidad">proportional</ConceptLink>, the triangles are similar. The proof uses the converse of <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-tales">Thales's theorem</ConceptLink>.
    </PropiedadItem>

    <PropiedadItem
      id="criterio-semejanza-lll"
      title="Side-side-side criterion (SSS)"
      formula="\frac{|A'B'|}{|AB|} = \frac{|B'C'|}{|BC|} = \frac{|C'A'|}{|CA|} \implies \triangle ABC \sim \triangle A'B'C'"
    >
      If the three <ConceptLink targetId="lado">sides</ConceptLink> of one triangle are proportional to those of another, the triangles are similar; it reduces to the SAS criterion and the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-lll">SSS congruence criterion</ConceptLink>.
    </PropiedadItem>
  </PropiedadesGrupo>

  <PropiedadesGrupo title="Structural properties">
    <PropiedadItem
      id="semejanza-relacion-equivalencia"
      title="Equivalence relation"
      formula="\triangle ABC \sim \triangle ABC, \quad \triangle ABC \sim \triangle A'B'C' \implies \triangle A'B'C' \sim \triangle ABC"
    >
      Similarity is an <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-equivalencia">equivalence relation</ConceptLink>: it is <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-reflexiva">reflexive</ConceptLink> (ratio $1$), <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-simetrica">symmetric</ConceptLink> (ratio $1/k$) and <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-transitiva">transitive</ConceptLink> (the ratios multiply).
    </PropiedadItem>

    <PropiedadItem
      id="razon-areas-semejanza"
      title="Ratio of areas"
      formula="\triangle ABC \sim \triangle A'B'C' \implies \operatorname{A}(\triangle A'B'C') = k^2\,\operatorname{A}(\triangle ABC)"
    >
      If two triangles are similar with ratio $k$, the ratio of their <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-medida-area">areas</ConceptLink> is $k^2$: base and height are both multiplied by $k$ in the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-triangulo">triangle area theorem</ConceptLink>. By <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-aditividad">additivity</ConceptLink>, the same holds for two similar <ConceptLink targetId="region-poligonal">polygonal regions</ConceptLink>.
    </PropiedadItem>
  </PropiedadesGrupo>

<Nota>
In <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-hiperbolico">hyperbolic geometry</ConceptLink> two triangles with congruent corresponding angles are congruent: there are no similar triangles with ratio $k \neq 1$. <ConceptLink targetId="wallis">John Wallis</ConceptLink> proposed in 1663 the existence of similar non-congruent triangles as a substitute for the fifth postulate; given the axioms of <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>, the two statements are equivalent.
</Nota>
