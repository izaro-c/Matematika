<Capitular letra="A" /> <VisualBind element="lineAB" color="musgo">**line**</VisualBind> is a <ConceptLink targetId="concepto-primitivo">primitive concept</ConceptLink> of geometry: the simplest linear object in space, possessing neither width nor thickness.

Being a basic notion, it is not defined in terms of other figures. Instead, its formal behavior and its relations to <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="plano">planes</ConceptLink> are established by the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma">axioms</ConceptLink> of the adopted <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-axiomatico">formal system</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal Definition">
  In a <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-geometrica">geometric structure</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{S}$, a **line** <VisualBind element="lineAB" color="musgo">$\ell$</VisualBind> is any element of the non-empty <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto">set</ConceptLink> of lines $\mathcal{L}$:
  $$
  \ell \in \mathcal{L}
  $$
  In isolation, a line possesses no intrinsic properties; its mathematical meaning arises exclusively from the relations that the axioms establish between lines and other elements of space.
</Definicion>

### Fundamental axiomatic relations

Since a line lacks internal structure, what can be asserted about it depends on which groups of axioms are incorporated into the adopted <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-axiomatico">geometric system</ConceptLink> (a specific system may be restricted to only the first few):

- **In <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence</ConceptLink> systems:** They establish positional membership between points and lines: two distinct points determine a unique line (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-1">I.1</ConceptLink>), every line contains at least two points (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-2">I.2</ConceptLink>), points not lying on a given line exist (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-3">I.3</ConceptLink>), a line is contained in a plane if the plane contains two of its points (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-6">I.6</ConceptLink>), and the intersection of two distinct planes is a line (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-7">I.7</ConceptLink>).
- **Upon adding <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">order or betweenness</ConceptLink>:** Points on the line are arranged sequentially without endpoints (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-1">II.1</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-2">II.2</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-3">II.3</ConceptLink>), formalizing boundless extension in both directions and enabling the definition of figures such as the <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink> and the <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta">ray</ConceptLink>.
- **Upon adding <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink>:** Allows pairs of points on the line to be compared as congruent segments (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-1">III.1</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-2">III.2</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-3">III.3</ConceptLink>), without yet presupposing a numerical notion of <ConceptLink targetId="distancia">distance</ConceptLink>.
- **Upon fixing <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallelism</ConceptLink>:** Determines how many lines not intersecting a given line can pass through an external point, distinguishing Euclidean geometry (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">Euclid's postulate</ConceptLink>, a unique parallel) from hyperbolic geometry (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-hiperbolico">hyperbolic postulate</ConceptLink>, multiple parallels).
- **Upon adding <ConceptLink targetId="continuidad">continuity</ConceptLink>:** Guarantees that the line of points behaves as a measurable continuum: free of infinitesimal magnitudes (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-arquimedes">Archimedes, V.1</ConceptLink>) and gaps (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-completitud">completeness, V.2</ConceptLink>).
