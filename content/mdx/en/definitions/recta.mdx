<Capitular letra="A" /> <VisualBind element="lineAB" color="musgo">**line**</VisualBind> is a <ConceptLink targetId="concepto-primitivo">primitive concept</ConceptLink> of geometry: the simplest linear object in <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink>, lacking width and thickness.

Being a basic notion, it is not defined in terms of other figures. Instead, its formal behavior and how it relates to <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="plano">planes</ConceptLink> are established by the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma">axioms</ConceptLink> of the adopted <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-axiomatico">formal system</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal Definition">
  In a <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-geometrica">geometric structure</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{S}$, a **line** <VisualBind element="lineAB" color="musgo">$\ell$</VisualBind> is any element of the <ConceptLink targetId="dominio">primitive domain of lines</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{L}$:
  $$
  \ell \in \mathcal{L}
  $$
  In isolation, the line possesses no intrinsic properties; its mathematical meaning arises exclusively from the relations that the axioms establish between lines and other elements of <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink>.
</Definicion>

### Fundamental axiomatic relations

Since the line lacks internal structure, what can be asserted about it depends on which groups of axioms are incorporated into the adopted <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-axiomatico">geometric system</ConceptLink> (a specific system may be restricted to only the first few):

- **In <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence</ConceptLink> systems:** The axioms of group I fix the incidence relations among points, lines, and planes: two distinct points determine a unique line (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-1">I.1</ConceptLink>), every line is incident with at least two points (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-2">I.2</ConceptLink>), there exist three non-<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> points (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-3">I.3</ConceptLink>), if two distinct points of a line are incident with a plane, all its points are incident with it (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-6">I.6</ConceptLink>), and if two distinct planes share a point, they share at least one more (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-7">I.7</ConceptLink>); from these axioms it follows that the <ConceptLink targetId="traza">traces</ConceptLink> of two distinct intersecting planes meet exactly in the trace of a line.
- **Upon adding <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">order or betweenness</ConceptLink>:** The points of the line are linearly ordered and without endpoints (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-1">II.1</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-2">II.2</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-3">II.3</ConceptLink>), which formalizes unbounded extension in both directions and allows bounding figures such as the <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink> and the <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta">ray</ConceptLink>.
- **Upon adding <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink>:** Pairs of points on the line can be transported and compared as congruent segments (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-1">III.1</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-2">III.2</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-3">III.3</ConceptLink>), without yet presupposing a numerical notion of <ConceptLink targetId="distancia">distance</ConceptLink>.
- **Upon fixing <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallelism</ConceptLink>:** <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">Axiom IV</ConceptLink> postulates that through a point not on a line there passes at most one parallel to it; the existence of at least one is a theorem of <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>.
- **Upon adding <ConceptLink targetId="continuidad">continuity</ConceptLink>:** The points of the line form a continuum: free of infinitesimal segments (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-arquimedes">Archimedes, V.1</ConceptLink>) and gaps (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-completitud">completeness, V.2</ConceptLink>).
