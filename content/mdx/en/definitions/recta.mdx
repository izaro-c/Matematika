<Capitular letra="A" /> <VisualBind element="lineAB" color="musgo">**line**</VisualBind> is a <ConceptLink targetId="concepto-primitivo">primitive concept</ConceptLink> of geometry: the simplest linear object in <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink>, lacking width and thickness.

Being a basic notion, it is not defined in terms of other figures. Instead, its formal behavior and how it relates to <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="plano">planes</ConceptLink> are established by the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma">axioms</ConceptLink> of the adopted <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-axiomatico">formal system</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal Definition">
  In a <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-geometrica">geometric structure</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{S}$, a **line** <VisualBind element="lineAB" color="musgo">$\ell$</VisualBind> is any element of the <ConceptLink targetId="dominio">primitive domain of lines</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{L}$:
  $$
  \ell \in \mathcal{L}
  $$
  In isolation, the line possesses no intrinsic properties; its mathematical meaning arises exclusively from the relations that the axioms establish between lines and other elements of <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink>.
</Definicion>

### Fundamental axiomatic relations

Since the line lacks internal structure, what can be asserted about it depends on which groups of axioms are incorporated into the adopted <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-axiomatico">geometric system</ConceptLink> (a specific system may be restricted to only the first few):

- **In <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence</ConceptLink> systems:** They establish positional incidence relations among points, lines, and planes: two distinct points determine a unique line (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-1">I.1</ConceptLink>), every line contains at least two incident points (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-2">I.2</ConceptLink>), there exist points not incident on a given line (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-3">I.3</ConceptLink>), a line lies entirely in a plane if two of its points are incident upon it (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-6">I.6</ConceptLink>), and if two distinct planes share a point, they share at least a second common point (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-7">I.7</ConceptLink>), deductively proving from these axioms that their mutual intersection is precisely a line.
- **Upon adding <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">order or betweenness</ConceptLink>:** The points of the line are arranged sequentially and without endpoints (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-1">II.1</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-2">II.2</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-3">II.3</ConceptLink>), enabling the formalization of unbounded extension in both directions and bounding figures such as the <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink> and the <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta">ray</ConceptLink>.
- **Upon adding <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink>:** Allows pairs of points on the line to be transported and compared as congruent segments (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-1">III.1</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-2">III.2</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-3">III.3</ConceptLink>), without yet presupposing a numerical notion of <ConceptLink targetId="distancia">distance</ConceptLink>.
- **Upon fixing <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallelism</ConceptLink>:** Postulates the upper bound and uniqueness of coplanar parallel lines (at most one parallel in <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">Euclid's postulate, IV</ConceptLink>), given that the existence of at least one parallel is a prior theorem of <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>.
- **Upon adding <ConceptLink targetId="continuidad">continuity</ConceptLink>:** Guarantees that the line of points behaves as a measurable continuum: free of infinitesimal magnitudes (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-arquimedes">Archimedes, V.1</ConceptLink>) and gaps (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-completitud">completeness, V.2</ConceptLink>).
