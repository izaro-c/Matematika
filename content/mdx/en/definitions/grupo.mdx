<Capitular letra="A" /> group is a <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto">set</ConceptLink> equipped with an <ConceptLink targetId="asociatividad">associative</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="operacion-binaria">binary operation</ConceptLink> that has an <ConceptLink targetId="elemento-neutro">identity element</ConceptLink> and in which every element has an <ConceptLink targetId="elemento-inverso">inverse</ConceptLink>. It is the <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-algebraica">algebraic structure</ConceptLink> that describes the composition of reversible <ConceptLink targetId="transformacion">transformations</ConceptLink>: the <ConceptLink targetId="isometria">isometries</ConceptLink> of the plane, the <ConceptLink targetId="simetria">symmetries</ConceptLink> of a figure and the <ConceptLink targetId="permutacion">permutations</ConceptLink> of a finite set all form groups.

<Definicion title="Group">
  A group is a pair $(G, \cdot)$ consisting of a <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto" isDependency={true}>set</ConceptLink> $G$ and a <ConceptLink targetId="operacion-binaria" isDependency={true}>binary operation</ConceptLink>
  $$
  \begin{aligned}
  \cdot\colon G \times G &\longrightarrow G \\
  (a, b) &\longmapsto a \cdot b
  \end{aligned}
  $$
  satisfying:
  1. **<ConceptLink targetId="asociatividad" isDependency={true}>Associativity</ConceptLink>**: $\forall a, b, c \in G, \ (a \cdot b) \cdot c = a \cdot (b \cdot c)$.
  2. **<ConceptLink targetId="elemento-neutro" isDependency={true}>Identity element</ConceptLink>**: $\exists e \in G \ \forall a \in G, \ a \cdot e = e \cdot a = a$.
  3. **<ConceptLink targetId="elemento-inverso" isDependency={true}>Inverse element</ConceptLink>**: $\forall a \in G \ \exists a^{-1} \in G, \ a \cdot a^{-1} = a^{-1} \cdot a = e$.
</Definicion>

That the operation takes values in $G$ (so-called *closure*) is part of the definition of a binary operation on $G$; the identity axiom also guarantees that $G \neq \emptyset$.

### Classification of Groups

Groups are distinguished by the <ConceptLink targetId="conmutatividad">commutativity</ConceptLink> of the operation and the <ConceptLink targetId="cardinalidad">cardinality</ConceptLink> of the set:

| Type of group | Condition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| <ConceptLink targetId="grupo-abeliano">Abelian</ConceptLink> (commutative) | $\forall a, b \in G, \ a \cdot b = b \cdot a$ | The <ConceptLink targetId="numero-entero">integers</ConceptLink> under addition, $(\mathbb{Z}, +)$ |
| Non-abelian | $\exists a, b \in G, \ a \cdot b \neq b \cdot a$ | The <ConceptLink targetId="matriz-invertible">invertible matrices</ConceptLink> under multiplication, $(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{R}), \cdot)$, with $n \geq 2$ |
| Finite | $\lvert G \rvert$ finite | The <ConceptLink targetId="grupo-simetrico">symmetric group</ConceptLink> $S_n$, with $\lvert S_n \rvert = n!$ |
| Infinite | $G$ infinite | The nonzero <ConceptLink targetId="numero-real">real numbers</ConceptLink> under multiplication, $(\mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}, \cdot)$ |

  <PropiedadesGrupo title="Uniqueness">
    <PropiedadItem id="unicidad-elemento-neutro" title="Uniqueness of the identity element">
      In every group $(G, \cdot)$ the <ConceptLink targetId="elemento-neutro">identity element</ConceptLink> is unique: if $e$ and $e'$ are identities, $e = e \cdot e' = e'$.
    </PropiedadItem>
    <PropiedadItem id="unicidad-elemento-inverso" title="Uniqueness of the inverse element">
      Each $a \in G$ has a unique <ConceptLink targetId="elemento-inverso">inverse</ConceptLink>: if $b$ and $c$ are inverses of $a$, by <ConceptLink targetId="asociatividad">associativity</ConceptLink> $b = b \cdot (a \cdot c) = (b \cdot a) \cdot c = c$.
    </PropiedadItem>
  </PropiedadesGrupo>

  <PropiedadesGrupo title="Computation rules">
    <PropiedadItem id="leyes-cancelacion-grupo" title="Cancellation laws">
      For all $a, b, c \in G$: $(a \cdot b = a \cdot c \implies b = c)$ and $(b \cdot a = c \cdot a \implies b = c)$; it suffices to multiply by $a^{-1}$ on the left or on the right.
    </PropiedadItem>
    <PropiedadItem id="inverso-producto-grupo" title="Inverse of a product">
      For all $a, b \in G$: $(a \cdot b)^{-1} = b^{-1} \cdot a^{-1}$.
    </PropiedadItem>
  </PropiedadesGrupo>
</SeccionPropiedades>
