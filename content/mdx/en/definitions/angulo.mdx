<Capitular letra="A" />n angle is the geometric figure constituted by the union of two <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta">rays</ConceptLink> sharing a common origin or <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertex</ConceptLink>. It is the fundamental concept for measuring angular divergence between directions and laying the groundwork for trigonometry in the <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="">
  Given two distinct <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta" isDependency={true}>rays</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="rayOA" color="pavo">$\overrightarrow{OA}$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="rayOB" color="pavo">$\overrightarrow{OB}$</VisualBind> originating at a common <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>point</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="pA" color="carbon">$O$</VisualBind>, the angle <VisualBind element="angleBOA" color="musgo">$\angle AOB$</VisualBind> is the planar figure formed by their union:
  $$
  \angle AOB = \overrightarrow{OA} \cup \overrightarrow{OB}
  $$
  Its metric aperture $\theta \in [0, \pi]$ radians is related to the dot product of the unit direction vectors $\vec{u}, \vec{v}$:
  $$
  \cos \theta = \frac{\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v}}{\|\vec{u}\| \|\vec{v}\|}
  $$
</Definicion>

### Classification by angular measure

In the Euclidean plane, convex angles are classified according to their angular measure $\theta$:

| Angle type | Measure in radians | Measure in sexagesimal degrees |
|---|---|---|
| <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-agudo">Acute</ConceptLink> | $0 < \theta < \frac{\pi}{2}$ | $0^\circ < \theta < 90^\circ$ |
| <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto">Right</ConceptLink> | $\theta = \frac{\pi}{2}$ | $\theta = 90^\circ$ |
| <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-obtuso">Obtuse</ConceptLink> | $\frac{\pi}{2} < \theta < \pi$ | $90^\circ < \theta < 180^\circ$ |
| <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-llano">Straight</ConceptLink> | $\theta = \pi$ | $\theta = 180^\circ$ |
