<Capitular letra="A" />n angle is the <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto">unordered pair</ConceptLink> of two distinct <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta" isDependency={true}>rays</ConceptLink> sharing a common <ConceptLink targetId="punto">origin</ConceptLink> whose carrier <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink> are distinct.

In the foundation of <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-sintetica">synthetic geometry</ConceptLink>, the angle is neither a numerical magnitude nor a metric measure, but a formal object constructed from the notion of a <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta">ray</ConceptLink> and non-<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad" isDependency={true}>collinearity</ConceptLink> in the <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal Definition of Angle">
Given a <ConceptLink targetId="punto">point</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="pO" color="carbon">$O$</VisualBind> in the primitive <ConceptLink targetId="dominio">domain</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{P}$ and two distinct points <VisualBind element="pA" color="terracota">$A$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pB" color="terracota">$B$</VisualBind> $\in \mathcal{P} \setminus \{O\}$ such that the triple $\{O, A, B\}$ is not <ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink>, the **angle** <VisualBind element="angleBOA" color="musgo">$\angle AOB$</VisualBind> (also denoted $\angle(h, k)$) is the <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto">pair</ConceptLink> formed by the two <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta">rays</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="rayOA" color="pavo">$h = \overrightarrow{OA}$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="rayOB" color="pavo">$k = \overrightarrow{OB}$</VisualBind>:

$$
\angle AOB = \{ \overrightarrow{OA}, \overrightarrow{OB} \} = \{h, k\}
$$

The rays $h$ and $k$ are called the <ConceptLink targetId="lado">sides</ConceptLink> of the angle, and the common point $O$ is called the <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertex</ConceptLink>. Its associated **point trace** $\operatorname{tr}(\angle AOB) \subset \mathcal{P}$ is the set union of the traces of its sides:

$$
\operatorname{tr}(\angle AOB) = \operatorname{tr}(\overrightarrow{OA}) \cup \operatorname{tr}(\overrightarrow{OB})
$$
</Definicion>

  <PropiedadesGrupo title="Structural and Order Properties">
    <PropiedadItem id="simetria-lados" title="Symmetry of Sides">
      Being defined as an unordered pair of rays, the angle is symmetric with respect to its sides: $\angle AOB = \angle BOA$ and $\angle(h, k) = \angle(k, h)$.
    </PropiedadItem>
    <PropiedadItem id="coplanaridad-soporte" title="Coplanarity and Unique Carrier Plane">
      By <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-4">Axiom I.4</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-5">Axiom I.5</ConceptLink>, the non-<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinearity</ConceptLink> of the triple $\{O, A, B\}$ uniquely determines a <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> $\pi \in \Pi$ that contains the trace of the angle: $\operatorname{tr}(\angle AOB) \subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\pi)$.
    </PropiedadItem>
    <PropiedadItem id="interior-angular" title="Angular Interior as Convex Region">
      Every angle $\angle AOB$ in a plane $\pi$ determines an open and <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-convexo">convex</ConceptLink> region called the **interior of the angle** ($\operatorname{Int}(\angle AOB)$), formally defined as the <ConceptLink targetId="interseccion">intersection</ConceptLink> of the open <ConceptLink targetId="semiplano">half-plane</ConceptLink> with respect to the <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> $\overleftrightarrow{OA}$ containing $B$ and the open half-plane with respect to the line $\overleftrightarrow{OB}$ containing $A$.
    </PropiedadItem>
  </PropiedadesGrupo>
