<Capitular letra="T" />wo coplanar <ConceptLink targetId="secante">secant</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>lines</ConceptLink> in a <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink> are **perpendicular** if their <ConceptLink targetId="punto">point</ConceptLink> of intersection forms <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto" isDependency={true}>right angles</ConceptLink>.

The perpendicularity relation formalizes positional rigidity between secant lines in <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>, establishing the foundation for the study of <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo-rectangulo">right triangles</ConceptLink> and geometric orthogonal constructions.

<Definicion title="Formal Definition of Perpendicular Lines">
Let <VisualBind element="lineAB" color="terracota">$\ell_1$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="perpABP" color="musgo">$\ell_2$</VisualBind> $\in \mathcal{L}$ be two coplanar <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>lines</ConceptLink> in a <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink> $\pi \in \Pi$ ($\operatorname{tr}(\ell_1) \cup \operatorname{tr}(\ell_2) \subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\pi)$) that are <ConceptLink targetId="secante">secant</ConceptLink> at a unique <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>point</ConceptLink> of intersection <VisualBind element="pP" color="musgo">$P$</VisualBind> $\in \mathcal{P}$ ($\operatorname{tr}(\ell_1) \cap \operatorname{tr}(\ell_2) = \{P\}$).

Let <VisualBind element="pA" color="terracota">$A$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pB" color="terracota">$B$</VisualBind> $\in \operatorname{tr}(\ell_1) \setminus \{P\}$ be two points such that $P$ <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre" isDependency={true}>is between</ConceptLink> $A$ and $B$ ($A * P * B$), determining the opposite <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta" isDependency={true}>rays</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="rayPA" color="terracota">$h_1 = \overrightarrow{PA}$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="rayPB" color="terracota">$h_1' = \overrightarrow{PB}$</VisualBind> on $\ell_1$. Let <VisualBind element="pC" color="musgo">$C$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pD" color="musgo">$D$</VisualBind> $\in \operatorname{tr}(\ell_2) \setminus \{P\}$ be two points such that $P$ is between $C$ and $D$ ($C * P * D$), determining the opposite rays <VisualBind element="rayPC" color="musgo">$h_2 = \overrightarrow{PC}$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="rayPD" color="musgo">$h_2' = \overrightarrow{PD}$</VisualBind> on $\ell_2$.

The lines $\ell_1$ and $\ell_2$ are **perpendicular** ($\ell_1 \perp \ell_2$) if and only if the <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angle</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="angleAPC" color="ocre">$\angle(h_1, h_2) = \angle APC$</VisualBind> formed at the <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertex</ConceptLink> $P$ is a <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto" isDependency={true}>right angle</ConceptLink>:

$$
\ell_1 \perp \ell_2 \iff \operatorname{Recto}(\angle(h_1, h_2))
$$
</Definicion>

<Nota>
In the axiomatic hierarchy, perpendicularity is a purely synthetic concept belonging to <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>, prior to and independent of any <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">parallel axiom</ConceptLink>. Unlike unique parallelism, the existence and uniqueness of the perpendicular through a point does not depend on the curvature of space.
</Nota>
