<Capitular letra="T" />wo intersecting <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink> in a <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> are perpendicular when their intersection forms four congruent adjacent <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink>. It is the foundational concept of orthogonality in Euclidean geometry, underpinning Cartesian coordinate frames and orthogonal projections.

<Definicion title="">
  Given two coplanar <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>lines</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="lineAB" color="musgo">$L_1$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="perpABP" color="pavo">$L_2$</VisualBind> in a plane $\mathcal{P}$ intersecting at a point $P = L_1 \cap L_2$, $L_1$ and $L_2$ are perpendicular ($L_1 \perp L_2$) if and only if the four angles formed at their intersection are <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto" isDependency={true}>right angles</ConceptLink> of measure $\frac{\pi}{2}$ radians ($90^\circ$):
  $$
  L_1 \perp L_2 \iff \forall i \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}, \; \theta_i \cong 90^\circ
  $$
  In vector terms, for non-zero direction vectors $\vec{u}, \vec{v}$ of $L_1$ and $L_2$, this corresponds to a vanishing dot product: $\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} = 0$.
</Definicion>

<Separador />

### Geometric criteria and representations

- **Synthetic geometry**: Two lines are perpendicular if and only if they form congruent adjacent supplementary angles.
- **Analytic geometry**: In $\mathbb{R}^2$, two non-vertical lines with slopes $m_1, m_2 \in \mathbb{R}$ are perpendicular if and only if $m_1 \cdot m_2 = -1$.
- **Vector geometry**: Two one-dimensional affine subspaces are orthogonal when their direction vectors satisfy $\langle \vec{u}, \vec{v} \rangle = 0$.
