<Capitular letra="A" /> **half-plane** is either of the two regions into which a <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink> divides the <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> of a <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink>. The dividing line is called the **boundary** or edge and is not part of the half-plane.

In <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-sintetica">synthetic geometry</ConceptLink>, the partition of the plane into exactly two <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-disjunto">disjoint</ConceptLink> regions is a consequence of the Plane Separation Theorem, proven from <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-4" isDependency={true}>Pasch's Axiom</ConceptLink> (Axiom II.4) and linear <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">order</ConceptLink> axioms.

<Definicion title="Formal Definition">
  Let $\pi$ be a <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink> and let $\ell$ be a <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink> contained in $\pi$. On the set of <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> of the plane outside the line ($\pi \setminus \ell$), define the binary relation $\sim_\ell$:
  $$
  A \sim_\ell B \iff A = B \lor \operatorname{seg}(AB) \cap \ell = \emptyset
  $$
  where $\operatorname{seg}(AB)$ denotes the open <ConceptLink targetId="segmento" isDependency={true}>segment</ConceptLink> joining points $A$ and $B$ (excluding its endpoints).

  By the Plane Separation Theorem, $\sim_\ell$ is an equivalence relation partitioning $\pi \setminus \ell$ into exactly two equivalence classes in the quotient space $(\pi \setminus \ell) / {\sim_\ell} = \{ \mathcal{H}_1, \mathcal{H}_2 \}$. Each of these classes $\mathcal{H}_1$ and $\mathcal{H}_2$ is an open **half-plane** determined by $\ell$.
</Definicion>

    <PropiedadItem id="conjunto-convexo" title="Convexity">
      Every half-plane is a <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-convexo">convex set</ConceptLink>: if it contains any two points $A$ and $B$, it entirely contains the open segment $\operatorname{seg}(AB)$ joining them.
    </PropiedadItem>
    <PropiedadItem id="angulo" title="Interior polygonal regions">
      The finite intersection of half-planes whose boundaries cross allows the formal definition of the interior region of an <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angle</ConceptLink>, a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink>, and any <ConceptLink targetId="poligono">convex polygon</ConceptLink>.
