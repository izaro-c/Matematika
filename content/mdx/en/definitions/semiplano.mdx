<Capitular letra="A" /> **half-plane** is either of the two regions into which a <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink> divides the <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> of a <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink> containing it. The dividing line is called the **edge** and is not part of the half-plane.

That the line divides the plane into exactly two <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-disjunto">disjoint</ConceptLink> regions is the content of the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-separacion-plano" isDependency={true}>plane separation theorem</ConceptLink>, which Hilbert derives from <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-4" isDependency={true}>Pasch's Axiom</ConceptLink> (Axiom II.4) and the other <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre" isDependency={true}>order</ConceptLink> axioms. The half-plane is thus the planar analogue of the <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta">ray</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal Definition of Half-Plane">
  Let $\pi$ be a plane and let <VisualBind element="recta-l" color="carbon">$\ell$</VisualBind> be a line with $\operatorname{tr}(\ell) \subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\pi)$. On the set of points of the plane not incident with the line, $\operatorname{tr}(\pi) \setminus \operatorname{tr}(\ell)$, define the <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-binaria">binary relation</ConceptLink> $\sim_\ell$:
  $$
  A \sim_\ell B \iff A = B \lor \operatorname{seg}(AB) \cap \operatorname{tr}(\ell) = \emptyset
  $$
  where <VisualBind element="segAB" color="terracota">$\operatorname{seg}(AB)$</VisualBind> is the open <ConceptLink targetId="segmento" isDependency={true}>segment</ConceptLink> with endpoints <VisualBind element="pA" color="terracota">$A$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="pB" color="terracota">$B$</VisualBind>.

  By the plane separation theorem, $\sim_\ell$ is an <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-equivalencia">equivalence relation</ConceptLink> with exactly two <ConceptLink targetId="clase-equivalencia">equivalence classes</ConceptLink>: $(\operatorname{tr}(\pi) \setminus \operatorname{tr}(\ell)) / {\sim_\ell} = \{ \mathcal{H}_1, \mathcal{H}_2 \}$. Each of these classes is an open **half-plane** with edge <VisualBind element="recta-l" color="carbon">$\ell$</VisualBind>: <VisualBind element="semiplano-1" color="terracota">$\mathcal{H}_1$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="semiplano-2" color="pavo">$\mathcal{H}_2$</VisualBind>. The half-plane containing a point $B$ is denoted $\mathcal{H}_\ell(B)$.
</Definicion>

  <PropiedadesGrupo title="Geometric Properties and Convexity">
    <PropiedadItem 
      id="convexidad-semiplano" 
      title="Convexity of the Half-Plane"
      formula="A, B \in \mathcal{H} \implies \operatorname{seg}(AB) \subseteq \mathcal{H}"
    >
      Every open half-plane $\mathcal{H}$ is a <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-convexo">convex set</ConceptLink>: if <VisualBind element="pA" color="terracota">$A$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pB" color="terracota">$B$</VisualBind> $\in$ <VisualBind element="semiplano-1" color="terracota">$\mathcal{H}_1$</VisualBind>, the open <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="segAB" color="terracota">$\operatorname{seg}(AB)$</VisualBind> is contained in $\mathcal{H}_1$. Conversely, for a point <VisualBind element="pC" color="pavo">$C \in \mathcal{H}_2$</VisualBind> in the opposite half-plane, the segment <VisualBind element="segAC" color="granada">$\operatorname{seg}(AC)$</VisualBind> crosses the edge line <VisualBind element="recta-l" color="carbon">$\ell$</VisualBind> at a point <VisualBind element="pX" color="granada">$X$</VisualBind>. This follows from the definition of $\sim_\ell$: if a point $X$ of $\operatorname{seg}(AB)$ were not in $\mathcal{H}$, either $X$ would be incident with $\ell$ or $\operatorname{seg}(AX)$ would meet $\ell$; in both cases $\operatorname{seg}(AB) \supseteq \operatorname{seg}(AX) \cup \{X\}$ would meet $\ell$, contradicting $A \sim_\ell B$.
    </PropiedadItem>

    <PropiedadItem 
      id="generacion-interiores-poligonales" 
      title="Interiors as Intersections of Half-Planes"
      formula="\operatorname{Int}(A_1 A_2 \cdots A_n) = \bigcap_{i=1}^n \mathcal{H}_i"
    >
      Finite intersections of open half-planes define the interior of an <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angle</ConceptLink>, $\operatorname{Int}(\angle AOB) = \mathcal{H}_{\overleftrightarrow{OA}}(B) \cap \mathcal{H}_{\overleftrightarrow{OB}}(A)$, of a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink>, and of a <ConceptLink targetId="poligono-convexo">convex polygon</ConceptLink> $A_1 A_2 \cdots A_n$, where $\mathcal{H}_i$ is the half-plane whose edge is the <ConceptLink targetId="recta-soporte">supporting line</ConceptLink> $\overleftrightarrow{A_i A_{i+1}}$ (indices modulo $n$) and which contains the remaining vertices. These regions are convex, since the <ConceptLink targetId="interseccion">intersection</ConceptLink> of convex sets is convex.
    </PropiedadItem>
  </PropiedadesGrupo>
</SeccionPropiedades>
