<Capitular letra="T" />he derivative is the core concept of <ConceptLink targetId="calculo-diferencial">differential calculus</ConceptLink> quantifying the <ConceptLink targetId="tasa-cambio-instantanea">instantaneous rate of change</ConceptLink> of a <ConceptLink targetId="funcion-real">real-valued function</ConceptLink> with respect to its <ConceptLink targetId="variable-independiente">independent variable</ConceptLink>. Geometrically, it represents the <ConceptLink targetId="pendiente">slope</ConceptLink> of the <ConceptLink targetId="recta-tangente">tangent line</ConceptLink> to the <ConceptLink targetId="grafica-funcion">function's graph</ConceptLink> at a given point.

<Definicion title="">
  Given a function $f: I \to \mathbb{R}$ defined on an <ConceptLink targetId="intervalo-abierto">open interval</ConceptLink> $I \subset \mathbb{R}$ and a point $x_0 \in I$, the derivative of $f$ at $x_0$, denoted by $f'(x_0)$, is defined as the <ConceptLink targetId="limite" isDependency={true}>limit</ConceptLink> of the <ConceptLink targetId="cociente-incremental">difference quotient</ConceptLink> as the increment $h$ approaches zero:
  $$
  f'(x_0) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x_0 + h) - f(x_0)}{h}
  $$
  If this limit exists and is finite, the function $f$ is said to be <ConceptLink targetId="diferenciabilidad">differentiable</ConceptLink> at $x_0$.
</Definicion>

### Historical notations and conventions

Throughout the development of mathematical analysis, several standard notations for the derivative of a function $y = f(x)$ have been established:

| Notation | Mathematical expression | Context / Creator |
|---|---|---|
| Lagrange | $f'(x), f''(x), \dots, f^{(n)}(x)$ | Standard calculus and <ConceptLink targetId="analisis-real">real analysis</ConceptLink> |
| Leibniz | $\frac{df}{dx}, \frac{d^2f}{dx^2}, \dots, \frac{d^n f}{dx^n}$ | Infinitesimal calculus and <ConceptLink targetId="ecuaciones-diferenciales">differential equations</ConceptLink> |
| Newton | $\dot{y}, \ddot{y}$ | <ConceptLink targetId="mecanica-clasica">Classical mechanics</ConceptLink> and time derivatives |
| Euler | $D f, D^2 f, \dots, D^n f$ | <ConceptLink targetId="operador-diferencial">Linear differential operators</ConceptLink> |

### Properties

- **<ConceptLink targetId="teorema-derivabilidad-implica-continuidad">Differentiability and continuity</ConceptLink>**: If a function is differentiable at a point, it is necessarily <ConceptLink targetId="continuidad">continuous</ConceptLink> at that point.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="teorema-linealidad-derivada">Linearity of derivative operator</ConceptLink>**: The differential operator preserves linear combinations: $(\alpha f + \beta g)' = \alpha f' + \beta g'$.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="teorema-regla-producto">Leibniz product rule</ConceptLink>**: Differentiation rule for the product of two differentiable functions: $(f \cdot g)' = f'g + fg'$.
- **<ConceptLink targetId="teorema-regla-cadena">Chain rule</ConceptLink>**: Differentiation of the <ConceptLink targetId="composicion-funciones">composition of functions</ConceptLink>: $(f \circ g)'(x) = f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x)$.
