<Capitular letra="T" />he derivative is the central concept of differential calculus, measuring the instantaneous rate of change of a real function with respect to its independent variable. Geometrically, it represents the slope of the tangent line to the function's graph at a given point.

<Definicion title="">
  Given a real function $f: I \to \mathbb{R}$ defined on an open interval $I \subset \mathbb{R}$ and a point $x_0 \in I$, the derivative of $f$ at $x_0$, denoted $f'(x_0)$, is defined as the <ConceptLink targetId="limite" isDependency={true}>limit</ConceptLink> of the difference quotient as the increment $h$ approaches zero:
  $$
  f'(x_0) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x_0 + h) - f(x_0)}{h}
  $$
  If this limit exists and is finite, the function $f$ is said to be differentiable at $x_0$.
</Definicion>

<Separador />

### Historical notations and conventions

Throughout the development of mathematical analysis, multiple standard notations for the derivative of a function $y = f(x)$ have been established:

| Notation | Mathematical expression | Primary context |
|---|---|---|
| Lagrange | $f'(x), f''(x), \dots, f^{(n)}(x)$ | Standard calculus and real analysis |
| Leibniz | $\frac{df}{dx}, \frac{d^2f}{dx^2}, \dots, \frac{d^n f}{dx^n}$ | Infinitesimal calculus and differential equations |
| Newton | $\dot{y}, \ddot{y}$ | Classical mechanics and time derivatives |
| Euler | $D f, D^2 f, \dots, D^n f$ | Linear differential operators |
