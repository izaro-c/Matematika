<Capitular letra="C" />ardinality **(or** cardinal number) is the rigorous mathematical generalization of the concept of the "number of elements" of a collection, applicable with full validity to both finite and infinite sets.

Introduced by Georg Cantor, the theory of cardinals overcomes the paradoxes of infinity by dispensing with sequential counting and relying instead on the principle of equipotence: two sets possess the same intrinsic size if and only if their elements can be paired one-to-one via a <ConceptLink targetId="biyeccion">bijection</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Equipotence and cardinal comparison">
  Two <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto" isDependency={true}>sets</ConceptLink> $A$ and $B$ are **equipotent** (denoted $A \sim B$) if and only if there exists a <ConceptLink targetId="biyeccion" isDependency={true}>bijection</ConceptLink> $f\colon A \longrightarrow B$:
  $$
  |A| = |B| \iff \exists f\colon A \longrightarrow B \quad (f \text{ bijective})
  $$
  The order between cardinals is defined via injections: one writes $|A| \le |B|$ if there exists an injective map from $A$ into $B$. By the Cantor-Bernstein-Schröder theorem, if $|A| \le |B|$ and $|B| \le |A|$, then necessarily $|A| = |B|$.
  
  By Cantor's theorem, for any set $A$, its power set <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-potencia">$\wp(A)$</ConceptLink> has strictly greater cardinality: $|A| < |\wp(A)|$.
</Definicion>

### Scale of infinities and dimensions in geometry

Cardinality enables the classification of geometric domains according to their ontological size:
- **Finite structures:** Sets with finite cardinal $n \in \mathbb{N}$, such as the 7 points of the <ConceptLink targetId="modelo-fano">Fano plane</ConceptLink> or the 3 points of the <ConceptLink targetId="modelo-tres-puntos">three-point model</ConceptLink>.
- **Countably infinite:** The cardinal $\aleph_0 = |\mathbb{N}| = |\mathbb{Q}|$, characteristic of discrete sequences and rational numbers.
- **The power of the continuum:** The cardinal $\mathfrak{c} = |\mathbb{R}| = 2^{\aleph_0}$. In continuous Euclidean geometry, every line contains $\mathfrak{c}$ <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink>, a density that formally guarantees geometric <ConceptLink targetId="continuidad">continuity</ConceptLink> and the absence of gaps in space.
