<Capitular letra="L" />ogical **consistency** (or syntactic consistency) is the metatheoretical property of a formal theory or deductive system guaranteeing the absence of internal contradictions.

In classical deductive systems, if a theory were contradictory, the principle of explosion (*ex falso quodlibet*) would permit the formal derivation of any arbitrary proposition formulated in its language. Consequently, inconsistency collapses the entire deductive edifice, stripping its <ConceptLink targetId="teorema">theorems</ConceptLink> of all discriminative or epistemic value.

<Definicion title="Syntactic and semantic consistency">
  Let $\mathcal{T}$ be a theory with axioms $\Phi$ formulated in a <ConceptLink targetId="lenguaje-formal">formal language</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{L}_\sigma$.
  
  $\mathcal{T}$ is said to be **syntactically consistent** if for no formula $\varphi$ is it possible to formally derive both $\varphi$ and its negation $\neg \varphi$:
  $$
  \mathcal{T} \text{ is consistent} \iff \forall \varphi \in \mathcal{L}_\sigma, \quad \neg (\Phi \vdash \varphi \land \Phi \vdash \neg \varphi)
  $$
  On the other hand, $\mathcal{T}$ is **semantically consistent** (or satisfiable) if there exists at least one <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-modelos">model</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{M}$ such that $\mathcal{M} \models \mathcal{T}$.
  
  By <ConceptLink targetId="godel">Gödel</ConceptLink>'s completeness theorem in first-order <ConceptLink targetId="logica">logic</ConceptLink>, both notions coincide completely:
  $$
  \Phi \nvdash \bot \iff \exists \mathcal{M} \ (\mathcal{M} \models \Phi)
  $$
</Definicion>

### Relative consistency in the foundations of geometry

By Gödel's second incompleteness theorem, no consistent, recursively axiomatizable formal theory that contains elementary arithmetic can prove its own consistency. Consistency proofs are, in general, relative (**relative consistency**): a model of one theory is built inside another, and the first is consistent if the second is.

- **<ConceptLink targetId="sistema-euclidiano">Euclidean geometry</ConceptLink>:** In Chapter II of the *Grundlagen*, <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink> established the relative consistency of Euclidean geometry by interpreting its <ConceptLink targetId="concepto-primitivo">primitive</ConceptLink> terms in the Cartesian <ConceptLink targetId="modelo-cartesiano">model</ConceptLink> $\mathbb{R}^3$. If the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-cuerpo-real">theory of real numbers</ConceptLink> $\mathbb{R}$ is consistent, Euclidean geometry is consistent.
- **Non-Euclidean geometries:** Historically, neither <ConceptLink targetId="bolyai">Bolyai</ConceptLink> nor <ConceptLink targetId="lobachevski">Lobachevsky</ConceptLink> succeeded in proving the consistency of <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-hiperbolico">hyperbolic geometry</ConceptLink>. It was <ConceptLink targetId="beltrami">Eugenio Beltrami</ConceptLink> (1868), Felix <ConceptLink targetId="klein">Klein</ConceptLink>, and <ConceptLink targetId="poincare">Henri Poincaré</ConceptLink> who proved it using analytic models in the Euclidean <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink>, such as the Poincaré <ConceptLink targetId="modelo-poincare">disk</ConceptLink>, showing that hyperbolic consistency reduces to Euclidean consistency.
