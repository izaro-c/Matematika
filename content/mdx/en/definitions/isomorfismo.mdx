<Capitular letra="A" />n **isomorphism** is a bijective correspondence between two mathematical <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-geometrica" isDependency={true}>structures</ConceptLink> sharing the same <ConceptLink targetId="signatura" isDependency={true}>signature</ConceptLink>, such that all <ConceptLink targetId="concepto-primitivo">primitive relations</ConceptLink>, operations, and distinguished constants are preserved.

Two isomorphic structures satisfy the same sentences of <ConceptLink targetId="logica">logic</ConceptLink> written in their signature: they differ only in the nature of their elements, about which no symbol of the signature says anything.

<Definicion title="Formal definition of isomorphism">
  Let <VisualBind element="estructura-m" color="carbon">$\mathcal{M}$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="estructura-n" color="carbon">$\mathcal{N}$</VisualBind> be two structures over a signature $\sigma$ with <ConceptLink targetId="dominio">domains</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="dominio-m" color="carbon">$M$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="dominio-n" color="carbon">$N$</VisualBind> respectively. A <ConceptLink targetId="biyeccion" isDependency={true}>bijection</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="biyeccion-f" color="terracota">$f\colon M \longrightarrow N$</VisualBind> is an **isomorphism** from $\mathcal{M}$ onto $\mathcal{N}$ if it satisfies:
  1. For each $n$-ary relation symbol $R \in \sigma$, interpreted as <VisualBind element="relacion-m" color="mora">$R^{\mathcal{M}} \subseteq M^n$</VisualBind> in $\mathcal{M}$ and as <VisualBind element="relacion-n" color="mora">$R^{\mathcal{N}} \subseteq N^n$</VisualBind> in $\mathcal{N}$, and for all $a_1, \dots, a_n \in M$:
     $$
     (a_1, \dots, a_n) \in R^{\mathcal{M}} \iff (f(a_1), \dots, f(a_n)) \in R^{\mathcal{N}}
     $$
  2. For each $k$-ary <ConceptLink targetId="funcion">function</ConceptLink> symbol $g \in \sigma$ and for all $a_1, \dots, a_k \in M$:
     $$
     f(g^{\mathcal{M}}(a_1, \dots, a_k)) = g^{\mathcal{N}}(f(a_1), \dots, f(a_k))
     $$
  3. For each constant symbol $c \in \sigma$:
     $$
     f(c^{\mathcal{M}}) = c^{\mathcal{N}}
     $$

  If $\sigma$ has several sorts, like the signature of geometry with <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="plano">planes</ConceptLink>, each structure has one domain per sort and $f$ is a family of bijections, one per sort; in the conditions above, each $a_i$ is mapped by the bijection of its sort. The structures are *isomorphic*, $\mathcal{M} \cong \mathcal{N}$, if there exists an isomorphism from $\mathcal{M}$ onto $\mathcal{N}$.
</Definicion>

Condition 1 requires the <ConceptLink targetId="equivalencia-logica">equivalence</ConceptLink>, not just the <ConceptLink targetId="implicacion">implication</ConceptLink> $\Rightarrow$. If $R^{\mathcal{M}} = \emptyset$ and $R^{\mathcal{N}} \neq \emptyset$ on the same domain, the <ConceptLink targetId="funcion-identidad">identity</ConceptLink> satisfies $\Rightarrow$ without being an isomorphism.

### Categoricity and automorphisms

Two notions of <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-modelos">model theory</ConceptLink> are defined through isomorphisms:

- **<ConceptLink targetId="categoricidad">Categoricity</ConceptLink>:** An <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-axiomatico">axiomatic system</ConceptLink> is categorical if all its models are mutually isomorphic. For example, the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-euclidiano">Euclidean system</ConceptLink>, which is <ConceptLink targetId="dimension">three-dimensional</ConceptLink> and includes the <ConceptLink targetId="continuidad">continuity</ConceptLink> axioms, is categorical: every model satisfying its axioms is isomorphic to the Cartesian <ConceptLink targetId="modelo-cartesiano">space</ConceptLink> $\mathbb{R}^3$.
- **Automorphisms and <ConceptLink targetId="simetria">symmetry</ConceptLink>:** An isomorphism from $\mathcal{M}$ onto $\mathcal{M}$ is called an <ConceptLink targetId="automorfismo">automorphism</ConceptLink>. The <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto">set</ConceptLink> of all automorphisms forms a <ConceptLink targetId="grupo">group</ConceptLink> under <ConceptLink targetId="composicion-funciones">composition</ConceptLink>, which algebraically captures the internal symmetries of the structure. In the <ConceptLink targetId="modelo-fano">Fano plane</ConceptLink>, for example, the automorphisms are the <ConceptLink targetId="colineacion">collineations</ConceptLink>, which permute points and lines while preserving <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence</ConceptLink>, and they form a group of 168 elements.
