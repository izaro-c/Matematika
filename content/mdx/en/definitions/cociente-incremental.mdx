<Capitular letra="T" />he **difference quotient** (or average rate of change) quantifies the average variation rate experienced by a <ConceptLink targetId="numero-real">real</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="funcion" isDependency={true}>function</ConceptLink> per unit of change in its independent variable across an <ConceptLink targetId="intervalo" isDependency={true}>interval</ConceptLink>. It constitutes the analytical and geometric core preceding the calculus of instantaneous variations, acting as a deductive bridge toward the <ConceptLink targetId="derivada">derivative</ConceptLink> through the operation of <ConceptLink targetId="limite">limit</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal definition of difference quotient">
  Let $I \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ be an <ConceptLink targetId="intervalo" isDependency={true}>interval</ConceptLink> of the <ConceptLink targetId="numero-real">real line</ConceptLink> and let $f\colon I \longrightarrow \mathbb{R}$ be a <ConceptLink targetId="funcion" isDependency={true}>function</ConceptLink>. Fixing a base point $x \in I$ and given a non-zero increment $h \in \mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$ such that $x + h \in I$, the **difference quotient** of $f$ between $x$ and $x + h$ is defined as:
  $$
  \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} = \frac{f(x + h) - f(x)}{h}
  $$
  Equivalently, setting $x_1 = x + h$ with $x_1 \neq x$, the quotient is expressed as:
  $$
  \frac{f(x_1) - f(x)}{x_1 - x}
  $$
  where $\Delta y = f(x + h) - f(x)$ represents the absolute change experienced by the dependent variable and $\Delta x = h$ represents the increment of the independent variable.
</Definicion>

If $f$ is <ConceptLink targetId="funcion-derivable">differentiable</ConceptLink> at $x$, as $h$ tends to zero these secant lines tend to the <ConceptLink targetId="recta-tangente">tangent line</ConceptLink> to the graph at $(x, f(x))$.

      The asymptotic behavior of the difference quotient as the increment tends to zero defines the notion of <ConceptLink targetId="derivada">derivative</ConceptLink>. If the <ConceptLink targetId="limite">limit</ConceptLink> exists in $\mathbb{R}$, the resulting value $f'(x)$ represents the instantaneous rate of change of the function at the point $x$.
