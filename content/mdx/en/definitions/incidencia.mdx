<Capitular letra="I" />**ncidence** is a <ConceptLink targetId="concepto-primitivo">primitive relation</ConceptLink> of geometry that formalizes the idea of membership or positional contact between geometric objects (such as <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink>, and <ConceptLink targetId="plano">planes</ConceptLink>).

Being a primitive concept, it is not defined via algebraic formulas; its behavior is established by the axioms of the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-incidencia">incidence system</ConceptLink>. In common language, saying that a point and a line "are incident" means that the point lies on the line, or equivalently, that the line passes through that point.

<Definicion title="Formal Declaration">
  In a many-sorted <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-geometrica">incidence structure</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{S} = (\mathcal{P}, \mathcal{L}, \Pi, \mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}}, \mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{P}\Pi})$, **incidence** is formulated through independent typed binary relations between <ConceptLink targetId="dominio">primitive domains</ConceptLink>:
  $$
  \mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}} \subseteq \mathcal{P} \times \mathcal{L}, \quad \mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{P}\Pi} \subseteq \mathcal{P} \times \Pi
  $$
  - **Point and line:** If $(P, \ell) \in \mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}}$, we formally write $P \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell$ (point $P$ is incident with line $\ell$; the expression $P \in \ell$ is a common informal shorthand by abuse of language identifying a line with its incident points).
  - **Point and plane:** If $(P, \pi) \in \mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{P}\Pi}$, we formally write $P \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi$ (point $P$ is incident with plane $\pi$; similarly, $P \in \pi$ by abuse of language).

  The relation of a line lying on a plane ($\ell \subseteq \pi$) is not an independent primitive relation, but a derived concept established by <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-6">Axiom I.6</ConceptLink>:
  $$
  \ell \subseteq \pi \iff \forall P \in \mathcal{P} \, (P \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell \implies P \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi)
  $$
</Definicion>

### Role in synthetic geometry

Incidence constitutes the first deductive stratum of <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-sintetica">synthetic geometry</ConceptLink>:

- **In incidence systems:** Establishes the basic configuration of <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink> via axioms (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-1">I.1</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-2">I.2</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-3">I.3</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-4">I.4</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-5">I.5</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-6">I.6</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-7">I.7</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-8">I.8</ConceptLink>), determining when points are <ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> or <ConceptLink targetId="coplanaridad">coplanar</ConceptLink>.
- **Independence from metrics:** Incidence does not require notions of <ConceptLink targetId="distancia">distance</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angle</ConceptLink>, or <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">order</ConceptLink>, making it equally applicable to <ConceptLink targetId="continuidad">continuous</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="modelo-fano">finite geometries</ConceptLink>.
