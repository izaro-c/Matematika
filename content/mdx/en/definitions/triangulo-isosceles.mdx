<Capitular letra="A" />n **isosceles triangle** is a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangle</ConceptLink> that has at least two <ConceptLink targetId="lado" isDependency={true}>sides</ConceptLink> that are mutually <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink>.

When two particular congruent sides are distinguished, they are called the lateral sides or legs, while the third side is called the <ConceptLink targetId="base">base</ConceptLink>. The <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angle</ConceptLink> formed by the two congruent sides is the angle at the <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertex</ConceptLink>, and the two angles adjacent to the base are the <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-base">base angles</ConceptLink>. The family of isosceles triangles includes <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo-equilatero">equilateral triangles</ConceptLink> as the special case in which all three sides are congruent.

<Definicion title="Formal definition of an isosceles triangle">
Let $\triangle ABC = \{\overline{AB}, \overline{BC}, \overline{CA}\}$ be a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangle</ConceptLink> in a <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> $\pi$, determined by three non-<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> $A$, $B$, and $C$.

The triangle $\triangle ABC$ is said to be an **isosceles triangle** if at least two of its <ConceptLink targetId="lado" isDependency={true}>sides</ConceptLink> are mutually <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink>:

$$
\overline{AB} \cong \overline{AC} \quad\lor\quad \overline{AB} \cong \overline{BC} \quad\lor\quad \overline{BC} \cong \overline{CA}
$$

In the setting where $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{AC}$, the sides $\overline{AB}$ and $\overline{AC}$ are called the lateral sides, and the remaining side $\overline{BC}$ is called the **<ConceptLink targetId="base">base</ConceptLink>** of the isosceles triangle. The common vertex $A$ is the main vertex, the angle $\angle BAC$ is the **angle at the <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertex</ConceptLink>**, and the angles $\angle ABC$ and $\angle ACB$ are the **<ConceptLink targetId="angulo-base">base angles</ConceptLink>**.
</Definicion>
