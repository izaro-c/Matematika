<Capitular letra="I" />**ntersection** is an operation between <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto" isDependency={true}>sets</ConceptLink> that gathers all elements belonging simultaneously to each of the considered sets. In the underlying propositional <ConceptLink targetId="logica">logic</ConceptLink>, it corresponds directly to the logical conjunction connective ($\land$).

It makes it possible to define composite geometric regions and to describe the points common to two figures.

<Definicion title="Formal definition of intersection">
  Let $A$ and $B$ be two sets. The **intersection** of $A$ and $B$ (denoted $A \cap B$) is the set consisting of all elements belonging to both $A$ and $B$:
  $$
  A \cap B = \{x \mid x \in A \land x \in B\}
  $$
  In general, for any non-empty family of sets $\{A_i\}_{i \in I}$, its generalized intersection is defined as:
  $$
  \bigcap_{i \in I} A_i = \{x \mid \forall i \in I, \ x \in A_i\}
  $$
</Definicion>

### Algebraic properties and geometric delimitation

The intersection operation satisfies the Boolean laws of <ConceptLink targetId="conmutatividad">commutativity</ConceptLink> ($A \cap B = B \cap A$), <ConceptLink targetId="asociatividad">associativity</ConceptLink> ($A \cap (B \cap C) = (A \cap B) \cap C$), and idempotence ($A \cap A = A$). When two sets share no elements in common ($A \cap B = \emptyset$), they are called <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-disjunto">disjoint sets</ConceptLink>.

The intersection of <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-convexo">convex sets</ConceptLink> always preserves convexity:
- The <ConceptLink targetId="interior-angulo">interior</ConceptLink> of an <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angle</ConceptLink> is the intersection of two open <ConceptLink targetId="semiplano">half-planes</ConceptLink>: for each side, the one bounded by its <ConceptLink targetId="recta-soporte">supporting line</ConceptLink> that contains the other side.
- The <ConceptLink targetId="interior-triangulo">interior</ConceptLink> of a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink> is the intersection of three open half-planes: for each <ConceptLink targetId="lado">side</ConceptLink>, the one bounded by its supporting line that contains the opposite <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertex</ConceptLink>. The interior of a convex <ConceptLink targetId="poligono">polygon</ConceptLink> is obtained in the same way, with one half-plane per side.

Intersection also distinguishes the positions of two distinct <ConceptLink targetId="coplanaridad">coplanar</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink>: two <ConceptLink targetId="secante">secant lines</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="lineL1" color="terracota">$\ell_1$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="lineL2" color="pavo">$\ell_2$</VisualBind> have exactly one common <ConceptLink targetId="punto">point</ConceptLink>, <VisualBind element="pInter" color="mora">$\operatorname{tr}(\ell_1) \cap \operatorname{tr}(\ell_2) = \{P\}$</VisualBind>, and two distinct <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallel lines</ConceptLink> have none: $\operatorname{tr}(\ell_1) \cap \operatorname{tr}(\ell_2) = \emptyset$.
