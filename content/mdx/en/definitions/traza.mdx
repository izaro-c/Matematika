<Capitular letra="T" />he **point trace** is the set-theoretic assignment associating each <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>line</ConceptLink> or <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink> in the <ConceptLink targetId="dominio">primitive domain</ConceptLink> with the exact <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto">set</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>points</ConceptLink> maintaining a formal relation of <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia" isDependency={true}>synthetic incidence</ConceptLink> with that entity.

Since the <ConceptLink targetId="concepto-primitivo">primitive concepts</ConceptLink> of point ($\mathcal{P}$), line ($\mathcal{L}$), and plane ($\Pi$) are ontological domains with <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-disjunto">pairwise empty intersections</ConceptLink> ($\mathcal{P} \cap \mathcal{L} = \emptyset$, $\mathcal{P} \cap \Pi = \emptyset$, $\mathcal{L} \cap \Pi = \emptyset$) in <ConceptLink targetId="logica">many-sorted logic</ConceptLink>, the point trace $\operatorname{tr}$ acts as the canonical mediation <ConceptLink targetId="operador">operator</ConceptLink> allowing set-theoretic operations (intersection, inclusion, or <ConceptLink targetId="cardinalidad">cardinality</ConceptLink>) without incurring type collapse.

<Definicion title="Point trace operator">
Given a <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-geometrica">geometric structure</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{S} = (\mathcal{P}, \mathcal{L}, \Pi, \mathbf{I})$ with a <ConceptLink targetId="signatura">many-sorted signature</ConceptLink>, the **trace** <ConceptLink targetId="operador">operator</ConceptLink> ($\operatorname{tr}$) maps each element in the primitive linear ($\mathcal{L}$) and planar ($\Pi$) <ConceptLink targetId="dominio">domains</ConceptLink> to its corresponding subset in the <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-potencia">power set</ConceptLink> of points $\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P})$:

1. **Point trace of a line**:
   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   \operatorname{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}\colon \mathcal{L} &\longrightarrow \mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}) \\
   \ell &\longmapsto \operatorname{tr}(\ell) = \{ P \in \mathcal{P} \mid P \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell \}
   \end{aligned}
   $$

2. **Point trace of a plane**:
   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   \operatorname{tr}_{\Pi}\colon \Pi &\longrightarrow \mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}) \\
   \pi &\longmapsto \operatorname{tr}(\pi) = \{ P \in \mathcal{P} \mid P \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi \}
   \end{aligned}
   $$
</Definicion>

By <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-2">Axiom I.2</ConceptLink> (existence of at least two points on a line) and <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-1">Axiom I.1</ConceptLink> (unique line determination), the mapping $\operatorname{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}\colon \mathcal{L} \to \mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P})$ is strictly <ConceptLink targetId="inyectividad">injective</ConceptLink>.
    </PropiedadItem>

    <PropiedadItem 
      id="inyectividad-planar" 
      title="Injectivity and unique plane determination"
      formula="\forall \pi_1, \pi_2 \in \Pi \ (\operatorname{tr}(\pi_1) = \operatorname{tr}(\pi_2) \iff \pi_1 = \pi_2)"

      Two distinct planes cannot share the exact same point trace. By <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-4">Axiom I.4</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-5">Axiom I.5</ConceptLink> (plane determination and uniqueness by three non-collinear points), the mapping $\operatorname{tr}_{\Pi}\colon \Pi \to \mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P})$ is strictly <ConceptLink targetId="inyectividad">injective</ConceptLink>.
    </PropiedadItem>
  </PropiedadesGrupo>

  <PropiedadesGrupo title="Incidence relations and relative spatial positions">
    <PropiedadItem 
      id="inclusion-recta-plano" 
      title="Inclusion of a line in a plane"
      formula="\operatorname{tr}(\ell) \subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\pi) \iff \forall P \in \mathcal{P} \ (P \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell \implies P \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi)"
    >
      A line $\ell$ lies in a plane $\pi$ if and only if its trace is contained in that of the plane ($\operatorname{tr}(\ell) \subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\pi)$). By <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-6">Axiom I.6</ConceptLink>, verifying that two distinct points of the line belong to the plane suffices to ensure total inclusion.
    </PropiedadItem>

    <PropiedadItem 
      id="interseccion-planos" 
      title="Intersection of secant planes"
      formula="\pi_1 \neq \pi_2 \land \operatorname{tr}(\pi_1) \cap \operatorname{tr}(\pi_2) \neq \emptyset \implies \exists! \ell \in \mathcal{L} \ (\operatorname{tr}(\pi_1) \cap \operatorname{tr}(\pi_2) = \operatorname{tr}(\ell))"
    >
      When two distinct planes intersect at least at one common point (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-7">Axiom I.7</ConceptLink>), the set-theoretic intersection of their point traces coincides uniquely with the trace of a single common secant line (<ConceptLink targetId="teorema-interseccion-planos">plane intersection line theorem</ConceptLink>).
    </PropiedadItem>

    <PropiedadItem 
      id="paralelismo-planos" 
      title="Parallel planes in space"
      formula="\pi_1 \parallel \pi_2 \iff (\pi_1 = \pi_2 \lor \operatorname{tr}(\pi_1) \cap \operatorname{tr}(\pi_2) = \emptyset)"
    >
      Two planes in space are <ConceptLink targetId="planos-paralelos">parallel</ConceptLink> if and only if they are identical or their point traces are mutually disjoint sets, ensuring the reflexivity of the relation.
    </PropiedadItem>

    <PropiedadItem 
      id="posicion-rectas-coplanares" 
      title="Coplanar lines: parallelism and secancy"
      formula="\operatorname{tr}(\ell_1) \cup \operatorname{tr}(\ell_2) \subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\pi) \implies (\ell_1 \parallel \ell_2 \iff \ell_1 = \ell_2 \lor \operatorname{tr}(\ell_1) \cap \operatorname{tr}(\ell_2) = \emptyset)"
    >
      For two lines with guaranteed <ConceptLink targetId="coplanaridad">coplanarity</ConceptLink> in a plane $\pi$, the relation of <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallelism</ConceptLink> ($\ell_1 \parallel \ell_2$) is reflexive and equivalent to them being identical or disjoint; if distinct and not parallel, they are <ConceptLink targetId="secante">secant</ConceptLink> with $\lvert \operatorname{tr}(\ell_1) \cap \operatorname{tr}(\ell_2) \rvert = 1$.
    </PropiedadItem>

    <PropiedadItem 
      id="rectas-cruzadas" 
      title="Characterization of skew lines in space"
      formula="\forall \pi \in \Pi \ (\operatorname{tr}(\ell_1) \cup \operatorname{tr}(\ell_2) \not\subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\pi)) \implies \operatorname{tr}(\ell_1) \cap \operatorname{tr}(\ell_2) = \emptyset"
    >
      Two lines in space are <ConceptLink targetId="rectas-cruzadas">skew lines</ConceptLink> if no common plane contains both simultaneously, which necessarily implies that their point traces are disjoint without being parallel.
    </PropiedadItem>
  </PropiedadesGrupo>
</SeccionPropiedades>
