<Capitular letra="A" /> **binary operation** (or internal law of composition) on a non-empty <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto" isDependency={true}>set</ConceptLink> is a <ConceptLink targetId="funcion" isDependency={true}>function</ConceptLink> that assigns to each <ConceptLink targetId="par-ordenado">ordered pair</ConceptLink> of elements of the set a unique result belonging to that set. The <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-algebraica">algebraic structures</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="algebra-abstracta">abstract algebra</ConceptLink>, such as <ConceptLink targetId="semigrupo">semigroups</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="monoide">monoids</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="grupo">groups</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="cuerpo">fields</ConceptLink>, are defined as sets equipped with one or more binary operations.

<Definicion title="Formal definition of binary operation">
  Let $S$ be a non-empty <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto" isDependency={true}>set</ConceptLink>. A **binary operation** on $S$ is a <ConceptLink targetId="funcion" isDependency={true}>function</ConceptLink> from the <ConceptLink targetId="producto-cartesiano" isDependency={true}>Cartesian product</ConceptLink> $S \times S$ to $S$
  $$
  \begin{aligned}
  *\colon S \times S &\longrightarrow S \\
  (a, b) &\longmapsto a * b
  \end{aligned}
  $$
  that associates with each ordered pair $(a, b) \in S \times S$ a unique element $a * b \in S$.
</Definicion>

Since the <ConceptLink targetId="codominio">codomain</ConceptLink> of the function is $S$ itself, $S$ is closed under the operation:
$$
\forall a, b \in S, \quad a * b \in S
$$

When $S$ is finite, a binary operation can be given by a table. In the figure, $S = \{0, 1, 2\}$ and $a * b$ is the larger of $a$ and $b$. The row of $a$ and the column of $b$ meet in the cell of $a * b$. The nine cells are the pairs of <VisualBind element="productoCartesiano" color="canela">$S \times S$</VisualBind>, and each one has a unique value in <VisualBind element="conjuntoS" color="carbon">$S$</VisualBind>. Dragging $a$ and $b$ picks a cell, and the <VisualBind element="flechaPar" color="terracota">arrow</VisualBind> points to its value. Pressing a cell changes its value. If some cell takes the value <VisualBind element="elementoFuera" color="granada">$3$</VisualBind>, as ordinary <ConceptLink targetId="adicion">addition</ConceptLink> would with $1 + 2$, the table no longer defines a binary operation on $S$, because $3 \notin S$.

<Separador />

### Properties of a binary operation

A binary operation $*$ on $S$ may be <ConceptLink targetId="asociatividad">associative</ConceptLink> or <ConceptLink targetId="conmutatividad">commutative</ConceptLink>, and may have an <ConceptLink targetId="elemento-neutro">identity element</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="elemento-inverso">inverse elements</ConceptLink> with respect to that identity.

    <PropiedadItem id="operacion-inducida" title="Induced operation on a stable subset">
      If a non-empty <ConceptLink targetId="subconjunto">subset</ConceptLink> $T \subseteq S$ is <ConceptLink targetId="subconjunto-estable">stable</ConceptLink> under $*$ ($a * b \in T$ for all $a, b \in T$), the restriction of $*$ to $T \times T$ is a binary operation $*|_T\colon T \times T \longrightarrow T$ on $T$, the induced operation.
