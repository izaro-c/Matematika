<Capitular letra="A" /> **bijection** (or bijective map) is a <ConceptLink targetId="funcion" isDependency={true}>function</ConceptLink> between two <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto" isDependency={true}>sets</ConceptLink> that associates each element of the <ConceptLink targetId="dominio-funcion">domain</ConceptLink> with a unique element of the <ConceptLink targetId="codominio">codomain</ConceptLink>, such that every element of the codomain is the image of exactly one element of the domain.

By combining the preservation of distinctions with exhaustive coverage of the codomain, a bijection establishes a one-to-one pairing between both sets that guarantees the existence of a well-defined inverse function.

<Definicion title="Formal definition of bijection">
  Let $A$ and $B$ be two sets. A function $f\colon A \longrightarrow B$ is a **bijection** if and only if it simultaneously satisfies two conditions:
  1. **<ConceptLink targetId="inyectividad">Injectivity</ConceptLink>:** Distinct elements of the domain have distinct images:
     $$
     \forall x_1, x_2 \in A, \quad f(x_1) = f(x_2) \implies x_1 = x_2
     $$
  2. **<ConceptLink targetId="sobreyectividad">Surjectivity</ConceptLink>:** Every element of the codomain is the image of at least one element of the domain:
     $$
     \forall y \in B, \quad \exists x \in A \quad (f(x) = y)
     $$
  
  Equivalently, $f$ is bijective if and only if there exists a unique inverse function $f^{-1}\colon B \longrightarrow A$ such that $f^{-1} \circ f = \operatorname{id}_A$ and $f \circ f^{-1} = \operatorname{id}_B$.
</Definicion>

### Structural importance and geometric applications

Bijections play a unifying role across multiple branches of mathematics:
- **Measure of size:** Two sets have the same <ConceptLink targetId="cardinalidad">cardinality</ConceptLink> if and only if there exists a bijection between them ($|A| = |B|$).
- **Structural preservation:** A formal <ConceptLink targetId="isomorfismo">isomorphism</ConceptLink> is a bijection between <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-geometrica">structures</ConceptLink> that additionally preserves all relations and operations of their <ConceptLink targetId="signatura">signature</ConceptLink>.
- **Transformations and congruence:** In geometry, the <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink> of composite figures (such as <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangles</ConceptLink> or <ConceptLink targetId="poligono">polygons</ConceptLink>) is defined via a bijective correspondence between their homologous <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertices</ConceptLink> that preserves the congruence of the respective <ConceptLink targetId="lado">sides</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink>.
