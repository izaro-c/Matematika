<Capitular letra="A" /> **primitive concept** (or undefined term) is a starting notion of a <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-formal">formal theory</ConceptLink> —a sort of object or a relation— that is not defined in terms of prior concepts.

Every mathematical theory requires starting terms. If one attempted to define every notion through simpler ones, one would fall into a circle or an infinite regress. To avoid this, axiomatic systems select a basic set of primitive notions and establish their behavior through the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma">axioms</ConceptLink> of the adopted <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-axiomatico">formal system</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Epistemological Definition">
  In a formal language specified by a <ConceptLink targetId="signatura">signature</ConceptLink> $\sigma$, **primitive concepts** are the basic non-logical starting symbols (sorts/object types and relation or function symbols) that lack a prior definition within the theory:
  $$
  \sigma = (\mathcal{S}_{\text{sorts}}, \mathcal{F}, \mathcal{R}, \text{ar})
  $$
  Their meaning does not lie in a physical description, but in the conditions that the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma">axioms</ConceptLink> impose on their interpretations within a <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-geometrica">mathematical structure</ConceptLink> or <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-modelos">model</ConceptLink>.
</Definicion>

<Separador />

### Primitive concepts in <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-sintetica">synthetic geometry</ConceptLink>

In <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert's</ConceptLink> system, primitive concepts are divided into two categories:

- **Object types (<ConceptLink targetId="dominio">primitive domains</ConceptLink>):** The three elementary sorts of entities: <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> ($\mathcal{P}$), <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink> ($\mathcal{L}$), and <ConceptLink targetId="plano">planes</ConceptLink> ($\Pi$). <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">Space</ConceptLink> is not a primitive sort: it denotes the totality of these objects (the <ConceptLink targetId="universo-discurso">universe of discourse</ConceptLink>) or, as a figure, the set of all points.
- **Primitive relations:** Basic links between objects, such as <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence</ConceptLink> (lying on), <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">betweenness</ConceptLink> (linear ordering), and <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink> (of <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segments</ConceptLink> and of <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink>).
