<Capitular letra="A" />n **acute triangle** is a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangle</ConceptLink> whose three <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-interior">interior angles</ConceptLink> are <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-agudo">acute</ConceptLink>, that is, each of them is strictly smaller than a <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto" isDependency={true}>right angle</ConceptLink> under the <ConceptLink targetId="orden-angulos" isDependency={true}>order of angles</ConceptLink>.

The notion is formulated within <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink> without appealing to any metric measure. By the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-angulo-exterior">exterior angle theorem</ConceptLink>, every triangle has at least two acute angles; the defining feature of an acute triangle is that its third angle also satisfies this upper bound.

<Definicion title="Formal definition of acute triangle">
Let $\triangle ABC = \{\overline{AB}, \overline{BC}, \overline{CA}\}$ be a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangle</ConceptLink> in a <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> $\pi$, determined by three non-<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> $A$, $B$, and $C$.

It is said that $\triangle ABC$ is an **acute triangle** if each of its three <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-interior">interior angles</ConceptLink> is strictly smaller than a <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto" isDependency={true}>right angle</ConceptLink> $R$ under the <ConceptLink targetId="orden-angulos" isDependency={true}>order of angles</ConceptLink>:

$$
\angle CAB < R \quad \land \quad \angle ABC < R \quad \land \quad \angle BCA < R
$$

Each angle satisfying this condition is called an <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-agudo">acute angle</ConceptLink>.
</Definicion>

Since the three <ConceptLink targetId="mediatriz">perpendicular bisectors</ConceptLink> of the sides intersect at a point equidistant from the vertices and each interior angle is acute, this concurrence point lies within the open triangular region.
    </PropiedadItem>

    <PropiedadItem 
      id="ortocentro-interior-acutangulo" 
      title="Interiority of the orthocenter"
      formula="H \in \operatorname{Int}(\triangle ABC)"
    >
      The <ConceptLink targetId="ortocentro">orthocenter</ConceptLink> $H$ of an acute triangle is located in the <ConceptLink targetId="interior-triangulo">interior of the triangle</ConceptLink>. The three <ConceptLink targetId="altura">altitudes</ConceptLink> have their feet in the interior of the segments determined by the opposite sides, so that their common point of concurrency lies inside the figure.
    </PropiedadItem>
  </PropiedadesGrupo>

<Nota>
Every equilateral triangle is acute in <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>, as its three congruent angles cannot be right or obtuse without contradicting the properties of adjacent supplementary angles and the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-angulo-exterior">exterior angle theorem</ConceptLink>.
</Nota>
