<Capitular letra="A" /> homogeneous binary relation is **symmetric** if the link connecting any two elements is reciprocal and does not depend on the order in which its terms are stated.

This abstract logical property must not be confused with the notion of geometric <ConceptLink targetId="simetria">symmetry</ConceptLink> of a figure (which denotes an isometry or transformation leaving invariant a subset of points): here we are concerned strictly with the reversibility of a formal relation.

<Definicion title="Formal definition of symmetric relation">
  Let $A$ be a <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto" isDependency={true}>set</ConceptLink> and let $R \subseteq A \times A$ be a <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-binaria">binary relation</ConceptLink> on $A$. The relation $R$ is **symmetric** if and only if:
  $$
  \forall x, y \in A, \quad x \, R \, y \implies y \, R \, x
  $$
  In algebraic terms, denoting by $R^{-1} = \{(y, x) \mid (x, y) \in R\}$ the inverse or transpose relation of $R$, symmetry is equivalent to the equality:
  $$
  R = R^{-1}
  $$
</Definicion>

### Presence in geometric axioms and theorems

Relational symmetry intervenes decisively across several deductive strata:

- **Symmetry of betweenness:** In <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-1">Axiom II.1</ConceptLink> of linear order, it is postulated that the betweenness relation is symmetric with respect to its endpoints: if $A * B * C$, then necessarily $C * B * A$.
- **Derivation in congruence:** In the <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink> of segments, the symmetric property ($\overline{AB} \cong \overline{CD} \implies \overline{CD} \cong \overline{AB}$) is not postulated independently, but rather proven as a rigorous <ConceptLink targetId="teorema">theorem</ConceptLink> by combining the reflexivity of <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-1">Axiom III.1</ConceptLink> with the transitivity of <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-2">Axiom III.2</ConceptLink>.
- **Parallelism:** The <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallelism</ConceptLink> relation between coplanar lines is symmetric ($\ell \parallel m \implies m \parallel \ell$) due to the intrinsic commutativity of disjunction and set intersection, a defining trait of every <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-equivalencia">equivalence relation</ConceptLink>.
