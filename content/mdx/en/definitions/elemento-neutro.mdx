<Capitular letra="A" />n **identity element** (or neutral element) with respect to a <ConceptLink targetId="operacion-binaria" isDependency={true}>binary operation</ConceptLink> is a distinguished element of a <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto">set</ConceptLink> that leaves every other element unaltered when operated with it. It serves as the cornerstone for characterizing fundamental algebraic structures such as <ConceptLink targetId="monoide">monoids</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="grupo">groups</ConceptLink>, and <ConceptLink targetId="anillo">rings</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal definition of identity element">
  Let $S$ be a <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto">set</ConceptLink> equipped with a <ConceptLink targetId="operacion-binaria" isDependency={true}>binary operation</ConceptLink> $*\colon S \times S \longrightarrow S$. An element $e \in S$ is a **two-sided identity element** (or simply identity) for $*$ if and only if it satisfies the two-sided condition:
  $$
  \forall a \in S, \quad e * a = a \quad \land \quad a * e = a
  $$
  If it satisfies only $e_L * a = a$ for all $a \in S$, it is called a left identity element; if it satisfies $a * e_R = a$ for all $a \in S$, it is called a right identity element. When an algebraic structure has a two-sided identity element, it is said to be a structure with identity.
</Definicion>

  <PropiedadesGrupo title="Uniqueness and algebraic properties">
    <PropiedadItem id="unicidad-elemento-neutro" title="Uniqueness of the identity element" formula="e_1 = e_1 * e_2 = e_2">
      In any structure $(S, *)$ equipped with a <ConceptLink targetId="operacion-binaria">binary operation</ConceptLink>, the two-sided identity element is unique. If $e_1, e_2 \in S$ are both identity elements, since $e_2$ is a right identity we have $e_1 * e_2 = e_1$, and since $e_1$ is a left identity we have $e_1 * e_2 = e_2$. Consequently:
      $$
      e_1 = e_1 * e_2 = e_2
      $$
      This deduction further demonstrates that if an operation possesses a left identity $e_L$ and a right identity $e_R$, they necessarily coincide ($e_L = e_L * e_R = e_R$), precluding the coexistence of distinct one-sided identities of opposite sides unless no two-sided identity exists.
    </PropiedadItem>
  </PropiedadesGrupo>
</SeccionPropiedades>
