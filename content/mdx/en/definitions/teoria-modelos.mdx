<Capitular letra="M" />odel **theory** is the branch of <ConceptLink targetId="logica">mathematical logic</ConceptLink> that investigates the semantic meaning of formal theories through the systematic study of relationships between formulas of a language and the structures in which these formulas make sense or hold true.

It constitutes the formal bridge between abstract syntax —the <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto">set</ConceptLink> of stated statements and <ConceptLink targetId="axioma">axioms</ConceptLink>— and concrete semantics, embodied by the mathematical <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-geometrica">structures</ConceptLink> that interpret those postulates.

<Definicion title="Structure, satisfaction, and model">
  Given a <ConceptLink targetId="signatura">signature</ConceptLink> $\sigma$, a **$\sigma$-structure** $\mathcal{M}$ comprises one or more non-empty <ConceptLink targetId="dominio">domains</ConceptLink> together with an assignment interpreting every relation, function, or constant symbol of $\sigma$ over those universes.
  
  A structure $\mathcal{M}$ is said to satisfy a formula $\varphi$ under an assignment (denoted $\mathcal{M} \models \varphi$) if the formula evaluates to true in the structure according to Tarski's classical semantic definitions.
  
  A structure $\mathcal{M}$ is a **model** of a theory or <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-axiomatico">axiomatic system</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{T}$ (denoted $\mathcal{M} \models \mathcal{T}$) if it simultaneously satisfies every axiom composing the theory:
  $$
  \mathcal{M} \models \mathcal{T} \iff \forall \varphi \in \mathcal{T}, \ \mathcal{M} \models \varphi
  $$
</Definicion>

### Relative consistency, independence, and categoricity

In the foundations of mathematics, model theory plays a decisive demonstrative role:

1. **Relative <ConceptLink targetId="consistencia-logica">logical consistency</ConceptLink>:** By Gödel's completeness theorem, a first-order theory is consistent if and only if it has at least one model. Thus, the analytic construction of the Cartesian <ConceptLink targetId="modelo-cartesiano">model</ConceptLink> $\mathbb{R}^3$ proves the consistency of <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-euclidiano">Euclidean geometry</ConceptLink>, while the Poincaré <ConceptLink targetId="modelo-poincare">disk</ConceptLink> guarantees the relative consistency of <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-hiperbolico">hyperbolic geometry</ConceptLink>.
2. **Axiomatic <ConceptLink targetId="independencia-logica">independence</ConceptLink>:** To show that an axiom cannot be deduced from the remainder, it suffices to construct a model satisfying all postulates except the axiom in question.
3. **<ConceptLink targetId="categoricidad">Categoricity</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="isomorfismo">isomorphism</ConceptLink>:** A theory is categorical in a given <ConceptLink targetId="cardinalidad">cardinality</ConceptLink> $\kappa$ if all of its models of size $\kappa$ are mutually isomorphic, which ensures that the theory describes a unique structure up to renaming of its elements.
