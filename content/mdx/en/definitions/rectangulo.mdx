<Capitular letra="A" /> **rectangle** is a convex <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrilatero" isDependency={true}>quadrilateral</ConceptLink> whose four <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-interior">interior angles</ConceptLink> are <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto" isDependency={true}>right angles</ConceptLink>.

The notion is stated in an absolute manner as an angular configuration within the theory of polygons. However, its effective existence characterizes Euclidean geometry: in <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink>, the existence of a single rectangle is equivalent to the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">parallel postulate</ConceptLink>, so that in <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-hiperbolico">hyperbolic geometry</ConceptLink> no quadrilateral can possess four right angles, as evidenced by the properties of <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrilatero-saccheri">Saccheri quadrilaterals</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrilatero-lambert">Lambert quadrilaterals</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal definition of rectangle">
Let $ABCD = \{\overline{AB}, \overline{BC}, \overline{CD}, \overline{DA}\}$ be a convex <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrilatero" isDependency={true}>quadrilateral</ConceptLink> in a <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> $\pi$, determined by four coplanar <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> $A$, $B$, $C$, and $D$ with no three of them <ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink>.

It is said that $ABCD$ is a **rectangle** if its four <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-interior">interior angles</ConceptLink> are <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruent</ConceptLink> to a <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto" isDependency={true}>right angle</ConceptLink> $R$:

$$
\angle DAB \cong R \quad \land \quad \angle ABC \cong R \quad \land \quad \angle BCD \cong R \quad \land \quad \angle CDA \cong R
$$

The points $A, B, C, D$ are the <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertices</ConceptLink> of the rectangle, and the segments $\overline{AB}, \overline{BC}, \overline{CD}, \overline{DA}$ are its <ConceptLink targetId="lado">sides</ConceptLink>.
</Definicion>

<SeccionPropiedades>
  <PropiedadesGrupo title="Euclidean properties of the rectangle">
    <PropiedadItem 
      id="paralelismo-lados-opuestos-rectangulo" 
      title="Parallelism of opposite sides"
      formula="\overleftrightarrow{AB} \parallel \overleftrightarrow{CD} \quad \land \quad \overleftrightarrow{BC} \parallel \overleftrightarrow{DA}"
    >
      In <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-euclidiano">Euclidean geometry</ConceptLink>, the lines supporting the opposite sides of a rectangle are <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallel</ConceptLink>. Since two lines perpendicular to a common transversal do not intersect by the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-angulos-alternos-internos">alternate interior angles theorem</ConceptLink>, every rectangle is in particular a <ConceptLink targetId="paralelogramo">parallelogram</ConceptLink>.
    </PropiedadItem>

    <PropiedadItem 
      id="congruencia-lados-opuestos-rectangulo" 
      title="Congruence of opposite sides"
      formula="\overline{AB} \cong \overline{CD} \quad \land \quad \overline{BC} \cong \overline{DA}"
    >
      The opposite sides of a rectangle are mutually congruent. Being a parallelogram, the diagonal $\overline{AC}$ determines two congruent right triangles $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle CDA$ by the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-ala">ASA criterion</ConceptLink>, which guarantees the congruence of the respective opposite sides.
    </PropiedadItem>

    <PropiedadItem 
      id="congruencia-diagonales-rectangulo" 
      title="Congruence of diagonals"
      formula="\overline{AC} \cong \overline{BD}"
    >
      The <ConceptLink targetId="diagonal">diagonals</ConceptLink> $\overline{AC}$ and $\overline{BD}$ of a rectangle are mutually congruent. The right triangles $\triangle DAB$ and $\triangle CBA$ share the leg $\overline{AB}$ and have congruent legs $\overline{AD} \cong \overline{BC}$; by the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-lal">SAS criterion</ConceptLink>, both triangles are congruent and their hypotenuses are congruent.
    </PropiedadItem>
  </PropiedadesGrupo>
</SeccionPropiedades>

<Separador />

<Nota>
The Euclidean parallel postulate plays a decisive role in the existence of rectangles: while the definition requires solely the orthogonality of four angles, the possibility of closing such a figure without contradiction requires that the sum of the interior angles of a quadrilateral be exactly four right angles.
</Nota>
