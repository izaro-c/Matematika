<Capitular letra="T" />wo open <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta" isDependency={true}>rays</ConceptLink> on the same <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> are **opposite rays** if they share their <ConceptLink targetId="punto">origin</ConceptLink> and, for every point on one and every point on the other, the <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre" isDependency={true}>betweenness</ConceptLink> relation holds with respect to the common origin.

The notion is based on the axioms of <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">order</ConceptLink> (Hilbert's Group II, §4). By <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-2">Axiom II.2</ConceptLink>, given any point on a line there exist points on both sides of it. Every point on a line determines a partition of the remaining points of the line into exactly two opposite rays, an indispensable concept for defining <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-suplementarios">supplementary angles</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-opuestos-vertice">vertical angles</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal Definition of Opposite Rays">
Let $\ell \in \mathcal{L}$ be a <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> and let $O \in \operatorname{tr}(\ell)$ be a <ConceptLink targetId="punto">point</ConceptLink>. Two open <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta" isDependency={true}>rays</ConceptLink> $h, h' \subset \operatorname{tr}(\ell)$ with origin $O$ are **opposite rays** if for every point $A \in h$ and every point $B \in h'$, the point $O$ is <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre" isDependency={true}>between</ConceptLink> $A$ and $B$ ($A * O * B$):

$$
\operatorname{op}(h, h') \iff \forall A \in h \, \forall B \in h' \, (A * O * B)
$$

Equivalently, if $h = \operatorname{sem}(OA)$ is the open ray determined by a point $A \in \operatorname{tr}(\ell) \setminus \{O\}$, its opposite ray $h' = \operatorname{sem}(OB)$ is described as the set of points $P$ such that $O$ is between $A$ and $P$:

$$
h' = \{ P \in \operatorname{tr}(\ell) \setminus \{O\} \mid A * O * P \}
$$

The corresponding closed rays, $\overrightarrow{OA} = h \cup \{O\}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB} = h' \cup \{O\}$, are called closed opposite rays and their <ConceptLink targetId="interseccion">intersection</ConceptLink> coincides with the common origin: $\overrightarrow{OA} \cap \overrightarrow{OB} = \{O\}$.
</Definicion>
