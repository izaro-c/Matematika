<Capitular letra="H" />**ilbertian geometry** is the formal <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-axiomatico">axiomatic system</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-sintetica">synthetic geometry</ConceptLink> developed by <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink> in *Grundlagen der Geometrie* (1899). It is cited here from the 7th edition (1930): the first edition ordered the groups differently (parallels before congruence) and included a redundant order axiom, pointed out by <ConceptLink targetId="moore">E. H. Moore</ConceptLink> in 1902.

It applies the modern <ConceptLink targetId="metodo-axiomatico">axiomatic method</ConceptLink>: it dispenses with spatial intuition and characterizes the <ConceptLink targetId="concepto-primitivo">primitive objects</ConceptLink> solely through the relations that the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma">axioms</ConceptLink> impose among them.

<Definicion title="Hilbert's Structure and Signature (3D)">
  **Three-dimensional Hilbertian geometry** is modeled by a <ConceptLink targetId="logica">many-sorted</ConceptLink> mathematical <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-geometrica">structure</ConceptLink> that satisfies the set $\mathcal{T}_{\text{Hil}}$ of the 20 axioms described below. The structure consists of three <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-disjunto">pairwise disjoint</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="dominio">domains</ConceptLink> and five <ConceptLink targetId="concepto-primitivo">primitive relations</ConceptLink>, which interpret the symbols of its <ConceptLink targetId="signatura">signature</ConceptLink>:
  $$
  \mathcal{S}_{\text{Hil}} = (\mathcal{P}, \mathcal{L}, \Pi, \mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}}, \mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{P}\Pi}, \mathbf{B}, \cong_{\text{seg}}, \cong_{\angle})
  $$
  where:
  - $\mathcal{P}$, $\mathcal{L}$, and $\Pi$ are the disjoint domains of <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>points</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>lines</ConceptLink>, and <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>planes</ConceptLink>.
  - $\mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}} \subseteq \mathcal{P} \times \mathcal{L}$ and $\mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{P}\Pi} \subseteq \mathcal{P} \times \Pi$ are the typed point-line and point-plane <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia" isDependency={true}>incidence</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-binaria">binary relations</ConceptLink>.
  - $\mathbf{B} \subseteq \mathcal{P}^3$ is the ternary <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre" isDependency={true}>order and betweenness</ConceptLink> relation ($A * B * C$).
  - $\cong_{\text{seg}} \, \subseteq \mathcal{P}^2 \times \mathcal{P}^2$ is the <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruence</ConceptLink> relation for <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segments</ConceptLink> (point pairs).
  - $\cong_{\angle} \, \subseteq \mathcal{P}^6$ is the <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink> relation for <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> (between non-<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> point triples, $\angle ABC \cong \angle DEF$, or, equivalently, between pairs of non-collinear <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta">rays</ConceptLink> with a common origin).
</Definicion>

### Hilbert's five axiomatic groups

The system consists of 20 axioms organized into five groups:

1. **Group I: <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-incidencia">Incidence Axioms</ConceptLink> (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-1">I.1</ConceptLink> to <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-8">I.8</ConceptLink>):** Govern incidence among points, lines, and planes and fix the <ConceptLink targetId="dimension">dimension</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink>: at least three by I.8 and at most three by I.7.
2. **Group II: <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">Order Axioms</ConceptLink> (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-1">II.1</ConceptLink> to <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-4">II.4</ConceptLink>):** Formalize betweenness $\mathbf{B}$ and plane separation via <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-4">Pasch's Axiom</ConceptLink> (<ConceptLink targetId="pasch">Moritz Pasch</ConceptLink>), enabling the definitions of <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segments</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta">rays</ConceptLink>, and <ConceptLink targetId="semiplano">half-planes</ConceptLink>.
3. **Group III: <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">Congruence Axioms</ConceptLink> (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-1">III.1</ConceptLink> to <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-5">III.5</ConceptLink>):** Govern the behavior of $\cong_{\text{seg}}$ and $\cong_{\angle}$ and the transport of segments and angles without numerical <ConceptLink targetId="distancia">distances</ConceptLink>.
4. **Group IV: <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">Parallel Axiom</ConceptLink> (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">IV</ConceptLink>):** Through a point not on a line there passes at most one parallel (<ConceptLink targetId="sistema-euclidiano">Euclidean geometry</ConceptLink>); replacing it with the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-hiperbolico">hyperbolic axiom</ConceptLink> yields <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-hiperbolico">hyperbolic geometry</ConceptLink>.
5. **Group V: <ConceptLink targetId="continuidad">Continuity Axioms</ConceptLink> (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-arquimedes">V.1</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-completitud">V.2</ConceptLink>):** The <ConceptLink targetId="arquimedes">Archimedean</ConceptLink> axiom, which excludes infinitesimal segments, and line completeness, which excludes gaps in the line.
