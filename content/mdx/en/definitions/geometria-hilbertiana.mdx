<Capitular letra="H" />**ilbertian geometry** is the formal axiomatic system of <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-sintetica">synthetic geometry</ConceptLink> developed by <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink> in his seminal treatise *Grundlagen der Geometrie* (1899).

It constitutes the foundational paradigm of modern formal synthetic mathematics: dispensing with spatial intuition and defining geometric entities solely through the formal relations that the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma">axioms</ConceptLink> establish among them.

<Definicion title="Hilbert's Structure and Signature (3D)">
  **Three-dimensional Hilbertian geometry** is modeled by a <ConceptLink targetId="logica">many-sorted</ConceptLink> mathematical <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-geometrica">structure</ConceptLink> whose <ConceptLink targetId="signatura">signature</ConceptLink> comprises three <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-disjunto">pairwise disjoint</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="dominio">domains</ConceptLink> and five irreducible <ConceptLink targetId="concepto-primitivo">primitive relations</ConceptLink>:
  $$
  \mathcal{S}_{\text{Hil}} = (\mathcal{P}, \mathcal{L}, \Pi, \mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}}, \mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{P}\Pi}, \mathbf{B}, \equiv_{\text{seg}}, \equiv_{\angle})
  $$
  where:
  - $\mathcal{P}$, $\mathcal{L}$, and $\Pi$ are the disjoint domains of <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink>, and <ConceptLink targetId="plano">planes</ConceptLink>.
  - $\mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}} \subseteq \mathcal{P} \times \mathcal{L}$ and $\mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{P}\Pi} \subseteq \mathcal{P} \times \Pi$ are the typed binary <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence</ConceptLink> relations point-line and point-plane.
  - $\mathbf{B} \subseteq \mathcal{P}^3$ is the ternary <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">order and betweenness</ConceptLink> relation ($A * B * C$).
  - $\equiv_{\text{seg}} \, \subseteq \mathcal{P}^2 \times \mathcal{P}^2$ is the <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink> relation for <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segments</ConceptLink> (point pairs).
  - $\equiv_{\angle} \, \subseteq \mathcal{P}^6$ is the <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink> relation for <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> (binary relation between non-<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> point triples $\angle ABC \equiv \angle DEF$, or on pairs of concurrent <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta">rays</ConceptLink>).
</Definicion>

### Hilbert's five axiomatic groups

The system is structured around 20 axioms grouped into five modular families:

1. **Group I: <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-incidencia">Incidence Axioms</ConceptLink> (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-1">I.1</ConceptLink> to <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-8">I.8</ConceptLink>):** Establish positional relations among points, lines, and planes, fixing the minimum <ConceptLink targetId="dimension">dimension</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink>.
2. **Group II: <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">Order Axioms</ConceptLink> (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-1">II.1</ConceptLink> to <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-4">II.4</ConceptLink>):** Formalize betweenness $\mathbf{B}$ and plane separation via <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-4">Pasch's Axiom</ConceptLink> (<ConceptLink targetId="pasch">Moritz Pasch</ConceptLink>), enabling rigorous definitions of <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segments</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta">rays</ConceptLink>, and <ConceptLink targetId="semiplano">half-planes</ConceptLink>.
3. **Group III: <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">Congruence Axioms</ConceptLink> (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-1">III.1</ConceptLink> to <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-5">III.5</ConceptLink>):** Govern the behavior of $\equiv_{\text{seg}}$ and $\equiv_{\angle}$ and rigid transport without numerical <ConceptLink targetId="distancia">distances</ConceptLink>.
4. **Group IV: <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">Parallel Axiom</ConceptLink> (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">IV</ConceptLink>):** <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-euclidiano">Euclidean</ConceptLink> parallel uniqueness postulate through an external point.
5. **Group V: <ConceptLink targetId="continuidad">Continuity Axioms</ConceptLink> (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-arquimedes">V.1</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-completitud">V.2</ConceptLink>):** The <ConceptLink targetId="arquimedes">Archimedean</ConceptLink> axiom and line completeness, ensuring the geometric continuum is gap-free.
