<Capitular letra="A" /> **field** is an <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-algebraica">algebraic structure</ConceptLink> endowed with two compatible internal binary operations —<ConceptLink targetId="adicion">addition</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="multiplicacion">multiplication</ConceptLink>— that generalizes the arithmetic properties of classical number systems such as the <ConceptLink targetId="numero-racional">rationals</ConceptLink> $\mathbb{Q}$, the <ConceptLink targetId="numero-real">reals</ConceptLink> $\mathbb{R}$, and the <ConceptLink targetId="numero-complejo">complexes</ConceptLink> $\mathbb{C}$.

In a field, it is possible to add, subtract, multiply, and divide by any non-zero element, satisfying the standard associative, commutative, and distributive laws.

<Definicion title="Formal structure of a field">
  A **field** is a triple $(\mathbb{K}, +, \cdot)$ formed by a non-empty <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto" isDependency={true}>set</ConceptLink> $\mathbb{K}$ and two internal <ConceptLink targetId="operacion-binaria" isDependency={true}>binary operations</ConceptLink> $+ \colon \mathbb{K} \times \mathbb{K} \to \mathbb{K}$ and $\cdot \colon \mathbb{K} \times \mathbb{K} \to \mathbb{K}$ such that:
  1. $(\mathbb{K}, +)$ is an abelian <ConceptLink targetId="grupo" isDependency={true}>group</ConceptLink> whose <ConceptLink targetId="elemento-neutro">identity element</ConceptLink> is denoted $0$.
  2. $(\mathbb{K} \setminus \{0\}, \cdot)$ is an abelian group whose identity element is denoted $1$, with $1 \neq 0$.
  3. Multiplication is distributive with respect to addition from the left and from the right:
     $$
     \forall a, b, c \in \mathbb{K}, \quad a \cdot (b + c) = (a \cdot b) + (a \cdot c) \quad \land \quad (a + b) \cdot c = (a \cdot c) + (b \cdot c)
     $$
</Definicion>

### Ordered fields and analytic <ConceptLink targetId="consistencia-logica">consistency</ConceptLink>

When a field is equipped with a <ConceptLink targetId="orden-total">total order</ConceptLink> relation compatible with addition and multiplication of positive elements, it is called an *<ConceptLink targetId="cuerpo-ordenado">ordered field</ConceptLink>*.

The field of real numbers $\mathbb{R}$ is the unique Archimedean ordered field satisfying the least upper bound property or <ConceptLink targetId="completitud-reales">completeness of the real numbers</ConceptLink>. This structural completeness enables the construction of the analytic Cartesian <ConceptLink targetId="modelo-cartesiano">model</ConceptLink> $\mathbb{R}^n$, grounding the axioms of <ConceptLink targetId="continuidad">continuity</ConceptLink> postulated synthetically by <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">Hilbert</ConceptLink> in <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-completitud">Axiom V.2</ConceptLink>.
