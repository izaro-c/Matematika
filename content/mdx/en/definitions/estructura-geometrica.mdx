<Capitular letra="A" /> **geometric structure** is the mathematical specification assigning meaning and interpretation to a formal <ConceptLink targetId="signatura">signature</ConceptLink>: defining initial object <ConceptLink targetId="dominio">domains</ConceptLink> and the <ConceptLink targetId="concepto-primitivo">primitive relations</ConceptLink> operating among them.

<Definicion title="General Formal Definition">
  A **mathematical structure** $\mathcal{S}$ (or model) is determined by a tuple of non-empty <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto">sets</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{D}_i$ (called <ConceptLink targetId="dominio">domains</ConceptLink>), functions or operations $\mathcal{F}_l$, and relations $\mathcal{R}_j$:
  $$
  \mathcal{S} = (\mathcal{D}_1, \dots, \mathcal{D}_k, \mathcal{F}_1, \dots, \mathcal{F}_p, \mathcal{R}_1, \dots, \mathcal{R}_m)
  $$
  where each function $\mathcal{F}_l: \mathcal{D}_{i_1} \times \dots \times \mathcal{D}_{i_n} \to \mathcal{D}_{i_{n+1}}$ operates on the domains and each relation $\mathcal{R}_j \subseteq \mathcal{D}_{i_1} \times \dots \times \mathcal{D}_{i_n}$ links their elements. In purely relational theories (such as Hilbert's synthetic geometry), the functional inventory is empty ($\mathcal{F} = \emptyset$).
</Definicion>

<Separador />

### Hilbert's synthetic structure (3D)

In <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert's</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-sintetica">synthetic geometry</ConceptLink>, the canonical formal structure gathers three <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-disjunto">disjoint</ConceptLink> domains and their corresponding typed primitive relations:
$$
\mathcal{S}_{\text{synthetic}} = (\mathcal{P}, \mathcal{L}, \Pi, \mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}}, \mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{P}\Pi}, \mathbf{B}, \cong_{\text{seg}}, \cong_{\angle})
$$
- **Object domains:** <ConceptLink targetId="punto">Points</ConceptLink> ($\mathcal{P}$), <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink> ($\mathcal{L}$), and <ConceptLink targetId="plano">planes</ConceptLink> ($\Pi$).
- **Primitive relations:**
  - $\mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}}, \mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{P}\Pi}$: Binary point-line ($P \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell$) and point-plane ($P \, \mathbf{I} \, \pi$) <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence</ConceptLink>.
  - $\mathbf{B}$: Ternary <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">betweenness or order</ConceptLink> on points ($A * B * C$, "$B$ is between $A$ and $C$").
  - $\cong_{\text{seg}}$: <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">Congruence</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segments</ConceptLink> (4-ary relation on points: $AB \cong CD$).
  - $\cong_{\angle}$: <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">Congruence</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> (binary relation on angles formed by pairs of non-<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> concurrent <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta">rays</ConceptLink>: $\angle ABC \cong \angle DEF$, or between pairs of rays $\angle(h, k) \cong \angle(h', k')$).

### Other geometric structures and signatures

- **Pure incidence plane:** $\mathcal{S}_{\text{inc}} = (\mathcal{P}, \mathcal{L}, \mathbf{I}_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}})$, foundation of the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-incidencia">incidence system</ConceptLink> in <ConceptLink targetId="modelo-fano">finite geometries</ConceptLink> or <ConceptLink targetId="espacio-proyectivo">projective geometries</ConceptLink>.
- **Analytic metric space:** $\mathcal{S}_{\text{met}} = (\mathcal{P}, d)$, where a numerical <ConceptLink targetId="distancia">distance</ConceptLink> function $d: \mathcal{P} \times \mathcal{P} \to \mathbb{R}$ replaces synthetic congruence and order.
- **Vector space:** $\mathcal{S}_{\text{vec}} = (V, \mathbb{K}, +, \cdot)$, based on <ConceptLink targetId="algebra-lineal">linear algebra</ConceptLink> over a <ConceptLink targetId="cuerpo">field</ConceptLink>.
