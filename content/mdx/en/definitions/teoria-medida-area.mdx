<Capitular letra="I" />n modern axiomatic foundations, the theory of area measure formalizes the two-dimensional quantification of space enclosed by planar geometric figures. Developed rigorously by David Hilbert as a theory of content, it replaces Euclid's intuitive notions through the equidecomposability of planar <ConceptLink targetId="poligono">polygons</ConceptLink> without requiring the analytic apparatus of continuous measure theory.

<Definicion title="">
  An area measure function $\mu: \mathcal{P}_{\text{pol}} \to \mathbb{R}^+$ on the set $\mathcal{P}_{\text{pol}}$ of all planar <ConceptLink targetId="poligono" isDependency={true}>polygons</ConceptLink> is an assignment satisfying:
  1. **Positivity**: $\forall P \in \mathcal{P}_{\text{pol}}, \; \mu(P) > 0$.
  2. **Invariance under congruence**: If $P \cong Q$ under an isometry, then $\mu(P) = \mu(Q)$.
  3. **Finite additivity**: If $P = P_1 \cup P_2$ with $\text{int}(P_1) \cap \text{int}(P_2) = \emptyset$, then $\mu(P) = \mu(P_1) + \mu(P_2)$.
  4. **Unit normalization**: If $U$ is a <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrado">square</ConceptLink> of unit side length ($l = 1$), $\mu(U) = 1$.
</Definicion>

### Equidecomposability and Hilbert's content

In Hilbert's synthetic geometry:
- **Polygons of equal content**: Two polygons $P$ and $Q$ are equidecomposable if there exist finite partitions $P = \bigcup_{i=1}^n T_i$ and $Q = \bigcup_{i=1}^n T'_i$ such that $T_i \cong T'_i$ for all $i$.
- **Polygons of equal complement**: Two polygons are equivalent in content if they can be supplemented by congruent polygons to yield equidecomposable figures.
