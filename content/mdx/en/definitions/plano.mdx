<Capitular letra="A" /> <VisualBind element="plano" color="musgo">**plane**</VisualBind> is a <ConceptLink targetId="concepto-primitivo">primitive concept</ConceptLink> of geometry: the elementary surface, lacking thickness or depth.

Being a basic starting notion, it is not defined in terms of other figures. Instead, its formal properties and the manner in which it relates to <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink> are determined by the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma">axioms</ConceptLink> of the adopted <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-axiomatico">formal system</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal Definition">
  In a <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-geometrica">geometric structure</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{S}$, a **plane** <VisualBind element="plano" color="musgo">$\pi$</VisualBind> is any element of the <ConceptLink targetId="dominio">primitive domain of planes</ConceptLink> $\Pi$:
  $$
  \pi \in \Pi
  $$
  In isolation, the plane possesses no intrinsic properties; its mathematical meaning arises from the relations that the axioms establish between planes and other entities of <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink>.
</Definicion>

### Fundamental axiomatic relations

Since the plane lacks internal structure, what can be asserted about it depends on which groups of axioms are incorporated into the adopted <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-axiomatico">geometric system</ConceptLink> (a specific system may be restricted to only the first few):

- **In <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence</ConceptLink> systems:** Three non-<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> points (<VisualBind element="pA" color="terracota">$A$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pB" color="terracota">$B$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pC" color="terracota">$C$</VisualBind>) are incident with a unique plane (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-4">I.4</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-5">I.5</ConceptLink>); if two distinct points of a line are incident with a plane, all points of the line are incident with it (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-6">I.6</ConceptLink>); if two distinct planes share a point, they share at least one more (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-7">I.7</ConceptLink>), whence, with I.1, I.5, and I.6, their <ConceptLink targetId="traza">traces</ConceptLink> meet exactly in the trace of a line; and not all points of <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink> are incident with one plane (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-8">I.8</ConceptLink>).
- **Upon adding <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">order or betweenness</ConceptLink>:** Every line of the plane divides the points of the plane not incident with it into two <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-disjunto">disjoint</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-convexo">convex</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="semiplano">half-planes</ConceptLink>, by the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-separacion-plana">plane separation theorem</ConceptLink> (a consequence of <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-4">Pasch's Axiom II.4</ConceptLink> and the other order axioms); this allows the interior of convex figures such as <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangles</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="poligono">convex polygons</ConceptLink> to be defined.
- **Upon adding <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink>:** <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">Angles</ConceptLink> can be transported and congruent figures constructed within the plane (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-4">III.4</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-5">III.5</ConceptLink>).
- **Upon fixing <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallelism</ConceptLink>:** Through a point not on a line there passes at most one parallel in the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-euclidiano">Euclidean plane</ConceptLink> (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">Axiom IV</ConceptLink>) and at least two in the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-hiperbolico">hyperbolic plane</ConceptLink> (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-hiperbolico">Axiom IV<sub>hyp</sub></ConceptLink>); both are planes in the same synthetic sense.
- **Upon adding <ConceptLink targetId="continuidad">continuity</ConceptLink>:** The continuity axioms (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-arquimedes">V.1</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-completitud">V.2</ConceptLink>) are stated for lines; holding on every line of the plane, they guarantee, for instance, that a line through an interior point of a <ConceptLink targetId="circunferencia">circle</ConceptLink> meets it.
