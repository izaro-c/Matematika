<Capitular letra="A" /> <VisualBind element="planeMesh" color="musgo">**plane**</VisualBind> is a <ConceptLink targetId="concepto-primitivo">primitive concept</ConceptLink> of geometry: the elementary flat surface with no curvature, thickness, or depth.

Being a basic starting notion, it is not defined in terms of other figures. Instead, its formal properties and the manner in which it contains and intersects <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink> are determined by the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma">axioms</ConceptLink> of the adopted <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-axiomatico">formal system</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal Definition">
  In a <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-geometrica">geometric structure</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{S}$, a **plane** <VisualBind element="planeMesh" color="musgo">$\pi$</VisualBind> is any element of the <ConceptLink targetId="dominio">primitive domain of planes</ConceptLink> $\Pi$:
  $$
  \pi \in \Pi
  $$
  In isolation, the plane possesses no intrinsic properties; its mathematical meaning arises from the relations that the axioms establish between planes and other entities of <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink>.
</Definicion>

### Fundamental axiomatic relations

Since the plane lacks internal structure, what can be asserted about it depends on which groups of axioms are incorporated into the adopted <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-axiomatico">geometric system</ConceptLink> (a specific system may be restricted to only the first few):

- **In <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence</ConceptLink> systems:** Three non-<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> points determine a unique plane containing them (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-4">I.4</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-5">I.5</ConceptLink>), if two points of a line are incident with a plane the entire line is contained in it (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-6">I.6</ConceptLink>), if two distinct planes share a point, they share at least a second common point (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-7">I.7</ConceptLink>, proving together with I.1 and I.6 that their intersection is exactly a line), and points outside a given plane exist to prevent all <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink> from reducing to it (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-8">I.8</ConceptLink>).
- **Upon adding <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">order or betweenness</ConceptLink>:** Every line contained in the plane divides its exterior points into two <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-disjunto">disjoint</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto-convexo">convex</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="semiplano">half-planes</ConceptLink> by the Plane Separation Theorem (proven from <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-4">Pasch's Axiom, II.4</ConceptLink> and linear order axioms), enabling the interior of convex figures such as <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangles</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="poligono">convex polygons</ConceptLink> to be defined.
- **Upon adding <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink>:** Enables transporting <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> and constructing congruent figures within the plane (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-4">III.4</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-5">III.5</ConceptLink>).
- **Upon fixing <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallelism</ConceptLink>:** Postulates the uniqueness or upper bound of coplanar parallel lines (at most one parallel in the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-euclidiano">Euclidean plane</ConceptLink> by <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">Euclid's postulate, IV</ConceptLink>), in contrast to the existence of multiple parallels in the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-hiperbolico">hyperbolic plane</ConceptLink>.
- **Upon adding <ConceptLink targetId="continuidad">continuity</ConceptLink>:** The continuity axioms (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-arquimedes">V.1</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-completitud">V.2</ConceptLink>) operate on lines; their validity across all lines of the plane induces the two-dimensional continuity of the plane without gaps or discrete jumps, founding the geometry of the <ConceptLink targetId="circunferencia">circle</ConceptLink>.
