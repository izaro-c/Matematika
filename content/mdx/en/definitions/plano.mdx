<Capitular letra="A" /> <VisualBind element="planeMesh" color="musgo">**plane**</VisualBind> is a <ConceptLink targetId="concepto-primitivo">primitive concept</ConceptLink> of geometry: the elementary flat surface with no curvature, thickness, or depth.

Being a basic starting notion, it is not defined in terms of other figures. Instead, its formal properties and the manner in which it contains and intersects <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink> are determined by the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma">axioms</ConceptLink> of the adopted <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-axiomatico">formal system</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal Definition">
  In a <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-geometrica">geometric structure</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{S}$, a **plane** <VisualBind element="planeMesh" color="musgo">$\pi$</VisualBind> is any element of the non-empty <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto">set</ConceptLink> of planes $\Pi$:
  $$
  \pi \in \Pi
  $$
  In isolation, a plane possesses no intrinsic properties; its mathematical meaning arises from the relations that the axioms establish between planes and other entities of space.
</Definicion>

### Fundamental axiomatic relations

Since a plane lacks internal structure, what can be asserted about it depends on which groups of axioms are incorporated into the adopted <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-axiomatico">geometric system</ConceptLink> (a specific system may be restricted to only the first few):

- **In <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence</ConceptLink> systems:** Three non-collinear points determine a unique plane containing them (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-4">I.4</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-5">I.5</ConceptLink>), if two points of a line lie in a plane the entire line is contained in it (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-6">I.6</ConceptLink>), two distinct planes sharing a point intersect along a line (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-7">I.7</ConceptLink>), and points outside a given plane exist (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-8">I.8</ConceptLink>).
- **Upon adding <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">order or betweenness</ConceptLink>:** Every line contained in the plane divides its remaining points into two disjoint convex <ConceptLink targetId="semiplano">half-planes</ConceptLink> (guaranteed by <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-4">Pasch's Axiom, II.4</ConceptLink>), allowing the interior of <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangles</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="poligono">polygons</ConceptLink> to be formally defined.
- **Upon adding <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink>:** Enables transporting <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> and constructing congruent figures within the plane (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-4">III.4</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-5">III.5</ConceptLink>).
- **Upon fixing <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallelism</ConceptLink>:** Determines whether through a point not on a given line in the plane there passes a unique line not intersecting it (<ConceptLink targetId="sistema-euclidiano">Euclidean plane</ConceptLink>) or more than one (<ConceptLink targetId="sistema-hiperbolico">hyperbolic plane</ConceptLink>).
- **Upon adding <ConceptLink targetId="continuidad">continuity</ConceptLink>:** Guarantees that the plane is a continuous medium without gaps or discrete jumps, founding the geometry of the <ConceptLink targetId="circunferencia">circle</ConceptLink> and area measurement.
