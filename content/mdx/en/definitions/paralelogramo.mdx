<Capitular letra="A" /> parallelogram is a convex <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrilatero">quadrilateral</ConceptLink> in planar <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-euclidiana">Euclidean geometry</ConceptLink> characterized by having both pairs of opposite <ConceptLink targetId="lado">sides</ConceptLink> lying along <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallel lines</ConceptLink>. This figure serves as a benchmark model for computing vector areas and defining the cross product in the <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="">
  Given a convex quadrilateral <VisualBind element="paralelogramo" color="musgo">$ABCD$</VisualBind> in a plane $\mathcal{P}$, it is defined to be a parallelogram if and only if opposite line <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segments</ConceptLink> are pairwise parallel:
  $$
  ABCD \text{ is a parallelogram} \iff (\overline{AB} \parallel \overline{CD}) \land (\overline{BC} \parallel \overline{DA})
  $$
  As a consequence of the parallel postulate and <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence axioms</ConceptLink>, opposite sides are congruent ($\overline{AB} \cong \overline{CD}$ and $\overline{BC} \cong \overline{DA}$).
</Definicion>

### Subfamily classification

Parallelograms are partitioned into specific categories based on side congruence and interior <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angle</ConceptLink> measures:

| Subfamily | Angular condition | Side condition |
|---|---|---|
| Rectangle | Four <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto">right angles</ConceptLink> ($\mu = 90^\circ$) | Pairwise congruent opposite sides |
| Rhombus | Congruent opposite angles | Four strictly congruent sides ($\overline{AB} \cong \overline{BC}$) |
| <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrado">Square</ConceptLink> | Four right angles | Four strictly congruent sides |
| Rhomboid | Non-right congruent opposite angles | Unequal adjacent sides |

### Properties

- **Bisection of diagonals**: The two <ConceptLink targetId="diagonal">diagonals</ConceptLink> $\overline{AC}$ and $\overline{BD}$ intersect at their common midpoint $M$, satisfying $d(A, M) = d(M, C)$ and $d(B, M) = d(M, D)$.
- **Congruence of opposite angles**: The opposite angles of any parallelogram are mutually congruent, while adjacent angles along any side are supplementary.
- **Area computation**: The area enclosed by the parallelogram is given by the product of a base length $b = \|\overline{AB}\|$ and its corresponding <ConceptLink targetId="perpendicular">perpendicular height</ConceptLink> $h$: $\mu(ABCD) = b \cdot h$.
