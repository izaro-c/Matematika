<Capitular letra="A" /> parallelogram is a convex <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrilatero">quadrilateral</ConceptLink> in planar Euclidean geometry characterized by having both pairs of opposite sides lying on <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallel lines</ConceptLink>. It serves as the standard geometric model for planar area computations and vector addition in the <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="">
  Given a convex <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrilatero" isDependency={true}>quadrilateral</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="poligono" color="musgo">$ABCD$</VisualBind> in a plane $\mathcal{P}$, it is defined as a parallelogram if and only if opposite sides are pairwise <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas" isDependency={true}>parallel</ConceptLink>:
  $$
  ABCD \text{ is a parallelogram} \iff (\overline{AB} \parallel \overline{CD}) \land (\overline{BC} \parallel \overline{DA})
  $$
  As a consequence of the parallel and congruence axioms, opposite sides are congruent (<VisualBind element="lados-iguales" color="pavo">$\overline{AB} \cong \overline{CD}$</VisualBind> and $\overline{BC} \cong \overline{DA}$).
</Definicion>

### Subfamily classification

Parallelograms are classified based on interior angle measures and side congruence:

| Subfamily | Angular condition | Side condition |
|---|---|---|
| Rectangle | Four <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto">right angles</ConceptLink> | Opposite sides pairwise congruent |
| Rhombus | <VisualBind element="angulos-opuestos" color="pavo">Opposite angles congruent</VisualBind> | All four sides strictly congruent |
| <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrado">Square</ConceptLink> | Four right angles | All four sides strictly congruent |
| Rhomboid | Opposite angles non-right | Adjacent sides of unequal length |
