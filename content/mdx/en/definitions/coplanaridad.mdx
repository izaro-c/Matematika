<Capitular letra="C" />oplanarity is the relation of <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia" isDependency={true}>incidence</ConceptLink> satisfied by a collection of <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>points</ConceptLink> or <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>lines</ConceptLink> when there exists at least one <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink> in which all of them simultaneously incident or whose <ConceptLink targetId="traza" isDependency={true}>point traces</ConceptLink> are contained in it.

Any three points always determine at least one plane by <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-4">Axiom I.4</ConceptLink>, so that every triple of points in <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink> is coplanar. Coplanarity acquires restrictive force when considering four or more points: <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-8">Axiom I.8</ConceptLink> postulates the existence of four non-coplanar points, ensuring that space does not collapse into a single plane. For two lines, coplanarity is the indispensable prerequisite to define <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallelism</ConceptLink> and distinguishes <ConceptLink targetId="secante">secant lines</ConceptLink> from <ConceptLink targetId="rectas-cruzadas">skew lines</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal definition of coplanarity">
Let $(\mathcal{P}, \mathcal{L}, \Pi; \, \mathbf{I})$ be a spatial <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence</ConceptLink> structure with pairwise disjoint primitive domains.

1. A non-empty set of points $S \subseteq \mathcal{P}$ is **coplanar** if there exists a plane $\pi \in \Pi$ such that every point of $S$ is incident with $\pi$:
$$
\operatorname{coplanar}(S) \iff \exists \pi \in \Pi \; \forall P \in S \; (P \,\mathbf{I}\, \pi)
$$
In particular, four points $A, B, C, D \in \mathcal{P}$ are coplanar if and only if:
$$
\operatorname{coplanar}(A, B, C, D) \iff \exists \pi \in \Pi \; (A \,\mathbf{I}\, \pi \land B \,\mathbf{I}\, \pi \land C \,\mathbf{I}\, \pi \land D \,\mathbf{I}\, \pi)
$$

2. Two lines $\ell_1, \ell_2 \in \mathcal{L}$ are **coplanar** if there exists a plane $\pi \in \Pi$ such that the point traces of both are contained in the trace of $\pi$:
$$
\operatorname{coplanar}(\ell_1, \ell_2) \iff \exists \pi \in \Pi \; (\operatorname{tr}(\ell_1) \cup \operatorname{tr}(\ell_2) \subseteq \operatorname{tr}(\pi))
$$
</Definicion>
