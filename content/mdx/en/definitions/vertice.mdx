<Capitular letra="A" /> **vertex** is each of the <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>points</ConceptLink> composing the generating configuration of a <ConceptLink targetId="poligono">polygonal</ConceptLink> figure, or that constitutes the common <ConceptLink targetId="punto">origin</ConceptLink> of the two <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta" isDependency={true}>rays</ConceptLink> forming an <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angle</ConceptLink>.

Unlike points situated in the interior of the segments of a figure, the vertex is a discrete structural element of the primitive <ConceptLink targetId="dominio">domain</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{P}$ that anchors the geometric configuration. In <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangles</ConceptLink>, three non-<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad" isDependency={true}>collinear</ConceptLink> vertices determine both the supporting lines (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-1">Axiom I.1</ConceptLink>) and the <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> of the figure (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-4">Axiom I.4</ConceptLink>), serving as the fundamental reference for cyclic ordering and <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink> criteria.

<Definicion title="Formal definition of vertex in polygonal figures and angles">
1. **Vertex in a polygonal figure:** Given a cyclically ordered sequence of $n$ points $(A_1, A_2, \dots, A_n) \in \mathcal{P}^n$, with $n \ge 3$, such that no three consecutive points are collinear, each point $A_i \in \mathcal{P}$ is called a **vertex** of the figure:

$$
\operatorname{Vertices}(\mathcal{F}) = \{ A_1, A_2, \dots, A_n \} \subset \mathcal{P}
$$

In particular, for a triangle determined by the triple of non-collinear points <VisualBind element="verticeA" color="terracota">$A$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="verticeB" color="pavo">$B$</VisualBind>, and <VisualBind element="verticeC" color="granada">$C$</VisualBind>, the set of its vertices is $\{A, B, C\}$, connected consecutively by segments <VisualBind element="segAB" color="carbon">$\overline{AB}$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="segBC" color="carbon">$\overline{BC}$</VisualBind>, and <VisualBind element="segCA" color="carbon">$\overline{CA}$</VisualBind>.

2. **Vertex in an angle:** Given an angle formed by the unordered pair of rays $\angle(h, k) = \{ \overrightarrow{OA}, \overrightarrow{OB} \}$, the point $O \in \mathcal{P}$ formally receives the name of **vertex** of the angle, defined as the common origin of both rays:

$$
O = \operatorname{origen}(h) = \operatorname{origen}(k)
$$
</Definicion>
