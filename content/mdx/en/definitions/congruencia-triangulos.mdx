<Capitular letra="T" />wo <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangles</ConceptLink> are **congruent** if there exists a one-to-one correspondence between their <ConceptLink targetId="vertice" isDependency={true}>vertices</ConceptLink> such that all three pairs of corresponding <ConceptLink targetId="lado" isDependency={true}>sides</ConceptLink> and all three pairs of corresponding <ConceptLink targetId="angulo" isDependency={true}>angles</ConceptLink> are mutually <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink>.

The triangle congruence relation extends the primitive congruence of <ConceptLink targetId="segmento" isDependency={true}>segments</ConceptLink> and angles to composite figures in the <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink>. Although the full definition requires simultaneously verifying all six congruences of their corresponding elements, the congruence criteria of <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink> show that the congruence of a minimal subset suffices: the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-lal">SAS criterion</ConceptLink> (Side-Angle-Side), the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-ala">ASA criterion</ConceptLink> (Angle-Side-Angle), and the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-lll">SSS criterion</ConceptLink> (Side-Side-Side).

<Definicion title="Formal definition of triangle congruence">
Let <VisualBind element="triangleABC" color="musgo">$\triangle ABC$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="triangleAABBCC" color="mora">$\triangle A'B'C'$</VisualBind> be two <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangles</ConceptLink> determined by non-<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> triples of <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> in $\mathcal{P}$, with <ConceptLink targetId="vertice" isDependency={true}>vertices</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="pA" color="carbon">$A$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pB" color="carbon">$B$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pC" color="carbon">$C$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="pAA" color="carbon">$A'$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pBB" color="carbon">$B'$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pCC" color="carbon">$C'$</VisualBind>.

The triangle $\triangle ABC$ is **congruent** to $\triangle A'B'C'$ with respect to the vertex correspondence $A \mapsto A'$, $B \mapsto B'$, $C \mapsto C'$, denoted <VisualBind element="congruencia-total" color="musgo">$\triangle ABC \cong \triangle A'B'C'$</VisualBind>, if and only if the congruences of all three corresponding <ConceptLink targetId="lado" isDependency={true}>sides</ConceptLink> and all three corresponding <ConceptLink targetId="angulo" isDependency={true}>angles</ConceptLink> hold simultaneously:

$$
\triangle ABC \cong \triangle A'B'C' \iff \begin{cases}
\overline{AB} \cong \overline{A'B'} \land \overline{BC} \cong \overline{B'C'} \land \overline{CA} \cong \overline{C'A'} \\
\angle CAB \cong \angle C'A'B' \land \angle ABC \cong \angle A'B'C' \land \angle BCA \cong \angle B'C'A'
\end{cases}
$$

Graphically, the pairs of corresponding sides <VisualBind element="sidesAB" color="terracota">$\overline{AB} \cong \overline{A'B'}$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="sidesBC" color="pavo">$\overline{BC} \cong \overline{B'C'}$</VisualBind>, and <VisualBind element="sidesCA" color="canela">$\overline{CA} \cong \overline{C'A'}$</VisualBind>, as well as the pairs of corresponding angles <VisualBind element="anglesA" color="terracota">$\angle A \cong \angle A'$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="anglesB" color="pavo">$\angle B \cong \angle B'$</VisualBind>, and <VisualBind element="anglesC" color="canela">$\angle C \cong \angle C'$</VisualBind>, are mutually congruent under this correspondence.
</Definicion>

    </PropiedadItem>

    <PropiedadItem 
      id="orden-correspondencia-vertices" 
      title="Dependence on the vertex correspondence order"
      formula="\triangle ABC \cong \triangle A'B'C' \not\implies \triangle ABC \cong \triangle B'A'C'"
    >
      The notation $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle A'B'C'$ strictly specifies the ordered correspondence between vertices; permuting the order in one of the triangles alters the required corresponding pairs and is only valid if the triangle possesses internal symmetries, as in an <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-triangulo-isosceles">isosceles triangle</ConceptLink>.
    </PropiedadItem>
