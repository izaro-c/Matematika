<Capitular letra="G" />eometric congruence is a primitive <ConceptLink targetId="relacion">relation</ConceptLink> of formal rigidity establishing the equivalence of size and shape between pairs of <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">line segments</ConceptLink> and between pairs of <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink>.

Congruence is postulated as a primitive relation in the formal <ConceptLink targetId="signatura">signature</ConceptLink> of the <ConceptLink targetId="estructura-geometrica">geometric structure</ConceptLink>, and its deductive behavior is governed by the third group of <ConceptLink targetId="axioma">axioms</ConceptLink> (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-1">Axiom III.1</ConceptLink> through <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-5">Axiom III.5</ConceptLink>).

<Definicion title="Formalization of Synthetic Congruence">
In the signature of <ConceptLink targetId="geometria-hilbertiana">Hilbertian geometry</ConceptLink>, **congruence** ($\cong$) comprises two independent primitive <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-binaria">binary relations</ConceptLink>:

1. **Segment Congruence**: A binary relation $\cong_{\text{seg}}$ on the <ConceptLink targetId="conjunto">set</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segments</ConceptLink> determined by pairs of distinct <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> in the <ConceptLink targetId="dominio">domain</ConceptLink> $\mathcal{P}$, denoted $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{CD}$ for endpoints $A \neq B$ and $C \neq D$.
2. **Angle Congruence**: A binary relation $\cong_{\angle}$ on the set of <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> in <ConceptLink targetId="espacio">space</ConceptLink>, denoted $\angle AOB \cong \angle A'O'B'$ for pairs of non-<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta">rays</ConceptLink> coplanar in some <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> $\pi \in \Pi$.
</Definicion>

<Nota>
From the primitive congruence relations on segments and angles, the notion formally extends to composite figures such as <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangles</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="poligono">polygons</ConceptLink>: two figures are congruent if there exists a bijection between their <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertices</ConceptLink> that preserves the congruence of all corresponding <ConceptLink targetId="lado">sides</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink>.
</Nota>
