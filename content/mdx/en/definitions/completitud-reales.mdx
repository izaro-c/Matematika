<Capitular letra="C" />ompleteness **of** the real numbers (or the least upper bound property) is the defining property that distinguishes the <ConceptLink targetId="orden-total" isDependency={true}>ordered</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="cuerpo" isDependency={true}>field</ConceptLink> of real numbers $\mathbb{R}$ from the field of rational numbers $\mathbb{Q}$, formally guaranteeing the absence of gaps, fissures, or discontinuities in the numerical line.

This property ensures that the real continuum contains no arithmetic voids, rigorously establishing infinitesimal calculus, limits of sequences, and analytic geometric constructions. There exist multiple formally equivalent formulations: the least upper bound property (Dedekind), the convergence of all Cauchy sequences, and Cantor's nested interval principle.

<Definicion title="Least upper bound property in ℝ">
  Let $S \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ be a non-empty subset of real numbers. If $S$ is bounded above —that is, if there exists an <ConceptLink targetId="cota-superior" isDependency={true}>upper bound</ConceptLink> $M \in \mathbb{R}$ such that for all $x \in S, \, x \le M$—, then there exists a real number $s = \sup(S) \in \mathbb{R}$ called the **supremum** (or least upper bound) of $S$, uniquely characterized by:
  $$
  \forall x \in S \ (x \le s) \quad \land \quad \forall \varepsilon > 0 \ \exists x \in S \ (x > s - \varepsilon)
  $$
</Definicion>

### Analytical foundation of Hilbertian continuity

The completeness of $\mathbb{R}$ plays a paramount demonstrative role in the analytical models of the encyclopedia:

In the Cartesian <ConceptLink targetId="modelo-cartesiano">model</ConceptLink>, the trace of each line is identified analytically via a monotonic <ConceptLink targetId="biyeccion">bijection</ConceptLink> with $\mathbb{R}$. In virtue of the completeness of the real field, any connected partition of the points of a line immediately transfers the Dedekind cut to the geometric line, deducing as a demonstrated result David Hilbert's <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-completitud">Axiom V.2 (Linear completeness)</ConceptLink>.

In this way, the consistency and satisfaction of the <ConceptLink targetId="continuidad">continuity</ConceptLink> axioms in Cartesian space $\mathbb{R}^3$ is entirely reduced to the analytical completeness of the field of real numbers.
