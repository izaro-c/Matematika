<Capitular letra="I" />n any <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo-rectangulo" isDependency={true}>right triangle</ConceptLink>, the **hypotenuse** is the <ConceptLink targetId="lado" isDependency={true}>side</ConceptLink> opposite to the <ConceptLink targetId="vertice" isDependency={true}>vertex</ConceptLink> of the <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto" isDependency={true}>right angle</ConceptLink>.

The hypotenuse is the <ConceptLink targetId="segmento" isDependency={true}>segment</ConceptLink> connecting the endpoints of the two <ConceptLink targetId="cateto">legs</ConceptLink>, completing the figure in the <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink>. Being opposite to the largest of the three angles of the triangle, it has strictly greater <ConceptLink targetId="longitud">length</ConceptLink> than either of the legs and plays the central role in the fundamental metric relationship stated in the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-pitagoras">Pythagorean theorem</ConceptLink>, where the area of the square constructed upon it equals the sum of the areas of the squares on the legs.

<Definicion title="Formal definition of the hypotenuse">
Let <VisualBind element="triangulo" color="musgo">$\triangle ABC$</VisualBind> $= \{\overline{AB}, \overline{BC}, \overline{CA}\}$ be a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo-rectangulo" isDependency={true}>right triangle</ConceptLink> in a <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink> $\pi \in \Pi$, whose vertices are the non-collinear <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>points</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="pA" color="carbon">$A$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pB" color="carbon">$B$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pC" color="carbon">$C$</VisualBind> $\in \mathcal{P}$, with a <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto" isDependency={true}>right angle</ConceptLink> at <ConceptLink targetId="vertice" isDependency={true}>vertex</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="pC" color="carbon">$C$</VisualBind>, so that <VisualBind element="rightAngle" color="ocre">$\angle BCA$</VisualBind> is right.

The <ConceptLink targetId="lado" isDependency={true}>side</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="segAB" color="pavo">$\overline{AB}$</VisualBind>, opposite to vertex $C$ and not incident with it, is the **hypotenuse** of triangle $\triangle ABC$:

$$
\text{Hipotenusa}(\triangle ABC) = \overline{AB}
$$

The other two sides, <VisualBind element="segCA" color="terracota">$\overline{CA}$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="segCB" color="terracota">$\overline{CB}$</VisualBind>, concurrent at the right angle vertex, are the <ConceptLink targetId="cateto">legs</ConceptLink> of the triangle.
</Definicion>
