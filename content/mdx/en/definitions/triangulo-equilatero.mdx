<Capitular letra="A" />n **equilateral triangle** is a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangle</ConceptLink> whose three <ConceptLink targetId="lado" isDependency={true}>sides</ConceptLink> are mutually <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink>.

Having all three sides congruent, every equilateral triangle is in particular an <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo-isosceles">isosceles triangle</ConceptLink> with respect to any chosen pair of sides, so that any of its sides can be regarded as a <ConceptLink targetId="base">base</ConceptLink>. The definition is purely relational: it classifies a configuration whenever it exists, without asserting a priori that an equilateral triangle can be constructed upon every given <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink>, as the synthetic construction via the intersection of two <ConceptLink targetId="circunferencia">circles</ConceptLink> requires axioms of <ConceptLink targetId="continuidad">continuity</ConceptLink>.

<Definicion title="Formal definition of an equilateral triangle">
Let $\triangle ABC = \{\overline{AB}, \overline{BC}, \overline{CA}\}$ be a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangle</ConceptLink> in a <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> $\pi$, determined by three non-<ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> $A$, $B$, and $C$.

The triangle $\triangle ABC$ is said to be an **equilateral triangle** if its three <ConceptLink targetId="lado" isDependency={true}>sides</ConceptLink> are mutually <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink>:

$$
\overline{AB} \cong \overline{BC} \land \overline{BC} \cong \overline{CA}
$$

By the <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-transitiva">transitive</ConceptLink> property of segment congruence (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-2">Axiom III.2</ConceptLink>), this condition is equivalent to mutual congruence among all three sides: $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{BC} \cong \overline{CA}$.
</Definicion>

<Nota>
Proposition I.1 of <ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid</ConceptLink>'s <ConceptLink targetId="elementos-euclides">Elements</ConceptLink> attempts to construct an equilateral triangle on a given segment using the intersection of two circles. In <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">Hilbert</ConceptLink>'s formalization, the existence of such an intersection point does not follow from the axioms of incidence, order, and congruence: it requires the axioms of <ConceptLink targetId="continuidad">continuity</ConceptLink> (Group V) or the circle-circle intersection axiom. Without these axioms, there exist models where the relational definition is valid for given triangles, but not every segment admits an equilateral triangle upon it.
</Nota>
