<DemonstrationSection>

<Capitular letra="D" />irect <ConceptLink targetId="metodo-directo" isDependency={true}>proof</ConceptLink> of the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-triangulo">triangle area theorem</ConceptLink> through the foot of the <ConceptLink targetId="altura-triangulo" isDependency={true}>altitude</ConceptLink>: we first prove that a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo-rectangulo" isDependency={true}>right triangle</ConceptLink> is half of a <ConceptLink targetId="rectangulo" isDependency={true}>rectangle</ConceptLink>; then three cases are distinguished according to the position of the foot of the altitude with respect to the base.

<Separador />

### Step-by-step proof

<ProofStep number={1} title="The foot of the altitude and the three cases">
Let $\triangle ABC$ be a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangle</ConceptLink> in a plane $\sigma$. By the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-existencia-perpendicular" isDependency={true}>existence and uniqueness theorem for the perpendicular</ConceptLink>, there is exactly one line through $C$ <ConceptLink targetId="perpendicular" isDependency={true}>perpendicular</ConceptLink> to $\overleftrightarrow{AB}$; let $H$ be its intersection point with $\overleftrightarrow{AB}$. Since $C$ is not incident with $\overleftrightarrow{AB}$, $H \neq C$ and $h = |CH| > 0$. Let $b = |AB|$. By the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-3" isDependency={true}>order axioms</ConceptLink> applied to the points $A$, $B$, $H$ of the line $\overleftrightarrow{AB}$, one of these cases occurs:
1. $H = A$ or $H = B$;
2. $A * H * B$, that is, $H$ lies <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre" isDependency={true}>between</ConceptLink> $A$ and $B$;
3. $A * B * H$ or $H * A * B$.
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={2} title="Lemma: a right triangle is half of a rectangle">
Let $\triangle PQR$ be a triangle with a <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto" isDependency={true}>right angle</ConceptLink> at $P$, $u = |PQ|$ and $v = |PR|$. The perpendicular to $\overleftrightarrow{PQ}$ through $Q$ and the <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas" isDependency={true}>parallel</ConceptLink> to $\overleftrightarrow{PQ}$ through $R$ meet at a point $S$, and $PQSR$ is a rectangle (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides" isDependency={true}>parallel axiom</ConceptLink>) whose opposite sides are congruent. Then $\triangle QPR \cong \triangle RSQ$ by the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-lll" isDependency={true}>SSS criterion</ConceptLink> ($\overline{QP} \cong \overline{RS}$, $\overline{PR} \cong \overline{SQ}$ and $\overline{QR}$ shared). Since $P$ and $S$ lie in opposite <ConceptLink targetId="semiplano" isDependency={true}>half-planes</ConceptLink> with respect to $\overleftrightarrow{QR}$, the two triangular regions only share $\overline{QR}$ and their union is the region of the rectangle. By the invariance of the <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-medida-area" isDependency={true}>area function</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-aditividad" isDependency={true}>additivity</ConceptLink> and the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-rectangulo" isDependency={true}>rectangle area theorem</ConceptLink>, $u \cdot v = 2 \operatorname{A}(\triangle PQR)$, that is,

$$
\operatorname{A}(\triangle PQR) = \frac{1}{2}\, u \cdot v
$$
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={3} title="Case 1: the foot coincides with a vertex">
If $H = A$, the angle $\angle BAC$ is a right angle and, by the preceding lemma (<ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-rectangulo" isDependency={true}>rectangle area theorem</ConceptLink>), $\operatorname{A}(\triangle ABC) = \tfrac{1}{2}\, |AB| \cdot |AC| = \tfrac{1}{2}\, b \cdot h$. The case $H = B$ is analogous.
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={4} title="Case 2: the foot lies between A and B">
If $A * H * B$, the <ConceptLink targetId="segmento" isDependency={true}>segment</ConceptLink> $\overline{CH}$ divides the <ConceptLink targetId="region-triangular" isDependency={true}>triangular region</ConceptLink> of $\triangle ABC$ into those of $\triangle AHC$ and $\triangle HBC$, which have a right angle at $H$ and only share $\overline{CH}$, since $A$ and $B$ lie in opposite half-planes with respect to $\overleftrightarrow{CH}$. By additivity, the lemma and the additivity of <ConceptLink targetId="longitud" isDependency={true}>length</ConceptLink> ($|AH| + |HB| = |AB|$),

$$
\operatorname{A}(\triangle ABC) = \frac{1}{2}\, |AH| \cdot h + \frac{1}{2}\, |HB| \cdot h = \frac{1}{2}\, (|AH| + |HB|) \cdot h = \frac{1}{2}\, b \cdot h
$$
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={5} title="Case 3: the foot falls outside the base">
If $A * B * H$ (the angle at $B$ is obtuse), the region of $\triangle AHC$ is the union of the regions of $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle BHC$, which only share $\overline{BC}$. Both $\triangle AHC$ and $\triangle BHC$ have a right angle at $H$, and $|AH| = |AB| + |BH|$. By <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-aditividad" isDependency={true}>area additivity</ConceptLink> and the lemma,

$$
\frac{1}{2}\, (b + |BH|) \cdot h = \operatorname{A}(\triangle ABC) + \frac{1}{2}\, |BH| \cdot h
$$

hence $\operatorname{A}(\triangle ABC) = \tfrac{1}{2}\, b \cdot h$. The case $H * A * B$ is the same with the roles of $A$ and $B$ exchanged. In all three cases, $\operatorname{A}(\triangle ABC) = \tfrac{1}{2}\, b \cdot h$.
</ProofStep>

<Nota>
<ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid</ConceptLink> obtains the result differently (Elements I.41): a <ConceptLink targetId="paralelogramo">parallelogram</ConceptLink> with the same base as a triangle and lying between the same parallels is double the triangle. The proof through the foot of the altitude avoids the theory of parallelograms and only uses rectangles.
</Nota>
