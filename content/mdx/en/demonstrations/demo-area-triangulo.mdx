<Capitular letra="L" />et $\triangle ABC$ be a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangle</ConceptLink> with base $b = |\overline{AB}|$ and height $h$ (the perpendicular distance from vertex $C$ to the line through $\overline{AB}$). The proof constructs an auxiliary rectangle that contains exactly twice the area of the triangle.

<Separador />

### Proof step by step

<ProofStep number={1} title="Construction of the circumscribed rectangle">
Construct rectangle $ABDE$ with base $\overline{AB}$ of length $b$ and height $h$, by drawing <ConceptLink targetId="perpendicular" isDependency={true}>perpendiculars</ConceptLink> through $A$ and $B$ and the <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas" isDependency={true}>parallel</ConceptLink> through $C$ to $AB$. By the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-rectangulo" isDependency={true}>rectangle area theorem</ConceptLink>:

<Formula>
  $$ \text{Area}(ABDE) = b \cdot h $$
</Formula>
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={2} title="Division by diagonal AC">
Diagonal $\overline{AC}$ divides rectangle $ABDE$ into two triangles. By the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-ala" isDependency={true}>AAS congruence criterion</ConceptLink> applied to triangles $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle CAE$ (right angles at $B$ and $E$, common side $\overline{AC}$, equal sides $|\overline{AB}| = |\overline{CE}|$), both triangles are <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink>.
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={3} title="Application of area additivity">
Since the two triangles are congruent, they have equal area. By the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-aditividad" isDependency={true}>area additivity theorem</ConceptLink>, the area of the rectangle equals the sum of the areas of the two triangles:

<Formula>
  $$ \text{Area}(ABDE) = \text{Area}(\triangle ABC) + \text{Area}(\triangle CAE) = 2 \cdot \text{Area}(\triangle ABC) $$
</Formula>
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={4} title="Conclusion">
Solving for the triangle's area:

<Formula>
  $$ \text{Area}(\triangle ABC) = \frac{1}{2} \cdot b \cdot h $$
</Formula>

This equality holds for any triangle — acute, right, or obtuse — since in all three cases the triangle can be inscribed in a rectangle of equal base and height.
</ProofStep>

<Separador />

### Deductive analysis

<Nota>
  For an obtuse triangle, the rectangle must be extended beyond the base, but the congruence and additivity argument remains intact. This proof operates strictly within the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-euclidiano">Euclidean axiomatic system</ConceptLink>.
</Nota>
