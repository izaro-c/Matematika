<Capitular letra="B" />oth directions of the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-angulos-alternos-internos">alternate interior angles theorem</ConceptLink> are proved. Direction (⇐) is proved by the <ConceptLink targetId="metodo-contradiccion" isDependency={true}>method of contradiction</ConceptLink> with the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-angulo-exterior" isDependency={true}>exterior angle theorem</ConceptLink>, without the parallel axiom or the continuity axioms; direction (⇒) is proved by the <ConceptLink targetId="metodo-directo" isDependency={true}>direct method</ConceptLink>, combining <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-4" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.4</ConceptLink>, direction (⇐) and <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides" isDependency={true}>Axiom IV</ConceptLink>.

<Separador />

### Step-by-step proof

<ProofStep number={1} target="config" diagramStep="step1" title="Hypotheses and notation">
Let <VisualBind element="lineL1" color="carbon">$\ell_1$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="lineL2" color="carbon">$\ell_2$</VisualBind> be two <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>lines</ConceptLink> of a <ConceptLink targetId="plano" isDependency={true}>plane</ConceptLink> $\pi$ and <VisualBind element="lineT" color="carbon">$t$</VisualBind> a <ConceptLink targetId="secante" isDependency={true}>transversal</ConceptLink> meeting them at the <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> $A$ and $B$, with $A \neq B$. Let $P_1 \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell_1$ and $P_2 \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell_2$ be points lying in opposite <ConceptLink targetId="semiplano" isDependency={true}>half-planes</ConceptLink> with respect to $t$, and let <VisualBind element="angleAlpha" color="terracota">$\alpha = \angle P_1AB$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="angleBeta" color="pavo">$\beta = \angle P_2BA$</VisualBind> be the <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-alternos-internos" isDependency={true}>alternate interior angles</ConceptLink>. Since $t$ meets $\ell_1$ only at $A$ and $\ell_2$ only at $B$, we have $\neg(B \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell_1)$ and $\neg(A \, \mathbf{I} \, \ell_2)$; in particular, $\ell_1 \neq \ell_2$.
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={2} target="trianguloABX" diagramStep="step2" title="Direction (⇐): contradiction hypothesis">
Assume $\alpha \cong \beta$ and, for contradiction, that $\ell_1$ and $\ell_2$ are not <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas" isDependency={true}>parallel</ConceptLink>. Since they are coplanar and distinct, their <ConceptLink targetId="traza">traces</ConceptLink> share a point, unique by <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-incidencia-2" isDependency={true}>Axiom I.2</ConceptLink>. That point is not incident with $t$: otherwise it would be both $A$ and $B$. By the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-orden-4" isDependency={true}>plane separation</ConceptLink>, the intersection point lies in $\mathcal{H}_t(P_2)$ or in $\mathcal{H}_t(P_1)$. Call it <VisualBind element="pointX" color="ocre">$X$</VisualBind> in the first case and <VisualBind element="pointY" color="ocre">$Y$</VisualBind> in the second.
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={3} target="angleABX" diagramStep="step3" title="Case X in the half-plane of P₂">
$P_1$ and $X$ are points of $\ell_1$ in opposite half-planes with respect to $t$, so $\operatorname{seg}(P_1X)$ meets $t$, and it does so at $A$, the only common point of $\ell_1$ and $t$: $P_1 * A * X$ (<ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre" isDependency={true}>betweenness</ConceptLink>). Thus $\alpha$ and $\angle XAB$ are <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-suplementarios" isDependency={true}>adjacent supplementary</ConceptLink> angles, and $\alpha$ is an <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-exterior">exterior angle</ConceptLink> at $A$ of the <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangle</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="trianguloABX" color="ocre">$\triangle ABX$</VisualBind>. Moreover, $X$ and $P_2$ are points of $\ell_2$ in the same half-plane with respect to $t$, so $\overrightarrow{BX} = \overrightarrow{BP_2}$ (<ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta" isDependency={true}>ray</ConceptLink>) and the non-adjacent interior angle <VisualBind element="angleABX" color="ocre">$\angle ABX$</VisualBind> is $\beta$. The exterior angle theorem and the <ConceptLink targetId="orden-angulos" isDependency={true}>ordering of angles</ConceptLink> give:

<Formula>
  $$ \beta = \angle ABX < \alpha $$
</Formula>

But $\alpha \cong \beta$, and an angle cannot be both congruent to another and smaller than it (trichotomy of angle order): contradiction.
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={4} target="trianguloABY" diagramStep="step4" title="Case Y in the half-plane of P₁">
This case is symmetric, exchanging the roles of $A$ and $B$. $P_2$ and $Y$ are points of $\ell_2$ in opposite half-planes with respect to $t$, so $P_2 * B * Y$, and $\beta$ is an exterior angle at $B$ of the triangle <VisualBind element="trianguloABY" color="ocre">$\triangle ABY$</VisualBind>. Since $\overrightarrow{AY} = \overrightarrow{AP_1}$, the non-adjacent interior angle <VisualBind element="angleBAY" color="ocre">$\angle BAY$</VisualBind> is $\alpha$, and the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-angulo-exterior" isDependency={true}>exterior angle theorem</ConceptLink> gives $\alpha < \beta$, again contradicting $\alpha \cong \beta$. Therefore $\ell_1 \parallel \ell_2$.
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={5} target="lineL2" diagramStep="step5" title="Direction (⇒): transport of the angle">
Now assume $\ell_1 \parallel \ell_2$. By <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-4" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.4</ConceptLink>, there is a unique ray $\overrightarrow{BQ}$ with $Q \in \mathcal{H}_t(P_2)$ such that $\angle QBA \cong \alpha$. Let $m = \overleftrightarrow{BQ}$, a line of $\pi$. With respect to the transversal $t$, the lines $\ell_1$ and $m$ form the alternate interior angles $\alpha$ and $\angle QBA$, which are <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink>; by direction (⇐), already proved, $m \parallel \ell_1$.
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={6} target="lineL2" diagramStep="step5" title="Conclusion with Axiom IV">
The lines $m$ and <VisualBind element="lineL2" color="carbon">$\ell_2$</VisualBind> pass through $B$, which is not incident with $\ell_1$, and both are parallel to $\ell_1$ in $\pi$. By <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides" isDependency={true}>Axiom IV</ConceptLink>, $m = \ell_2$. Then $Q$ is a point of $\ell_2$ in the same half-plane as $P_2$ with respect to $t$, so $\overrightarrow{BQ} = \overrightarrow{BP_2}$ and $\angle QBA = \beta$. Consequently:

<Formula>
  $$ \beta = \angle QBA \cong \alpha $$
</Formula>

The two directions together give $\ell_1 \parallel \ell_2 \iff \alpha \cong \beta$.
</ProofStep>

### Deductive analysis

<Nota>
  Direction (⇐) is Proposition I.27 of <ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid</ConceptLink>'s <ConceptLink targetId="elementos-euclides">Elements</ConceptLink> and uses only the axioms of <ConceptLink targetId="incidencia">incidence</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre">order</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink>: it holds in <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-absoluto">absolute geometry</ConceptLink> without continuity axioms. Direction (⇒), Proposition I.29, uses Axiom IV only in step 6; in the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-hiperbolico">hyperbolic system</ConceptLink> that step fails, because several parallels to $\ell_1$ pass through $B$.
</Nota>
