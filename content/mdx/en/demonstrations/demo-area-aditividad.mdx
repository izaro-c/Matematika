<Capitular letra="T" />he <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-aditividad" isDependency={true}>area additivity theorem</ConceptLink> establishes that the area measure function over polygons satisfies the extensional property: the area of the union of two interiorly disjoint regions equals the sum of their individual areas.

<Separador />

### Proof step by step

<ProofStep number={1} title="Axiomatic definition of the area measure">
Following Hilbert, one postulates a function $\mu: \mathcal{P} \to \mathbb{R}^+$ on the set $\mathcal{P}$ of polygonal regions satisfying:
- $\mu(\text{unit square}) = 1$ (normalization).
- If $P \cong Q$ are <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink> polygons, then $\mu(P) = \mu(Q)$ (isometric invariance).
- (Additivity) If $P = P_1 \cup P_2$ with $P_1 \cap P_2$ of zero area, then $\mu(P) = \mu(P_1) + \mu(P_2)$.

Additivity is an axiom; the theorem asserts that the formula $b \cdot h$ for rectangles and its derivatives yield the unique coherent extension.
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={2} title="Decomposition of a generic polygon into triangles">
Every <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangle</ConceptLink> $\triangle$ has a well-defined area by the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangle area theorem</ConceptLink>: $\mu(\triangle) = \frac{1}{2} b h$. Every convex $n$-gon admits a triangulation into $n-2$ interiorly disjoint triangles. By induction on $n$:

<Formula>
  $$ \mu(P) = \sum_{k=1}^{n-2} \mu(\triangle_k) $$
</Formula>
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={3} title="Independence of the triangulation">
If $P = \bigsqcup_i \triangle_i = \bigsqcup_j \triangle_j'$ are two distinct triangulations of the same polygon, their superposition produces a common refined triangulation. Since each resulting triangle appears in both decompositions, additivity implies that the sums are the same for both original triangulations:

<Formula>
  $$ \sum_i \mu(\triangle_i) = \sum_k \mu(\triangle_k^*) = \sum_j \mu(\triangle_j') $$
</Formula>

where $\{\triangle_k^*\}$ is the common refined triangulation.
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={4} title="Additivity for finite decompositions">
Let $R_1, R_2$ be two polygonal regions with $R_1 \cap R_2$ of zero area (intersection in at most a segment or point). Taking compatible triangulations of $R_1$, $R_2$, and $R_1 \cup R_2$, the previous step guarantees:

<Formula>
  $$ \mu(R_1 \cup R_2) = \mu(R_1) + \mu(R_2) $$
</Formula>

By induction, the property extends to arbitrary finite unions.
</ProofStep>

### Deductive analysis

<Nota>
  This result is specific to two-dimensional geometry. In three dimensions, the Dehn–Sydler theorem shows that volume additivity is insufficient to resolve equidecomposability: there exist polyhedra of equal volume that are not equidecomposable, illustrating the depth of Hilbert's Third Problem.
</Nota>
