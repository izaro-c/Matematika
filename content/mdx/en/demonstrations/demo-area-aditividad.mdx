<DemonstrationSection>

<Capitular letra="P" />roof of the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-aditividad">area additivity theorem</ConceptLink> by <ConceptLink targetId="metodo-induccion" isDependency={true}>induction</ConceptLink> on the number $n$ of pieces, starting from additivity for two pieces, which is part of the definition of an <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-medida-area" isDependency={true}>area function</ConceptLink>.

The only geometric observation needed is that joining the first $n$ pieces yields again a <ConceptLink targetId="region-poligonal" isDependency={true}>polygonal region</ConceptLink> that does not overlap the next piece; thus the case of $n + 1$ pieces reduces to the case of two.

<Separador />

### Step-by-step proof

<ProofStep number={1} title="Statement proved by induction">
For each $n \geq 1$, let $P(n)$ be the statement: if $R_1, \dots, R_n$ are <ConceptLink targetId="region-poligonal" isDependency={true}>polygonal regions</ConceptLink> of a <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink> $\sigma$ with pairwise disjoint interiors, then

$$
\operatorname{A}(R_1 \cup \dots \cup R_n) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \operatorname{A}(R_i)
$$

By the <ConceptLink targetId="metodo-induccion" isDependency={true}>principle of induction</ConceptLink>, it suffices to prove $P(1)$ and that $P(n)$ implies $P(n + 1)$ for every $n \geq 1$.
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={2} title="Base case">
$P(1)$ reduces to the equality $\operatorname{A}(R_1) = \operatorname{A}(R_1)$. (The case $n = 2$ is exactly the additivity condition of the <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-medida-area" isDependency={true}>area function</ConceptLink>.)
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={3} title="The union of the first n pieces is a polygonal region">
Assume $P(n)$ and let $R_1, \dots, R_{n+1}$ be polygonal regions with pairwise disjoint interiors. Let $S = R_1 \cup \dots \cup R_n$. Each $R_i$ is a finite union of <ConceptLink targetId="region-triangular" isDependency={true}>triangular regions</ConceptLink>; hence so is $S$ and, by definition, $S$ is a <ConceptLink targetId="region-poligonal" isDependency={true}>polygonal region</ConceptLink>.
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={4} title="S and the next piece have disjoint interiors">
By the <ConceptLink targetId="distributividad">distributivity</ConceptLink> of intersection over union,

$$
S \cap R_{n+1} = \bigcup_{i=1}^{n} \left( R_i \cap R_{n+1} \right)
$$

By hypothesis, each $R_i \cap R_{n+1}$ with $i \leq n$ is contained in a finite union of <ConceptLink targetId="segmento" isDependency={true}>segments</ConceptLink>; a union of $n$ finite unions of segments is a finite union of segments. Hence $S$ and $R_{n+1}$ have disjoint interiors.
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={5} title="Additivity for two pieces and induction hypothesis">
Since $R_1 \cup \dots \cup R_{n+1} = S \cup R_{n+1}$, additivity of the <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-medida-area" isDependency={true}>area function</ConceptLink> for two pieces gives $\operatorname{A}(S \cup R_{n+1}) = \operatorname{A}(S) + \operatorname{A}(R_{n+1})$. The regions $R_1, \dots, R_n$ have pairwise disjoint interiors, so the induction hypothesis $P(n)$ gives $\operatorname{A}(S) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \operatorname{A}(R_i)$. Therefore,

$$
\operatorname{A}(R_1 \cup \dots \cup R_{n+1}) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \operatorname{A}(R_i) + \operatorname{A}(R_{n+1}) = \sum_{i=1}^{n+1} \operatorname{A}(R_i)
$$

which is $P(n + 1)$. By <ConceptLink targetId="metodo-induccion" isDependency={true}>induction</ConceptLink>, $P(n)$ holds for every $n \geq 1$.
</ProofStep>
