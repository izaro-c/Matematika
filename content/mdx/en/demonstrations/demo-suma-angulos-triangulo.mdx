<Capitular letra="G" />iven a planar <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink> $\triangle ABC$ with interior <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> $\alpha = \angle A$, $\beta = \angle B$, and $\gamma = \angle C$, the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-suma-angulos-triangulo">triangle angle sum theorem</ConceptLink> is demonstrated. The proof shows that the sum of the three interior angle measures is equal to a <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-suplementarios">straight angle</ConceptLink> ($180^\circ$).

<Separador />

### Step-by-step proof

<ProofStep number={1} target="triangulo" title="Configuration of the base triangle">
Let <VisualBind element="triangulo" color="carbon">$\triangle ABC$</VisualBind> be a triangle with its three interior angles denoted by <VisualBind element="angulo-a" color="terracota">$\alpha$</VisualBind> at vertex $A$, <VisualBind element="angulo-b" color="pavo">$\beta$</VisualBind> at vertex $B$, and <VisualBind element="angulo-c" color="ocre">$\gamma$</VisualBind> at vertex $C$.
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={2} target="paralela" title="Construction of the parallel line through the opposite vertex">
By <ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid's</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides" isDependency={true}>parallel postulate</ConceptLink>, there exists a unique line <VisualBind element="paralela" color="carbon"><ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallel</ConceptLink></VisualBind> to the base side <VisualBind element="base-ab" color="carbon">$\overline{AB}$</VisualBind> passing through the opposite vertex <VisualBind element="vertice-c" color="carbon">$C$</VisualBind>.
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={3} target="alterno-a" title="Congruence of the first pair of alternate interior angles">
Taking the line containing side $\overline{AC}$ as a <VisualBind element="transversal-ac" color="terracota">transversal</VisualBind>, the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-angulos-alternos-internos" isDependency={true}>alternate interior angles theorem</ConceptLink> ensures the <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink> between interior angle <VisualBind element="angulo-a" color="terracota">$\alpha$</VisualBind> and upper alternate angle <VisualBind element="alterno-a" color="terracota">$\alpha'$</VisualBind> at $C$:

<Formula>
  $$ \alpha \cong \alpha' $$
</Formula>
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={4} target="alterno-b" title="Congruence of the second pair of alternate interior angles">
Similarly, treating side $\overline{BC}$ as a <VisualBind element="transversal-bc" color="pavo">transversal</VisualBind>, interior angle <VisualBind element="angulo-b" color="pavo">$\beta$</VisualBind> is congruent to upper alternate angle <VisualBind element="alterno-b" color="pavo">$\beta'$</VisualBind> at $C$ by the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-angulos-alternos-internos" isDependency={true}>alternate interior angles theorem</ConceptLink>:

<Formula>
  $$ \beta \cong \beta' $$
</Formula>
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={5} target="angulo-llano" title="Composition of the straight angle and conclusion">
The three consecutive angles <VisualBind element="alterno-a" color="terracota">$\alpha'$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="angulo-c" color="ocre">$\gamma$</VisualBind>, and <VisualBind element="alterno-b" color="pavo">$\beta'$</VisualBind> lie adjacently on the parallel line through $C$, forming a <VisualBind element="angulo-llano" color="carbon"><ConceptLink targetId="angulos-suplementarios">straight angle</ConceptLink></VisualBind> summing to $180^\circ$:

<Formula>
  $$ \alpha' + \gamma + \beta' = 180^\circ $$
</Formula>

Substituting $\alpha'$ with $\alpha$ and $\beta'$ with $\beta$ from the established congruences yields:

<Formula>
  $$ \alpha + \beta + \gamma = 180^\circ $$
</Formula>
</ProofStep>

### Deductive analysis
