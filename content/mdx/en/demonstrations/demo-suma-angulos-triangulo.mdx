<Capitular letra="T" />he sum of the measures of the three interior <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> of any <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink> in the Euclidean plane equals exactly the measure of a <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-suplementarios" isDependency={true}>straight angle</ConceptLink> ($180^\circ$, or two right angles). The formal deduction of the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-suma-angulos-triangulo">triangle angle sum theorem</ConceptLink> develops through the <ConceptLink targetId="metodo-directo" isDependency={true}>direct method</ConceptLink> following Euclid's Proposition I.32, constructing the parallel to the base through the opposite vertex.

<Separador />

### Step-by-step proof

<ProofStep number={1} target="triangulo" diagramStep="step1" title="Configuration of the base triangle">
Consider <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="triangulo" color="carbon">$\triangle ABC$</VisualBind> with its three interior <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> denoted by <VisualBind element="angulo-a" color="terracota">$\alpha$</VisualBind> at vertex $A$, <VisualBind element="angulo-b" color="pavo">$\beta$</VisualBind> at vertex $B$, and <VisualBind element="angulo-c" color="ocre">$\gamma$</VisualBind> at vertex $C$.
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={2} target="paralela" diagramStep="step2" title="Construction of the parallel line through the opposite vertex">
By <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides" isDependency={true}>Euclid's parallel postulate</ConceptLink>, there exists a unique <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="paralela" color="carbon">$\ell$</VisualBind> <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas">parallel</ConceptLink> to base <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="base-ab" color="carbon">$\overline{AB}$</VisualBind> incident with opposite vertex <VisualBind element="vertice-c" color="carbon">$C$</VisualBind> ($C\,\mathbf{I}\,\ell$).
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={3} target="alterno-a" diagramStep="step3" title="First pair of alternate interior angles">
Taking the supporting <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> of side <VisualBind element="transversal-ac" color="terracota">$\overline{AC}$</VisualBind> as a <ConceptLink targetId="secante">transversal</ConceptLink> secant intersecting parallel lines $\ell$ and $\overleftrightarrow{AB}$, the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-angulos-alternos-internos" isDependency={true}>alternate interior angles theorem</ConceptLink> establishes the <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruence</ConceptLink> between interior angle <VisualBind element="angulo-a" color="terracota">$\alpha$</VisualBind> and upper alternate angle <VisualBind element="alterno-a" color="terracota">$\alpha'$</VisualBind> at $C$:

<Formula>
  $$ \alpha \cong \alpha' $$
</Formula>
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={4} target="alterno-b" diagramStep="step4" title="Second pair of alternate interior angles">
Similarly, the supporting <ConceptLink targetId="recta">line</ConceptLink> of side <VisualBind element="transversal-bc" color="pavo">$\overline{BC}$</VisualBind> acts as a <ConceptLink targetId="secante">transversal</ConceptLink> secant relative to the same pair of parallel lines. By the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-angulos-alternos-internos" isDependency={true}>alternate interior angles theorem</ConceptLink>, interior angle <VisualBind element="angulo-b" color="pavo">$\beta$</VisualBind> is congruent to upper alternate angle <VisualBind element="alterno-b" color="pavo">$\beta'$</VisualBind> at $C$:

<Formula>
  $$ \beta \cong \beta' $$
</Formula>
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={5} target="angulo-llano" diagramStep="step5" title="Composition of the straight angle and formal conclusion">
The three consecutive angles <VisualBind element="alterno-a" color="terracota">$\alpha'$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="angulo-c" color="ocre">$\gamma$</VisualBind>, and <VisualBind element="alterno-b" color="pavo">$\beta'$</VisualBind> share common vertex $C$ and lie contiguously along parallel line $\ell$, forming a <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-suplementarios" isDependency={true}>straight angle</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="angulo-llano" color="carbon">($180^\circ$)</VisualBind> summing to two right angles:

<Formula>
  $$ \alpha' + \gamma + \beta' = 180^\circ $$
</Formula>

Substituting $\alpha$ for $\alpha'$ and $\beta$ for $\beta'$ from the congruences established in the preceding steps yields the formal conclusion:

<Formula>
  $$ \alpha + \beta + \gamma = 180^\circ $$
</Formula>
</ProofStep>

### Deductive analysis

<Nota>
  Proposition I.32 of <ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid's</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="elementos-euclides">Elements</ConceptLink>. The existence and uniqueness of the parallel line through $C$ relies irreducibly on the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides">parallel postulate</ConceptLink>. In <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-hiperbolico">hyperbolic geometry</ConceptLink>, where infinitely many parallel lines pass through an exterior point, the interior angle sum is strictly less than $180^\circ$.
</Nota>
