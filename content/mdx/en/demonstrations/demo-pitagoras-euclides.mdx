<Capitular letra="G" />iven a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo-rectangulo">right triangle</ConceptLink> $\triangle ABC$ with a right angle at vertex $C$, the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-pitagoras">Pythagorean theorem</ConceptLink> is formally demonstrated. The proof establishes that the area of the <VisualBind element="cuadrado-c" color="ocre"><ConceptLink targetId="cuadrado">square constructed on the hypotenuse</ConceptLink></VisualBind> is equal to the sum of the areas of the <VisualBind element="cuadrado-a" color="pavo">square on cathetus $a$</VisualBind> and the <VisualBind element="cuadrado-b" color="terracota">square on cathetus $b$</VisualBind>.

<Separador />

### Step-by-step proof

<ProofStep number={1} target="triangulo" title="Configuration of the base right triangle">
Let <VisualBind element="triangulo-base" color="carbon">$\triangle ABC$</VisualBind> be a right triangle with the right angle positioned at vertex $C$. The legs are denoted by <VisualBind element="cateto-a" color="pavo">$a = |\overline{BC}|$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="cateto-b" color="terracota">$b = |\overline{CA}|$</VisualBind>, and the hypotenuse by <VisualBind element="hipotenusa-c" color="ocre">$c = |\overline{AB}|$</VisualBind>.
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={2} target="cuadrados" title="Construction of the three exterior squares">
On each of the three sides of $\triangle ABC$, three squares exterior to the polygon are constructed: the <VisualBind element="cuadrado-b" color="terracota">square on $b$</VisualBind> with area $b^2$, the <VisualBind element="cuadrado-a" color="pavo">square on $a$</VisualBind> with area $a^2$, and the <VisualBind element="cuadrado-c" color="ocre">square on the hypotenuse</VisualBind> with area $c^2$.
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={3} target="altura" title="Division of the hypotenuse square by the altitude">
From the right vertex $C$, the <VisualBind element="recta-altura" color="carbon"><ConceptLink targetId="perpendicular">perpendicular line</ConceptLink></VisualBind> to the hypotenuse $\overline{AB}$ is drawn. Extended through the square on the hypotenuse, this line partitions it into two rectangles: the <VisualBind element="rectangulo-izq" color="terracota">left rectangle</VisualBind> $R_L$ ($ADML$) and the <VisualBind element="rectangulo-der" color="pavo">right rectangle</VisualBind> $R_R$ ($BEML$).
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={4} target="triangulos-izq" title="Construction and congruence of the left triangles">
Auxiliary <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segments</ConceptLink> are drawn to form triangle <VisualBind element="triangulo-acd" color="terracota">$\triangle ACD$</VisualBind> and triangle <VisualBind element="triangulo-akb" color="terracota">$\triangle AKB$</VisualBind>. Applying the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-5" isDependency={true}>SAS congruence axiom</ConceptLink> yields <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink>:

<Formula>
  $$ \triangle ACD \cong \triangle AKB $$
</Formula>
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={5} target="areas-izq" title="Area equivalence between the left triangle and rectangle">
Triangle $\triangle ACD$ shares a base with the <VisualBind element="rectangulo-izq" color="terracota">left rectangle</VisualBind> $R_L$, so its area is exactly half of the area of $R_L$. Likewise, triangle $\triangle AKB$ shares a base with the <VisualBind element="cuadrado-b" color="terracota">square on $b$</VisualBind>, having an area equal to half of $b^2$. From the triangle congruence, it is deduced that:

<Formula>
  $$ \text{Area}(R_L) = b^2 $$
</Formula>
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={6} target="areas-der" title="Area equivalence for the right rectangle">
Through a strictly symmetrical argument applied to the right sector using auxiliary triangles <VisualBind element="triangulo-bce" color="pavo">$\triangle BCE$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="triangulo-abf" color="pavo">$\triangle ABF$</VisualBind>, it is proven that the <VisualBind element="rectangulo-der" color="pavo">right rectangle</VisualBind> $R_R$ has an area identical to $a^2$:

<Formula>
  $$ \text{Area}(R_R) = a^2 $$
</Formula>
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={7} target="cuadrados-final" title="Area addition and formal conclusion">
By the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-aditividad" isDependency={true}>area additivity theorem</ConceptLink>, the total area of the <VisualBind element="cuadrado-c" color="ocre">square on the hypotenuse</VisualBind> is the sum of its two constituent rectangles $R_L$ and $R_R$:

<Formula>
  $$ a^2 + b^2 = c^2 $$
</Formula>
</ProofStep>
