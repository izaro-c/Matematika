<Capitular letra="G" />iven a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo-rectangulo" isDependency={true}>right triangle</ConceptLink> $\triangle ABC$ with a <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto" isDependency={true}>right angle</ConceptLink> at <ConceptLink targetId="vertice" isDependency={true}>vertex</ConceptLink> $C$, the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-pitagoras">Pythagorean theorem</ConceptLink> is formally demonstrated. The proof establishes that the <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-medida-area" isDependency={true}>area</ConceptLink> of the <VisualBind element="sqC" color="ocre"><ConceptLink targetId="cuadrado">square constructed on the hypotenuse</ConceptLink></VisualBind> is equal to the sum of the areas of the squares constructed on the <ConceptLink targetId="cateto" isDependency={true}>legs</ConceptLink>: the <VisualBind element="sqA" color="terracota">square on leg $a$</VisualBind> and the <VisualBind element="sqB" color="canela">square on leg $b$</VisualBind>.

<Separador />

### Step-by-step proof

<ProofStep number={1} target="triangulo" title="Configuration of the base right triangle">
Let <VisualBind element="polyABC" color="granada">$\triangle ABC$</VisualBind> be the <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo-rectangulo" isDependency={true}>right triangle</ConceptLink> with the <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto" isDependency={true}>right angle</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="rightAngle" color="carbon">$\angle ACB$</VisualBind> located at <ConceptLink targetId="vertice" isDependency={true}>vertex</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="pC" color="carbon">$C$</VisualBind>. The <ConceptLink targetId="longitud" isDependency={true}>lengths</ConceptLink> of the <ConceptLink targetId="cateto" isDependency={true}>legs</ConceptLink> are denoted by $a = |BC|$ and $b = |CA|$, and the length of the <ConceptLink targetId="hipotenusa" isDependency={true}>hypotenuse</ConceptLink> by $c = |AB|$, with endpoints at <VisualBind element="pA" color="carbon">$A$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="pB" color="carbon">$B$</VisualBind>.
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={2} target="cuadrados" title="Construction of the three exterior squares">
On each of the three sides of triangle $\triangle ABC$, three exterior <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrado">squares</ConceptLink> are constructed: the <VisualBind element="sqB" color="canela">square on $b$</VisualBind> of area $b^2$, the <VisualBind element="sqA" color="terracota">square on $a$</VisualBind> of area $a^2$, and the <VisualBind element="sqC" color="mora">square on the hypotenuse</VisualBind> of area $c^2$, with auxiliary vertices <VisualBind element="pK" color="canela">$K$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pF" color="terracota">$F$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pD" color="mora">$D$</VisualBind>, and <VisualBind element="pE" color="mora">$E$</VisualBind>.
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={3} target="altura" title="Division of the hypotenuse square by the altitude">
From the right vertex $C$, the <VisualBind element="altSegment" color="carbon"><ConceptLink targetId="perpendicular" isDependency={true}>perpendicular line</ConceptLink></VisualBind> to hypotenuse $\overline{AB}$ is drawn, with foot <VisualBind element="pL" color="carbon">$L$</VisualBind>. Extended through the square on the hypotenuse to <VisualBind element="pM" color="carbon">$M$</VisualBind>, this line partitions the square into two rectangles: the <VisualBind element="rectADML" color="canela">left rectangle</VisualBind> $R_L$ ($ADML$) and the <VisualBind element="rectBEML" color="terracota">right rectangle</VisualBind> $R_R$ ($BEML$).
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={4} target="triangulos-izq" title="Construction and congruence of the left triangles">
Auxiliary <ConceptLink targetId="segmento" isDependency={true}>segments</ConceptLink> $\overline{CD}$ and $\overline{KB}$ are drawn to define triangles $\triangle ACD$ and $\triangle AKB$. One observes that $\overline{AD} \cong \overline{AB}$ (sides of square $c^2$) and $\overline{AC} \cong \overline{AK}$ (sides of square $b^2$). Furthermore, angles $\angle CAD$ and $\angle KAB$ are each the sum of a right angle and $\angle CAB$, whence $\angle CAD \cong \angle KAB$. By the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-lal" isDependency={true}>SAS congruence theorem</ConceptLink> (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-5" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.5</ConceptLink>), one has the <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruence</ConceptLink>:

$$
\triangle ACD \cong \triangle AKB
$$
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={5} target="areas-izq" title="Area equivalence between the left triangle and rectangle">
Triangle $\triangle ACD$ and the <VisualBind element="rectADML" color="canela">left rectangle</VisualBind> $R_L$ share base $\overline{AD}$ and have the same height $AL$; by the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangle area theorem</ConceptLink> and the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-rectangulo" isDependency={true}>rectangle area theorem</ConceptLink>, $\operatorname{A}(\triangle ACD) = \frac{1}{2} \operatorname{A}(R_L)$. Analogously, triangle $\triangle AKB$ and the <VisualBind element="sqB" color="canela">square on $b$</VisualBind> share base $\overline{AK}$ and height $AC$, so that $\operatorname{A}(\triangle AKB) = \frac{1}{2} b^2$. Since congruent triangles have equal area by the <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-medida-area" isDependency={true}>theory of area measure</ConceptLink>, it follows that:

$$
\operatorname{A}(R_L) = b^2
$$
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={6} target="areas-der" title="Area equivalence for the right rectangle">
Through a strictly symmetrical construction applied to the right side with auxiliary triangles $\triangle BCE$ and $\triangle ABF$, congruence $\triangle BCE \cong \triangle ABF$ by the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-lal" isDependency={true}>SAS criterion</ConceptLink> and comparing bases and heights via the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangle area theorem</ConceptLink> and the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-rectangulo" isDependency={true}>rectangle area theorem</ConceptLink> prove that the <VisualBind element="rectBEML" color="terracota">right rectangle</VisualBind> $R_R$ has area identical to that of the <VisualBind element="sqA" color="terracota">square on leg $a$</VisualBind>:

$$
\operatorname{A}(R_R) = a^2
$$
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={7} target="cuadrados-final" title="Area addition and formal conclusion">
By the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-aditividad" isDependency={true}>area additivity theorem</ConceptLink>, the total area of the <VisualBind element="sqC" color="mora">square on the hypotenuse</VisualBind> is the sum of its two disjoint rectangles $R_L$ and $R_R$:

$$
a^2 + b^2 = c^2
$$
</ProofStep>
