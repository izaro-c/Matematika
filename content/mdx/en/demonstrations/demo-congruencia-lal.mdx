<DemonstrationSection>

<Capitular letra="T" />he proof of the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-lal">SAS congruence theorem</ConceptLink> is based on the <ConceptLink targetId="metodo-contradiccion" isDependency={true}>method of contradiction</ConceptLink> developed by <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink> in §6 of the *Grundlagen der Geometrie*.

From the conditions postulated by <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-5" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.5</ConceptLink>, the reasoning deduces that the third <ConceptLink targetId="lado">side</ConceptLink> must necessarily be <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruent</ConceptLink>, for assuming the contrary would allow transporting an auxiliary <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink> via <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-1" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.1</ConceptLink> that would violate the uniqueness of the transport of <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> guaranteed by <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-4" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.4</ConceptLink>.

<Separador />

### Step-by-step proof

<ProofStep number={1} title="Hypotheses and application of Axiom III.5">
Let two <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangles</ConceptLink> $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle A'B'C'$ satisfy by hypothesis the <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink> relations:

$$
\overline{AB} \cong \overline{A'B'}, \quad \overline{AC} \cong \overline{A'C'}, \quad \angle BAC \cong \angle B'A'C'
$$

Applying <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-5" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.5</ConceptLink> to triangles $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle A'B'C'$, one deduces the congruence of the remaining angles at the corresponding <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertices</ConceptLink>:

$$
\angle ABC \cong \angle A'B'C', \quad \angle ACB \cong \angle A'C'B'
$$
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={2} title="Proof by contradiction hypothesis">
To prove that the third <ConceptLink targetId="lado">side</ConceptLink> is also <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruent</ConceptLink>, we proceed by the <ConceptLink targetId="metodo-contradiccion" isDependency={true}>method of contradiction</ConceptLink>. Suppose that the <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segments</ConceptLink> $\overline{BC}$ and $\overline{B'C'}$ are not congruent to each other:

$$
\overline{BC} \not\cong \overline{B'C'}
$$
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={3} title="Transport of the auxiliary segment onto the ray">
By the axiom of segment transport (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-1" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.1</ConceptLink>), there exists on the <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta">ray</ConceptLink> $\overrightarrow{B'C'}$ a unique <ConceptLink targetId="punto">point</ConceptLink> $D$ such that the <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink> $\overline{B'D}$ is <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruent</ConceptLink> to $\overline{BC}$:

$$
D \in \overrightarrow{B'C'}, \quad \overline{BC} \cong \overline{B'D}
$$

Since by the contradiction hypothesis $\overline{BC} \not\cong \overline{B'C'}$, point $D$ does not coincide with endpoint $C'$:

$$
D \neq C'
$$
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={4} title="Construction of the auxiliary triangle and second application of III.5">
We consider the auxiliary <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink> $\triangle A'B'D$. Comparing $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle A'B'D$, we have:
1. $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{A'B'}$ (by initial hypothesis).
2. $\overline{BC} \cong \overline{B'D}$ (by construction of point $D$).
3. Since $D \in \overrightarrow{B'C'}$, the <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta">ray</ConceptLink> $\overrightarrow{B'D}$ coincides with $\overrightarrow{B'C'}$, whence $\angle A'B'D = \angle A'B'C'$; therefore, $\angle ABC \cong \angle A'B'D$.

As the premises of <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-5" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.5</ConceptLink> are satisfied for the pair of triangles $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle A'B'D$, this axiom establishes the congruence of the remaining angles at the top <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertex</ConceptLink>:

$$
\angle BAC \cong \angle B'A'D
$$
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={5} title="Uniqueness of angle transport and contradiction">
By the original hypothesis we have $\angle BAC \cong \angle B'A'C'$, which together with the preceding result implies:

$$
\angle B'A'D \cong \angle B'A'C'
$$

Since point $D$ belongs to the <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta">ray</ConceptLink> $\overrightarrow{B'C'}$, points $D$ and $C'$ lie in the same <ConceptLink targetId="semiplano">half-plane</ConceptLink> with respect to the supporting line $\overleftrightarrow{A'B'}$. Therefore, the rays $\overrightarrow{A'D}$ and $\overrightarrow{A'C'}$ lie in the same half-plane with respect to $\overleftrightarrow{A'B'}$.

By the axiom of angle transport (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-4" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.4</ConceptLink>), in a given half-plane there exists a unique ray originating at $A'$ that forms with $\overrightarrow{A'B'}$ a given <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angle</ConceptLink>. Consequently, both rays coincide:

$$
\overrightarrow{A'D} = \overrightarrow{A'C'}
$$

The line $\overleftrightarrow{A'C'}$ intersects the line $\overleftrightarrow{B'C'}$ at the unique point $C'$. Since $D$ belongs to both $\overrightarrow{A'D} \subset \operatorname{tr}(\overleftrightarrow{A'C'})$ and $\overrightarrow{B'C'} \subset \operatorname{tr}(\overleftrightarrow{B'C'})$, one necessarily deduces:

$$
D = C'
$$

which contradicts the condition $D \neq C'$ obtained in step 3.
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={6} title="Conclusion and global congruence">
The contradiction establishes the falsity of the hypothesis $\overline{BC} \not\cong \overline{B'C'}$. Consequently, the third <ConceptLink targetId="lado">side</ConceptLink> is necessarily <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruent</ConceptLink>:

$$
\overline{BC} \cong \overline{B'C'}
$$

Having established the mutual congruence of the three pairs of corresponding sides ($\overline{AB} \cong \overline{A'B'}$, $\overline{BC} \cong \overline{B'C'}$, $\overline{AC} \cong \overline{A'C'}$) and of the three pairs of corresponding angles ($\angle BAC \cong \angle B'A'C'$, $\angle ABC \cong \angle A'B'C'$, $\angle ACB \cong \angle A'C'B'$), one concludes the <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia-triangulos">triangle congruence</ConceptLink>:

$$
\triangle ABC \cong \triangle A'B'C'
$$
</ProofStep>
