<Capitular letra="G" />iven an <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angle</ConceptLink> $\angle AOB$ with vertex at $O$, the existence and uniqueness of an interior ray $\overrightarrow{OM}$ dividing the angle into two <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruent</ConceptLink> parts is established, founded upon the theory of <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-ala">triangle congruence</ConceptLink>.

<Separador />

### Step-by-step proof

<ProofStep number={1} target="rayOA" title="Transportation of segments along the angle sides">
On the ray of side $OA$, fix a <ConceptLink targetId="punto">point</ConceptLink> $C$ distinct from $O$. By the axiom of <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segment</ConceptLink> transport (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-1" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.1</ConceptLink>), construct a point $D$ on the ray of side $OB$ such that their distances from the origin are congruent:

<Formula>
  $$ \overline{OC} \cong \overline{OD} $$
</Formula>
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={2} target="p4" title="Auxiliary segment and midpoint">
Draw the auxiliary segment $\overline{CD}$. By the axioms of order and incidence, there exists a unique midpoint $M$ on segment $\overline{CD}$ satisfying the equidistance:

<Formula>
  $$ \overline{CM} \cong \overline{DM} $$
</Formula>
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={3} target="bisAOB" title="Congruence of auxiliary triangles">
Consider triangles $\triangle OCM$ and $\triangle ODM$. Since $\overline{OC} \cong \overline{OD}$, $\overline{CM} \cong \overline{DM}$, and segment $\overline{OM}$ is shared by both, applying the congruence criteria derived from the SAS axiom (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-5" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.5</ConceptLink>) yields triangle congruence:

<Formula>
  $$ \triangle OCM \cong \triangle ODM $$
</Formula>
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={4} target="nonReflexAngleBO6" title="Equality of interior angles and existence">
From the congruence of the triangles, the congruence of their corresponding homologous angles is deduced: $\angle COM \cong \angle DOM$. Consequently, the ray $\overrightarrow{OM}$ is a valid bisector of angle $\angle AOB$.
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={5} target="nonReflexAngle6OA" title="Proof of uniqueness">
If there existed another bisecting ray $\overrightarrow{OM'}$, it would form an identical angle with ray $OA$. By the uniqueness of angle transport (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-4" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.4</ConceptLink>) on a given half-plane, the rays $\overrightarrow{OM'}$ and $\overrightarrow{OM}$ must coincide. Therefore, the bisector is unique.
</ProofStep>
