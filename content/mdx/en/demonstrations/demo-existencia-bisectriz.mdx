<Capitular letra="T" />he <ConceptLink targetId="metodo-directo" isDependency={true}>direct deductive proof</ConceptLink> of the existence and uniqueness of the <ConceptLink targetId="bisectriz">bisector</ConceptLink> for the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-existencia-bisectriz">theorem of existence and uniqueness of the angle bisector</ConceptLink> follows <ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid's</ConceptLink> classical construction (<ConceptLink targetId="elementos-euclides">Elements</ConceptLink>, Proposition I.9). It is founded upon congruent segment transport, an auxiliary equilateral triangle construction, and the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-lll" isDependency={true}>SSS congruence criterion</ConceptLink> for triangles.

<Separador />

### Step-by-step formal proof

<ProofStep number={1} target="rayOA" title="Transportation of congruent segments along the sides">
Consider a convex <ConceptLink targetId="angulo" isDependency={true}>angle</ConceptLink> with <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertex</ConceptLink> at <VisualBind element="pO" color="carbon">$O$</VisualBind> bounded by the <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta" isDependency={true}>rays</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="rayOA" color="pavo">$\overrightarrow{OA}$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="rayOB" color="pavo">$\overrightarrow{OB}$</VisualBind>.

Fix an arbitrary <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>point</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="pA" color="terracota">$A$</VisualBind> on ray $\overrightarrow{OA}$ distinct from $O$, determining the <ConceptLink targetId="segmento" isDependency={true}>segment</ConceptLink> $\overline{OA}$. By the segment transport axiom (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-1" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.1</ConceptLink>), there exists a unique point <VisualBind element="pB" color="terracota">$B$</VisualBind> on ray $\overrightarrow{OB}$ such that segment $\overline{OB}$ is <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink> to $\overline{OA}$:

$$
\overline{OA} \cong \overline{OB}
$$
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={2} target="p6" title="Construction of the auxiliary equilateral triangle">
Construct the <ConceptLink targetId="segmento" isDependency={true}>segment</ConceptLink> $\overline{AB}$. Taking this segment as a <ConceptLink targetId="base">base</ConceptLink>, apply the Euclidean construction of an <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo-equilatero" isDependency={true}>equilateral triangle</ConceptLink> (<ConceptLink targetId="elementos-euclides">Elements</ConceptLink>, Proposition I.1) on the <ConceptLink targetId="semiplano" isDependency={true}>half-plane</ConceptLink> bounded by line $\overleftrightarrow{AB}$ opposite to $O$, or in its interior. This yields a <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>point</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="p6" color="ocre">$C$</VisualBind> such that <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangle</ConceptLink> $\triangle ABC$ is equilateral:

$$
\overline{AC} \cong \overline{BC} \cong \overline{AB}
$$

In particular, the two <ConceptLink targetId="lado" isDependency={true}>sides</ConceptLink> $\overline{AC}$ and $\overline{BC}$ are mutually congruent ($\overline{AC} \cong \overline{BC}$).
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={3} target="bisAOB" title="Ray tracing and triangle congruence by the SSS criterion">
Draw the <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta" isDependency={true}>ray</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="bisAOB" color="pavo">$\overrightarrow{OC}$</VisualBind> joining vertex <VisualBind element="pO" color="carbon">$O$</VisualBind> with point <VisualBind element="p6" color="ocre">$C$</VisualBind>.

Compare <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangles</ConceptLink> $\triangle OAC$ and $\triangle OBC$. Three congruences of their corresponding <ConceptLink targetId="lado" isDependency={true}>sides</ConceptLink> hold:
1. $\overline{OA} \cong \overline{OB}$, by the initial construction via segment transport (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-1" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.1</ConceptLink>).
2. $\overline{AC} \cong \overline{BC}$, as sides of the equilateral triangle $\triangle ABC$.
3. $\overline{OC} \cong \overline{OC}$, by the <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-reflexiva">reflexivity</ConceptLink> of segment <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruence</ConceptLink>.

By the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-lll" isDependency={true}>SSS criterion</ConceptLink> for <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia-triangulos" isDependency={true}>triangle congruence</ConceptLink> (Euclid I.8), both triangles are congruent:

$$
\triangle OAC \cong \triangle OBC
$$
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={4} target="nonReflexAngle6OA" title="Existence of the angle bisection">
From the <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia-triangulos" isDependency={true}>triangle congruence</ConceptLink> $\triangle OAC \cong \triangle OBC$, the congruence of all corresponding homologous elements follows. In particular, the <ConceptLink targetId="angulo" isDependency={true}>angles</ConceptLink> with <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertex</ConceptLink> at $O$ opposite to the congruent sides $\overline{AC}$ and $\overline{BC}$ are mutually congruent:

$$
\angle AOC \cong \angle BOC
$$

Because point $C$ lies in the interior of angle $\angle AOB$, ray <VisualBind element="bisAOB" color="pavo">$\overrightarrow{OC}$</VisualBind> divides the angle into two congruent parts, demonstrating the existence of the <ConceptLink targetId="bisectriz">bisector</ConceptLink>.
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={5} target="bisAOB" title="Uniqueness of the bisecting ray by Axiom III.4">
To prove uniqueness, suppose there exists another <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta" isDependency={true}>ray</ConceptLink> $\overrightarrow{OC'}$ originating at $O$ and contained in the interior of $\angle AOB$ satisfying $\angle AOC' \cong \angle BOC'$.

By the <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-transitiva">transitivity</ConceptLink> of angular congruence, $\angle AOC \cong \angle AOC'$ holds. However, by the axiom of angle transport and uniqueness (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-4" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.4</ConceptLink>), in the <ConceptLink targetId="semiplano" isDependency={true}>half-plane</ConceptLink> bounded by line $\overleftrightarrow{OA}$ containing $B$, there exists a **unique** ray originating at $O$ that forms an angle congruent to $\angle AOC$ with $\overrightarrow{OA}$. Consequently:

$$
\overrightarrow{OC'} = \overrightarrow{OC}
$$

The uniqueness of the <ConceptLink targetId="bisectriz">bisector</ConceptLink> is established.
</ProofStep>
