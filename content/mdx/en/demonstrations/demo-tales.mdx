<Capitular letra="T" />he <ConceptLink targetId="metodo-directo" isDependency={true}>direct proof</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-tales">Thales's theorem</ConceptLink> follows <ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid</ConceptLink> (Elements VI.2): the <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangle</ConceptLink> case is proved first by comparing <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-medida-area" isDependency={true}>areas</ConceptLink> of triangles with the same <ConceptLink targetId="altura">height</ConceptLink>, and the general case of three <ConceptLink targetId="paralelas" isDependency={true}>parallels</ConceptLink> and two <ConceptLink targetId="secante">transversals</ConceptLink> is then reduced to it.

The central tool is the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangle area theorem</ConceptLink>, $\operatorname{A}(\triangle XYZ) = \tfrac{1}{2}\,b\,h$: two triangles with the same height have areas proportional to their bases.

<Separador />

### Step-by-step proof

<ProofStep number={1} target="step1" title="Setting of the triangle case">
Let <VisualBind element="polyABC" color="carbon">$\triangle ABC$</VisualBind> be a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangle</ConceptLink> and let $\ell$ be the <VisualBind element="parDBC" color="ocre">line parallel</VisualBind> to $\overleftrightarrow{BC}$, distinct from it, that meets the side <VisualBind element="segAB" color="carbon">$\overline{AB}$</VisualBind> at <VisualBind element="pD" color="terracota">$D$</VisualBind> and the side <VisualBind element="segCA" color="carbon">$\overline{CA}$</VisualBind> at <VisualBind element="pE" color="terracota">$E$</VisualBind>, with $A * D * B$ and $A * E * C$ (<ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre" isDependency={true}>betweenness</ConceptLink>). We must prove:

$$
\frac{|AD|}{|DB|} = \frac{|AE|}{|EC|}.
$$
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={2} target="step2" title="Triangles BDE and CDE have the same area">
The triangles <VisualBind element="polyBDE" color="ocre">$\triangle BDE$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="polyCDE" color="ocre">$\triangle CDE$</VisualBind> share the base <VisualBind element="segDE" color="ocre">$\overline{DE}$</VisualBind>. Let $B_0$ and $C_0$ be the feet of the <ConceptLink targetId="perpendicular" isDependency={true}>perpendiculars</ConceptLink> from $B$ and $C$ to $\ell$. The lines $\overleftrightarrow{BB_0}$ and $\overleftrightarrow{CC_0}$ are perpendicular to $\ell$ and hence parallel to each other (<ConceptLink targetId="teorema-angulos-alternos-internos" isDependency={true}>alternate interior angles theorem</ConceptLink>); moreover $\overleftrightarrow{BC} \parallel \ell$. So $BB_0C_0C$ is a <ConceptLink targetId="paralelogramo" isDependency={true}>parallelogram</ConceptLink> and its opposite sides are congruent (<ConceptLink targetId="teorema-lados-opuestos-paralelogramo" isDependency={true}>opposite sides of a parallelogram theorem</ConceptLink>, which uses the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides" isDependency={true}>parallel axiom</ConceptLink>): $|BB_0| = |CC_0| = h$. By the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangle area theorem</ConceptLink>:

$$
\operatorname{A}(\triangle BDE) = \tfrac{1}{2}\,|DE|\,h = \operatorname{A}(\triangle CDE).
$$
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={3} target="step3" title="Ratio of ADE to BDE">
Let $H_1$ be the foot of the perpendicular from $E$ to $\overleftrightarrow{AB}$ and $h_1 = |EH_1|$. Since $A$, $D$ and $B$ lie on $\overleftrightarrow{AB}$, the <ConceptLink targetId="altura" isDependency={true}>height</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="segH1" color="ocre">$\overline{EH_1}$</VisualBind> is at the same time a height of <VisualBind element="polyADE" color="ocre">$\triangle ADE$</VisualBind> (base $\overline{AD}$) and of <VisualBind element="polyBDE" color="ocre">$\triangle BDE$</VisualBind> (base $\overline{DB}$). By the triangle area theorem:

$$
\frac{\operatorname{A}(\triangle ADE)}{\operatorname{A}(\triangle BDE)} = \frac{\tfrac{1}{2}\,|AD|\,h_1}{\tfrac{1}{2}\,|DB|\,h_1} = \frac{|AD|}{|DB|}.
$$
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={4} target="step4" title="Ratio of ADE to CDE">
Likewise, let $H_2$ be the foot of the perpendicular from $D$ to $\overleftrightarrow{AC}$ and $h_2 = |DH_2|$. The height <VisualBind element="segH2" color="ocre">$\overline{DH_2}$</VisualBind> is common to <VisualBind element="polyADE" color="ocre">$\triangle ADE$</VisualBind> (base $\overline{AE}$) and <VisualBind element="polyCDE" color="ocre">$\triangle CDE$</VisualBind> (base $\overline{EC}$). By the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangle area theorem</ConceptLink>:

$$
\frac{\operatorname{A}(\triangle ADE)}{\operatorname{A}(\triangle CDE)} = \frac{|AE|}{|EC|}.
$$
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={5} target="step5" title="Conclusion in the triangle">
The denominators in steps 3 and 4 are equal by step 2, and non-zero by positivity of <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-medida-area" isDependency={true}>area</ConceptLink>. Equating:

$$
\frac{|AD|}{|DB|} = \frac{|AE|}{|EC|}.
$$

Since $A * D * B$ and $A * E * C$, <ConceptLink targetId="longitud" isDependency={true}>length</ConceptLink> is additive: $|AB| = |AD| + |DB|$ and $|AC| = |AE| + |EC|$. Adding $1$ to the reciprocals of both sides also gives $|AB|/|AD| = |AC|/|AE|$, that is, $|AD|/|AB| = |AE|/|AC|$.
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={6} target="step6" title="General case: three parallels and two transversals">
Now let $\ell_1, \ell_2, \ell_3$ be distinct parallels meeting $t$ at $A, B, C$ and $t'$ at $A', B', C'$, with $A * B * C$.

*Order on $t'$.* $A$ and $C$ lie in different <ConceptLink targetId="semiplano" isDependency={true}>half-planes</ConceptLink> with respect to $\ell_2$. The segment $\overline{AA'}$ is contained in $\operatorname{tr}(\ell_1)$, which does not meet $\ell_2$, so $A'$ lies in the half-plane of $A$; likewise $C'$ lies in that of $C$. Hence $\operatorname{seg}(A'C')$ meets $\ell_2$, and the intersection point is $B'$, the only common point of $t'$ and $\ell_2$: $A' * B' * C'$.

*If $t \parallel t'$*, the quadrilaterals $ABB'A'$ and $BCC'B'$ are parallelograms, so $|AB| = |A'B'|$ and $|BC| = |B'C'|$, and the proportion is immediate.

*If $t$ and $t'$ meet*, let $m$ be the parallel to $t'$ through $A$, unique by the <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides" isDependency={true}>parallel axiom</ConceptLink> ($m = t'$ if $A = A'$). The line $m$ meets $\ell_2$ and $\ell_3$ at $B''$ and $C''$ and, by the previous argument, $A * B'' * C''$. The quadrilaterals $AB''B'A'$ and $B''C''C'B'$ are parallelograms (or degenerate into coinciding points if $A = A'$), so that $|AB''| = |A'B'|$ and $|B''C''| = |B'C'|$. Since $m \neq t$, the points $A$, $C$, $C''$ are not collinear and $\ell_2$ is a parallel to $\overleftrightarrow{CC''} = \ell_3$ meeting $\operatorname{seg}(AC)$ at $B$ and $\operatorname{seg}(AC'')$ at $B''$. By the triangle case applied to $\triangle ACC''$:

$$
\frac{|AB|}{|BC|} = \frac{|AB''|}{|B''C''|} = \frac{|A'B'|}{|B'C'|}.
$$

The second proportion of the statement follows as in step 5.
</ProofStep>
