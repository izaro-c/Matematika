<Capitular letra="T" />he proof of the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-angulos-equivalencia">equivalence theorem of angle congruence</ConceptLink> follows the <ConceptLink targetId="metodo-directo" isDependency={true}>direct method</ConceptLink> developed by <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink> in §6 of the *Grundlagen der Geometrie*.

Beginning from reflexivity postulated in <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-4" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.4</ConceptLink>, the argument transports <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruent</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segments</ConceptLink> onto the <ConceptLink targetId="lado">arms</ConceptLink> of the <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> via <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-1" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.1</ConceptLink> and applies the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-lal" isDependency={true}>SAS congruence theorem</ConceptLink> to establish the congruence of <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangles</ConceptLink>, deducing <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-simetrica">symmetry</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-transitiva">transitivity</ConceptLink>.

### Demostración paso a paso

<ProofStep number={1} title="Reflexivity of angle congruence">
Let $\angle\alpha = \angle AOB$ be any <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angle</ConceptLink> with <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertex</ConceptLink> $O$ and arms the <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta">rays</ConceptLink> $\overrightarrow{OA}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB}$. By the statement of <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-4" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.4</ConceptLink>, every angle is congruent to itself, directly satisfying <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-reflexiva">reflexivity</ConceptLink>:
$$
\angle AOB \cong \angle AOB
$$
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={2} title="Construction of auxiliary triangles and symmetry">
To prove <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-simetrica">symmetry</ConceptLink>, suppose two <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> $\angle AOB$ and $\angle A'O'B'$ such that:
$$
\angle AOB \cong \angle A'O'B'
$$
By the axiom of segment transport (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-1" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.1</ConceptLink>), we can choose points $A \in \overrightarrow{OA} \setminus \{O\}$ and $B \in \overrightarrow{OB} \setminus \{O\}$, and determine points $A' \in \overrightarrow{O'A'} \setminus \{O'\}$ and $B' \in \overrightarrow{O'B'} \setminus \{O'\}$ such that:
$$
\overline{OA} \cong \overline{O'A'}, \quad \overline{OB} \cong \overline{O'B'}
$$
Since the points are not <ConceptLink targetId="colinealidad">collinear</ConceptLink>, they determine <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangles</ConceptLink> $\triangle OAB$ and $\triangle O'A'B'$. By the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-lal" isDependency={true}>SAS congruence theorem</ConceptLink>, we deduce $\triangle OAB \cong \triangle O'A'B'$, which implies $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{A'B'}$ and $\angle OAB \cong \angle O'A'B'$.

By <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-4" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.4</ConceptLink>, there exists a unique ray $\overrightarrow{OB''}$ in the <ConceptLink targetId="semiplano">half-plane</ConceptLink> of $\overleftrightarrow{OA}$ containing $B$ such that $\angle A'O'B' \cong \angle AOB''$. On this ray we choose, by <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-1" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.1</ConceptLink>, the point $B''$ with $\overline{OB''} \cong \overline{O'B'}$. Applying again the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-lal" isDependency={true}>SAS theorem</ConceptLink> to $\triangle O'A'B'$ and $\triangle OAB''$, we obtain $\triangle O'A'B' \cong \triangle OAB''$, whence $\overline{A'B'} \cong \overline{AB''}$ and $\angle O'A'B' \cong \angle OAB''$.

By symmetry and transitivity of segment congruence (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-2">Axiom III.2</ConceptLink>), $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{AB''}$, and by uniqueness of angle transport in <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-4" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.4</ConceptLink>, the ray $\overrightarrow{OB''}$ must necessarily coincide with $\overrightarrow{OB}$, which implies $B'' = B$ and concludes:
$$
\angle A'O'B' \cong \angle AOB
$$
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={3} title="Deduction of transitivity via triangle congruence">
To prove <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-transitiva">transitivity</ConceptLink>, let three <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> be such that:
$$
\angle AOB \cong \angle A'O'B' \quad \land \quad \angle A'O'B' \cong \angle A''O''B''
$$
By <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-1" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.1</ConceptLink>, we fix points on their arms satisfying:
$$
\overline{OA} \cong \overline{O'A'} \cong \overline{O''A''}, \quad \overline{OB} \cong \overline{O'B'} \cong \overline{O''B''}
$$
Applying the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-lal" isDependency={true}>SAS congruence theorem</ConceptLink> to the first pair of triangles, we obtain $\triangle OAB \cong \triangle O'A'B'$, which ensures $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{A'B'}$. Analogously, for the second pair, the SAS criterion gives $\triangle O'A'B' \cong \triangle O''A''B''$, implying $\overline{A'B'} \cong \overline{A''B''}$. By transitivity of segment congruence (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-2">Axiom III.2</ConceptLink>), it follows that:
$$
\overline{AB} \cong \overline{A''B''}
$$
Triangles $\triangle OAB$ and $\triangle O''A''B''$ have their three pairs of corresponding <ConceptLink targetId="lado">sides</ConceptLink> congruent. By the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-lll">SSS congruence theorem</ConceptLink> (or by transporting angle $\angle AOB$ onto $\overrightarrow{O''A''}$ and applying SAS), we deduce $\triangle OAB \cong \triangle O''A''B''$, establishing the congruence of the corresponding angles at vertices $O$ and $O''$:
$$
\angle AOB \cong \angle A''O''B''
$$
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={4} title="Conclusion of the equivalence relation">
Having verified that <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink> between <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> is reflexive (step 1), symmetric (step 2), and transitive (step 3), it is formally proven that it constitutes an <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-equivalencia">equivalence relation</ConceptLink> on the set of all angles:
$$
\angle\alpha \cong \angle\beta \implies (\angle\alpha \sim \angle\beta)
$$
</ProofStep>
