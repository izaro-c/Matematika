<Capitular letra="I" />n a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo-rectangulo">right triangle</ConceptLink> with legs $a, b$ and hypotenuse $c$, the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-pitagoras">Pythagorean theorem</ConceptLink> is proven through the equidecomposition of an <VisualBind element="outerSquare" color="carbon"><ConceptLink targetId="cuadrado">outer square</ConceptLink></VisualBind> of side length $(a+b)$.

<Separador />

### Step-by-step proof

<ProofStep number={1} target="outerSquare" title="Construction of the bounding outer square">
Construct an enclosing <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrado">square</ConceptLink> of side length $(a+b)$. The total area of this outer square is given by algebraic expansion:

<Formula>
  $$ \text{Total Area} = (a+b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2 $$
</Formula>
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={2} target="t1" title="Placement of the four right triangles">
In each of the four corners of the outer square, place a right triangle with legs $a$ and $b$. By the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-triangulo" isDependency={true}>area of a triangle theorem</ConceptLink>, the combined area of the four congruent triangles is:

<Formula>
  $$ 4 \times \left( \frac{1}{2} a b \right) = 2ab $$
</Formula>
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={3} target="emptyC" title="Characterization of the interior square">
The four hypotenuses of the triangles form the boundary of a central region of side length $c$. Because the acute angles of each right triangle sum to $90^\circ$, the interior angle at each vertex of the central quadrilateral is $180^\circ - 90^\circ = 90^\circ$. Thus, it is an interior square of area $c^2$.
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={4} target="emptyA" title="Application of the area additivity principle">
By the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-aditividad" isDependency={true}>area additivity theorem</ConceptLink>, the area of the outer square equals the sum of the central interior square's area and the areas of the four triangles:

<Formula>
  $$ (a+b)^2 = c^2 + 4 \cdot \left( \frac{1}{2} a b \right) $$
</Formula>
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={5} target="emptyB" title="Algebraic simplification and conclusion">
Expanding the left-hand side and cancelling the $2ab$ term on both sides yields:

<Formula>
  $$ a^2 + 2ab + b^2 = c^2 + 2ab \implies a^2 + b^2 = c^2 $$
</Formula>
</ProofStep>

### Deductive analysis

<Nota>
  Visual and algebraic dissection proof historically attributed to Pythagorean and Indian mathematicians (Bhaskara). It is founded on the isometry invariance of area and the <ConceptLink targetId="teoria-medida-area">theory of area measurement</ConceptLink>.
</Nota>
