### Step-by-step proof

<ProofStep number={1} target="outerSquare" title="Construction of the bounding outer square">
An enclosing exterior <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrado">square</ConceptLink> of side $(a+b)$ is constructed. By the definition of the area of a square (<ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-rectangulo" isDependency={true}>rectangle area theorem</ConceptLink>), its total area satisfies the algebraic identity:

$$
\operatorname{A}(\text{exterior}) = (a+b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2
$$
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={2} target="t1" title="Placement of the four right triangles">
In each of the four corners of the outer square, a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo-rectangulo" isDependency={true}>right triangle</ConceptLink> with legs $a$ and $b$ is placed (<VisualBind element="t1" color="carbon">$T_1$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="t2" color="carbon">$T_2$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="t3" color="carbon">$T_3$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="t4" color="carbon">$T_4$</VisualBind>). By the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-lal" isDependency={true}>SAS congruence theorem</ConceptLink>, the four triangles are mutually congruent. By the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangle area theorem</ConceptLink>, the sum of the areas of the four triangles is:

$$
4 \cdot \left(\frac{1}{2} a b\right) = 2ab
$$
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={3} target="emptyC" title="Characterization of the interior square">
The four hypotenuses of the triangles form the boundary of a central quadrilateral region <VisualBind element="emptyC" color="ocre">$Q_c$</VisualBind> with sides of length $c$. Since the two acute <ConceptLink targetId="angulo" isDependency={true}>angles</ConceptLink> of each right triangle are <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-suplementarios" isDependency={true}>complementary</ConceptLink> and sum to $90^\circ$, the interior angle at each vertex of the central quadrilateral measures $180^\circ - 90^\circ = 90^\circ$. Having all four sides congruent and all four angles right, $Q_c$ is an interior <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrado">square</ConceptLink> of area $c^2$.
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={4} target="emptyA" title="Application of the area additivity theorem">
By the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-aditividad" isDependency={true}>area additivity theorem</ConceptLink>, the area of the outer square equals the area of the central interior square plus the sum of the areas of the four corner triangles:

$$
(a+b)^2 = \operatorname{A}(Q_c) + 2ab = c^2 + 2ab
$$
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={5} target="emptyB" title="Algebraic simplification and conclusion">
Subtracting the term $2ab$ from both sides of the equality, or alternatively rearranging the four triangles into two rectangles of dimensions $a \times b$ (<ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-rectangulo" isDependency={true}>rectangle area theorem</ConceptLink>) that leave exposed two interior <ConceptLink targetId="cuadrado">squares</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="emptyA" color="terracota">$Q_a$</VisualBind> (of area $a^2$) and <VisualBind element="emptyB" color="canela">$Q_b$</VisualBind> (of area $b^2$), one formally concludes:

$$
a^2 + b^2 = c^2
$$
</ProofStep>

### Deductive analysis

<Nota>
This geometric proof by dissection and rearrangement of areas is attested with great antiquity in the Chinese mathematical tradition (in the *Gougu* figure of the *Zhoubi suanjing*, with commentary by Zhao Shuang in the 3rd century) and in several classical Mediterranean and Indian traditions. Its rigorous validity rests on the invariance of area under congruence and the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-area-aditividad">area additivity theorem</ConceptLink>.
</Nota>
