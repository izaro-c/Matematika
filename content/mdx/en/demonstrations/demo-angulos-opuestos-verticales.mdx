<Capitular letra="T" />he formal deduction of the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-angulos-opuestos-verticales">vertical angles theorem</ConceptLink> is established by the <ConceptLink targetId="metodo-directo" isDependency={true}>direct method</ConceptLink>, applying the synthetic definition of adjacent <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-suplementarios" isDependency={true}>supplementary angles</ConceptLink> and the reflexive property of angular <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruence</ConceptLink> (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-4" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.4</ConceptLink>).

### Step-by-step formal proof

<ProofStep number={1} target="supp12" title="First pair of adjacent supplementary angles">
Given the intersecting <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>lines</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="lineL" color="carbon">$l$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="lineM" color="carbon">$m$</VisualBind> meeting at the <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>point</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="O" color="carbon">$O$</VisualBind>, the <ConceptLink targetId="punto">points</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="A" color="carbon">$A$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="Ap" color="carbon">$A'$</VisualBind> on line $l$ satisfy the <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre" isDependency={true}>betweenness relation</ConceptLink> $A * O * A'$, determining the opposite <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta" isDependency={true}>rays</ConceptLink> $\overrightarrow{OA}$ and $\overrightarrow{OA'}$. The ray $\overrightarrow{OB}$ determined by point <VisualBind element="B" color="carbon">$B$</VisualBind> on line $m$ forms two adjacent <ConceptLink targetId="angulo" isDependency={true}>angles</ConceptLink>: <VisualBind element="angle1" color="pavo">$\alpha = \angle AOB = \angle(\overrightarrow{OA}, \overrightarrow{OB})$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="angle2" color="musgo">$\beta = \angle BOA' = \angle(\overrightarrow{OB}, \overrightarrow{OA'})$</VisualBind>. By sharing the common ray $\overrightarrow{OB}$ while their remaining sides are opposite rays along line $l$, $\alpha$ and $\beta$ form an adjacent <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-suplementarios" isDependency={true}>supplementary</ConceptLink> pair:

$$
\alpha \text{ is an adjacent supplement to } \beta
$$
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={2} target="supp23" title="Second pair of adjacent supplementary angles">
Considering line $m$, points <VisualBind element="B" color="carbon">$B$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="Bp" color="carbon">$B'$</VisualBind> satisfy $B * O * B'$, determining the opposite <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta">rays</ConceptLink> $\overrightarrow{OB}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB'}$. The ray $\overrightarrow{OA'}$ on line $l$ forms with them the adjacent <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="angle2" color="musgo">$\beta = \angle BOA' = \angle(\overrightarrow{OB}, \overrightarrow{OA'})$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="angle3" color="pavo">$\alpha' = \angle A'OB' = \angle(\overrightarrow{OA'}, \overrightarrow{OB'})$</VisualBind>. Since they share ray $\overrightarrow{OA'}$ and their other sides are opposite rays along line $m$, $\alpha'$ and $\beta$ are adjacent <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-suplementarios" isDependency={true}>supplements</ConceptLink>:

$$
\alpha' \text{ is an adjacent supplement to } \beta
$$
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={3} target="angle2" title="Reflexivity of the common adjacent angle">
By the angle congruence axiom (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-4" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.4</ConceptLink>), every <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angle</ConceptLink> is congruent to itself. In particular, for the common angle <VisualBind element="angle2" color="musgo">$\beta$</VisualBind>, <ConceptLink targetId="reflexividad">reflexivity</ConceptLink> holds:

$$
\beta \cong \beta
$$
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={4} target="alpha" title="Congruence of supplementary angles">
Since both <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="angle1" color="pavo">$\alpha$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="angle3" color="pavo">$\alpha'$</VisualBind> are adjacent supplements to the same angle <VisualBind element="angle2" color="musgo">$\beta$</VisualBind>, and $\beta \cong \beta$, the <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-suplementarios" isDependency={true}>supplementary angle congruence theorem</ConceptLink> (*Satz 13* of <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">Hilbert</ConceptLink>) guarantees that their supplements are mutually <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink>:

$$
\alpha \cong \alpha'
$$
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={5} target="beta" title="Extension to the complementary opposite pair">
Applying symmetric reasoning to the remaining adjacent angles with respect to the supplementary angle <VisualBind element="angle3" color="pavo">$\alpha'$</VisualBind>, it follows that the second pair of <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> vertically opposite at the <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertex</ConceptLink> formed by <VisualBind element="angle2" color="musgo">$\beta = \angle BOA'$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="angle4" color="musgo">$\beta' = \angle B'OA$</VisualBind> satisfies:

$$
\beta \cong \beta'
$$
</ProofStep>
