<Capitular letra="L" />et <VisualBind element="lineL" color="carbon">$l$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="lineM" color="carbon">$m$</VisualBind> be two intersecting secant <ConceptLink targetId="recta">lines</ConceptLink> meeting at a <ConceptLink targetId="punto">point</ConceptLink> $O$. The <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-angulos-opuestos-verticales" isDependency={true}>vertical angles theorem</ConceptLink> is demonstrated: any pair of vertical opposite angles formed at the intersection are <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruent</ConceptLink> to each other.

<Separador />

### Step-by-step proof

<ProofStep number={1} target="supp12" title="Supplementary adjacent angles on the first line">
The intersecting lines $l$ and $m$ define four <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> around vertex $O$. Angles <VisualBind element="angle1" color="terracota">$\angle 1$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="angle2" color="pavo">$\angle 2$</VisualBind> are arranged adjacently along line $l$, forming a <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-suplementarios">straight angle</ConceptLink>:

<Formula>
  $$ m(\angle 1) + m(\angle 2) = 180^\circ $$
</Formula>
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={2} target="supp23" title="Supplementary adjacent angles on the second line">
Analogously, along line $m$, adjacent angles <VisualBind element="angle2" color="pavo">$\angle 2$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="angle3" color="terracota">$\angle 3$</VisualBind> are <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-suplementarios">supplementary</ConceptLink>:

<Formula>
  $$ m(\angle 2) + m(\angle 3) = 180^\circ $$
</Formula>
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={3} target="angle2" title="Equality of angle sums">
Since both angle sums equal a straight angle ($180^\circ$), the equality of real measures implies:

<Formula>
  $$ m(\angle 1) + m(\angle 2) = m(\angle 2) + m(\angle 3) $$
</Formula>
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={4} target="congruence13" title="Subtraction of the common angle">
Applying <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-4" isDependency={true}>angle congruence axioms</ConceptLink>, subtracting common angle <VisualBind element="angle2" color="pavo">$\angle 2$</VisualBind> from both sides yields the congruence of the first pair of vertical angles:

<Formula>
  $$ \angle 1 \cong \angle 3 $$
</Formula>
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={5} target="angle4" title="Proof for the second vertical pair">
An identical argument applied to the adjacent pair formed by <VisualBind element="angle3" color="terracota">$\angle 3$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="angle4" color="pavo">$\angle 4$</VisualBind> concludes that <VisualBind element="angle2" color="pavo">$\angle 2$</VisualBind> $\cong$ <VisualBind element="angle4" color="pavo">$\angle 4$</VisualBind>.
</ProofStep>
