<Capitular letra="T" />he proof of the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-angulos-opuestos-verticales">vertical angles theorem</ConceptLink> follows the <ConceptLink targetId="metodo-directo" isDependency={true}>direct method</ConceptLink>: each angle of an opposite pair is <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-suplementarios" isDependency={true}>supplementary</ConceptLink> to one and the same angle, and the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-suplementos" isDependency={true}>supplement congruence theorem</ConceptLink>, together with the <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-reflexiva">reflexivity</ConceptLink> of angle <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruence</ConceptLink> (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-4" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.4</ConceptLink>), yields the congruence.

<Separador />

### Step-by-step proof

<ProofStep number={1} target="supp12" title="First pair of supplementary angles">
Let <VisualBind element="lineL" color="carbon">$\ell$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="lineM" color="carbon">$m$</VisualBind> be two <ConceptLink targetId="secante">intersecting</ConceptLink> <ConceptLink targetId="recta" isDependency={true}>lines</ConceptLink> meeting at the <ConceptLink targetId="punto" isDependency={true}>point</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="O" color="carbon">$O$</VisualBind>. The points <VisualBind element="A" color="carbon">$A$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="Ap" color="carbon">$A'$</VisualBind> of $\ell$ satisfy the <ConceptLink targetId="estar-entre" isDependency={true}>betweenness</ConceptLink> relation $A * O * A'$, so $\overrightarrow{OA}$ and $\overrightarrow{OA'}$ are <ConceptLink targetId="semirrectas-opuestas" isDependency={true}>opposite rays</ConceptLink>. The <ConceptLink targetId="angulo" isDependency={true}>angles</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="angle1" color="pavo">$\alpha = \angle AOB$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="angle2" color="musgo">$\beta = \angle BOA'$</VisualBind> share the <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta" isDependency={true}>ray</ConceptLink> $\overrightarrow{OB}$ and their other sides are opposite rays; by the definition of <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-suplementarios" isDependency={true}>supplementary angles</ConceptLink>, $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are supplementary.
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={2} target="supp23" title="Second pair of supplementary angles">
The points <VisualBind element="B" color="carbon">$B$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="Bp" color="carbon">$B'$</VisualBind> of $m$ satisfy $B * O * B'$, so $\overrightarrow{OB}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB'}$ are <ConceptLink targetId="semirrectas-opuestas">opposite rays</ConceptLink>. The <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="angle2" color="musgo">$\beta = \angle BOA'$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="angle3" color="pavo">$\alpha' = \angle A'OB'$</VisualBind> share the ray $\overrightarrow{OA'}$ and their other sides are opposite; hence $\alpha'$ and $\beta$ are <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-suplementarios">supplementary</ConceptLink>.
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={3} target="angle2" title="Reflexivity of the common angle">
By the <ConceptLink targetId="relacion-reflexiva">reflexivity</ConceptLink> of angle <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink> (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-4" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.4</ConceptLink>), the common angle <VisualBind element="angle2" color="musgo">$\beta$</VisualBind> is congruent to itself:

$$
\beta \cong \beta
$$
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={4} target="alpha" title="Congruence of the first opposite pair">
<VisualBind element="angle1" color="pavo">$\alpha$</VisualBind> is supplementary to <VisualBind element="angle2" color="musgo">$\beta$</VisualBind> (step 1), <VisualBind element="angle3" color="pavo">$\alpha'$</VisualBind> is supplementary to $\beta$ (step 2) and $\beta \cong \beta$ (step 3). By the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-suplementos" isDependency={true}>supplement congruence theorem</ConceptLink> (<ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">Hilbert</ConceptLink>, §6), supplements of congruent angles are <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink>:

$$
\alpha \cong \alpha'
$$
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={5} target="beta" title="Congruence of the second opposite pair">
The angles <VisualBind element="angle2" color="musgo">$\beta = \angle BOA'$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="angle4" color="musgo">$\beta' = \angle B'OA$</VisualBind> are both <ConceptLink targetId="angulos-suplementarios">supplementary</ConceptLink> to <VisualBind element="angle1" color="pavo">$\alpha = \angle AOB$</VisualBind>: $\beta$ by step 1, and $\beta'$ because it shares with $\alpha$ the ray $\overrightarrow{OA}$ and their other sides, $\overrightarrow{OB'}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB}$, are opposite ($B * O * B'$). Since $\alpha \cong \alpha$ (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-4">Axiom III.4</ConceptLink>), the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-suplementos">supplement congruence theorem</ConceptLink> gives:

$$
\beta \cong \beta'
$$
</ProofStep>
