<Capitular letra="T" />he <ConceptLink targetId="metodo-directo" isDependency={true}>direct deductive proof</ConceptLink> of the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-triangulo-isosceles">isosceles triangle theorem</ConceptLink> attributed to <ConceptLink targetId="pappus">Pappus of Alexandria</ConceptLink> (formally adopted by <ConceptLink targetId="hilbert">David Hilbert</ConceptLink> in §5, Satz 11, of the *Grundlagen der Geometrie*) establishes the equality of the <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-base">base angles</ConceptLink> by comparing the <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangle</ConceptLink> with itself under a transposition of its <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertices</ConceptLink>.

This reasoning highlights the intrinsic <ConceptLink targetId="simetria">symmetry</ConceptLink> of the figure and directly applies <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-5" isDependency={true}>Hilbert's Axiom III.5</ConceptLink> of angular <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruence</ConceptLink> without requiring auxiliary constructions.

### Step-by-step formal proof

<ProofStep number={1} target="step-triangulo" title="Hypothesis of lateral congruence">
We consider <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangle</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="polyABC" color="carbon">$\triangle ABC$</VisualBind>, in which the two lateral <ConceptLink targetId="lado" isDependency={true}>sides</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="ladoAB" color="carbon">$\overline{AB}$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="ladoAC" color="carbon">$\overline{AC}$</VisualBind> are mutually <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink> by initial hypothesis:

$$
\overline{AB} \cong \overline{AC}
$$

The bottom <ConceptLink targetId="segmento" isDependency={true}>segment</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="ladoBC" color="carbon">$\overline{BC}$</VisualBind> acts as the <ConceptLink targetId="base">base</ConceptLink> of the triangle between <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertices</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="pB" color="carbon">$B$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="pC" color="carbon">$C$</VisualBind>.
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={2} target="step-angulo-vertice" title="Reflexivity of the vertex angle">
At the upper <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertex</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="pA" color="terracota">$A$</VisualBind>, the interior <ConceptLink targetId="angulo" isDependency={true}>angle</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="anguloA" color="ocre">$\angle BAC$</VisualBind> coincides with itself. By the axiom of angle congruence and transport (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-4" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.4</ConceptLink>), every angle satisfies the <ConceptLink targetId="reflexividad">reflexivity</ConceptLink> of congruence when the order of its bounding <ConceptLink targetId="semirrecta" isDependency={true}>rays</ConceptLink> is reversed:

$$
\angle BAC \cong \angle CAB
$$
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={3} target="step-correspondencia" title="Pappus correspondence and satisfaction of III.5">
We establish a <ConceptLink targetId="biyeccion">one-to-one correspondence</ConceptLink> between the <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertices</ConceptLink> of <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangle</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="polyABC" color="carbon">$\triangle ABC$</VisualBind> and those of its transposed version $\triangle ACB$, swapping the <ConceptLink targetId="base">base</ConceptLink> endpoints (<VisualBind element="pB" color="carbon">$B \leftrightarrow C$</VisualBind>, <VisualBind element="pC" color="carbon">$C \leftrightarrow B$</VisualBind>) and keeping the upper <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertex</ConceptLink> fixed (<VisualBind element="pA" color="terracota">$A \leftrightarrow A$</VisualBind>):

$$
A \longleftrightarrow A, \quad B \longleftrightarrow C, \quad C \longleftrightarrow B
$$

Under this correspondence, the three conditions of <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-5" isDependency={true}>Hilbert's Axiom III.5</ConceptLink> are immediately satisfied:
1. First pair of corresponding <ConceptLink targetId="lado" isDependency={true}>sides</ConceptLink>: <VisualBind element="ladoAB" color="carbon">$\overline{AB}$</VisualBind> $\cong$ <VisualBind element="ladoAC" color="carbon">$\overline{AC}$</VisualBind> (by initial hypothesis).
2. Included <ConceptLink targetId="angulo" isDependency={true}>angle</ConceptLink> between them: <VisualBind element="anguloA" color="ocre">$\angle BAC$</VisualBind> $\cong$ <VisualBind element="anguloA" color="ocre">$\angle CAB$</VisualBind> (by <ConceptLink targetId="reflexividad">reflexivity</ConceptLink> of the angle at <VisualBind element="pA" color="terracota">$A$</VisualBind>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-4" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.4</ConceptLink>).
3. Second pair of corresponding sides: <VisualBind element="ladoAC" color="carbon">$\overline{AC}$</VisualBind> $\cong$ <VisualBind element="ladoAB" color="carbon">$\overline{AB}$</VisualBind> (by the <ConceptLink targetId="simetria">symmetry</ConceptLink> of segment <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruence</ConceptLink>, derived from <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-1" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.1</ConceptLink> and <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-2" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.2</ConceptLink>).
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={4} target="step-angulos-base" title="Deduction of base angle congruence">
Because two <ConceptLink targetId="lado" isDependency={true}>sides</ConceptLink> and their included <ConceptLink targetId="angulo" isDependency={true}>angle</ConceptLink> are <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia" isDependency={true}>congruent</ConceptLink>, <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-5" isDependency={true}>Hilbert's Axiom III.5</ConceptLink> directly implies that the remaining angles opposite those sides are congruent. In particular, for the <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-base">base angles</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="anguloB" color="mora">$\angle ABC$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="anguloC" color="mora">$\angle ACB$</VisualBind> at <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertices</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="pB" color="carbon">$B$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="pC" color="carbon">$C$</VisualBind>:

$$
\angle ABC \cong \angle ACB
$$

It is formally proven that in any <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo" isDependency={true}>triangle</ConceptLink> with two congruent <ConceptLink targetId="lado" isDependency={true}>sides</ConceptLink>, the <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-base">base angles</ConceptLink> are mutually congruent.
</ProofStep>
