<Capitular letra="F" />or any <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink> $\triangle ABC$ possessing two <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruent</ConceptLink> sides, the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-triangulo-isosceles" isDependency={true}>isosceles triangle theorem</ConceptLink> is demonstrated. The proof establishes that the <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> opposite to those congruent sides (the base angles) are congruent to each other.

<Separador />

### Step-by-step proof

<ProofStep number={1} target="triangulo-base" title="Isosceles triangle hypothesis">
Let <VisualBind element="triangulo-base" color="carbon">$\triangle ABC$</VisualBind> be a triangle with sides <VisualBind element="lado-ab" color="terracota">$\overline{AB}$</VisualBind> and <VisualBind element="lado-ac" color="pavo">$\overline{AC}$</VisualBind> of equal length:

<Formula>
  $$ \overline{AB} \cong \overline{AC} $$
</Formula>
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={2} target="correspondencia" title="Establishment of vertex self-correspondence">
Consider triangle $\triangle ABC$ and a second copy with reversed base vertices: $\triangle ACB$. The correspondence between vertices is given by $(A, B, C) \leftrightarrow (A, C, B)$.
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={3} target="criterio-lal" title="Verification of the SAS congruence criterion">
Comparing corresponding elements of $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle ACB$:
1. Side <VisualBind element="lado-ab" color="terracota">$\overline{AB}$</VisualBind> is congruent to side <VisualBind element="lado-ac" color="pavo">$\overline{AC}$</VisualBind> by hypothesis.
2. Included <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angle</ConceptLink> <VisualBind element="angulo-a" color="carbon">$\angle BAC$</VisualBind> is identical to itself ($\angle CAB$).
3. Side <VisualBind element="lado-ac" color="pavo">$\overline{AC}$</VisualBind> is congruent to side <VisualBind element="lado-ab" color="terracota">$\overline{AB}$</VisualBind> by hypothesis.

By <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-1" isDependency={true}>SAS congruence axioms</ConceptLink> (Side-Angle-Side), <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">triangle congruence</ConceptLink> is deduced:

<Formula>
  $$ \triangle ABC \cong \triangle ACB $$
</Formula>
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={4} target="conclusion" title="Equality of base angles">
Since both triangles are congruent, their corresponding parts are congruent. In particular, angles opposite equal sides in the correspondence are identical: the angle at <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertex</ConceptLink> $B$ (<VisualBind element="angulo-b" color="terracota">$\angle ABC$</VisualBind>) is congruent to the angle at vertex $C$ (<VisualBind element="angulo-c" color="pavo">$\angle ACB$</VisualBind>):

<Formula>
  $$ \angle ABC \cong \angle ACB $$
</Formula>
</ProofStep>
