<Capitular letra="G" />iven a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink> $\triangle ABC$ having two <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruent</ConceptLink> sides, the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-triangulo-isosceles">isosceles triangle theorem</ConceptLink> is demonstrated. The proof establishes that the <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> opposite those congruent sides (the base angles) are mutually congruent.

<Separador />

### Step-by-step proof

<ProofStep number={1} target="step-triangulo" title="Isosceles triangle hypothesis">
Let $\triangle ABC$ be an isosceles triangle with sides $\overline{AB}$ and $\overline{AC}$ of equal metric length:

<Formula>
  $$ \overline{AB} \cong \overline{AC} $$
</Formula>
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={2} target="step-bisectriz" title="Construction of the vertex angle bisector">
Draw the angle bisector ray from vertex $A$, intersecting the base side $\overline{BC}$ at point $D$. By definition of <ConceptLink targetId="demo-existencia-bisectriz">angle bisector</ConceptLink>, it divides $\angle BAC$ into two congruent angles:

<Formula>
  $$ \angle BAD \cong \angle CAD $$
</Formula>
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={3} target="step-congruencia" title="Triangle congruence via the SAS criterion">
Comparing subtriangles $\triangle ABD$ and $\triangle ACD$:
1. $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{AC}$ by the initial hypothesis.
2. $\angle BAD \cong \angle CAD$ by construction of the bisector.
3. The segment $\overline{AD}$ is common to both triangles ($\overline{AD} \cong \overline{AD}$).

By the SAS congruence axiom (<ConceptLink targetId="axioma-congruencia-5" isDependency={true}>Axiom III.5</ConceptLink>), triangle congruence is established:

<Formula>
  $$ \triangle ABD \cong \triangle ACD $$
</Formula>
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={4} target="step-angulos-base" title="Equality of the base angles">
Since both triangles are congruent, all corresponding homologous elements are congruent. In particular, the angles at the base vertices satisfy:

<Formula>
  $$ \angle ABC \cong \angle ACB $$
</Formula>
</ProofStep>
