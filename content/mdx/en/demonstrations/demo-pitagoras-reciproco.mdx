<Capitular letra="T" />he proof of the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-pitagoras-reciproco">converse of the Pythagorean theorem</ConceptLink> follows the <ConceptLink targetId="metodo-directo" isDependency={true}>direct method</ConceptLink> formulated by <ConceptLink targetId="euclides">Euclid</ConceptLink> in Proposition I.48 of the <ConceptLink targetId="elementos-euclides">Elements</ConceptLink>. The proof constructs an auxiliary <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo-rectangulo" isDependency={true}>right triangle</ConceptLink> whose <ConceptLink targetId="cateto">legs</ConceptLink> are <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruent</ConceptLink> to the sides containing the angle under study. Applying the direct <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-pitagoras" isDependency={true}>Pythagorean theorem</ConceptLink> to this auxiliary triangle —which depends on <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides" isDependency={true}>Euclid's parallel axiom</ConceptLink>— and combining it with the initial hypothesis yields the congruence of the third side, completing the proof via the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-lll" isDependency={true}>SSS congruence theorem</ConceptLink>.

### Step-by-step demonstration

<ProofStep number={1} title="Hypothesis on the initial triangle">
Consider an arbitrary <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo">triangle</ConceptLink> $\triangle ABC$ whose <ConceptLink targetId="lado">sides</ConceptLink> have <ConceptLink targetId="longitud">lengths</ConceptLink> $a = |BC|$, $b = |AC|$, and $c = |AB|$.

By initial hypothesis, these three lengths satisfy the algebraic Pythagorean relation:

$$
a^2 + b^2 = c^2
$$

which is equivalently expressed in terms of the vertices as $|BC|^2 + |AC|^2 = |AB|^2$.

The deductive goal is to prove that the <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angle</ConceptLink> $\angle ACB$ opposite side $\overline{AB}$ is a <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto">right angle</ConceptLink>.
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={2} title="Construction of the auxiliary right triangle">
In the same <ConceptLink targetId="plano">plane</ConceptLink>, we construct an auxiliary <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo-rectangulo">right triangle</ConceptLink> $\triangle DEF$ having a <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto">right angle</ConceptLink> at its <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertex</ConceptLink> $D$, such that $\angle EDF$ is right.

Along the rays containing the <ConceptLink targetId="cateto">legs</ConceptLink> $\overline{DE}$ and $\overline{DF}$, we determine their <ConceptLink targetId="longitud">lengths</ConceptLink> so that they coincide exactly with those of $\overline{BC}$ and $\overline{AC}$:

$$
|DE| = a = |BC| \quad \text{and} \quad |DF| = b = |AC|
$$

By the definition of congruence of <ConceptLink targetId="segmento">segments</ConceptLink>, this establishes that $\overline{DE} \cong \overline{BC}$ and $\overline{DF} \cong \overline{AC}$.
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={3} title="Application of the direct Pythagorean theorem to the auxiliary triangle">
Since $\triangle DEF$ is by construction a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo-rectangulo">right triangle</ConceptLink> with a right angle at vertex $D$, it satisfies the conditions of the direct <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-pitagoras" isDependency={true}>Pythagorean theorem</ConceptLink> in the <ConceptLink targetId="sistema-euclidiano">Euclidean system</ConceptLink>, which rests on <ConceptLink targetId="axioma-paralelas-euclides" isDependency={true}>Euclid's parallel axiom</ConceptLink>.

Applying the theorem to the <ConceptLink targetId="hipotenusa">hypotenuse</ConceptLink> $\overline{EF}$:

$$
|EF|^2 = |DE|^2 + |DF|^2
$$

Substituting the leg values fixed in the construction ($|DE| = a$ and $|DF| = b$):

$$
|EF|^2 = a^2 + b^2
$$
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={4} title="Substitution of the metric hypothesis and hypotenuse congruence">
By the hypothesis established on $\overline{AB}$, we know that $a^2 + b^2 = c^2 = |AB|^2$.

Substituting this equality into the quadratic relation obtained for the auxiliary hypotenuse:

$$
|EF|^2 = |AB|^2
$$

Since lengths are positive real quantities, extracting the positive square root preserves equality:

$$
|EF| = |AB|
$$

which proves that the segments are congruent: $\overline{EF} \cong \overline{AB}$.
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={5} title="Application of the SSS congruence criterion">
We now compare the original triangle $\triangle ABC$ with the auxiliary triangle $\triangle DEF$. Their three pairs of corresponding <ConceptLink targetId="lado">sides</ConceptLink> are mutually congruent:
1. First pair of sides: $\overline{BC} \cong \overline{DE}$ (by construction, $|BC| = |DE| = a$).
2. Second pair of sides: $\overline{AC} \cong \overline{DF}$ (by construction, $|AC| = |DF| = b$).
3. Third pair of sides: $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{EF}$ (proven in the preceding step).

By the <ConceptLink targetId="teorema-congruencia-lll" isDependency={true}>SSS congruence theorem</ConceptLink>, since the measures of all three pairs of sides match, both triangles are congruent under the correspondence $C \mapsto D$, $B \mapsto E$, $A \mapsto F$:

$$
\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF
$$
</ProofStep>

<ProofStep number={6} title="Conclusion of orthogonality and final deduction">
From the <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia-triangulos">congruence of triangles</ConceptLink> $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF$, it follows that all corresponding <ConceptLink targetId="angulo">angles</ConceptLink> are <ConceptLink targetId="congruencia">congruent</ConceptLink>. In particular, comparing the angles at <ConceptLink targetId="vertice">vertices</ConceptLink> $C$ and $D$:

$$
\angle ACB \cong \angle EDF
$$

Since angle $\angle EDF$ is a <ConceptLink targetId="angulo-recto">right angle</ConceptLink> by the initial auxiliary construction, it follows that $\angle ACB$ is also a right angle.

Consequently, triangle $\triangle ABC$ has a right angle at $C$ and is a <ConceptLink targetId="triangulo-rectangulo">right triangle</ConceptLink> with hypotenuse $\overline{AB}$, completing the proof.
</ProofStep>
